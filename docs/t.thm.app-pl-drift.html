Theorem SG.2 (Drift crossover)
Assume Assumption S4 and let \(i=i_N\to\infty\) be a row sequence.
  1. (i)
    Let \(0<\alpha\le1\) and put
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.32} \begin{gathered} d\defeq\frac{c_0}{\sqrt{1-\alpha}},\quad i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\defeq\Big[\frac{BN^{\alpha-1}}{\sw2d^2\log^2\gamma}\Big]^{1/\alpha},\quad a_{\mathfrak d}\defeq d\,a_N\log\gamma\quad(\alpha<1);\\ d\defeq\frac23c_0,\quad i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\defeq\frac{B}{\sw2d^2\log^3\gamma},\quad a_{\mathfrak d}\defeq d\,a_N(\log\gamma)^{3/2}\quad(\alpha=1). \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/a_{\mathfrak d}\to1+x^{-\alpha/2}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to0\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to\infty\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\log\big((N/i)^\alpha\big)\ \ (\alpha<1),\qquad \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\big[\log(N/i)\big]^{3/2}\ \ (\alpha=1). \]
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\alpha>1\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/b_N\to\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(x^{-\alpha})\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to0\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to\infty\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d_\alpha\sqrt{c_NK_\alpha}\,N^{-1/2}\,i^{-(\alpha-1)/2}. \]

Definitions and assumptions used

Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
The exponent \(\alpha>0\) and the momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) are fixed. Along a sequence of positive integers \(N\) and \(B=B_N\),
\[ N\to\infty,\qquad B\to\infty,\qquad \gamma=\frac NB\to\infty, \]
and the spectrum is \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\) for \(1\le i\le N\), with amplitudes \(c_N>0\), with \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\) and \(\Nout=\Nin=N\).
This theorem gives the drift column of Table 1 for \(\bmom>0\), with the crossover between its two rows. Rows below the crossover index are resolved: \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\), which is \(\mathfrak d_i\sim\sqrt{\mu_iB_{\rm eff}/(2N)}\), the square-root law of the large-batch section. Rows above it are not whitened individually, and their drift \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), with \(a_N=\sqrt{c_N}N^{-\alpha/2}\), is a profile of \(i/N\) that does not depend on \(\bmom\); for \(i\ll N\) it decays only logarithmically (\(\alpha\le1\)) or like \(i^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) (\(\alpha>1\)), far more slowly than \(\sqrt{\mu_i}\). For \(\alpha\le1\) the two laws add at the crossover, so that \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/a_{\mathfrak d}\to1+x^{-\alpha/2}\); for \(\alpha>1\) the crossover is described by the profile \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\), which keeps the momentum. This sketch also proves the drift half of the uniform approximation behind Corollary SG.1.
Proof sketch.
The drift is the sum of a head and a tail, each with an explicit limit (Section G.5). Changing variables from \(u\) to the participation \(m\) gives (Lemma SG.8)
\[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)=\frac1{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\gamma\mathcal A(m)\,\mathcal B_i(m)\,E(m)\vartheta_N(m)\,\dif m, \]
\[ \mathcal B_i(\gamma p)=a_N\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p\,V_N(p)}}\,\frac{V_N(p)}{V_N(p)+x^\alpha},\quad x=\frac iN, \]
where \(\vartheta_N=-\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log s_N\) comes from the Jacobian of \(u^2=s_N(m)\), and \(E\vartheta_N\) is within \(C(1/m+1/(\gamma-m))\) of one for \(m\ge M\). On \(m>M\) the averaging of Section G.5 replaces \(\mathcal A\) by \(\sqrt{2\pi}\), the factor \(E\vartheta_N\) is replaced by one, and \(V_N\) by \(V_\alpha\), all with relative error \(C/M+o(1)\) uniformly in the row; the tail is therefore \(a_N\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\), the profile with the integral restricted to \(p\ge M/\gamma\) (Lemma SG.10). The head \(m\le M\) is at most \(C_M\sqrt{\mu_i/\gamma}\) for every row and equals \(\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}(1+o(1))\) on resolved rows (Lemma SG.11). For bulk rows, \(i/N\to x_0>0\), the head is \(o(a_N)\) and \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(x_0)\).
For \(\alpha\le1\) the crossover is the row at which head and tail are equal. With \(c_N=1\), write the head in units of \(a_N\) as
\[ W\defeq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}}{a_N}=\sw2^{-1/2}e^{(T-\log\gamma)/2},\qquad T=\log(x^{-\alpha})\ (\alpha<1),\quad T=\log(1/x)\ (\alpha=1), \]
and recall \(\Psi_\alpha(x)\sim dT^\nu\) with \(\nu=1\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(\nu=3/2\) for \(\alpha=1\) (Proposition SG.5). The cutoff \(p\ge M/\gamma\) removes a part of the profile that is negligible as long as \(W\lesssim(\log\gamma)^\nu\), and when \(W\gg(\log\gamma)^\nu\) both profiles are \(o(W)\) (Lemma SG.12). In every case, after \(M\to\infty\),
\[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)=a_N\big(W+\Psi_\alpha(x)\big)\big(1+o(1)\big), \]
which is the drift approximant of (SG.29). The index \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) is where \(W=d(\log\gamma)^\nu\): at \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to x_0\) one has \(W=d(\log\gamma)^\nu x_0^{-\alpha/2}(1+o(1))\) and \(T=\log\gamma+O(\log\log\gamma)\), so both terms are multiples of \(a_{\mathfrak d}=d\,a_N(\log\gamma)^\nu\), which gives \(1+x_0^{-\alpha/2}\). On the rows \(i\asymp i_\#\), where \(i_\#=(c_N\resscale)^{1/\alpha}\) is the no-momentum threshold of Proposition SG.6, the drift exceeds the no-momentum drift scale \((\gamma\resscale)^{-1/2}\) by a factor of order \(\log\gamma\), which comes from the growth \(\log Q_m\sim2\abs{\log\bmom}\,m\) of the momentum participation (Remark SG.2); this is why \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) lies below the no-momentum threshold \(i_\#\) by a factor of order \((\log\gamma)^{-2/\alpha}\) (Proposition SG.6). The main-text display (15), which also writes \(i_\#\), is for \(\bmom>0\) and \(\alpha\le1\) of the order of \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) itself; in this chapter \(i_\#\) always means the no-momentum threshold.
For \(\alpha>1\) the crossover sits at participation of order one, where the weights cannot be averaged, so its profile keeps the momentum. At \(i\asymp B\) put \(y=(i_{\mathrm c}/i)^\alpha\). The scaling \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\sim(Bm/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) of Section G.5 gives \(\Lambda_N(m)\mu_i\to ym^\alpha\) and \(m\Lambda_N'(m)/\Lambda_N(m)\to\alpha\), so that on compact sets of \(m\) the drift integrand, divided by \(b_N\), converges to that of ((SG.46))
\[ \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\mathcal A(m)E(m)\vartheta_\infty(m)\,m^{-\frac{\alpha+1}2}\,\frac{ym^\alpha}{1+ym^\alpha}\,\dif m, \]
while the head bounds and the tail bound \(CK_\alpha^{-\alpha/2}M^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) make small and large \(m\) negligible (Lemma SG.13). On the two windows the head and the tail are estimated directly, as for \(\alpha\le1\) (Lemma SG.13), and the identities \(b_N\sqrt y=\sqrt{\mu_i/\gamma}\) and \(b_Nd_\alpha y^{(\alpha-1)/(2\alpha)}=a_Nd_\alpha\sqrt{K_\alpha}(i/N)^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) show that the results match the two endpoints of \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\) (Proposition SG.5(ii)).
