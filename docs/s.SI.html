This section studies the row recursion (14) of the main text in continuous time, with drift and volatility coefficients that are fixed numbers. The coefficients are adopted: they are inputs of a model and are not proved to be the coefficients of the algorithm at the times and ridges where the model is used. The one place in the appendix where the actual dynamics are proved is Theorem SE.1 (with its row form Corollary SE.1); it holds at a fixed positive normalized ridge, in proportional dimensions and on a finite interval of macroscopic time (Remark SE.1). The model of this section is used for all times and with zero-ridge coefficients, so its conclusions are statements about the model.
The risk of the model solves a nonlinear Volterra equation. Section SI.2 proves that this equation has a unique solution, identifies its limit (the floor), reconstructs the row equations through a change of clock, and bounds first hitting times from below by the forcing and from above by a supersolution. Section SI.3 solves the model exactly when all drift coefficients are equal and proves the comparison of times behind the isotropic rate ratio of Sec. 3.2 of the main text in Theorem SI.6. Section SI.4 collects the power-sum estimates. Section SI.5 introduces the adopted power-law arrays of SignSVD and SignSGD, states the hitting-time orders behind Theorem 5.1 of the main text as Theorem SI.7, and then states the sharper results on which it rests: the order of the hitting time in every phase with its joint window of dimension and accuracy, the exact constants on the two phase boundaries and in saturation, the accuracy-first law for fixed arrays, and the ratio of the two hitting times with its dimension factor. Section SI.6 removes the windows: it introduces the accuracy scale \(\epsilon_N=N^{-(\alpha+\texp-1)}\), gives the hitting times for all joint \((N,\epsilon)\), shows that below \(\epsilon_N\) the ratio freezes at its accuracy-first value, and states the resulting phase map in the accuracy exponent. Section SI.7 and Section SI.8 prove the comparison and kernel lemmas, Section SI.9 proves the phase theorems, and Section SI.10 proves the results of Section SI.6.
Standing conventions. Time \(t\ge0\) is the continuous step count, and the step size \(\lr>0\) is constant. In the notation of Section SE, \(\lr=\macrostep/B\) and \(t=B\macrotime\). The target exponent of the initial row energies is \(\texp\), written \(\beta\) in the main text; \(\beta\) is reserved for the momentum. For positive quantities, \(x\sim y\) means \(x/y\to1\) along the stated limit, and \(x\asymp y\) means that \(x/y\) lies between two positive constants whose dependence is stated. A first hitting time is the infimum of a set of times, and the infimum of the empty set is \(+\infty\). For functions \(g\) and \(h\) on \([0,\infty)\) we write
\[ (g*h)(u)\defeq\int_0^u g(v)\,h(u-v)\,\dif v . \]
A constant called fixed does not depend on the dimension \(N\), the batch \(B\) or the accuracy \(\epsilon\).

SI.1 The deterministic model

Let \((\mu_i,u_i)\) be the eigenpairs of \(\Sigmaout\). For an error matrix \(\Delta\) the row energy of the eigenvector \(u_i\) is \(\norm{\Delta^\T u_i}^2\), and in the half-anisotropic case \(\Sigmain=\Id\) the risk is \(\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\norm{\Delta^\T u_i}^2\) (Section 4).
Definition SI.1 (Deterministic row model)
Let the index set \(\mathcal I\) be \(\{1,\dots,N\}\) for an integer \(N\ge1\), or \(\N\). The deterministic row model consists of the following four items.
  1. (M1)
    Adopted coefficients. Numbers \(\mu_i>0\), \(\mathfrak d_i>0\), \(\mathfrak v_i\ge0\) and initial row energies \(\fQ_i(0)\ge0\), \(i\in\mathcal I\), and a constant step \(\lr>0\) are fixed, with
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.1} F_0\defeq\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mu_i\fQ_i(0)\in(0,\infty),\qquad \mathcal S\defeq\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\frac{\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}{2\mathfrak d_i}<\infty . \end{equation}
    They do not depend on time or on the state. An array carrying the superscript \(\text{s-SVD}\) or \(\text{s-SGD}\), such as \(\mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}\) or \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}\), is an array adopted in this sense, and not a coefficient proved for an algorithm.
  2. (M2)
    Kernel replacement. For an update matrix \({\mathsf S}\) computed at a state \(\Delta\) of positive risk \(\cR(\Delta)\), the conditional row pairing and the conditional raw second moment of the row are replaced by
    \[ \EE\big[\langle\Delta^\T u_i,{\mathsf S}^\T u_i\rangle\,\big|\,\Delta\big]\ \leadsto\ \frac{\mathfrak d_i\,\norm{\Delta^\T u_i}^2}{\sqrt{\cR(\Delta)}},\qquad \EE\big[\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u_i}^2\,\big|\,\Delta\big]\ \leadsto\ \mathfrak v_i . \]
  3. (M3)
    Dynamical closure. The row energies \(\fQ_i(t)\) solve
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.2} \frac{\dif\fQ_i}{\dif t}=-\frac{2\lr\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}}\,\fQ_i(t)+\lr^2\mathfrak v_i,\qquad \fRisk(t)\defeq\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mu_i\fQ_i(t), \end{equation}
    from the initial values of (M1), on an interval starting at \(0\) on which \(\fRisk\) is continuous and positive.
  4. (M4)
    Transfer. A statement about the stochastic algorithm is drawn from the model only through an approximation theorem with its own regime, horizon and observable.
In this appendix the approximation theorem of (M4) is Theorem SE.1.
Within this section the letters \(\mathfrak d_i\) and \(\mathfrak v_i\) denote the numbers of (M1). They are the coefficients of Corollary SE.1 only when that identification is made, which is the content of (M2). The first time at which the model reaches a level \(a>0\) is
\[ t_a\defeq\inf\{t\ge0:\ \fRisk(t)\le a\}, \]
and the main text’s \(t_{4\epsilon}\) is \(t_a\) at \(a=4\epsilon\).
The factors in (SI.2) come from the following identity for one step.
Proposition SI.1 (One step of a row)
Let \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be fixed, let \(u\) be a unit vector in \(\R^{\Nout}\), let \(\lr>0\), and let \({\mathsf S}\) be a random matrix with \(\EE\norm {\mathsf S}_\F^2<\infty\). Then
\[ \EE\norm{(\Delta-\lr {\mathsf S})^\T u}^2-\norm{\Delta^\T u}^2 =-2\lr\,\EE\langle\Delta^\T u,{\mathsf S}^\T u\rangle+\lr^2\,\EE\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u}^2 . \]
Proof
Proof.
Expanding the square gives
\[ \norm{(\Delta-\lr {\mathsf S})^\T u}^2=\norm{\Delta^\T u}^2-2\lr\langle\Delta^\T u,{\mathsf S}^\T u\rangle+\lr^2\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u}^2 . \]
Each term has finite expectation, because \(\abs{\langle\Delta^\T u,{\mathsf S}^\T u\rangle}\le\norm\Delta_\F\norm {\mathsf S}_\F\) and \(\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u}^2\le\norm {\mathsf S}_\F^2\). Taking expectations proves the identity.
□
Applied at the state \(\Delta=\Dt\) to the update \({\mathsf S}\) of step \(t\), with the expectation conditional on the past, the first-order term of Proposition SI.1 is the row pairing and the second-order term is the raw second moment of the row, not its variance. Under (M2) one step of the identity is one step of (14), and (M3) is that recursion with a continuous step count. In the macroscopic time \(\macrotime=t/B\) and with \(\lr=\macrostep/B\), (SI.2) reads
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\big(B\fQ_i\big)=-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,B\fQ_i+\macrostep^2\mathfrak v_i , \]
which is (SE.13). The identity itself does not prove (M2) or (M3).
Hypothesis (M2) is not implied by the positive-ridge theorems. Theorem SE.1 and Corollary SE.1 prove that the algorithm follows (SE.13) at a fixed normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\), in proportional dimensions and on the interval \([0,T_\star]\) of (SE.10), with the coefficients \(\mathfrak d(\mu_i,1)\) and \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\). By Remark SE.1 they say nothing about the zero ridge, about times beyond \(T_\star\), or about an exchange of the ridge and dimension limits. The model of this section uses zero-ridge coefficients for all times, and therefore rests on (M1)–(M3).
Remark SI.1 (Where the adopted coefficients come from)
The power-law arrays of Definition SI.3 are modelled on the coefficients of the main text’s half-anisotropic table in the oversampled regime \(B\ge N\) (Section 4), at zero momentum and zero ridge. For momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), Theorem SG.1 computes the zero-ridge row coefficients in the thin-batch regime \(N/B\to\infty\), and Remark SG.3 inserts them into the row equations as adopted coefficients. The oversampled regime is not covered by that theorem. The resolved-window endpoint of Corollary SG.1(ii),
\[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}{\gamma\,\sw2}},\qquad \mathfrak v_i(0)\to\frac1{\sw2}, \]
has the dependence on \(\mu_i\) and \(\gamma\) of the adopted SignSVD array, up to constant factors, and is the evidence for that array. In the oversampled regime itself, for \(B\gg N\) and rows \(i\ll N\) of the power law, the drift coefficient is \(\mathfrak d_i\sim\sqrt{\mu_iB/(2N)}\), the value in the main text’s table; the adopted SignSVD array is larger by the uniform factor \(\sqrt2\). A uniform factor \(c\) in the drift at a fixed volatility rescales the matched-step clock of SignSVD by \(c^{-2}\), so with the factor removed every ratio of first hitting times below is halved; no exponent and no phase boundary changes. The adopted arrays with momentum reduce to it at a rescaled step (Remark SI.5). The SignSGD array is compared with the corrected mode array of the SignSGD section in Remark SI.4. No statement of this section identifies a trajectory of an algorithm.

SI.2 The Volterra equation of the risk

Throughout this subsection the data of (M1) are fixed. The Volterra equation is written in an intrinsic time \(s\ge0\), related to the step count by the clock of Theorem SI.2. Its forcing and kernel are
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.3} F(s)\defeq\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mu_i\fQ_i(0)\,e^{-2\mathfrak d_is},\qquad k(s)\defeq\frac\lr2\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mu_i\mathfrak v_i\,e^{-2\mathfrak d_is}\quad(s>0),\qquad \bar k\defeq\frac{\lr\mathcal S}2 . \end{equation}
For a countable index set the series defining \(k\) may diverge at \(s=0\); it is finite for \(s>0\) by Lemma SI.1. The equation is
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.4} Q=F+k*\sqrt Q , \end{equation}
and a solution is a continuous nonnegative function on \([0,\infty)\) that satisfies it at every time.
Lemma SI.1 (Forcing and kernel)
Assume (M1). The forcing \(F\) is continuous and strictly decreasing on \([0,\infty)\), with \(F(0)=F_0\), \(F>0\) and \(F(s)\to0\) as \(s\to\infty\). The kernel satisfies
\[ \int_0^\infty k(s)\,\dif s=\bar k,\qquad k(s)\le\frac{\bar k}{e\,s}\quad(s>0). \]
For every \(S'>0\) and every bounded function \(f\) on \([0,S']\), the convolution \(k*f\) is continuous on \([0,S']\), vanishes at \(0\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.5} \sup_{0\le s\le S'}\abs{(k*f)(s)}\le\bar k\,\sup_{0\le s\le S'}\abs{f(s)} . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Each term of \(F\) is continuous and nonincreasing. The tail of the series beyond the index \(M\) is at most \(\frac12\sum_{i>M}\mu_i\fQ_i(0)\) uniformly in \(s\), which tends to zero because \(F_0<\infty\). The partial sums therefore converge uniformly, and \(F\) is continuous. Since \(F_0>0\), some term has a positive coefficient, and that term is strictly decreasing; hence \(F\) is strictly decreasing and positive. Each term tends to zero and the tails are uniformly small, so \(F(s)\to0\).
Integrating each exponential gives
\[ \int_0^\infty k(s)\,\dif s=\frac\lr2\sum_i\frac{\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}{2\mathfrak d_i}=\bar k . \]
The scalar inequality \(be^{-bs}\le1/(es)\), valid for \(b,s>0\), applied with \(b=2\mathfrak d_i\), gives
\[ k(s)=\sum_i\frac{\lr\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}{4\mathfrak d_i}\,(2\mathfrak d_i)e^{-2\mathfrak d_is}\le\frac{\bar k}{es}. \]
For bounded \(f\), the convolution with the \(i\)-th term of \(k\) is
\[ \frac{\lr\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}2\,e^{-2\mathfrak d_is}\int_0^se^{2\mathfrak d_iv}f(v)\,\dif v , \]
which is continuous and vanishes at \(0\). The tail of the sum of these convolutions beyond the index \(M\) is at most \(\sup\abs f\cdot\frac\lr2\sum_{i>M}\mu_i\mathfrak v_i/(2\mathfrak d_i)\), which tends to zero. Uniform convergence gives continuity of \(k*f\), and the same bound for the full sum gives (SI.5).
□
Lemma SI.2 (Tail of a convolution)
Assume (M1), and let \(f\ge0\) be bounded on \([0,\infty)\) with \(\ell_-\defeq\liminf_{s\to\infty}f(s)\) and \(\ell_+\defeq\limsup_{s\to\infty}f(s)\). Then
\[ \bar k\,\ell_-\le\liminf_{s\to\infty}(k*f)(s)\le\limsup_{s\to\infty}(k*f)(s)\le\bar k\,\ell_+ . \]
Proof
Proof.
Fix \(\varepsilon'>0\) and choose \(s_0\) such that \((\ell_--\varepsilon')_+\le f(v)\le\ell_++\varepsilon'\) for \(v\ge s_0\). For \(s>s_0\) split the convolution as
\[ (k*f)(s)=\int_0^{s-s_0}k(v)f(s-v)\,\dif v+\int_{s-s_0}^sk(v)f(s-v)\,\dif v . \]
The first integral lies between \((\ell_--\varepsilon')_+\int_0^{s-s_0}k\) and \((\ell_++\varepsilon')\int_0^{s-s_0}k\). The second lies between \(0\) and \(\sup f\cdot\int_{s-s_0}^\infty k\), which tends to zero because \(k\) is integrable. Letting \(s\to\infty\) and then \(\varepsilon'\to0\) proves the claim.
□
The next lemma is the comparison principle used throughout the section. It is stated for a general kernel, because the power-law analysis applies it to rescaled kernels.
Lemma SI.3 (Comparison)
Let \(S'>0\), let \(\mathsf k\ge0\) be integrable on \((0,S')\), let \(G\ge0\) be continuous on \([0,S']\), and let \(m>0\). Let \(x\) and \(y\) be continuous and nonnegative on \([0,S']\), with \(y\ge m\) and
\[ x\le G+\mathsf k*\sqrt x,\qquad y\ge G+\mathsf k*\sqrt y\qquad\text{on }[0,S']. \]
Then \(x\le y\) on \([0,S']\).
Proof
Proof.
Put \(d\defeq(x-y)_+\). Where \(x>y\) one has
\[ \sqrt x-\sqrt y=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt x+\sqrt y}\le\frac{d}{\sqrt m}, \]
so \((\sqrt x-\sqrt y)_+\le d/\sqrt m\) everywhere. Subtracting the two inequalities and using \(\mathsf k\ge0\) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.6} d\le\frac1{\sqrt m}\,\mathsf k*d\qquad\text{on }[0,S']. \end{equation}
Since \(\mathsf k\) is integrable, there is \(\iota>0\) with \(m^{-1/2}\int_0^{\iota}\mathsf k\le\frac12\). Suppose \(d=0\) on \([0,j\iota]\cap[0,S']\) for some integer \(j\ge0\). For \(s\) in \([j\iota,(j+1)\iota]\cap[0,S']\) the integrand of \(\mathsf k*d\) vanishes for \(v\le j\iota\), so (SI.6) gives
\[ d(s)\le\frac1{\sqrt m}\int_{j\iota}^s\mathsf k(s-v)\,d(v)\,\dif v\le\frac12\sup_{[j\iota,(j+1)\iota]\cap[0,S']}d . \]
Taking the supremum over \(s\) shows that this supremum vanishes. Finitely many steps cover \([0,S']\).
□
Theorem SI.1 (Existence, uniqueness and floor)
Assume (M1). The Volterra equation (SI.4) has a unique solution \(Q\) on \([0,\infty)\). It satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.7} 0<F(s)\le Q(s)\le M\defeq\Big(\frac{\bar k+\sqrt{\bar k^2+4F_0}}2\Big)^2\qquad(s\ge0), \end{equation}
and
\[ \lim_{s\to\infty}Q(s)=\bar k^2=\frac{\lr^2\mathcal S^2}4 . \]
If \(\mathcal S>0\) and \(\epsilon>0\), the unique step \(\lr>0\) with floor \(\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4=\epsilon\) is \(\lr=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S\).
The theorem allows countably many modes and a kernel that is unbounded at the origin; only the two sums of (SI.1) are assumed finite.
Proof
Proof.
The solution is constructed by monotone iteration. Put \(Q_0\defeq F\) and \(Q_{n+1}\defeq F+k*\sqrt{Q_n}\). Positivity of \(k\) gives \(Q_1\ge Q_0\), and induction gives \(Q_{n+1}\ge Q_n\) for every \(n\). The constant \(M\) is the positive root of
\[ M=F_0+\bar k\sqrt M . \]
If \(Q_n\le M\), then (SI.5) gives \(Q_{n+1}\le F_0+\bar k\sqrt M=M\). Each iterate is continuous by Lemma SI.1. The iterates therefore increase to a limit \(Q\) with \(F\le Q\le M\), and letting \(n\to\infty\) in the iteration gives \(Q=F+k*\sqrt Q\). The function \(\sqrt Q\) is bounded, so \(k*\sqrt Q\) is continuous by Lemma SI.1, and \(Q\) is continuous.
Every solution is at least \(F\), because \(k*\sqrt Q\ge0\). On a compact interval \([0,S']\) every solution is therefore at least \(F(S')>0\). Lemma SI.3, with \(G=F\), \(\mathsf k=k\) and \(m=F(S')\), applied in both directions to two solutions, shows that they agree on \([0,S']\). This proves uniqueness.
For the limit, suppose first \(\mathcal S=0\). Then every \(\mathfrak v_i\) vanishes, \(k=0\), and \(Q=F\to0=\bar k^2\).
Suppose \(\mathcal S>0\). The limit inferior of \(Q\) is positive. To see this, choose an index \(j\) with \(\mathfrak v_j>0\), put \(b_j\defeq2\mathfrak d_j\) and \(c_j\defeq\lr\mu_j\mathfrak v_j/2\), and let
\[ z(s)\defeq c_j\int_0^se^{-b_j(s-v)}\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v . \]
Then \(0<z\le k*\sqrt Q\le Q\) for \(s>0\). Differentiating this single integral gives
\[ z'=-b_jz+c_j\sqrt Q\ge-b_jz+c_j\sqrt z . \]
For a fixed \(s_0>0\) the function \(\mathsf r\defeq\sqrt z\) is differentiable on \([s_0,\infty)\) and satisfies \(\mathsf r'\ge-\frac{b_j}2\mathsf r+\frac{c_j}2\). Multiplying by \(e^{b_js/2}\) and integrating from \(s_0\) gives
\[ \mathsf r(s)\ge\frac{c_j}{b_j}+\Big(\mathsf r(s_0)-\frac{c_j}{b_j}\Big)e^{-b_j(s-s_0)/2} . \]
Hence \(\liminf_{s\to\infty}Q(s)\ge(c_j/b_j)^2>0\).
Write \(\ell_-\defeq\liminf Q\) and \(\ell_+\defeq\limsup Q\), both finite by (SI.7). Since \(F\to0\), Lemma SI.2 applied to \(f=\sqrt Q\) in (SI.4) gives
\[ \ell_-\ge\bar k\sqrt{\ell_-},\qquad \ell_+\le\bar k\sqrt{\ell_+} . \]
Because \(\ell_->0\), the first inequality gives \(\ell_-\ge\bar k^2\), and the second gives \(\ell_+\le\bar k^2\). Thus \(Q(s)\to\bar k^2\). Solving \(\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4=\epsilon\) for \(\lr>0\) gives the last assertion.
□
The clock converts the intrinsic time \(s\) into the step count \(t\).
Theorem SI.2 (Clock and row equations)
Assume (M1) and let \(Q\) be the solution of Theorem SI.1. For \(i\in\mathcal I\) and \(s\ge0\) put
\[ E_i(s)\defeq\fQ_i(0)e^{-2\mathfrak d_is}+\lr\mathfrak v_i\int_0^se^{-2\mathfrak d_i(s-v)}\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v,\qquad t(s)\defeq\frac1\lr\int_0^s\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v, \]
and \(t_*\defeq\lim_{s\to\infty}t(s)\in(0,\infty]\). Then \(\frac12\sum_i\mu_iE_i(s)=Q(s)\) for every \(s\), and \(t\) is continuously differentiable and strictly increasing from \([0,\infty)\) onto \([0,t_*)\). Its inverse is the clock
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.8} \clock(t)=\int_0^t\frac{\lr}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t')}}\,\dif t', \end{equation}
where \(\fQ_i(t)\defeq E_i(\clock(t))\) and \(\fRisk(t)\defeq Q(\clock(t))\) solve (SI.2) on \([0,t_*)\) with continuous positive risk. Every solution of (SI.2) with continuous positive risk on an interval \([0,t_1)\) agrees with this one there, and \([0,t_*)\) is the maximal such interval. If \(\mathcal S>0\), then \(t_*=\infty\) and \(\fRisk(t)\to\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4\) as \(t\to\infty\). If \(\mathcal S=0\), then \(t_*=\lr^{-1}\int_0^\infty\sqrt{F(s)}\,\dif s\), and \(\fRisk(t)\to0\) as \(t\uparrow t_*\) when \(t_*<\infty\).
Proof
Proof.
Summing the formulas for \(E_i\) with weights \(\mu_i/2\) and using (SI.3) gives
\[ \frac12\sum_i\mu_iE_i(s)=F(s)+\int_0^sk(s-v)\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v=Q(s). \]
Each \(E_i\) is differentiable, with
\[ E_i'=-2\mathfrak d_iE_i+\lr\mathfrak v_i\sqrt Q . \]
Since \(Q\) is continuous and positive, \(t'=\sqrt Q/\lr>0\), so \(t\) is a continuously differentiable increasing bijection onto \([0,t_*)\). Its inverse satisfies \(\clock'(t)=\lr/\sqrt{Q(\clock(t))}\) and \(\clock(0)=0\), which is (SI.8). The chain rule applied to \(E_i\circ\clock\) gives
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif t}E_i(\clock(t))=\frac{\lr}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}}\big(-2\mathfrak d_iE_i(\clock(t))+\lr\mathfrak v_i\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}\big), \]
which is (SI.2).
Conversely, let \((\fQ_i)\) solve (SI.2) on \([0,t_1)\) with continuous positive risk. Define \(\clock\) by (SI.8); it is continuously differentiable with positive derivative. In the time \(s=\clock(t)\) the chain rule gives \(\frac{\dif}{\dif s}\fQ_i=-2\mathfrak d_i\fQ_i+\lr\mathfrak v_i\sqrt{\fRisk}\). Multiplying by \(e^{2\mathfrak d_is}\) and integrating gives the formula for \(E_i\) with \(\fRisk\) in place of \(Q\), and summing with weights \(\mu_i/2\) gives (SI.4) on the image interval. Uniqueness on compact intervals (Theorem SI.1) identifies the risk and every row with the construction.
If \(\mathcal S>0\), the positive limit of Theorem SI.1 gives \(\sqrt Q\ge\bar k/2\) for large \(s\), so \(t_*=\infty\), and \(\fRisk(t)=Q(\clock(t))\) converges to \(\bar k^2\). If \(\mathcal S=0\), then \(Q=F\) and \(t_*=\lr^{-1}\int_0^\infty\sqrt F\). When this is finite, \(\clock(t)\to\infty\) as \(t\uparrow t_*\) and \(\fRisk(t)=F(\clock(t))\to0\), so no solution with positive continuous risk extends to \(t_*\).
□
For a level \(a>0\) write
\[ H_Q(a)\defeq\inf\{s\ge0:\ Q(s)\le a\},\qquad H_F(a)\defeq\inf\{s\ge0:\ F(s)\le a\} \]
for the first intrinsic times at which the risk and the forcing reach \(a\).
Theorem SI.3 (Hitting times)
Assume (M1) and \(\mathcal S>0\), fix \(\epsilon>0\), and take \(\lr=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S\), so that \(\bar k=\sqrt\epsilon\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(a>0\), \(H_Q(a)\ge H_F(a)\) and
    \[ t_a\ge\frac1\lr\int_0^{H_F(a)}\sqrt{F(s)}\,\dif s . \]
    If \(H_Q(a)<\infty\), then \(t_a=t(H_Q(a))\) with \(t(\cdot)\) of Theorem SI.2.
  2. (ii)
    Fix \(c_0>1\) with \(F_0>c_0\epsilon\), let \(s_c\) be the time with \(F(s_c)=c_0\epsilon\), and put
    \[ C_\ast\defeq\max_{0\le s\le s_c}\frac{(k*\sqrt F)(s)}{\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}},\qquad m_\ast\defeq\frac{C_\ast}{1-1/(2\sqrt{c_0})} . \]
    Then \(0\le C_\ast\le\sqrt{F_0/(c_0\epsilon)}\) and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.9} Q(s)\le F(s)+m_\ast\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}\qquad(0\le s\le s_c). \end{equation}
    Consequently, for every \(T\ge c_0+m_\ast\sqrt{c_0}\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.10} \frac1\lr\int_0^{H_F(T\epsilon)}\sqrt{F(s)}\,\dif s\ \le\ t_{T\epsilon}\ \le\ \frac1\lr\int_0^{s_c}\sqrt{F(s)+m_\ast\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}}\,\dif s . \end{equation}
Neither bound assumes that the risk is monotone.
Proof
Proof.
For (i), the convolution in (SI.4) is nonnegative, so \(Q\ge F\). Hence \(\{Q\le a\}\subseteq\{F\le a\}\), and taking infima gives \(H_Q(a)\ge H_F(a)\). If the first set is nonempty, it is closed by continuity and contains its infimum. The clock is a strictly increasing bijection, so the first time at which \(\fRisk\) reaches \(a\) is \(t(H_Q(a))\). Using \(\sqrt Q\ge\sqrt F\) in the integral defining \(t(\cdot)\) and \(H_Q(a)\ge H_F(a)\) gives the lower bound. If the first set is empty, then \(t_a=\infty\) and the bound holds.
For (ii), the time \(s_c\) exists and is unique because \(F\) is continuous and strictly decreasing to zero. The ratio defining \(C_\ast\) is continuous on \([0,s_c]\), since \(F\ge c_0\epsilon>0\) there, and attains its maximum. Because \(\sqrt F\le\sqrt{F_0}\) and \(\int k=\sqrt\epsilon\),
\[ (k*\sqrt F)(s)\le\sqrt{F_0\epsilon},\qquad \frac{(k*\sqrt F)(s)}{\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}}\le\sqrt{\frac{F_0}{F(s)}}\le\sqrt{\frac{F_0}{c_0\epsilon}} . \]
The function \(\mathsf B\defeq F+m_\ast\sqrt{\epsilon F}\) is a supersolution on \([0,s_c]\). The inequality \(\sqrt{1+x}\le1+x/2\) for \(x\ge0\) gives
\[ \sqrt{\mathsf B(v)}\le\sqrt{F(v)}+\frac{m_\ast}2\sqrt\epsilon . \]
Hence, for \(0\le s\le s_c\), using \(F(s)\ge c_0\epsilon\) in the last step,
\[ F(s)+(k*\sqrt{\mathsf B})(s)\le F(s)+(k*\sqrt F)(s)+\frac{m_\ast\epsilon}2 \le F(s)+\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}\Big(C_\ast+\frac{m_\ast}{2\sqrt{c_0}}\Big)=\mathsf B(s). \]
Both \(Q\) and \(\mathsf B\) are at least \(F\ge c_0\epsilon\) on \([0,s_c]\), so Lemma SI.3 with \(m=c_0\epsilon\) gives (SI.9). At \(s_c\) it gives
\[ Q(s_c)\le\epsilon\,(c_0+m_\ast\sqrt{c_0})\le T\epsilon , \]
so \(H_Q(T\epsilon)\le s_c\). Part (i) and the clock integral, bounded with (SI.9), give (SI.10).
□
Remark SI.2 (The whole-interval ratio)
The constant \(C_\ast\) of Theorem SI.3(ii) is a maximum over \([0,s_c]\), not the value of the ratio at \(s_c\). The endpoint value does not dominate the ratio in general. For two modes with \(\lr=1\), \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1\), \(\mathfrak d_1=1\), \(\mathfrak d_2=\frac12\), \(\mathfrak v_1=2\), \(\mathfrak v_2=0\), one has \(k(s)=e^{-2s}\) and \(\epsilon=\bar k^2=\frac14\). Choose the initial energies so that \(F(s)=M'(e^{-2s}+2^{-20}e^{-s})\), with \(M'\) fixed by \(F(40\log2)=2\epsilon\). Bounding \(\sqrt F\) between \(\sqrt{M'}e^{-s}\) and \(\sqrt{M'}(e^{-s}+2^{-10}e^{-s/2})\) and integrating the exponentials shows that the quotient \((k*\sqrt F)/\sqrt F\) exceeds \(\frac9{10}\) at \(s=4\log2\) and is below \(\frac7{10}\) at \(s=40\log2\); the ratio of Theorem SI.3 is this quotient divided by \(\sqrt\epsilon=\frac12\). The upper bound (SI.9) therefore needs the ratio on the whole interval.

SI.3 Common drift and the isotropic comparison

When all drift coefficients are equal, the model closes on the risk alone and can be solved exactly. Throughout this subsection the index set is finite, \(\mathfrak d_i=\mathfrak d>0\) for every \(i\), and
\[ \mathfrak v_{\rm tot}\defeq\sum_{i=1}^N\mu_i\mathfrak v_i>0 , \]
which is the volatility \(\mathfrak v\) of (8).
Proposition SI.2 (Common drift)
Assume (M1) with a finite index set, \(\mathfrak d_i=\mathfrak d>0\) for all \(i\) and \(\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}>0\). Then
\[ F(s)=F_0e^{-2\mathfrak ds},\qquad k(s)=\frac{\lr\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}2e^{-2\mathfrak ds},\qquad \mathcal S=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{2\mathfrak d}, \]
and the risk solves
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.11} \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif t}=-2\lr\mathfrak d\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\lr^2\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}2 . \end{equation}
Put \(a_\circ\defeq\lr\mathfrak d\), \(b_\circ\defeq\lr\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}/(4\mathfrak d)=\lr\mathcal S/2\) and \(u_0\defeq\sqrt{F_0}\).
  1. (i)
    If \(u_0\ne b_\circ\), then for every \(t\ge0\)
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.12} a_\circ t=u_0-\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}+b_\circ\log\frac{\abs{u_0-b_\circ}}{\abs{\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}-b_\circ}} , \end{equation}
    and \(\sqrt{\fRisk}\) is strictly decreasing with limit \(b_\circ\) if \(u_0>b_\circ\), strictly increasing with limit \(b_\circ\) if \(u_0<b_\circ\), and never equal to \(b_\circ\). If \(u_0=b_\circ\), then \(\fRisk\equiv b_\circ^2\).
  2. (ii)
    Let \(a\ge0\). If \(F_0\le a\), then \(t_a=0\). If \(F_0>a\), then \(t_a<\infty\) if and only if \(a>b_\circ^2\), and then \(t_a\) is given by (SI.12) with \(\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}=\sqrt a\).
The closure needs only a common drift; the weights \(\mu_i\), the volatilities and the initial energies may differ from row to row.
Proof
Proof.
Summing (SI.2) with weights \(\mu_i/2\) gives
\[ \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif t}=-\frac{\lr\mathfrak d}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i+\frac{\lr^2}2\sum_i\mu_i\mathfrak v_i =-2\lr\mathfrak d\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\lr^2\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}2 . \]
The formulas for \(F\), \(k\) and \(\mathcal S\) follow from (SI.3) and (SI.1) by finite summation. Since \(\mathcal S>0\), Theorem SI.2 gives a global solution with positive risk and \(\fRisk(t)\to b_\circ^2\). In the variable \(\mathsf u\defeq\sqrt{\fRisk}\) the relation (SI.11) reads
\[ \mathsf u'=-\frac{a_\circ(\mathsf u-b_\circ)}{\mathsf u} . \]
The function \(\mathsf w\defeq\mathsf u-b_\circ\) therefore satisfies \(\mathsf w'=-(a_\circ/\mathsf u)\mathsf w\), so that
\[ \mathsf w(t)=\mathsf w(0)\exp\Big(-a_\circ\int_0^t\frac{\dif t'}{\mathsf u(t')}\Big). \]
Thus \(\mathsf w\) keeps the sign of \(\mathsf w(0)\), \(\abs{\mathsf w}\) is strictly decreasing when \(\mathsf w(0)\ne0\), and \(\mathsf w\equiv0\) when \(\mathsf w(0)=0\). When \(\mathsf w(0)\ne0\), the equation is separable:
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif t}\Big(\mathsf u+b_\circ\log\abs{\mathsf u-b_\circ}\Big)=\frac{\mathsf u}{\mathsf u-b_\circ}\,\mathsf u'=-a_\circ , \]
and integrating from \(0\) to \(t\) gives (SI.12). This proves (i).
For (ii), \(\fRisk(0)=F_0\) gives \(t_a=0\) when \(F_0\le a\). Let \(F_0>a\). If \(u_0>b_\circ\), the risk decreases strictly to \(b_\circ^2\) without reaching it, so it reaches \(a\) at a finite time exactly when \(a>b_\circ^2\), and that time solves (SI.12). If \(u_0\le b_\circ\), the risk never falls below \(F_0>a\), so \(t_a=\infty\), and in this case \(a<F_0\le b_\circ^2\).
□
Theorem SI.4 (Matched floors)
In the setting of Proposition SI.2, let \(\epsilon>0\), \(T>1\) and \(F_0>T\epsilon\), and take the matched step \(\lr=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S=4\mathfrak d\sqrt\epsilon/\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}\). Put \(X\defeq\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}>\sqrt T\). Then the floor is \(\epsilon\) and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.13} t_{T\epsilon}=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}\Big[X-\sqrt T+\log\frac{X-1}{\sqrt T-1}\Big]. \end{equation}
If \(x\defeq1/X\le\frac12\), then
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.14} \Big|\frac{t_{T\epsilon}}{\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}}-1\Big|\le x\Big[\sqrt T+\abs{\log(\sqrt T-1)}+\log\frac1x+2x\Big]. \end{equation}
For two such models with the same \(F_0\), \(\epsilon\) and \(T\),
\[ \frac{t^{(1)}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{(2)}_{T\epsilon}}=\frac{\mathfrak v^{(1)}_{\rm tot}/(\mathfrak d^{(1)})^2}{\mathfrak v^{(2)}_{\rm tot}/(\mathfrak d^{(2)})^2} . \]
Proof
Proof.
At the matched step \(b_\circ=\sqrt\epsilon\) and \(a_\circ=4\mathfrak d^2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}\). Since \(T\epsilon>b_\circ^2\) and \(F_0>T\epsilon\), Proposition SI.2(ii) applies with \(a=T\epsilon\), and dividing (SI.12) by \(a_\circ\) gives (SI.13). Dividing (SI.13) by its leading term \(\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}X\) and using \(X-1=X(1-x)\) gives
\[ \frac{t_{T\epsilon}}{\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}X}-1=x\Big[-\sqrt T+\log\frac1x+\log(1-x)-\log(\sqrt T-1)\Big]. \]
For \(0<x\le\frac12\),
\[ \abs{\log(1-x)}=\int_0^x\frac{\dif y}{1-y}\le2x , \]
which proves (SI.14). The bracket in (SI.13) is positive and depends only on \(F_0\), \(\epsilon\) and \(T\), so it cancels in the ratio.
□
The isotropic arrays. Let \(N,B\ge1\) be integers, \(\gamma\defeq N/B\) and \(m\defeq\min(B,N)\). Let \(\mathcal N_B\defeq\EE\big[(\sum_{b=1}^Bx_b^2y_b^2)^{1/2}\big]\) for independent standard Gaussian \(x_b,y_b\), as in Section 3. The isotropic arrays have \(N\) rows with \(\mu_i=1\), common initial energies, and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.15} \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}=C(\gamma),\quad \sum_{i=1}^N\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SVD}}=m;\qquad \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac{\mathcal N_B}{\sqrt\pi},\quad \sum_{i=1}^N\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=N^2 , \end{equation}
where \(C(\gamma)=C_0(\gamma)>0\) is the zero-ridge drift constant (SF.21) at \(\bmom=0\); (9) gives an approximate form of it. These are the kernels of the table following (8) without momentum, and they are adopted in the sense of (M1). The arrays with momentum, with the pair \((C_\bmom(\gamma),\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom})\) of (SF.21) and Definition SF.1 in place of \((C(\gamma),m)\), leave the Volterra algebra of Proposition SI.2 and Theorem SI.4 unchanged, because that algebra uses only a common drift and a total volatility. The bounds
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.16} \max\Big\{\frac2\pi\sqrt B,\ \sqrt B-\frac4{\sqrt B}\Big\}\le\mathcal N_B\le\sqrt B \end{equation}
are Proposition SJ.1. Put
\[ \rho(\gamma)\defeq\pi\,C(\gamma)^2\,\gamma\max(1,\gamma) . \]
Proposition SI.3 (The isotropic ratio)
Take the arrays (SI.15) with a common \(F_0\), the matched steps \(\lr^\star=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S\) of each, and \(T>1\) with \(F_0>T\epsilon\). Then
\[ \mathcal S^{\text{s-SVD}}=\frac m{2C(\gamma)},\qquad \mathcal S^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac{N^2\sqrt\pi}{2\mathcal N_B},\qquad \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}=\frac{\pi N^2C(\gamma)^2}{m\,\mathcal N_B^2}=\rho(\gamma)\,\frac B{\mathcal N_B^2} . \]
Moreover \(1\le B/\mathcal N_B^2\le\pi^2/4\) for every \(B\), and for \(B>4\)
\[ 0\le\frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}/t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}{\rho(\gamma)}-1\le\Big(1-\frac4B\Big)^{-2}-1 . \]
Proof
Proof.
The noise constants are \(\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}/(2\mathfrak d)\) by Proposition SI.2. The exact ratio of Theorem SI.4 is
\[ \frac{N^2/(\mathcal N_B^2/\pi)}{m/C(\gamma)^2}=\frac{\pi N^2C(\gamma)^2}{m\,\mathcal N_B^2} . \]
Since \(N/B=\gamma\) and \(N/m=\max(1,\gamma)\),
\[ \frac{N^2}{mB}=\gamma\max(1,\gamma), \]
which gives the second form. Squaring and inverting (SI.16) gives \(1\le B/\mathcal N_B^2\le\pi^2/4\), and for \(B>4\) the lower bound \(\mathcal N_B\ge\sqrt B(1-4/B)>0\) gives \(B/\mathcal N_B^2\le(1-4/B)^{-2}\).

The coefficient \(C(\gamma)\) is now described through a scalar measure. Let \(Z\) be a standard Gaussian variable and
\[ \Phi(v)\defeq\EE\frac{vZ^2}{1+vZ^2}\qquad(v\ge0) . \]
Definition SI.2 (Scalar measure hypothesis)
Let \(\gamma>0\). The scalar measure hypothesis at \(\gamma\) is the existence of a finite nonnegative measure \(\mu_\gamma\) on \([0,\infty)\) of mass \(\gamma\) whose transform \(\sigma_\gamma(t)\defeq\int(x+t)^{-1}\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\), \(t>0\), satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.17} \psi_\gamma(t)\defeq\gamma-t\,\sigma_\gamma(t)=\Phi\big(t\,\sigma_\gamma(t)^2\big)\qquad(t>0). \end{equation}
Under it, \(C(\gamma)\defeq(\sqrt2\,\gamma^2)^{-1}\int\sqrt x\,\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\).
This is the square-case scalar equation of the isotropic analysis, with the same measure for both companions, and \(C(\gamma)\) is its half-moment drift coefficient; the negative-axis form of the same quantity is (SF.21) at \(\bmom=0\), and Proposition SF.7 identifies the two forms. The hypothesis holds at every \(\gamma>0\), with exactly one measure \(\mu_\gamma\): Proposition SF.6 gives exactly one isotropic measure pair at \((\gamma,\gamma)\), and the correspondence stated after Definition SF.2 identifies both of its measures with \(\mu_\gamma\). The definition of \(C(\gamma)\) therefore applies at every \(\gamma>0\).
Lemma SI.4 (An integral for the drift constant)
The map \(\Phi\) is a continuously differentiable increasing bijection from \((0,\infty)\) onto \((0,1)\); let \(V\) be its inverse and \(w(y)\defeq V(y)^{-1/2}\) for \(0<y<1\). The function \(w_\circ(y)\defeq\sqrt y\,w(y)\) is positive, strictly decreasing, at most \(1\), and tends to \(1\) as \(y\downarrow0\). For every \(\gamma>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.18} C(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma^2}\,I(\gamma),\qquad I(\gamma)\defeq\int_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}(\gamma-y)\,w(y)\,\dif y . \end{equation}
Moreover
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.19} \mathcal A\defeq\int_0^1w(y)\,\dif y=\sqrt{\frac\pi2},\qquad \mathcal D\defeq\int_0^1y\,w(y)\,\dif y\in(0,\mathcal A),\qquad I(\gamma)=\gamma\mathcal A-\mathcal D\quad(\gamma\ge1). \end{equation}
In particular \(C(\gamma)\) does not depend on the choice of the measure.
Proof
Proof.
The map \(\Phi\) is continuously differentiable with
\[ \Phi'(v)=\EE\frac{Z^2}{(1+vZ^2)^2}>0 . \]
The quotient \(\Phi(v)/v=\EE[Z^2/(1+vZ^2)]\) is strictly decreasing in \(v\), tends to \(\EE Z^2=1\) as \(v\downarrow0\), and \(\Phi(v)\to1\) as \(v\to\infty\). Hence \(\Phi\) is an increasing bijection onto \((0,1)\) with a continuously differentiable inverse. Since
\[ w_\circ(y)^2=\frac{y}{V(y)}=\frac{\Phi(V(y))}{V(y)} \]
and \(V\) is increasing, \(w_\circ\) is strictly decreasing, at most \(1\), and tends to \(1\) at \(0\). In particular \(w(y)\le y^{-1/2}\), so every integral in the statement is finite.
The function \(\psi_\gamma\) is the inverse of an explicit function. For \(0<y<\min(\gamma,1)\) put \(\Theta_\gamma(y)\defeq(\gamma-y)^2/V(y)\). It is strictly decreasing. It tends to \(\infty\) as \(y\downarrow0\), because \(V(y)/y\to1\). It tends to \(0\) at the upper endpoint, because the numerator tends to \(0\) if \(\gamma<1\) and the denominator tends to \(\infty\) if \(\gamma\ge1\). So \(\Theta_\gamma\) is a bijection onto \((0,\infty)\). The quantity \(t\sigma_\gamma(t)=\int t(x+t)^{-1}\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\) lies in \((0,\gamma]\), and (SI.17) gives \(\psi_\gamma(t)=\Phi(\cdot)\in(0,1)\); hence \(0<\psi_\gamma(t)<\min(\gamma,1)\). By (SI.17), \(V(\psi_\gamma(t))=t\sigma_\gamma(t)^2=(\gamma-\psi_\gamma(t))^2/t\), that is,
\[ t=\Theta_\gamma(\psi_\gamma(t))\qquad(t>0). \]
The half moment is an integral of \(\psi_\gamma\). The half-moment formula (SF.26) of Lemma SF.3(v), applied to \(\nu=\mu_\gamma\), expresses \(\int\sqrt x\,\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\) through \(\int x(x+t)^{-1}\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\). This inner integral is
\[ \int\frac{x}{x+t}\,\mu_\gamma(\dif x)=\gamma-t\sigma_\gamma(t)=\psi_\gamma(t), \]
so that
\[ \int\sqrt x\,\mu_\gamma(\dif x)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\psi_\gamma(t)\,\dif t . \]
The change of variables \(t=\Theta_\gamma(y)\) and an integration by parts evaluate this integral:
\begin{align*} \int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\psi_\gamma(t)\,\dif t&=\int_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}y\,\big(-\Theta_\gamma'(y)\big)\Theta_\gamma(y)^{-1/2}\,\dif y\\ &=\Big[-2y\sqrt{\Theta_\gamma(y)}\Big]_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}+2\int_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}\sqrt{\Theta_\gamma(y)}\,\dif y . \end{align*}
The boundary term vanishes at \(0\) because \(\sqrt{\Theta_\gamma(y)}=(\gamma-y)w(y)\le\gamma y^{-1/2}\), and at the upper endpoint because \(y\le1\) and \(\Theta_\gamma\to0\). Hence the integral equals \(2I(\gamma)\), and the definition of \(C(\gamma)\) gives (SI.18).
For (SI.19), the substitution \(y=\Phi(v)\) gives
\[ \mathcal A=\int_0^\infty v^{-1/2}\Phi'(v)\,\dif v=\EE\int_0^\infty\frac{Z^2v^{-1/2}}{(1+vZ^2)^2}\,\dif v=\EE\abs Z\int_0^\infty\frac{y'^{-1/2}}{(1+y')^2}\,\dif y'=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\cdot\frac\pi2 , \]
with \(y'=vZ^2\) in the third step. The last integral is the Beta integral \(\Gamma(\frac12)\Gamma(\frac32)/\Gamma(2)=\pi/2\) [21, 5.12.3], and \(\EE\abs Z=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). Since \(0<y<1\) and \(w>0\), \(0<\mathcal D<\mathcal A\). For \(\gamma\ge1\) the range of integration in \(I(\gamma)\) is \((0,1)\), which gives the last formula.

Theorem SI.5 (Extrema of the isotropic ratio)
The ratio \(\rho\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\), strictly decreasing on \((0,1]\) and strictly increasing on \([1,\infty)\), and
\[ \min_{\gamma>0}\rho(\gamma)=\rho(1)=\pi C(1)^2,\qquad \sup_{\gamma>0}\rho(\gamma)=\lim_{\gamma\downarrow0}\rho(\gamma)=\frac{32}{9\pi},\qquad \lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\rho(\gamma)=1 . \]
The minimum is attained only at \(\gamma=1\) and the supremum is not attained. For \(\gamma\ge1\), \(\rho(\gamma)=(1-\mathsf d/\gamma)^2\) with \(\mathsf d\defeq\mathcal D/\mathcal A=1-\sqrt\pi\,C(1)\in(0,1)\).
Proof
Proof.
For \(0<\gamma<1\), (SI.18) gives
\[ \rho(\gamma)=\frac2\pi\,\gamma^{-3}I(\gamma)^2,\qquad I'(\gamma)=\int_0^\gamma w(y)\,\dif y , \]
where the derivative has no boundary term because the integrand of \(I\) vanishes at \(y=\gamma\). The derivative of \(\gamma^{-3}I^2\) is \(\gamma^{-4}I\,(2\gamma I'-3I)\), and
\[ 2\gamma I'(\gamma)-3I(\gamma)=\int_0^\gamma(3y-\gamma)\,y^{-1/2}w_\circ(y)\,\dif y . \]
The same integral with \(w_\circ(y)\) replaced by the constant \(w_\circ(\gamma/3)\) vanishes, since \(\int_0^\gamma(3y-\gamma)y^{-1/2}\dif y=2\gamma^{3/2}-2\gamma^{3/2}=0\). Subtracting it gives
\[ 2\gamma I'(\gamma)-3I(\gamma)=\int_0^\gamma(3y-\gamma)\,y^{-1/2}\big(w_\circ(y)-w_\circ(\gamma/3)\big)\,\dif y<0 , \]
because \(w_\circ\) is strictly decreasing, so the integrand is negative except at \(y=\gamma/3\). Hence \(\rho\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,1)\).
For \(\gamma\ge1\), (SI.19) and \(\mathcal A^2=\pi/2\) give
\[ \rho(\gamma)=\frac2\pi\big(\mathcal A-\mathcal D/\gamma\big)^2=\Big(1-\frac{\mathsf d}\gamma\Big)^2 , \]
which increases strictly to \(1\). The formula (SI.18) is continuous in \(\gamma\), which gives continuity at \(1\), and \(\rho(1)=(1-\mathsf d)^2=\pi C(1)^2\) identifies \(\mathsf d\).
As \(\gamma\downarrow0\), the substitution \(y=\gamma y'\) gives
\[ \gamma^{-3/2}I(\gamma)=\int_0^1(1-y')\,y'^{-1/2}\,w_\circ(\gamma y')\,\dif y'\longrightarrow\int_0^1(1-y')\,y'^{-1/2}\,\dif y'=\frac43 , \]
since \(0<w_\circ\le1\) and \(w_\circ(\gamma y')\to1\) for each \(y'\). Hence \(\rho(\gamma)\to\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{16}9=\frac{32}{9\pi}\), and \(\frac{32}{9\pi}>1\) because \(\pi<\frac{32}9\). Strict monotonicity on each side of \(1\) gives the remaining claims.
□

Proof.

Theorem SI.6 (Isotropic comparison)
Let \(N,B\ge1\), \(\gamma=N/B\), \(m=\min(B,N)\), and take the isotropic arrays (SI.15) with a common initial risk \(F_0=\fRisk^{\text{s-SVD}}(0)=\fRisk^{\text{s-SGD}}(0)\). Proof
Proof.

SI.4 Power sums

The forcing and the kernels of the power-law models are finite sums of decaying exponentials with power-law weights and rates. This subsection collects the estimates used for them. For \(p,q>0\) put
\[ M_{p,q}\defeq\Big(\frac p{eq}\Big)^{p/q}, \]
and write \(\Gamma(d)=\int_0^\infty y^{d-1}e^{-y}\,\dif y\) for \(d>0\) [21, 5.2.1].
Lemma SI.5 (Sum and integral)
Let \(p,q,a>0\) and \(f_a(x)\defeq x^{-p}e^{-ax^{-q}}\) for \(x>0\), \(f_a(0)\defeq0\). The function \(f_a\) has a unique maximum, at \(x=(qa/p)^{1/q}\), with value \(M_{p,q}a^{-p/q}\). For every integer \(N\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.20} \begin{gathered} \Big|\sum_{i=1}^Nf_a(i)-\int_0^Nf_a(x)\,\dif x\Big|\le2M_{p,q}a^{-p/q},\\ \int_0^Nf_a(x)\,\dif x=\frac{a^{(1-p)/q}}q\int_{a/N^q}^\infty y^{(p-1)/q-1}e^{-y}\,\dif y . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The derivative is
\[ f_a'(x)=x^{-p-1}e^{-ax^{-q}}\big(-p+qa\,x^{-q}\big), \]
which changes sign once, from positive to negative, at \(x=(qa/p)^{1/q}\). Evaluating there gives the maximum. Since \(f_a\) tends to \(0\) at both ends of \((0,\infty)\), its total variation is twice the maximum. On each interval \([i-1,i]\),
\[ \Big|f_a(i)-\int_{i-1}^if_a(x)\,\dif x\Big|\le\int_{i-1}^i\abs{f_a(i)-f_a(x)}\,\dif x\le\int_{i-1}^i\abs{f_a'(y)}\,\dif y , \]
and summing over \(i\le N\) bounds the difference by the total variation. The substitution \(y=ax^{-q}\) gives the formula for the integral.
□
Lemma SI.6 (Convergent power sums)
Let \(p>1\), \(q>0\) and \(d\defeq(p-1)/q\). For every \(a>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.21} \Big|\sum_{i\ge1}i^{-p}e^{-ai^{-q}}-\frac{\Gamma(d)}q\,a^{-d}\Big|\le2M_{p,q}a^{-p/q}, \end{equation}
and for every integer \(N\ge1\), \(0\le\sum_{i>N}i^{-p}\le N^{1-p}/(p-1)\).
Proof
Proof.
The series converges by comparison with \(\sum_ii^{-p}\). Letting \(N\to\infty\) in (SI.20) gives
\[ \int_0^\infty f_a(x)\,\dif x=\frac{a^{-d}}q\,\Gamma(d) , \]
and the error bound passes to the limit. The tail bound is the comparison \(\sum_{i>N}i^{-p}\le\int_N^\infty x^{-p}\,\dif x\).
□
For \(a>1\) and \(q>0\) define
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.22} \begin{gathered} d(a,q)\defeq\frac{a-1}q,\qquad Z_a\defeq1+\frac1{a-1},\qquad A_a\defeq2^{-2a}e^{-2},\\ E_{a,q}\defeq\max\Big\{2^{d(a,q)}Z_a,\ \frac{\Gamma(d(a,q))}q+2^{1-1/q}M_{a,q}\Big\}, \end{gathered} \end{equation}
and, for an integer \(N\ge1\) or \(N=\infty\),
\[ L_{N,a,q}(u)\defeq\sum_{i=1}^Ni^{-a}e^{-2ui^{-q}}\qquad(u\ge0). \]
Lemma SI.7 (Finite power sums)
Let \(a>1\) and \(q>0\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(N\in\N\cup\{\infty\}\) and \(u\ge0\), \(L_{N,a,q}(u)\le E_{a,q}(1+u)^{-d(a,q)}\).
  2. (ii)
    For every integer \(N\ge4\) and \(0\le u\le(N/4)^q\), \(\frac12L_{N,a,q}(u)\ge A_a(1+u)^{-d(a,q)}\).
Proof
Proof.
Write \(d=d(a,q)\). Comparison with an integral gives \(\sum_{i\ge1}i^{-a}\le1+\int_1^\infty x^{-a}\dif x=Z_a\). For \(0\le u\le1\), this and \((1+u)^{-d}\ge2^{-d}\) give (i). For \(u\ge1\), Lemma SI.6 with \(2u\) in place of \(a\) gives
\[ L_{N,a,q}(u)\le L_{\infty,a,q}(u)\le\frac{\Gamma(d)}q(2u)^{-d}+2M_{a,q}(2u)^{-a/q}\le\Big[\frac{\Gamma(d)}q2^{-d}+2^{1-a/q}M_{a,q}\Big]u^{-d}, \]
where \(u^{-a/q}\le u^{-d}\) because \(a/q>d\). Since \(u^{-d}\le2^d(1+u)^{-d}\) for \(u\ge1\) and \(d-a/q=-1/q\), this gives (i).
For (ii) and \(0\le u\le1\), the first term gives \(\frac12L_{N,a,q}(u)\ge\frac12e^{-2}\ge A_a\). For \(1\le u\le(N/4)^q\) put \(j\defeq\lceil u^{1/q}\rceil\). Then \(j\le2u^{1/q}\) and \(2j\le N\). The \(j\) indices \(j\le i<2j\) satisfy \(i^{-a}\ge(2j)^{-a}\) and \(ui^{-q}\le1\), so
\[ \frac12L_{N,a,q}(u)\ge\frac12\,j\,(2j)^{-a}e^{-2}=2^{-a-1}e^{-2}j^{1-a}\ge2^{-2a}e^{-2}u^{-d}\ge A_a(1+u)^{-d} . \qedhere \]
□
For the forcing exponent \(p>1\) and a rate exponent \(q>0\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.23} f_{N,q}(u)\defeq\frac12L_{N,p,q}(u),\qquad r_q\defeq\frac{p-1}{2q},\qquad C_{p,q}\defeq\sqrt{E_{p,q}/2} , \end{equation}
for \(N\in\N\cup\{\infty\}\). By Lemma SI.7, \(f_{N,q}(u)\le C_{p,q}^2(1+u)^{-2r_q}\) for all \(u\ge0\) and all \(N\), and \(f_{N,q}(u)\ge A_p(1+u)^{-2r_q}\) for \(N\ge4\) and \(0\le u\le(N/4)^q\). When \(r_q>1\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.24} J(p,q)\defeq\int_0^\infty\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}(u)}\,\dif u , \end{equation}
which is finite because \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\le C_{p,q}(1+u)^{-r_q}\) and positive because \(f_{\infty,q}(u)\ge\frac12e^{-2u}\). For every integer \(N\) put \(J_N(p,q)\defeq\int_0^\infty\sqrt{f_{N,q}(u)}\,\dif u\), which is finite for every \(q>0\) since every rate of the finite sum is at least \(2N^{-q}\).
Lemma SI.8 (Harmonic sums)
For \(a>0\) and \(N\ge1\) let \(H_N(a)\defeq\sum_{i=1}^Ni^{-a}\). Then
\[ \int_1^{N+1}x^{-a}\,\dif x\le H_N(a)\le1+\int_1^Nx^{-a}\,\dif x , \]
and \(H_N(a)\ge N^{1-a}\) for \(a\le1\). Consequently, as \(N\to\infty\), \(H_N(a)\sim N^{1-a}/(1-a)\) for \(0<a<1\), \(H_N(1)\sim\log N\) with \(H_N(1)\ge\log(N+1)\), and \(0\le\zeta(a)-H_N(a)\le N^{1-a}/(a-1)\) for \(a>1\), where \(\zeta(a)=\sum_{i\ge1}i^{-a}\). Moreover \(\zeta(2)<2\).
Proof
Proof.
The function \(x\mapsto x^{-a}\) is decreasing, so \(i^{-a}\) lies between its integrals over \([i,i+1]\) and \([i-1,i]\); summing gives the two bounds, and evaluating the integrals gives the asymptotics. For \(a\le1\) every term is at least \(N^{-a}\). The tail bound is Lemma SI.6. Finally \(\zeta(2)<1+\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\dif x=2\).
□

SI.5 The adopted power-law arrays and the phase map

Definition SI.3 (Adopted power-law arrays)
Fix \(\alpha>0\) and \(\texp\in\R\) with \(p\defeq\alpha+\texp>1\), and integers \(B\ge N\ge1\). Put \(\gamma\defeq N/B\in(0,1]\) and \(\bar\mu\defeq H_N(\alpha)/N\), and for \(1\le i\le N\) let \(\mu_i\defeq i^{-\alpha}\) and \(\fQ_i(0)\defeq i^{-\texp}\). The SignSVD and SignSGD arrays are
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.25} \begin{aligned} \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}&\defeq\gamma^{-1/2}i^{-\alpha/2}=\sqrt{\mu_i}\sqrt{B/N},&\qquad \mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SVD}}&\defeq1,\\ \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SGD}}&\defeq\mu_i\sqrt{\frac B{\pi\bar\mu}},&\qquad \mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}&\defeq N\Big[1+\frac2\pi\Big(\frac{\mu_i}{\bar\mu}-1\Big)\Big]. \end{aligned} \end{equation}
For \(j\in\{\text{s-SVD},\text{s-SGD}\}\), the time scales, rate exponents and forcing exponents are
\[ T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq\sqrt\gamma,\quad T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}\defeq\sqrt{\frac{\pi\bar\mu}B},\qquad q_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq\frac\alpha2,\quad q_{\text{s-SGD}}\defeq\alpha,\qquad r_j\defeq r_{q_j}=\frac{p-1}{2q_j} . \]
For \(\epsilon>0\) each model is run at its matched step \(\lr^\star_j\defeq2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S_j\). Its risk \(Q_j\) solves (SI.4) with forcing \(F_j\) and kernel \(k_j\), and its dimensionless risk, kernel and first hit at a fixed multiplier \(T\) are
\[ Y_j(u)\defeq Q_j(T_0^ju),\qquad g_j(u)\defeq\frac{T_0^j\,k_j(T_0^ju)}{\sqrt\epsilon},\qquad \hat u_j\defeq\inf\{u\ge0:\ Y_j(u)\le T\epsilon\} . \]
The SignSGD volatility equals \(N(1-\frac2\pi)+\frac2\pi N^2i^{-\alpha}/H_N(\alpha)\), a positive constant plus a nonnegative spectral term, and its total is \(\sum_i\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=N^2\). The SignSVD array has \(\mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}\propto i^{-\alpha/2}\) and the SignSGD array has \(\mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SGD}}\propto i^{-\alpha}\); this difference of rate exponents moves the phase boundaries. In terms of \(\texp\),
\[ r_{\text{s-SVD}}=\frac{\alpha+\texp-1}\alpha,\qquad r_{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac{\alpha+\texp-1}{2\alpha}, \]
so \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}=1\) exactly at \(\texp=1\) and \(r_{\text{s-SGD}}=1\) exactly at \(\texp=\alpha+1\). For \(r>0\) and \(x\ge0\) put
\[ I_r(x)\defeq\int_0^x(1+w)^{-r}\,\dif w=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(1+x)^{1-r}-1}{1-r},&r\ne1,\\[4pt]\log(1+x),&r=1.\end{cases} \]
Proposition SI.4 (Noise constants, kernels and clocks)
The arrays of Definition SI.3 satisfy (M1), with \(F_0=\frac12H_N(p)\), and the following hold.
  1. (i)
    The noise constants, matched steps and time-scale products are
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.26} \begin{aligned} \mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}&=\tfrac12\sqrt\gamma\,H_N(\alpha/2),&\quad \lr^\star_{\text{s-SVD}}&=\frac{4\sqrt\epsilon}{\sqrt\gamma\,H_N(\alpha/2)},\\ \mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}&=\frac{N^2}2\sqrt{\frac{\pi\bar\mu}B},&\quad \lr^\star_{\text{s-SGD}}&=\frac{4\sqrt\epsilon}{N^2}\sqrt{\frac B{\pi\bar\mu}},\\ \mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}&=\tfrac\gamma2H_N(\alpha/2),&\quad \mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}&=\frac{\pi N^2\bar\mu}{2B}=\tfrac{\pi\gamma}2H_N(\alpha), \end{aligned} \end{equation}
    and the dimension factor is
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.27} D_N\defeq\frac{\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}}{\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}}=\frac{\pi H_N(\alpha)}{H_N(\alpha/2)} . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    The dimensionless risks solve \(Y_j=f_{N,q_j}+\sqrt\epsilon\,g_j*\sqrt{Y_j}\), with \(f_{N,q}\) of (SI.23) and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.28} g_{\text{s-SVD}}=\frac2{H_N(\alpha/2)}L_{N,\alpha,\alpha/2},\qquad g_{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac{2(1-2/\pi)}N\,L_{N,\alpha,\alpha}+\frac4{\pi H_N(\alpha)}\,L_{N,2\alpha,\alpha} . \end{equation}
    Both kernels are positive, continuous and decreasing. The kernel \(g_{\text{s-SVD}}\) has integral \(1\), and the two terms of \(g_{\text{s-SGD}}\) have integrals \(1-2/\pi\) and \(2/\pi\). Their maxima are
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.29} \begin{gathered} k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}\defeq g_{\text{s-SVD}}(0)=\frac{2H_N(\alpha)}{H_N(\alpha/2)},\\ k_{N,\text{s-SGD}}\defeq g_{\text{s-SGD}}(0)=\frac{2(1-2/\pi)H_N(\alpha)}N+\frac{4H_N(2\alpha)}{\pi H_N(\alpha)} . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    If \(\hat u_j<\infty\), then
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.30} t^j_{T\epsilon}=\frac{\mathcal S_jT_0^j}{2\sqrt\epsilon}\int_0^{\hat u_j}\sqrt{Y_j(u)}\,\dif u . \end{equation}
  4. (iv)
    As \(N\to\infty\) at fixed \(\alpha\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.31} D_N\sim\begin{cases} \pi\,\dfrac{1-\alpha/2}{1-\alpha}\,N^{-\alpha/2},&0<\alpha<1,\\[4pt] \dfrac\pi2N^{-1/2}\log N,&\alpha=1,\\[4pt] \pi(1-\alpha/2)\,\zeta(\alpha)\,N^{\alpha/2-1},&1<\alpha<2,\\[4pt] \pi\zeta(2)/\log N,&\alpha=2,\\[4pt] \pi\zeta(\alpha)/\zeta(\alpha/2),&\alpha>2. \end{cases} \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
All coefficients are positive, since \(\mu_i/\bar\mu=Ni^{-\alpha}/H_N(\alpha)>0\) and \(2/\pi<1\), and the sums in (SI.1) are finite. For SignSVD,
\[ \mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}=\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{i^{-\alpha}}{2\gamma^{-1/2}i^{-\alpha/2}}=\frac{\sqrt\gamma}2H_N(\alpha/2) . \]
For SignSGD the factor \(\mu_i\) cancels and \(\sum_i\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=N^2\), so
\[ \mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac12\sqrt{\frac{\pi\bar\mu}B}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac{N^2}2\sqrt{\frac{\pi\bar\mu}B} . \]
The steps and the products follow, using \(N\bar\mu=H_N(\alpha)\) and \(N/B=\gamma\). This proves (i).
For (ii), substituting \(v=T_0^jw\) in the convolution gives
\[ Y_j(u)=F_j(T_0^ju)+T_0^j\int_0^uk_j\big(T_0^j(u-w)\big)\sqrt{Y_j(w)}\,\dif w=F_j(T_0^ju)+\sqrt\epsilon\,(g_j*\sqrt{Y_j})(u). \]
Since \(\mathfrak d_i^jT_0^j=i^{-q_j}\), the forcing is \(F_j(T_0^ju)=\frac12\sum_ii^{-p}e^{-2ui^{-q_j}}=f_{N,q_j}(u)\). At the matched step,
\[ g_j(u)=\frac{T_0^j}{\mathcal S_j}\sum_{i=1}^N\mu_i\mathfrak v_i^je^{-2ui^{-q_j}} , \]
and inserting (i) and the arrays gives (SI.28). Each exponential satisfies \(\int_0^\infty e^{-2ui^{-q}}\dif u=i^q/2\), which gives the three integrals. Each kernel is a positive combination of decreasing exponentials, and its value at \(0\) is (SI.29).
For (iii), Theorem SI.3(i) gives \(t^j_{T\epsilon}=t(H_Q(T\epsilon))\) with \(H_Q(T\epsilon)=T_0^j\hat u_j\), and the change of variables \(v=T_0^ju\) in the clock gives
\[ t(T_0^j\hat u_j)=\frac{T_0^j}{\lr^\star_j}\int_0^{\hat u_j}\sqrt{Y_j(u)}\,\dif u , \]
which is (SI.30). Part (iv) follows from (SI.27) and Lemma SI.8.

Windows. The phase theorems hold jointly in the dimension \(N\) and the accuracy \(\epsilon\), on explicit windows. For \(T\ge4\) fixed, write \(C_j\defeq C_{p,q_j}\) and \(A\defeq A_p\), and put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.32} \epsilon_0\defeq\min\Big\{\frac14,\ \frac1{8T},\ C_{\text{s-SVD}}^2,\ C_{\text{s-SGD}}^2,\ \Big(\frac AT\Big)^2,\ \min_{j:\,r_j<1}\frac A{T\,2^{2r_j/(1-r_j)}}\Big\}, \end{equation}
where a minimum over the empty set is \(1\). For \(0<\epsilon<C_j^2\) put
\[ H_j(\epsilon)\defeq\Big(\frac{C_j}{\sqrt\epsilon}\Big)^{1/r_j}-1 . \]
The low-exponent window is the set of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(N\ge4\), \(B\ge N\), \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\) and, for both \(j\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.33} k_{N,j}\,C_j\,I_{r_j}\big(H_j(\epsilon)\big)\le\sqrt\epsilon,\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_j(\epsilon)\le(N/4)^{q_j}\ \text{ if }r_j\le1 . \end{equation}
Finally, put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.34} \Pi_r(\epsilon)\defeq\begin{cases}\epsilon^{-1/(2r)},&0<r<1,\\ \epsilon^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsilon),&r=1,\\ \epsilon^{-1/2},&r>1.\end{cases} \end{equation}

Proposition SI.5 (The low-exponent window)
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1\). The kernel heights (SI.29) satisfy, as \(N\to\infty\),

Remark SI.3 (The windows cannot be dropped)
Corollary SI.2 shows that at fixed \(N\) the exponent of \(\epsilon\) is \(-\frac12\) in every phase, so the exponents of Theorem SI.7 require \(N\to\infty\) jointly with \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). In the family of Proposition SI.7, \(\texp=1\) and \(\epsilon N\to\infty\). The order \(\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}\epsilon^{-1}\), which Theorem SI.10 gives for SignSGD at \(\texp=1\) inside its window, fails on this family by the factor \(N^{-(\omega-\alpha)/2}\); the SignSVD time keeps its order but not the constant of (SI.37). The family violates the height condition (SI.33): by Proposition SI.5, \(k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}C_{\text{s-SVD}}\log(C_{\text{s-SVD}}/\sqrt{\epsilon_N})/\sqrt{\epsilon_N}\) grows like a constant times \(N^{(\omega-\alpha)/2}\log N\). The cause is the finite cutoff: the hitting clock is governed by the finite forcing integral \(J_N\), while the infinite-forcing accuracy scale lies beyond the cutoff. Within the windows, Proposition SI.6 gives the following map of the ratio \(t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}/t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}\): it tends to \(0\) for \(\alpha<1\) in every phase; it tends to \(\infty\) for \(\alpha>2\) and \(\texp\le\alpha+1\), and to a positive constant for \(\alpha>2\) and \(\texp>\alpha+1\); for \(1<\alpha\le2\) it tends to \(0\) on and above the curve \(\texp=\texp_c(\alpha)\) (for \(\alpha<2\)) and depends on the joint family below it, as it does in the range \(\texp<1\) for \(1\le\alpha\le2\).
Remark SI.4 (The adopted SignSGD array and the corrected modes)
The SignSGD section computes the raw annealed row volatility of the actual sign update, \(\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}\), for a Haar eigenbasis independent of the batch, comparable dimensions and a covariance bounded below (Theorem SJ.4). At \(\Nout=\Nin=N\), with \(\Sigmaout=U\Lambda U^\T\), the state \(\Delta=UD\) and \(\cR=\frac12\Tr(\Lambda DD^\T)\), its difference from the adopted array is
\[ \mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}-\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac2\pi\,N\Big[\frac{2\mu_i}{\Tr\Sigmaout}+\frac{(B+2N+3)\,(\Lambda DD^\T\Lambda)_{ii}}{2\cR\,\Tr\Sigmaout}\Big]\ \ge0 , \]
where \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=N(1-\frac2\pi)+\frac2\pi N^2\mu_i/\Tr\Sigmaout\). The corrected array depends on the state, its total exceeds \(N^2\), and the power-law family has no covariance lower bound fixed in \(N\). The phase theorems of this subsection concern the adopted array (SI.25). Replacing it by another nonnegative array with total \(N^2\) and the same drift leaves \(\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}\) unchanged, but not the kernel \(g_{\text{s-SGD}}\) on which the joint estimates rest.
Remark SI.5 (Momentum arrays)
For \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) put \(B_{\rm eff}\defeq B(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)=B/\sw2\). The adopted Muon array with momentum \(\bmom\) has the \(\mu_i\) and \(\fQ_i(0)\) of Definition SI.3, the batch \(B_{\rm eff}\) in place of \(B\) in the drift, and the volatility multiplied by \(B_{\rm eff}/B\):
\[ \mathfrak d_i^{\bmom}\defeq\sqrt{\mu_i}\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}N}=\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}B}\;\mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}},\qquad \mathfrak v_i^{\bmom}\defeq\frac{B_{\rm eff}}B=\frac{B_{\rm eff}}B\;\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SVD}} . \]
At \(\bmom=0\) it is the SignSVD array. The evidence for it is the resolved-window endpoint of Corollary SG.1(ii), quoted in Remark SI.1, which reads \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_iB_{\rm eff}/N}\) and \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to B_{\rm eff}/B\) in this notation.
The momentum array at a step \(\lr\) is the SignSVD array at the step \(\lr\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/B}\). For every \(\lr>0\), the right side of (SI.2) satisfies
\[ -\frac{2\lr\,\mathfrak d_i^{\bmom}}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,\fQ_i+\lr^2\mathfrak v_i^{\bmom} =-\frac{2\lr'\,\mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,\fQ_i+\lr'^2\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SVD}}, \qquad \lr'\defeq\lr\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}B} . \]
By (SI.1) the noise constant of the momentum array is \(\mathcal S^{\bmom}=\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/B}\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}\), so its matched step \(2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S^{\bmom}\) is mapped to the matched step \(\lr^\star_{\text{s-SVD}}\). At matched steps the two models therefore solve the same row equations from the same initial values, and they have the same solution by the uniqueness in Theorem SI.2. In particular \(t^{\bmom}_{T\epsilon}=t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}\), and Theorem SI.7, Corollary SI.3 and Proposition SI.6 hold for the momentum array without change. Written with the noise constant of the momentum array, the saturated prefactor \(\sqrt\gamma\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}\) of (SI.35) is \(\sqrt{N/B_{\rm eff}}\,\mathcal S^{\bmom}\).
The oversampled isotropic case refines the volatility factor. There the volatility with momentum is \(c_\bmom B_{\rm eff}/B\) times the volatility without momentum, with \(c_\bmom\defeq\sw2V_\bmom\in(\sw2,1)\), up to a factor \(1+O(\gamma)\) (Remark SF.1 and Lemma SF.10). A factor \(c>0\) on every volatility of an array multiplies every matched-step hitting time by \(c\). Indeed, the noise constant becomes \(c\,\mathcal S\) and the matched step becomes \(\lr^\star/c\), so the right side of (SI.2) becomes \(1/c\) times its value for the original array at its matched step. The risk of the modified model at time \(t\) is therefore the original risk at time \(t/c\).

SI.6 The accuracy scale

The phase theorems above hold in windows that keep the accuracy \(\epsilon\) above a power of \(N\). This subsection describes the two models of Definition SI.3 at every accuracy, jointly in \(N\) and \(\epsilon\). The organizing quantity is the accuracy scale
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.39} \epsilon_N\defeq N^{-(p-1)} . \end{equation}
It bounds the risk carried by the modes beyond \(N\). Indeed, by Lemma SI.6, for every \(q>0\) and \(u\ge0\),
\[ 0\le f_{\infty,q}(u)-f_{N,q}(u)\le\frac12\sum_{i>N}i^{-p}\le\frac{\epsilon_N}{2(p-1)} . \]
In this subsection \(\epsilon_N\) always denotes the scale (SI.39), and accuracies that vary with \(N\) are written \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\).
Two scale variables organize the estimates. For each model put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.40} x\defeq\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_N}=\epsilon N^{p-1},\qquad \mathsf y_j\defeq\epsilon N^{2q_j},\qquad \sqrt{\mathsf y_j}=\sqrt x\,N^{q_j(1-r_j)} , \end{equation}
where the last identity holds because \(2q_jr_j=p-1\). The regime \(x\to0\), in which the accuracy falls below the accuracy scale, is called frozen. Neither \(f_{N,q_j}\) nor \(g_j\) depends on \(B\) (Proposition SI.4(ii)). Hence the clock integral and the ratio of the two times,
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.41} \mathsf I_j\defeq\int_0^{\hat u_j}\sqrt{Y_j(u)}\,\dif u,\qquad \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}=D_N\,\frac{\mathsf I_{\text{s-SGD}}}{\mathsf I_{\text{s-SVD}}} , \end{equation}
do not depend on \(B\); the ratio follows from (SI.30) for the two models and (SI.27). In this subsection constants depend only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\) unless stated otherwise, and \(a\lesssim b\) means \(a\le cb\) for such a constant \(c\).
Forcing integrals. The finite forcing integral grows like a power of \(N\) when \(r_q<1\). For \(0<r<1\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.42} \mathcal K(r)\defeq\int_0^\infty y^{-r}\,\Gamma(2r,y)^{1/2}\,\dif y,\qquad J_\circ(p,q)\defeq(8q)^{-1/2}\,\mathcal K(r_q)\quad(r_q<1), \end{equation}
where \(\Gamma(a,y)\defeq\int_y^\infty t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,\dif t\) is the upper incomplete Gamma function [21, 8.2.2]. The integral converges. Indeed, \(e^{-t}\le e^{-y/2}e^{-t/2}\) for \(t\ge y\), so
\[ \Gamma(2r,y)\le e^{-y/2}\int_0^\infty t^{2r-1}e^{-t/2}\,\dif t=2^{2r}\,\Gamma(2r)\,e^{-y/2} , \]
and the integrand of \(\mathcal K(r)\) is at most \(2^r\Gamma(2r)^{1/2}y^{-r}e^{-y/4}\), which is integrable because \(r<1\).
Proposition SI.8 (Forcing integrals below the critical exponent)
Let \(p>1\) and \(q>0\) with \(r\defeq r_q<1\), and write \(C=C_{p,q}\) and \(A=A_p\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(N\ge1\), \(J_N(p,q)\le2\Gamma(1-r)\,C\,N^{q(1-r)}\).
  2. (ii)
    For every \(N\) with \((N/4)^{q(1-r)}\ge2\),
    \[ J_N(p,q)\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{2(1-r)}\,4^{-q(1-r)}N^{q(1-r)} . \]
  3. (iii)
    As \(N\to\infty\), \(J_N(p,q)/N^{q(1-r)}\to J_\circ(p,q)\), and \(0<J_\circ(p,q)\le2\Gamma(1-r)\,C\).
  4. (iv)
    \(\mathcal K(\frac12)=\sqrt{2\pi}\); consequently \(J_\circ(q+1,q)=\frac12\sqrt{\pi/q}\).
Together with the two other regimes, the growth of the forcing integral is
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.43} J_N(p,q)\ \begin{cases} \sim J_\circ(p,q)\,N^{q(1-r_q)},&r_q<1,\\ \sim\sqrt{q/8}\,\log N,&r_q=1,\\ \to J(p,q)\in(0,\infty),&r_q>1 . \end{cases} \end{equation}
The case \(r_q=1\) is Lemma SI.12(i), since \(a_qq=\sqrt{q/8}\). The case \(r_q>1\) is the bound \(0\le J(p,q)-J_N(p,q)\le[(2(p-1))^{-1/2}+C_{p,q}/(r_q-1)]N^{-q(r_q-1)}\) proved in the proof of Lemma SI.10(iii).
The accuracy-first ratio. At fixed dimension the accuracy limit is explicit. By Corollary SI.2, for fixed \(N\), \(B\), \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\ge4\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.44} \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}} =\rho_N\defeq D_N\,\frac{J_N(p,\alpha)}{J_N(p,\alpha/2)} . \end{equation}
The next theorem lets \(N\to\infty\) in \(\rho_N\). Write \(s\defeq r_{\text{s-SVD}}=(p-1)/\alpha\), so that \(r_{\text{s-SGD}}=s/2\); thus \(s<1\), \(s=1\), \(s>1\) according as \(\texp<1\), \(\texp=1\), \(\texp>1\). Put
\[ Q_A\defeq\frac{\mathcal K(s/2)}{\sqrt2\,\mathcal K(s)}\ \ (\texp<1),\qquad K_A\defeq\frac{\pi(2-\alpha)}{2\sqrt2\,(1-\alpha)}\,\frac{\mathcal K(s/2)}{\mathcal K(s)}\ \ (0<\alpha<1,\ \texp<1), \]
\[ K_c\defeq\frac{\pi(1-\alpha/2)\,\zeta(\alpha)\,\mathcal K\big(\frac{3\alpha-2}{2\alpha}\big)}{\sqrt{8\alpha}\;J(3\alpha-1,\alpha/2)}\ \ (1<\alpha<2) . \]

Theorem SI.13 (Frozen regime)
Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(\texp\) with \(p=\alpha+\texp>1\), and \(T\ge4\). Consider a sequence of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(N\to\infty\), integers \(B\ge N\) and \(x=\epsilon/\epsilon_N\to0\). For all large indices both first hits are attained, and for both \(j\)
\[ \frac{\mathsf I_j}{J_N(p,q_j)}\longrightarrow1,\qquad t^j_{T\epsilon}\sim\frac{\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,J_N(p,q_j)}{2\sqrt\epsilon},\qquad \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\sim\rho_N . \]
Moreover, if \(r_j<1\), there are \(N_2\) and \(c\) such that
\[ \Big|\frac{\mathsf I_j}{J_N(p,q_j)}-1\Big|\le c\,\Big(x^{1/4}+x^{1/2}\log\big(e+x^{-1/2}\big)\Big)^{1/2} \]
for all \(N\ge N_2\), \(B\ge N\), \(\epsilon<1/(2T)\) and \(x\le1\).
Since the ratio is asymptotic to \(\rho_N\), every limit, order and winner of Theorem SI.12 and Corollary SI.4 holds for the actual ratio of hitting times along every frozen sequence. The family of Proposition SI.7 is frozen, since \(x=N^{\alpha-\omega}\to0\) there; its ratio is therefore asymptotic to \(\pi^{3/2}(2-\alpha)/(\alpha(1-\alpha)\log N)\) by Theorem SI.12(b).
The unified clock. For a model with exponents \(q=q_j\) and \(r=r_j\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.45} \Upsilon_j(N,\epsilon)\defeq\begin{cases} \min\big\{\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)},\ N^{q(1-r)}\big\},&r<1,\\ \min\big\{\log(1/\epsilon),\ \log N\big\},&r=1,\\ 1,&r>1, \end{cases} \end{equation}

Equivalently, \((\alpha,\texp,j)\notin\mathcal U\) exactly for SignSVD at \(\alpha=1\), \(\texp\ge1\) and for SignSGD at \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\), \(\texp\ge\alpha+1\). For \(r<1\) the two branches of \(\Upsilon_j\) cross at the accuracy scale, since \(q(1-r)=(p-1)(1-r)/(2r)\):

Theorem SI.14 (Unified clock)
Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(\texp\) with \(\alpha+\texp>1\), \(T\ge4\) and \(j\in\{\text{s-SVD},\text{s-SGD}\}\).

    There are \(N_0\), \(\epsilon_0>0\) and \(c_->0\) such that for all \(N\ge N_0\), \(B\ge N\) and \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\) the first hit \(\hat u_j\) is attained and \(\mathsf I_j\ge c_-\Upsilon_j(N,\epsilon)\).
  2. (ii)

The threshold \(\epsilon_0\) may be taken to be \(\epsilon_0(p,q_j,T)\) of Lemma SI.10, and \(N_0\) the largest of the finitely many explicit thresholds of the proof; \(c_\pm\) are the displayed expressions of the proof. The unified clock contains the main-range window of Theorem SI.9 and extends it from \(\epsilon N\ge E_*\) to \(\epsilon\ge\epsilon_N\).

Corollary SI.6 (Ratio at every accuracy)

The accuracy exponent. Parametrize the accuracy by \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\) with \(\omega>0\). Then \(\omega>p-1\) is the frozen regime, and \(\omega<p-1\) lies above the accuracy scale. Put
\[ S(\omega)\defeq e_D+\chi\min\{\omega,p-1\},\qquad \mathsf E\defeq\{\alpha=1,\ \texp\ge1\}\cup\{\alpha=\tfrac12,\ \texp\ge\tfrac32\} , \]

Theorem SI.15 (Accuracy-exponent map)
Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(\texp\) with \(p=\alpha+\texp>1\), \(\omega>0\) and \(T\ge4\). As \(N\to\infty\):
  1. (i)
    if \(\omega>p-1\), then \(\mathsf R\sim\rho_N\), so every conclusion of Theorem SI.12 and Corollary SI.4 holds for \(\mathsf R\);
  2. (ii)
    if \((\alpha,\texp)\notin\mathsf E\), then \(\mathsf R\asymp N^{S(\omega)}(\log N)^L\);
  3. (iii)
    if \(\alpha=1\) and \(1\le\texp<2\), then \(\mathsf R\lesssim N^{S(\omega)}(\log N)^L\).
Reading off the sign of \(S(\omega)\), and of \(L\) where \(S(\omega)=0\), gives the winner. We say that SignSVD wins, that SignSGD wins, or that the two are balanced according as \(\mathsf R\to\infty\), \(\mathsf R\to0\) or \(\mathsf R\asymp1\). For \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(\texp<2\alpha-1\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.50} \omega_c\defeq\begin{cases}\dfrac{(2-\alpha)(p-1)}{\alpha},&\texp<1,\\[6pt] \dfrac{(2-\alpha)(p-1)}{\alpha+1-\texp},&1\le\texp<2\alpha-1,\end{cases} \qquad\text{so that}\quad0<\omega_c<p-1 . \end{equation}
Corollary SI.7 (Phases of the accuracy-exponent map)
Under the hypotheses of Theorem SI.15:
  1. (a)
    Phase A, \(\alpha>2\), \(\texp\le\alpha+1\): SignSVD wins for every \(\omega\).
  2. (b)
    Phase B, \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(\texp\ge1\) with \((\alpha,\texp)\notin\mathsf E\), or \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(\texp>2\alpha-1\): SignSGD wins for every \(\omega\).
  3. (c)
    Phase C, \(1<\alpha<2\), \(\texp<2\alpha-1\): SignSGD wins if \(\omega<\omega_c\) and SignSVD wins if \(\omega>\omega_c\); at \(\omega=\omega_c\) the two are balanced if \(\texp\ne1\), and SignSGD wins, with order \(1/\log N\), if \(\texp=1\).
  4. (d)
    Phase D, \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\texp<1\): SignSGD wins if \(\omega<p-1\), and the two are balanced if \(\omega\ge p-1\), with limit \(K_A\) if \(\omega>p-1\).
  5. (e)
    Tie, \(\alpha>2\), \(\texp>\alpha+1\): balanced for every \(\omega\).
  6. (f)
    Chord, \(1<\alpha<2\), \(\texp=2\alpha-1\): SignSGD wins if \(\omega<p-1\), and the two are balanced if \(\omega\ge p-1\), with limit \(K_c\) if \(\omega>p-1\).
  7. (g)
    \(\alpha=1\): for \(\texp<1\), SignSGD wins if \(\omega<p-1\) and SignSVD wins, with order \(\log N\), if \(\omega\ge p-1\); for \(\texp=1\), SignSGD wins if \(\omega<1\), and \(\mathsf R\to\pi^{3/2}\) if \(\omega>1\); for \(1<\texp<2\), SignSGD wins for every \(\omega\).
  8. (h)
    \(\alpha=2\): SignSVD wins for \(\texp<3\), the two are balanced for \(\texp=3\), and SignSGD wins for \(\texp>3\), for every \(\omega\).
The threshold \(\omega_c\) is the critical exponent of the windowed map. For \(\texp<1\) it is the \(\omega_c=r_{\text{s-SVD}}(2-\alpha)\) of Proposition SI.6(i), and for \(1\le\texp<2\alpha-1\) it is the formula of Proposition SI.6(v)(b). For \(\texp<1\), \(\omega_c<2-\alpha<1\), because \(p-1<\alpha\). For \(1\le\texp<2\alpha-1\), (SI.58) shows that \(\omega_c<1\) exactly when \(\texp<\texp_c(\alpha)\), and \(\omega_c=1\) exactly on the curve \(\texp=\texp_c(\alpha)\). The curve of Proposition SI.6(v) is therefore the cut of the map at \(\epsilon=N^{-1}\), the accuracy at which the window of Theorem SI.9 ends, while the chord \(\texp=2\alpha-1\) is its cut at \(\epsilon=\epsilon_N\). Since \(\omega_c<p-1\), SignSVD wins at every point of Phase C once \(\omega\ge p-1\); as \(\omega\to\infty\) the boundary between Phases C and B becomes the chord.
What is not covered. Three points are not proved. First, the winner on \(\{\alpha=1,\ \texp\ge2\}\cup\{\alpha=\frac12,\ \texp\ge\frac32\}\) for \(\omega\le p-1\), outside the windows of Proposition SI.5. There one of the two models lies outside \(\mathcal U\): the kernel templates of Lemma SI.25, on which the upper bound of Theorem SI.14 rests, do not cover the SignSVD kernel at \(\alpha=1\) or the SignSGD kernel at \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\). Second, at \(\alpha=\texp=\omega=1\) only \(\mathsf R\lesssim1\) is proved. Third, for \(\omega\le p-1\) on the balanced regions only orders are proved, not limit constants.

SI.7 Comparison through the first hit

The phase theorems are proved in two steps. A supersolution of square form, \((h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\), bounds the dimensionless risk up to a horizon at which it is at most \(4\epsilon\); this places the first hit and bounds the contribution of the noise to the clock. The clock integral through the hit is then evaluated from the forcing alone. The first step needs a bound on the convolution of the kernel with \(h\), which Section SI.8 supplies for each kernel; the second step is common to all phases.
Lemma SI.9 (Square supersolution)
Let \(\epsilon>0\), \(T\ge4\) and \(H>0\). Let \(g\ge0\) be continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with \(\int_0^\infty g\le1\), let \(f\ge0\) be continuous with \(f(0)>T\epsilon\), and let \(Y>0\) be continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with \(Y=f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt Y\). Let \(h\) be continuous on \([0,H]\) with \(h\ge\sqrt\epsilon\), \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\) and \(f\le h^2\). Assume that on \([0,H]\) either
\[ \text{(a)}\ \ g*h\le2h,\qquad\text{or}\qquad\text{(b)}\ \ g*h\le\sqrt\epsilon . \]
  1. (i)
    On \([0,H]\), \(f\le Y\le(h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) and \(Y-f\le2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon\); under (b), moreover \(Y-f\le2\epsilon\).
  2. (ii)
    The first hit \(\hat u\defeq\inf\{u\ge0:\ Y(u)\le T\epsilon\}\) satisfies \(0<\hat u\le H\) and \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\), and
    \[ 0\le\mathcal E\defeq\int_0^{\hat u}\big(\sqrt Y-\sqrt f\big)\,\dif u\le\int_0^H\sqrt{2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon}\,\dif u ; \]
    under (b), moreover \(\mathcal E\le\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,H\).
  3. (iii)
    Let \(C>0\), \(D\ge1\), \(0<r_0<2\) and \(0<\epsilon<C^2\), and take \(h(u)=C(1+u/D)^{-r_0}\) and \(H=D[(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}-1]\). Then \(h\ge\sqrt\epsilon\) on \([0,H]\), \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\), \(1+H\le D(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}\), and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.51} \int_0^H\sqrt{2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon}\,\dif u\le D\,C^{1/r_0}\Big[\frac{\sqrt2}{1-r_0/2}+1\Big]\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)} . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Put \(U\defeq(h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\), so that \(U\ge\epsilon\). Since \(\int g\le1\), \(g*(h+\sqrt\epsilon)\le g*h+\sqrt\epsilon\). Under (a),
\[ f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt U\le h^2+\sqrt\epsilon(2h+\sqrt\epsilon)=U . \]
Under (b), using \(h\ge\sqrt\epsilon\),
\[ f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt U\le h^2+2\epsilon\le h^2+2\sqrt\epsilon h+\epsilon=U . \]
Lemma SI.3, with kernel \(\sqrt\epsilon g\), \(G=f\) and \(m=\epsilon\), gives \(Y\le U\) on \([0,H]\). The equation gives \(Y\ge f\) and
\[ Y-f=\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt Y\le\sqrt\epsilon\,g*(h+\sqrt\epsilon), \]
which is at most \(2\sqrt\epsilon h+\epsilon\) under (a) and at most \(2\epsilon\le2\sqrt\epsilon h+\epsilon\) under (b). This proves (i).
At \(H\) the bound gives \(Y(H)\le(2\sqrt\epsilon)^2=4\epsilon\le T\epsilon\), while \(Y(0)=f(0)>T\epsilon\). The set \(\{u\le H:\ Y(u)\le T\epsilon\}\) is closed, nonempty and does not contain \(0\); its least element \(\hat u\) is the first hit, and continuity gives \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\). No monotonicity of \(Y\) is used. The inequality \(\sqrt x-\sqrt y\le\sqrt{x-y}\) for \(x\ge y\ge0\) and (i) give the bounds on \(\mathcal E\). This proves (ii).
For (iii), \(h\) is decreasing with \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\), and \(1+H/D=(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}\) gives the bound on \(1+H\) because \(D\ge1\). The inequality \(\sqrt{x+y}\le\sqrt x+\sqrt y\) gives
\[ \int_0^H\sqrt{2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon}\,\dif u\le\sqrt{2C}\,\epsilon^{1/4}D\,I_{r_0/2}(H/D)+\sqrt\epsilon\,H . \]
Since \(r_0<2\), \(I_{r_0/2}(x)\le(1+x)^{1-r_0/2}/(1-r_0/2)\). Inserting \(1+H/D=(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}\),
\[ \sqrt{2C}\,\epsilon^{1/4}D\,I_{r_0/2}(H/D)\le\frac{\sqrt2\,D\,C^{1/r_0}}{1-r_0/2}\,\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)},\qquad \sqrt\epsilon\,H\le D\,C^{1/r_0}\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)} , \]
which proves (SI.51).

The clock integral through the hit is evaluated from the forcing alone. For \(p>1\) and \(q>0\) write \(C=C_{p,q}\), \(A=A_p\), \(r=r_q\), and
\[ a_q\defeq(8q)^{-1/2},\qquad \epsilon_0(p,q,T)\defeq\min\Big\{\frac14,\ \frac1{8T},\ C^2,\ \Big(\frac AT\Big)^2,\ \frac A{T\,2^{2r/(1-r)}}\Big\}, \]
where the last entry is omitted if \(r\ge1\). The threshold \(\epsilon_0\) of (SI.32) is the smaller of \(\epsilon_0(p,q_j,T)\) over the two models.
Lemma SI.10 (The clock integral)
Fix \(p>1\), \(q>0\) and \(T\ge4\), and let \(N\ge4\) and \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0(p,q,T)\). Let \(Y\) be continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with \(Y\ge f_{N,q}\), and suppose that its first \(T\epsilon\) hit \(\hat u\) is attained, \(0<\hat u<\infty\) with \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\). Let \(H\ge\hat u\) and \(\mathcal E\defeq\int_0^{\hat u}(\sqrt Y-\sqrt{f_{N,q}})\,\dif u\).
  1. (i)
    If \(r<1\) and \(H\le(N/4)^q\), then
    \[ \frac{\sqrt A}{2(1-r)}\Big(\frac AT\Big)^{(1-r)/(2r)}\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)}\le\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\le C\,I_r(H)+\mathcal E . \]
  2. (ii)
    If \(r=1\) and \(H\le(N/4)^q\), then \(\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\ge\frac{\sqrt A}4\log\frac1\epsilon\), and
    \[ \Big|\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u-\frac{a_q}2\log\frac1\epsilon\Big|\le K_{p,q,T}+a_q\log_+\big((1+H)\sqrt\epsilon\big)+\frac{HN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}+\mathcal E , \]
    where \(K_{p,q,T}\defeq C+a_q\log2+qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}/a_q+\frac{a_q}2\log(T/A)\).
  3. (iii)
    If \(r>1\), then \(\frac1{2\sqrt2}\le\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\le J(p,q)+\mathcal E\), and
    \begin{multline*} \Big|\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u-J(p,q)\Big|\\ \le\Big[\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}+\frac C{r-1}\Big]N^{-q(r-1)}+\frac C{r-1}\Big(1+\frac12\log\frac1{2T\epsilon}\Big)^{1-r}+\mathcal E . \end{multline*}
Proof
Proof.
Write \(f=f_{N,q}\). By definition,
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.52} \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u=\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u+\mathcal E . \end{equation}
The level at the hit bounds the hit from below: \(f(\hat u)\le Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\).
For (i) and (ii), \(\hat u\le H\le(N/4)^q\), so the lower envelope of (SI.23) applies at \(\hat u\) and on \([0,\hat u]\). At \(\hat u\) it gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.53} 1+\hat u\ge\Big(\frac A{T\epsilon}\Big)^{1/(2r)} . \end{equation}
For \(r<1\), integrating the lower envelope gives
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u\ge\sqrt A\,I_r(\hat u)\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{1-r}\Big[\Big(\frac A{T\epsilon}\Big)^{(1-r)/(2r)}-1\Big]\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{2(1-r)}\Big(\frac AT\Big)^{(1-r)/(2r)}\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)} , \]
where the last step uses \((A/(T\epsilon))^{(1-r)/(2r)}\ge2\), which is the last entry of \(\epsilon_0\). The upper envelope gives \(\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\le C\,I_r(H)\), and (SI.52) proves (i).
For \(r=1\), integrating the lower envelope and using (SI.53) gives
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u\ge\sqrt A\log(1+\hat u)\ge\frac{\sqrt A}2\log\frac A{T\epsilon}\ge\frac{\sqrt A}4\log\frac1\epsilon , \]
where the last step uses \(\epsilon\le(A/T)^2\). For the constant, \(r=1\) means \(p=2q+1\), so \(d(p,q)=2\) and \(\Gamma(2)=1\). Lemma SI.6 with \(2u\) in place of \(a\), divided by \(2\), gives \(\abs{f_{\infty,q}(u)-a_q^2u^{-2}}\le M_{p,q}(2u)^{-p/q}\). Dividing by \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}+a_q/u\ge a_q/u\) gives, for \(u\ge1\),
\[ \Big|\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}(u)}-\frac{a_q}u\Big|\le\frac{M_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q}\,u^{-1-1/q} . \]
The right side has integral \(qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}/a_q\) over \([1,\infty)\), and \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\le C\) on \([0,1]\). Together with \(0\le\log(1+z)-\log z\le\log2\) for \(z\ge1\), this gives, for every \(z\ge0\),
\[ \Big|\int_0^z\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\,\dif u-a_q\log(1+z)\Big|\le C+a_q\log2+\frac{qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q} . \]
The finite sum differs from the infinite one by \(0\le f_{\infty,q}-f\le N^{1-p}/(2(p-1))\) (Lemma SI.6), and \(N^{(1-p)/2}=N^{-q}\), so
\[ 0\le\int_0^{\hat u}\big(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}-\sqrt f\big)\,\dif u\le\frac{\hat uN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}\le\frac{HN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}} . \]
Finally (SI.53) and \(\hat u\le H\) give
\[ -\frac12\log\frac TA\le\log(1+\hat u)-\frac12\log\frac1\epsilon\le\log\big((1+H)\sqrt\epsilon\big) . \]
Combining the last three displays with (SI.52) proves (ii).
For (iii), \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\le C(1+u)^{-r}\) is integrable and \(J(p,q)>0\). Since \(f\ge\frac12e^{-2u}\), the level at the hit gives \(\hat u\ge l_\epsilon\defeq\frac12\log\frac1{2T\epsilon}\), and \(l_\epsilon\ge\log2\) because \(\epsilon\le1/(8T)\). Hence
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\ge\int_0^{\log2}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt2}\,\dif u=\frac1{2\sqrt2} . \]
The upper bound follows from (SI.52) and \(f\le f_{\infty,q}\). For the limit, split
\[ J(p,q)-\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u=\big(J(p,q)-J_N(p,q)\big)+\int_{\hat u}^\infty\sqrt f\,\dif u . \]
On \([0,N^q]\) the pointwise bound \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}-\sqrt f\le N^{(1-p)/2}/\sqrt{2(p-1)}=N^{-qr}/\sqrt{2(p-1)}\) holds, and on \([N^q,\infty)\) the integrand is at most \(C(1+u)^{-r}\); hence
\[ 0\le J(p,q)-J_N(p,q)\le\Big[\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}+\frac C{r-1}\Big]N^{-q(r-1)} . \]
The upper envelope and \(\hat u\ge l_\epsilon\) give
\[ 0\le\int_{\hat u}^\infty\sqrt f\,\dif u\le\frac C{r-1}(1+l_\epsilon)^{1-r} . \]
With (SI.52) this proves (iii).

Lemma SI.11 (Finite exponential mixtures)
Let \(b_{\min}>0\), let
\[ f(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_ie^{-b_iu},\qquad g(u)=\sum_{l=1}^{n'}w_l\,c_l\,e^{-c_lu}, \]
with \(a_i\ge0\), \(F_0\defeq\sum_ia_i>0\), \(b_i,c_l\ge b_{\min}\), \(w_l\ge0\) and \(\sum_lw_l=1\). Let \(T\ge4\), \(0<\epsilon<F_0/T\), and let \(Y>0\) be the continuous solution of \(Y=f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt Y\). Put
\[ \mathsf J\defeq\int_0^\infty\sqrt{f(u)}\,\dif u,\qquad H\defeq\frac2{b_{\min}}\log\frac{F_0}\epsilon,\qquad\mathsf d\defeq\sqrt\epsilon\,H . \]
Then \(0<\mathsf J\le2\sqrt{F_0}/b_{\min}\), the first \(T\epsilon\) hit \(\hat u\) satisfies \(0<\hat u\le H\) and \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\), and
\[ \mathsf J-\frac{2\sqrt{T\epsilon}}{b_{\min}}\le\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\le\mathsf J+2\sqrt{\mathsf d\,\mathsf J}+\mathsf d . \]
Proof
Proof.
The equation has a unique positive continuous solution by Theorem SI.1, applied with the rates \(b_i\) and \(c_l\) and zero coefficients where needed. Since \(f\le F_0e^{-b_{\min}u}\), the integral \(\mathsf J\) is positive and at most \(2\sqrt{F_0}/b_{\min}\).
Let \(h(u)\defeq\sqrt{F_0}\,e^{-b_{\min}u/4}\), so that \(f\le h^2\) and \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\). Integrating each exponential gives
\[ \frac{(g*h)(u)}{h(u)}=\sum_lw_l\,\frac{c_l}{c_l-b_{\min}/4}\big(1-e^{-(c_l-b_{\min}/4)u}\big)\le\frac43 . \]
Hence \(U\defeq(h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) satisfies
\[ f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt U\le h^2+\tfrac43\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon\le U , \]
and Lemma SI.3 with \(m=\epsilon\) gives \(Y\le U\) on every compact interval. At \(H\), \(Y(H)\le4\epsilon\le T\epsilon\), while \(Y(0)=F_0>T\epsilon\), so the first hit is attained in \((0,H]\) with \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\).
For the lower bound, all rates are at least \(b_{\min}\), so \(f(v)\le f(\hat u)e^{-b_{\min}(v-\hat u)}\) for \(v\ge\hat u\), and \(f(\hat u)\le Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\). Hence
\[ \int_{\hat u}^\infty\sqrt f\,\dif v\le\frac{2\sqrt{T\epsilon}}{b_{\min}},\qquad \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\ge\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u\ge\mathsf J-\frac{2\sqrt{T\epsilon}}{b_{\min}} . \]
For the upper bound, write \(\mathsf I\defeq\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\). The inequality \(\sqrt{x+y}\le\sqrt x+\sqrt y\) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give
\[ \mathsf I\le\mathsf J+\epsilon^{1/4}\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt{(g*\sqrt Y)(u)}\,\dif u\le\mathsf J+\epsilon^{1/4}\Big(\hat u\int_0^{\hat u}(g*\sqrt Y)(u)\,\dif u\Big)^{1/2} . \]
Exchanging the order of integration and using \(\int g=1\),
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}(g*\sqrt Y)(u)\,\dif u=\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt{Y(v)}\int_0^{\hat u-v}g(w)\,\dif w\,\dif v\le\mathsf I . \]
Since \(\sqrt\epsilon\,\hat u\le\mathsf d\), this gives \(\mathsf I\le\mathsf J+\sqrt{\mathsf d\,\mathsf I}\). The quadratic \(x^2-\sqrt{\mathsf d}\,x-\mathsf J\) is increasing beyond its positive root and nonnegative at \(x=\sqrt{\mathsf J}+\sqrt{\mathsf d}\), so \(\sqrt{\mathsf I}\le\sqrt{\mathsf J}+\sqrt{\mathsf d}\). Squaring proves the upper bound.

Lemma SI.12 (Two finite forcing integrals)
Let \(p>1\), \(q>0\), \(C=C_{p,q}\) and \(A=A_p\).
  1. (i)
    If \(p=2q+1\), then for every \(N\ge1\)
    \[ \big|J_N(p,q)-a_q\,q\log N\big|\le2C+\frac{qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q}+\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}} . \]
  2. (ii)
    If \(p=q+1\), then for every \(N\ge4\cdot4^{1/q}\)
    \[ \sqrt A\,4^{-q/2}N^{q/2}\le J_N(p,q)\le3C\,N^{q/2} . \]
Proof
Proof.
Put \(L\defeq N^q\). Every rate of \(f_{N,q}\) is at least \(2/L\), so \(f_{N,q}(u)\le f_{N,q}(L)e^{-2(u-L)/L}\) for \(u\ge L\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.54} \int_L^\infty\sqrt{f_{N,q}}\,\dif u\le L\sqrt{f_{N,q}(L)}\le L\,C(1+L)^{-r_q} . \end{equation}
For (i), \(r_q=1\), so (SI.54) is at most \(C\). As in the proof of Lemma SI.10(ii),
\begin{gather*} 0\le\int_0^L\big(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}-\sqrt{f_{N,q}}\big)\,\dif u\le\frac{LN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}=\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}},\\ \Big|\int_0^L\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\,\dif u-a_q\log L\Big|\le C+\frac{qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q} , \end{gather*}
where the second bound splits the integral at \(1\) and uses \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\le C\) on \([0,1]\). Since \(a_q\log L=a_qq\log N\), adding the three errors proves (i).
For (ii), \(r_q=\frac12\). The upper envelope gives
\[ \int_0^L\sqrt{f_{N,q}}\,\dif u\le C\int_0^L(1+u)^{-1/2}\,\dif u\le2C\sqrt L , \]
and (SI.54) is at most \(C\sqrt L\), which proves the upper bound. For the lower bound put \(V\defeq(N/4)^q\ge4\). The lower envelope on \([0,V]\) gives
\[ J_N(p,q)\ge2\sqrt A\big(\sqrt{1+V}-1\big)\ge\sqrt A\sqrt V=\sqrt A\,4^{-q/2}N^{q/2} , \]
using \(\sqrt V\ge2\).

SI.8 Kernel estimates

This subsection proves the convolution bounds (a) and (b) of Lemma SI.9 for the kernels (SI.28). Three mechanisms appear. A decreasing kernel of small local mass contracts a slowly decaying envelope (Lemma SI.13). A kernel with an integrable power tail contracts a dilated envelope (Lemma SI.14). For the SignSVD kernel with \(1<\alpha\le2\), whose tail is not integrable, the harmonic normalization \(H_N(\alpha/2)\) makes the late part of the convolution small (Lemma SI.16, Lemma SI.17 and Lemma SI.18).
Lemma SI.13 (Local mass)
Let \(g\ge0\) be continuous and nonincreasing on \([0,\infty)\), and put \(m_g(u)\defeq\int_0^ug(v)\,\dif v\). Let \(C,D>0\), \(0<r<1\) and \(h(u)\defeq C(1+u/D)^{-r}\). Then
\[ (g*h)(u)\le\frac{m_g(u)}{1-r}\,h(u)\qquad(u\ge0). \]
Proof
Proof.
At \(u=0\) both sides vanish. Fix \(u>0\). On \([0,u]\) the function \(g\) is nonincreasing and \(v\mapsto h(u-v)\) is nondecreasing, so
\[ \int_0^u\!\!\int_0^u\big(g(v)-g(v')\big)\big(h(u-v)-h(u-v')\big)\,\dif v\,\dif v'\le0 . \]
Expanding the product gives
\[ u\,(g*h)(u)\le m_g(u)\int_0^uh(v)\,\dif v . \]
Direct integration gives
\[ \frac1{u\,h(u)}\int_0^uh(v)\,\dif v=\frac{D+u-D(1+u/D)^r}{(1-r)\,u}\le\frac1{1-r} , \]
since \((1+u/D)^r\ge1\).
□
For \(\upsilon>1\), \(0<r_0\le\upsilon\) and \(K>0\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.55} \begin{gathered} \delta_\circ\defeq1-(2/3)^{1/r_0},\qquad b(r_0,\upsilon)\defeq\begin{cases}(1-r_0)^{-1},&r_0<1,\\ (e(\upsilon-1))^{-1},&r_0=1,\\ (r_0-1)^{-1},&r_0>1,\end{cases}\\ D(K,r_0,\upsilon)\defeq\max\Big\{1,\big[2K\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon}b(r_0,\upsilon)\big]^{1/(\upsilon-1)}\Big\} . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Lemma SI.14 (Dilated envelope)
Let \(g\ge0\) be continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with \(\int_0^\infty g\le1\), and suppose \(g(v)\le K(1+v)^{-\upsilon}\) for \(0\le v\le H_*\), where \(H_*>0\), \(\upsilon>1\) and \(K>0\). Let \(0<r_0\le\upsilon\), \(C>0\), \(D=D(K,r_0,\upsilon)\) and \(h(u)\defeq C(1+u/D)^{-r_0}\). Then
\[ \frac{(g*h)(u)}{h(u)}\le\frac32+K\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon}D^{1-\upsilon}b(r_0,\upsilon)\le2\qquad(0\le u\le H_*). \]
Proof
Proof.
Split the convolution at \(v=\delta_\circ(D+u)\). For \(0\le v\le\min(u,\delta_\circ(D+u))\),
\[ \frac{h(u-v)}{h(u)}=\Big(\frac{D+u}{D+u-v}\Big)^{r_0}\le(1-\delta_\circ)^{-r_0}=\frac32 , \]
and \(\int g\le1\) bounds this part by \(\frac32h(u)\). If \(\delta_\circ(D+u)\ge u\) there is nothing else. Otherwise, on the remaining part \(v\le u\le H_*\) and \(g(v)\le K\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon}(D+u)^{-\upsilon}\). Enlarging the integral of \(h(u-v)\) to \([0,u]\) and writing \(y=u/D\) gives
\[ \frac1{h(u)}\int_{\delta_\circ(D+u)}^ug(v)h(u-v)\,\dif v\le K\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon}D^{1-\upsilon}(1+y)^{r_0-\upsilon}I_{r_0}(y) . \]
It remains to bound \((1+y)^{r_0-\upsilon}I_{r_0}(y)\) by \(b(r_0,\upsilon)\). For \(r_0<1\), \(I_{r_0}(y)\le(1+y)^{1-r_0}/(1-r_0)\) and \((1+y)^{1-\upsilon}\le1\). For \(r_0=1\), \(I_1(y)=\log(1+y)\) and \(\sup_{x\ge1}x^{1-\upsilon}\log x=1/(e(\upsilon-1))\). For \(r_0>1\), \(I_{r_0}\le1/(r_0-1)\) and \((1+y)^{r_0-\upsilon}\le1\). The choice of \(D\) makes the second term at most \(\frac12\).
□
The next three lemmas concern the SignSVD kernel. Write \(q\defeq\alpha/2\) and \(g\defeq g_{\text{s-SVD}}\).
Lemma SI.15 (SignSVD kernel, \(0<\alpha<2\), small times)
Let \(0<\alpha<2\) and \(0<r<1\), and put
\[ \delta_1\defeq\Big[\frac{1-r}8\Big]^{1/(1-q)},\qquad \mathsf c_1\defeq\frac{(1-r)\delta_1^q}8, \]
\[ N_1\defeq\Big\lceil\max\Big\{4,\ 2^{1/(1-q)},\ \Big[\frac{8(1-q)}{1-r}\Big]^{1/(1-q)}\Big\}\Big\rceil . \]
For \(N\ge N_1\) and \(0\le u\le\mathsf c_1N^q\), \(m_g(u)\le\frac34(1-r)\). Consequently, for every \(C>0\) and \(h(u)=C(1+u)^{-r}\), \((g*h)(u)\le\frac34h(u)\) on \([0,\mathsf c_1N^q]\).
Proof
Proof.
Integrating the kernel gives exactly
\[ m_g(u)=\frac1{H_N(q)}\sum_{i=1}^Ni^{-q}\big(1-e^{-2ui^{-q}}\big) . \]
By Lemma SI.8 and \(N^{1-q}\ge2\), \(H_N(q)\ge(N^{1-q}-1)/(1-q)\ge N^{1-q}/(2(1-q))\). Let \(J\defeq\lfloor\delta_1N\rfloor\). The indices \(i\le J\) contribute at most \(H_J(q)/H_N(q)\), with \(H_0(q)=0\), and for \(J\ge1\), \(H_J(q)\le1+J^{1-q}/(1-q)\); hence
\[ \frac{H_J(q)}{H_N(q)}\le2(1-q)N^{-(1-q)}+2\delta_1^{1-q}\le\frac{1-r}4+\frac{1-r}4 . \]
For \(i>J\) one has \(i>\delta_1N\), and \(1-e^{-x}\le x\) gives a contribution of at most
\[ \frac{2u}{(\delta_1N)^q}\cdot\frac1{H_N(q)}\sum_{i>J}i^{-q}\le2\mathsf c_1\delta_1^{-q}=\frac{1-r}4\qquad(u\le\mathsf c_1N^q). \]
Adding gives \(m_g(u)\le\frac34(1-r)\). The kernel is decreasing, so Lemma SI.13 with \(D=1\) gives the last assertion.
□
Lemma SI.16 (SignSVD kernel, \(1<\alpha<2\), \(\texp\ge1\))
Let \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(\texp\ge1\), so that \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}\ge1\). Put \(r_0\defeq1\) if \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}=1\) and \(r_0\defeq\min\{r_{\text{s-SVD}},(1+2/\alpha)/2\}\) if \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}>1\). Put \(\upsilon_\circ\defeq2-2/\alpha\in(0,1)\), \(K_{\rm V}\defeq2E_{\alpha,q}\), \(\delta_\circ\defeq1-(2/3)^{1/r_0}\), \(d_\alpha\defeq(\alpha-1)(2-\alpha)/(2\alpha)\),
\[ (J_{\rm V},d_{\rm V})\defeq\begin{cases}\big(K_{\rm V}\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon_\circ}/(e\,d_\alpha),\ d_\alpha/2\big),&r_0=1,\\[2pt] \big(K_{\rm V}\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon_\circ}/(r_0-1),\ 1-q-(r_0-\upsilon_\circ)/(2r_0)\big),&r_0>1,\end{cases} \]
\[ N_{\rm V}\defeq\big\lceil\max\{1,(2J_{\rm V})^{1/d_{\rm V}}\}\big\rceil . \]
Then \(d_{\rm V}>0\). Let \(C=C_{p,q}\), \(N\ge N_{\rm V}\), \(0<\epsilon<C^2\) with \(\epsilon N\ge C^2\), \(h(u)=C(1+u)^{-r_0}\) and \(H=(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}-1\). Then \(1+H\le\sqrt N\) and \((g*h)(u)\le2h(u)\) on \([0,H]\).
Proof
Proof.
The quantity \(1-q-(r_0-\upsilon_\circ)/(2r_0)=\frac12-q+\upsilon_\circ/(2r_0)\) decreases in \(r_0\) and vanishes at \(r_0=\upsilon_\circ/(2q-1)=2/\alpha\). Since \(r_0\le(1+2/\alpha)/2<2/\alpha\) for \(\alpha<2\), \(d_{\rm V}>0\). The window gives \(1+H=(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}\le N^{1/(2r_0)}\le\sqrt N\), because \(r_0\ge1\).
By Lemma SI.7(i) with \(a=\alpha>1\),
\[ g(v)=\frac2{H_N(q)}L_{N,\alpha,q}(v)\le\frac{K_{\rm V}}{H_N(q)}(1+v)^{-\upsilon_\circ} . \]
Split the convolution at \(v=\delta_\circ(1+u)\). As in the proof of Lemma SI.14, the early part is at most \(\frac32h(u)\). On the late part \(g(v)\le K_{\rm V}\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon_\circ}(1+u)^{-\upsilon_\circ}/H_N(q)\), and
\[ \frac1{h(u)}\int_{\delta_\circ(1+u)}^ug(v)h(u-v)\,\dif v\le\frac{K_{\rm V}\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon_\circ}}{H_N(q)}(1+u)^{r_0-\upsilon_\circ}I_{r_0}(u) . \]
By Lemma SI.8, \(H_N(q)\ge N^{1-q}\). If \(r_0=1\), then \((1+u)^{1-\upsilon_\circ}\log(1+u)\le N^{(1-\upsilon_\circ)/2}\cdot\frac12\log N\) for \(u\le H\), and \((1-\upsilon_\circ)/2-(1-q)=-d_\alpha\); with \(\sup_{x\ge1}x^{-d_\alpha/2}\log x=2/(e\,d_\alpha)\) the late part is at most
\[ \frac{K_{\rm V}\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon_\circ}}2N^{-d_\alpha}\log N\le J_{\rm V}N^{-d_{\rm V}} . \]
If \(r_0>1\), then \(I_{r_0}\le1/(r_0-1)\) and \((1+u)^{r_0-\upsilon_\circ}\le(1+H)^{r_0-\upsilon_\circ}\le N^{(r_0-\upsilon_\circ)/(2r_0)}\), so the late part is at most \(J_{\rm V}N^{-d_{\rm V}}\). In both cases \(N\ge N_{\rm V}\) makes the late part at most \(\frac12\), and the sum is at most \(2\).

Lemma SI.17 (SignSVD kernel, \(\alpha=2\))
Let \(\alpha=2\), so \(q=1\) and \(g=2L_{N,2,1}/H_N(1)\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(v\ge0\),
    \[ g(v)\le\frac1{H_N(1)}\Big[\frac1{1+v}+\frac{16}{(1+v)^2}\Big] . \]
  2. (ii)
    For \(0<r<1\), \(C>0\) and \(h(u)=C(1+u)^{-r}\),
    \[ \frac{(g*h)(u)}{h(u)}\le\frac{\log(1+u)+A^{\log}_r}{H_N(1)},\qquad A^{\log}_r\defeq2^r\Big(r+\frac1{1-r}\Big)+\frac{16}{1-r} . \]
  3. (iii)
    For \(C>0\) and \(h(u)=C(1+u)^{-1}\),
    \[ \frac{(g*h)(u)}{h(u)}\le\frac{2\log(1+u)+64}{H_N(1)} . \]
Proof
Proof.
For (i) and \(v\ge1\), Lemma SI.6 with \(p=2\), \(q=1\), \(a=2v\) gives, since \(\Gamma(1)=1\) and \(M_{2,1}=(2/e)^2<1\),
\[ 2L_{N,2,1}(v)\le2L_{\infty,2,1}(v)\le\frac1v+\frac{M_{2,1}}{v^2}\le\frac1{1+v}+\frac2{(1+v)^2}+\frac4{(1+v)^2} , \]
using \(\frac1v-\frac1{1+v}=\frac1{v(1+v)}\le\frac2{(1+v)^2}\) and \(v^{-2}\le4(1+v)^{-2}\) for \(v\ge1\). For \(0\le v\le1\), \(2L_{N,2,1}(v)\le2\zeta(2)\le4\le16(1+v)^{-2}\) by Lemma SI.8. Dividing by \(H_N(1)\) proves (i).

\[ \frac{\big((1+\cdot)^{-1}*h\big)(u)}{h(u)}-\log(1+u)=\int_0^u\frac{\big((1+u)/(1+u-v)\big)^r-1}{1+v}\,\dif v\le\int_0^1\frac{(1-w)^{-r}-1}w\,\dif w , \]
where \(w=v/(1+u)\) and \(\dif v/(1+v)\le\dif w/w\) with a nonnegative integrand. For \(0<w\le\frac12\) the mean value theorem bounds the integrand by \(r2^{r+1}\), and for \(\frac12<w<1\) it is at most \(2(1-w)^{-r}\); the two integrals are at most \(r2^r\) and \(2^r/(1-r)\). The kernel \((1+v)^{-2}\) is decreasing with integral at most \(1\), so Lemma SI.13 bounds its ratio by \(1/(1-r)\). Part (i) then gives (ii).
For (iii), partial fractions give
\[ (1+u)\int_0^u\frac{\dif v}{(1+v)(1+u-v)}=\frac{2(1+u)}{2+u}\log(1+u)\le2\log(1+u) . \]
For the second term split at \(v=u/2\): on \([0,u/2]\), \(1+u-v\ge(1+u)/2\), and on \([u/2,u]\), \((1+v)^{-2}\le4(1+u)^{-2}\). Hence
\[ (1+u)\int_0^u\frac{\dif v}{(1+v)^2(1+u-v)}\le2+\frac{4\log(1+u)}{1+u}\le2+\frac4e\le4 . \]
Part (i) then gives (iii).

Lemma SI.18 (SignSVD kernel, \(\alpha=2\), \(\texp>1\))
Let \(\alpha=2\) and \(\texp>1\), so \(q=1\) and \(r\defeq r_{\text{s-SVD}}>1\). Put \(C\defeq C_{p,1}\), \(K\defeq2E_{2,1}\), \(L_N\defeq H_N(1)\) and
\[ \mathsf A_0\defeq\frac{CK}{r-1}\Big(\frac{2^r}e+2\Big),\qquad L_0\defeq\max\Big\{1,\frac{4K}{2-\sqrt2}\Big\}, \]
\[ h(u)\defeq C(1+u)^{-r},\qquad \mathsf w(u)\defeq\sqrt{\mathsf A_0}\,\epsilon^{1/4}L_N^{-1/2}(1+u)^{-1/2} . \]
Let \(N\ge e^{L_0}\), \(B\ge N\) and \(\epsilon>0\). Then \(f_{N,1}\le Y_{\text{s-SVD}}\le(h+\mathsf w+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) on \([0,\infty)\). If moreover \(T\ge4\) and \(0<\epsilon<\min\{1,C^2,1/(8T)\}\), the first hit satisfies \(0<\hat u\le H\defeq\max\{(2C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r},4\mathsf A_0/(L_N\sqrt\epsilon)\}-1\), and
\begin{multline*} 0\le\mathcal E\le\epsilon^{1/8}\sqrt{C+\sqrt{\mathsf A_0}+1}+\frac C{r-1}\big(1+\epsilon^{-1/8}\big)^{1-r}\\ +2\sqrt{\mathsf A_0}(2C)^{1/(2r)}L_N^{-1/2}\epsilon^{(r-1)/(4r)}+(2C)^{1/r}\epsilon^{(r-1)/(2r)}+\frac{8\mathsf A_0}{L_N} . \end{multline*}
In particular \(\mathcal E\to0\) along every sequence with \(N\to\infty\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).
Proof
Proof.
By Lemma SI.7(i), \(g(v)\le K/(L_N(1+v))\). The convolution with \(h\) is bounded by splitting at \(u/2\). On \([0,u/2]\), \(h(u-v)\le C2^r(1+u)^{-r}\), so this part is at most
\[ \frac{CK2^r}{L_N}(1+u)^{-r}\log(1+u)\le\frac{CK2^r}{e(r-1)L_N(1+u)} , \]
using \(\sup_{x\ge1}x^{-(r-1)}\log x=1/(e(r-1))\). On \([u/2,u]\), \(g(v)\le2K/(L_N(1+u))\) and \(\int h\le C/(r-1)\), so this part is at most \(2CK/((r-1)L_N(1+u))\). Hence
\[ \sqrt\epsilon\,(g*h)(u)\le\frac{\sqrt\epsilon\,\mathsf A_0}{L_N(1+u)}=\mathsf w(u)^2 . \]
The convolution with \(\mathsf w\) is bounded in the same way. On \([0,u/2]\), \(\mathsf w(u-v)\le\sqrt2\,\mathsf w(u)\) and \(\int g=1\). On \([u/2,u]\), \(g(v)\le2K/(L_N(1+u))\) and \(\int_0^{u/2}(1+z)^{-1/2}\dif z\le2\sqrt{1+u}\). Hence
\[ (g*\mathsf w)(u)\le\Big(\sqrt2+\frac{4K}{L_N}\Big)\mathsf w(u)\le2\,\mathsf w(u) , \]
because \(L_N\ge\log(N+1)\ge L_0\). Put \(U\defeq(h+\mathsf w+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\ge\epsilon\). Using \(\int g=1\),
\[ f_{N,1}+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt U\le h^2+\mathsf w^2+2\sqrt\epsilon\,\mathsf w+\epsilon\le U , \]
and Lemma SI.3 with \(m=\epsilon\) gives \(Y\le U\) on every compact interval.
At \(H\), \(h(H)\le\sqrt\epsilon/2\) and \(\mathsf w(H)^2=\sqrt\epsilon\mathsf A_0/(L_N(1+H))\le\epsilon/4\), so \(Y(H)\le4\epsilon\le T\epsilon\). Since \(Y(0)\ge\frac12>T\epsilon\), the first hit is attained in \((0,H]\) as in Lemma SI.9(ii).
For \(\mathcal E\), the kernel has integral \(1\) and \(h+\mathsf w+\sqrt\epsilon\) is decreasing, so
\[ 0\le Y-f_{N,1}\le\sqrt\epsilon\,\big(C+\sqrt{\mathsf A_0}\epsilon^{1/4}L_N^{-1/2}+\sqrt\epsilon\big)\le\sqrt\epsilon\,\big(C+\sqrt{\mathsf A_0}+1\big) . \]
Let \(R_0\defeq\epsilon^{-1/8}\). On \([0,\min(\hat u,R_0)]\) bound \(\sqrt Y-\sqrt f\) by \(\sqrt{Y-f}\), and on \([R_0,\hat u]\) by \(h+\mathsf w+\sqrt\epsilon\). This gives
\[ \mathcal E\le R_0\,\epsilon^{1/4}\sqrt{C+\sqrt{\mathsf A_0}+1}+\frac C{r-1}(1+R_0)^{1-r}+\int_0^H\mathsf w\,\dif u+\sqrt\epsilon\,H . \]
Integrating \((1+u)^{-1/2}\) and using \(\sqrt{\max(x,y)}\le\sqrt x+\sqrt y\),
\[ \int_0^H\mathsf w\,\dif u\le2\sqrt{\mathsf A_0}(2C)^{1/(2r)}L_N^{-1/2}\epsilon^{(r-1)/(4r)}+\frac{4\mathsf A_0}{L_N},\qquad \sqrt\epsilon\,H\le(2C)^{1/r}\epsilon^{(r-1)/(2r)}+\frac{4\mathsf A_0}{L_N} . \]
This proves the bound. Each term tends to zero, since \(r>1\) and \(L_N\ge\log(N+1)\to\infty\).

SI.10 Proofs for the accuracy scale

Lemma SI.23 (Kernel with a slow part)

with \(K_f,K_s\ge0\), \(\upsilon>1\), \(0\le\mathsf b<1\), \(N\ge1\) and \(q>0\). Let \(0<r_0\le\upsilon\), \(D\ge1\), \(C>0\), \(h(u)\defeq C(1+u/D)^{-r_0}\), \(\delta_\circ=1-(2/3)^{1/r_0}\) and \(\mathsf k_f\defeq K_f\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon}D^{1-\upsilon}b(r_0,\upsilon)\), with \(b(r_0,\upsilon)\) of (SI.55). \[ (g*h)(u)\le\Big(\frac32+\mathsf k_f\Big)h(u)+K_s\delta_\circ^{-\mathsf b}N^{-q}\Big(\frac{D+u}{N^q}\Big)^{-\mathsf b}C\,D\,I_{r_0}(u/D), \]

Proof
Proof.
Fix \(u\in(0,U_0]\) and split the convolution at \(v=\delta_\circ(D+u)\). For \(0\le v\le\min\{u,\delta_\circ(D+u)\}\) one has \(D+u-v\ge(1-\delta_\circ)(D+u)\), so \(h(u-v)\le(1-\delta_\circ)^{-r_0}h(u)=\frac32h(u)\), and the mass of \(g\) bounds this part by \(\frac32h(u)\). For \(\delta_\circ(D+u)<v\le u\) one has \(1+v>\delta_\circ(D+u)\), so (SI.60) gives
\[ g(v)\le K_f\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon}(D+u)^{-\upsilon}+K_s\delta_\circ^{-\mathsf b}N^{-q}\Big(\frac{D+u}{N^q}\Big)^{-\mathsf b}, \]
while \(\int_0^uh=C\,D\,I_{r_0}(u/D)\). With \(z=u/D\) the fast part is at most
\[ K_f\delta_\circ^{-\upsilon}D^{1-\upsilon}(1+z)^{r_0-\upsilon}I_{r_0}(z)\,h(u)\le\mathsf k_f\,h(u) , \]
