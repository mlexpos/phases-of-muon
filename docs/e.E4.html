E4: From the algorithm to the deterministic dynamics

SN.10 From the algorithm to the deterministic dynamics

This is the argument of Theorem SE.1, sketched in full after that theorem. It takes in the drift kernel \(\mathfrak d\) (Section C.2), the forcing measure \(\zeta\) with the covariance of consecutive updates (Section C.3), and an initial error that is diffuse: the normalized error \(\widehat A_0\), of Frobenius norm one, has vanishing operator norm. It produces the comparison, up to a time \(T_\star\), of the spectral energy measure \(\chi(\Delta_t)\), which places \(\CMscr Q_{ij}\) at \((\mu_i,\lambda_j)\), with the solution of the deterministic measure equation ((SE.9)).
The argument has four ideas. The algorithm is compared, over blocks of \(L=\lfloor B^{1/8}\rfloor\) steps, with the model in which the state is held at its value at the start of the block and the momentum buffer is a stationary average of independent batches; because a test of the energy measure is a quadratic form in the error, its expected change over a block has an exact expansion ((SE.28)). The two leading terms of that expansion are the drift and the volatility, and because the block is long the momentum correlations of consecutive updates are summed in full. The motion of the state inside a block changes the expected update only through a causal response, which Stein’s identity reduces to projections that are small because the error is diffuse (Proposition SE.4). Finally, diffuseness persists, by an operator-norm bound on the mean update that holds at every state and a matrix martingale inequality (Lemma SE.24). Gronwall’s inequality and routine estimates close the comparison.
