Assumption S1 (Data-target normalization)
The input and output features are independent and Gaussian, \(\Xin\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigmain)\) and \(\Xout\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigmaout)\), with positive semidefinite covariances \(\Sigmain\in\R^{\Nin\times\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nout}\). They are represented in whitened form as \(\Xin=\Sigmain^{1/2}w\) and \(\Xout=\Sigmaout^{1/2}z\) with \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) and \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) independent; the whitened columns of a batch are written \((w_a,z_a)_{a\le B}\).
This is the data model of Section 1.1. In the whitened form the residual of a sample is \(d=z^\T\At w\) with the rotated error \(\At=\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Dt\Sigmain^{1/2}\), and \(\cR(t)=\tfrac12\norm{\At}_\F^2\) ((SA.9)). Every random-matrix statement uses the normalized residual \(d/\sqrt{2\cR(t)}=z^\T\Ahat w\), which has unit variance and is close to a standard Gaussian when the normalized error \(\Ahat\) has small operator norm (Lemma SA.7). Gaussianity is used for these computations (Wick pairings and Stein’s identity); the exact one-step risk recursion needs only second moments.
