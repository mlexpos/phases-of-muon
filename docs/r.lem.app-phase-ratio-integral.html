Lemma SI.4 (An integral for the drift constant)
The map \(\Phi\) is a continuously differentiable increasing bijection from \((0,\infty)\) onto \((0,1)\); let \(V\) be its inverse and \(w(y)\defeq V(y)^{-1/2}\) for \(0<y<1\). The function \(w_\circ(y)\defeq\sqrt y\,w(y)\) is positive, strictly decreasing, at most \(1\), and tends to \(1\) as \(y\downarrow0\). For every \(\gamma>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.18} C(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma^2}\,I(\gamma),\qquad I(\gamma)\defeq\int_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}(\gamma-y)\,w(y)\,\dif y . \end{equation}
Moreover
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.19} \mathcal A\defeq\int_0^1w(y)\,\dif y=\sqrt{\frac\pi2},\qquad \mathcal D\defeq\int_0^1y\,w(y)\,\dif y\in(0,\mathcal A),\qquad I(\gamma)=\gamma\mathcal A-\mathcal D\quad(\gamma\ge1). \end{equation}
Proof.
The map \(\Phi\) is continuously differentiable with
\[ \Phi'(v)=\EE\frac{Z^2}{(1+vZ^2)^2}>0 . \]
The quotient \(\Phi(v)/v=\EE[Z^2/(1+vZ^2)]\) is strictly decreasing in \(v\), tends to \(\EE Z^2=1\) as \(v\downarrow0\), and \(\Phi(v)\to1\) as \(v\to\infty\). Hence \(\Phi\) is an increasing bijection onto \((0,1)\) with a continuously differentiable inverse. Since
\[ w_\circ(y)^2=\frac{y}{V(y)}=\frac{\Phi(V(y))}{V(y)} \]
and \(V\) is increasing, \(w_\circ\) is strictly decreasing, at most \(1\), and tends to \(1\) at \(0\). In particular \(w(y)\le y^{-1/2}\), so every integral in the statement is finite.
The function \(\psi_\gamma\) is the inverse of an explicit function. For \(0<y<\min(\gamma,1)\) put \(\Theta_\gamma(y)\defeq(\gamma-y)^2/V(y)\). It is strictly decreasing. It tends to \(\infty\) as \(y\downarrow0\), because \(V(y)/y\to1\). It tends to \(0\) at the upper endpoint, because the numerator tends to \(0\) if \(\gamma<1\) and the denominator tends to \(\infty\) if \(\gamma\ge1\). So \(\Theta_\gamma\) is a bijection onto \((0,\infty)\). The quantity \(t\sigma_\gamma(t)=\int t(x+t)^{-1}\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\) lies in \((0,\gamma]\), and (SI.17) gives \(\psi_\gamma(t)=\Phi(\cdot)\in(0,1)\); hence \(0<\psi_\gamma(t)<\min(\gamma,1)\). By (SI.17), \(V(\psi_\gamma(t))=t\sigma_\gamma(t)^2=(\gamma-\psi_\gamma(t))^2/t\), that is,
\[ t=\Theta_\gamma(\psi_\gamma(t))\qquad(t>0). \]
The half moment is an integral of \(\psi_\gamma\). The half-moment formula (SF.26) of Lemma SF.3(v), applied to \(\nu=\mu_\gamma\), expresses \(\int\sqrt x\,\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\) through \(\int x(x+t)^{-1}\mu_\gamma(\dif x)\). This inner integral is
\[ \int\frac{x}{x+t}\,\mu_\gamma(\dif x)=\gamma-t\sigma_\gamma(t)=\psi_\gamma(t), \]
so that
\[ \int\sqrt x\,\mu_\gamma(\dif x)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\psi_\gamma(t)\,\dif t . \]
The change of variables \(t=\Theta_\gamma(y)\) and an integration by parts evaluate this integral:
\begin{align*} \int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\psi_\gamma(t)\,\dif t&=\int_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}y\,\big(-\Theta_\gamma'(y)\big)\Theta_\gamma(y)^{-1/2}\,\dif y\\ &=\Big[-2y\sqrt{\Theta_\gamma(y)}\Big]_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}+2\int_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}\sqrt{\Theta_\gamma(y)}\,\dif y . \end{align*}
The boundary term vanishes at \(0\) because \(\sqrt{\Theta_\gamma(y)}=(\gamma-y)w(y)\le\gamma y^{-1/2}\), and at the upper endpoint because \(y\le1\) and \(\Theta_\gamma\to0\). Hence the integral equals \(2I(\gamma)\), and the definition of \(C(\gamma)\) gives (SI.18).
For (SI.19), the substitution \(y=\Phi(v)\) gives
\[ \mathcal A=\int_0^\infty v^{-1/2}\Phi'(v)\,\dif v=\EE\int_0^\infty\frac{Z^2v^{-1/2}}{(1+vZ^2)^2}\,\dif v=\EE\abs Z\int_0^\infty\frac{y'^{-1/2}}{(1+y')^2}\,\dif y'=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\cdot\frac\pi2 , \]
with \(y'=vZ^2\) in the third step. The last integral is the Beta integral \(\Gamma(\frac12)\Gamma(\frac32)/\Gamma(2)=\pi/2\) [85, 5.12.3], and \(\EE\abs Z=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). Since \(0<y<1\) and \(w>0\), \(0<\mathcal D<\mathcal A\). For \(\gamma\ge1\) the range of integration in \(I(\gamma)\) is \((0,1)\), which gives the last formula.
□
