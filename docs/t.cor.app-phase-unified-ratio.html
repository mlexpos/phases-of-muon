The phase map and time to accuracy · cited in the main text
Proved in LeanTheorem numbering in the paper: Corollary M.2
For model \(j\), with \(q=q_j\) and \(r=r_j\), put
\[ \Upsilon_j(N,\epsilon)=\begin{cases} \min\big\{\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)},\ N^{q(1-r)}\big\},&r<1,\\ \min\big\{\log(1/\epsilon),\ \log N\big\},&r=1,\\ 1,&r>1. \end{cases} \]
Let \(\mathcal U\) be the set of triples \((\alpha,\texp,j)\) with \(p>1\) such that \(r_j<1\), or \(j=\text{s-SVD}\) and \(\alpha\ne1\), or \(j=\text{s-SGD}\) and \(\alpha\notin\{\frac12,1\}\). Let \(e_j=\max\{\frac12,\frac1{2r_j}\}\), which are \(e_{\text{Muon}}\) and \(e_{\text{s-SGD}}\) of (18), and \(\chi=e_{\text{s-SGD}}-e_{\text{s-SVD}}\), the \(\kappa\) of Theorem 5.1. Finally \(e_D=-\frac\alpha2,-\frac12,\frac\alpha2-1,0\) for \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\alpha=1\), \(1<\alpha<2\), \(\alpha\ge2\), and \(L=[\alpha=1]-[\alpha=2]+[\texp=\alpha+1]-[\texp=1]\), where \([\cdot]\) is \(1\) if the condition holds and \(0\) otherwise.
Corollary SI.6 (Ratio at every accuracy)
Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(\texp\) with \(\alpha+\texp>1\), and \(T\ge4\), and let \(N_0\) and \(\epsilon_0\) be thresholds of Theorem SI.14 valid for both models. If \((\alpha,\texp,\text{s-SVD})\) and \((\alpha,\texp,\text{s-SGD})\) both lie in \(\mathcal U\), then for \(N\ge N_0\), \(B\ge N\) and \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.49} \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\asymp D_N\,\frac{\Upsilon_{\text{s-SGD}}(N,\epsilon)}{\Upsilon_{\text{s-SVD}}(N,\epsilon)} , \end{equation}
and if moreover \(\texp\notin\{1,\alpha+1\}\), the right side equals \(D_N\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-\chi}\). If only \((\alpha,\texp,\text{s-SGD})\in\mathcal U\), the upper half of (SI.49) holds; if only \((\alpha,\texp,\text{s-SVD})\in\mathcal U\), the lower half holds. For \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\) with fixed \(\omega>0\),
\[ D_N\,\frac{\Upsilon_{\text{s-SGD}}(N,\epsilon)}{\Upsilon_{\text{s-SVD}}(N,\epsilon)}\asymp N^{e_D+\chi\min\{\omega,p-1\}}\,(\log N)^{L}, \]
with constants depending also on \(\omega\).
This is the precise form of Theorem 5.1. For all large \(N\), all \(B\ge N\) and all small \(\epsilon\), with no window, the ratio is, when both models lie in \(\mathcal U\), \(D_N\) times the quotient of the clock orders \(\Upsilon_j\), and off the lines \(\texp=1\) and \(\texp=\alpha+1\) this is \(D_N\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-\kappa}\). Above the accuracy scale the ratio grows like \(\epsilon^{-\kappa}\) as \(\epsilon\) decreases, the main text’s reading of Phase C, and below \(\epsilon_N\) it levels off. The set \(\mathcal U\) excludes SignSVD at \(\alpha=1\), \(\texp\ge1\) and SignSGD at \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\), \(\texp\ge\alpha+1\), hence the main text’s exclusion of \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\); with one model excluded, one half of the order survives. At \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\) the ratio is a power of \(N\) times a power of \(\log N\), the input of Theorem SI.15.
Proof sketch.
The clock integral of each model is of order \(\Upsilon_j\) at every accuracy: it grows as in Section G.6 until \(\epsilon\) reaches the accuracy scale, then freezes at the order \(N^{q_j(1-r_j)}\) of \(J_N\) (Theorem SI.14). The lower bound needs no kernel estimate. Since \(Y_j\ge f_{N,q_j}\), the first passage is no earlier than that of the forcing, and the lower envelope of the forcing, valid up to \((N/4)^{q_j}\), keeps it above \(T\epsilon\) until \(\min\{\sigma_*,(N/4)^{q_j}\}\), where \(1+\sigma_*=(A_p/T\epsilon)^{1/(2r_j)}\). Integrating the envelope up to that time gives the two branches of \(\Upsilon_j\), which for \(r_j<1\) cross at \(\epsilon_N\) because \(q_j(1-r_j)=(p-1)(1-r_j)/(2r_j)\):
\[ \Upsilon_j(N,\epsilon)=\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)}\qquad(r_j<1). \]
The upper bound needs the kernel controlled up to times of order \(N^{q_j}\), and this is where \(\mathcal U\) comes from. For \(r_j<1\) the comparison of Section G.6 covers \(\epsilon\ge E_*\epsilon_N\), and the comparison followed by a restart, as in Theorem SI.13, covers the band below. For \(r_j\ge1\) the forcing envelope decays faster than a slow part of the kernel: each kernel in \(\mathcal U\) is bounded by an integrable fast part plus a slow part of height of order \(N^{-q}\) (Lemma SI.25),
\[ g_j(v)\le K_f(1+v)^{-\upsilon}+K_sN^{-q}\Big(\frac{1+v}{N^q}\Big)^{-\mathsf b},\qquad \upsilon>1,\ 0<\mathsf b<1,\ 0\le v\le N^q-1 . \]
The slow part acting on the envelope \(h\) leaves a remainder \(w^2/\sqrt\epsilon\), where \(w\) is a power-law envelope of exponent \(\mathsf b/2<1\) that the kernel does not amplify; then \((h+w+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) dominates the equation (Lemma SI.20), and a restart as in Theorem SI.13 closes the bound. This bound is not available for the SignSVD kernel at \(\alpha=1\) and the SignSGD kernel at \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\), the triples excluded from \(\mathcal U\); SignSVD at \(\alpha\ge2\) is treated directly.
The ratio law is then a division. Exactly, \(t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}/t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}=D_N\mathsf I_{\text{s-SGD}}/\mathsf I_{\text{s-SVD}}\) ((SI.41)), and the lower bound holds for every model, which gives the one-sided statements. For \(r_j\ne1\), \(e_j-\frac12\) is \((1-r_j)/(2r_j)\) or \(0\) as \(r_j<1\) or \(r_j>1\), so \(\Upsilon_j=\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-(e_j-1/2)}\) and the quotient is \(\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-\chi}\). At \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\), \(\Upsilon_j\asymp N^{\min\{\omega,p-1\}(e_j-1/2)}(\log N)^{[r_j=1]}\) and \(D_N\asymp N^{e_D}(\log N)^{[\alpha=1]-[\alpha=2]}\), which gives the last display.
