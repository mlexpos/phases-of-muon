Corollary SH.1 (Expected regularized update)
Assume Assumption S3 with \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\), and assume that \(\epsilon_{\rm os}=3(1-\bmom^2)\Nin/B\le\bar\epsilon\) along the sequence of dimensions for a fixed \(\bar\epsilon<1\). For \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\) let \(\vartheta_\ridge\) be the function of Theorem SH.1(iv) at the current dimension. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SH.12} \sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\Big\|\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt) -\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}{\Nin}}\;\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\, \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_{\hat\nu_{\mathrm{out}},r}\Big(\Sigmaout;\frac{\ridge}{\sw2\gin}\Big)\, \frac{\Dt}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\Big\|_\F\longrightarrow0, \end{equation}
and \(\operatorname{spec}\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\subset\big[1-\epsilon_{\rm os},(1-\epsilon_{\rm os})^{-1}\big]\) at every dimension and for every \(\ridge\).

Definitions and assumptions used

Assumption S3 (Spectrally diffuse proportional regime)
Assumption S3 (Spectrally diffuse proportional regime)
Assumption S1 and Assumption S2 hold, and we fix \(\gamma_\star<\infty\) and \(C_\Sigma\ge1\) with \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), \(\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\norm{\Sigmaout}\le C_\Sigma\) along the whole sequence of dimensions; the covariances are deterministic. The momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) is fixed, and the normalized ridge satisfies \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\) for fixed \(0<\ridge_-\le\ridge_+<\infty\). The state is frozen as in Definition SA.3, with \(\cR(t)>0\), and the normalized state is spectrally diffuse: \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\).
This corollary is the precise form of the main-text display \(W_{t+1}-W_t\approx-\eta\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/(2N)}\,\sqrt{\mathcal K}\,(W_t-W^*)/\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}\) of Section 4.1. When \(\epsilon_{\rm os}=3(1-\bmom^2)\Nin/B\) stays below a fixed \(\bar\epsilon<1\), the expected regularized update is, up to an error of vanishing Frobenius norm, a function of \(\Sigmaout\) applied to the normalized error \(\Dt/\sqrt{\cR(t)}\): the factor \(\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/\Nin}\) times the oversampled profile \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}\) evaluated at \(\Sigmaout\), times an operator \(\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\) that is within \(\epsilon_{\rm os}/(1-\epsilon_{\rm os})\) of the identity at every dimension. On packed modes and at zero rescaled ridge the profile is within constants of \(\sqrt{\mu/2}\) (Corollary SH.2), which is the square-root preconditioner of the main text. The statement holds at ridges in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), in the proportional regime; it says nothing about the algorithm at zero ridge.
Proof sketch.
For an isotropic input side the deterministic equivalent of the expected update is an explicit function of \(\Sigmaout\) applied to the error. The drift theorem gives \(\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\) up to a vanishing Frobenius error as an integral, over the spectral parameters \(s=\ridge+u^2\), of a candidate built from the deterministic resolvents (Theorem SC.2, Section G.2). With \(\Sigmain=\Id\) these resolvents are \((s\Id+\tilde s_1\Sigmaout)^{-1}\) and \((s+s_1)^{-1}\Id\), so the candidate is the scalar kernel \(\mathsf k(s;\cdot)\) evaluated at \(\Sigmaout\) and applied to \(\Dt/\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\), and the integral is exactly ((SH.13))
\[ \Gtildebar_\ridge=\mathfrak d_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\,\frac{\Dt}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}} . \]
The oversampled law rewrites \(\mathfrak d_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\) at every finite dimension, with a relative error that depends on nothing but \(\epsilon_{\rm os}\). By the sandwich of Section G.5, applied eigenvalue by eigenvalue (Theorem SH.1(iv)),
\[ \mathfrak d_\ridge(\Sigmaout)=\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}{\Nin}}\;\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\,\mathfrak d^{\rm os}\Big(\Sigmaout;\frac{\ridge}{\sw2\gin}\Big),\qquad \operatorname{spec}\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\subset\big[1-\epsilon_{\rm os},(1-\epsilon_{\rm os})^{-1}\big]. \]
Inserting this into the previous display gives the corollary. The bound on \(\vartheta_\ridge\) holds at every dimension, uniformly in the spectrum, the eigenvalue and the ridge, so the dimension limit of the drift theorem is taken with a factor that is already controlled and no exchange of limits occurs. Because the error is relative to the profile, the law is accurate also on modes where \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}(\mu)\ll\sqrt\mu\).
The profile sees the ridge only through the rescaled ridge \(\hat\ridge=\ridge/(\sw2\gin)\), and this decides when the square-root law is visible. The profile is at most \(\min\{\sqrt{\mu/2},\,\mu/\sqrt{2\hat\ridge}\}\) (Lemma SH.2), so at a fixed normalized ridge and \(\gin\downarrow0\) it becomes linear in \(\mu\); the square-root law concerns bounded \(\hat\ridge\), in particular the zero-ridge coefficient of Corollary SH.2.
