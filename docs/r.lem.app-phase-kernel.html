Lemma SI.1 (Forcing and kernel)
Assume (M1). The forcing \(F\) is continuous and strictly decreasing on \([0,\infty)\), with \(F(0)=F_0\), \(F>0\) and \(F(s)\to0\) as \(s\to\infty\). The kernel satisfies
\[ \int_0^\infty k(s)\,\dif s=\bar k,\qquad k(s)\le\frac{\bar k}{e\,s}\quad(s>0). \]
For every \(S'>0\) and every bounded function \(f\) on \([0,S']\), the convolution \(k*f\) is continuous on \([0,S']\), vanishes at \(0\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.5} \sup_{0\le s\le S'}\abs{(k*f)(s)}\le\bar k\,\sup_{0\le s\le S'}\abs{f(s)} . \end{equation}
Proof.
Each term of \(F\) is continuous and nonincreasing. The tail of the series beyond the index \(M\) is at most \(\frac12\sum_{i>M}\mu_i\fQ_i(0)\) uniformly in \(s\), which tends to zero because \(F_0<\infty\). The partial sums therefore converge uniformly, and \(F\) is continuous. Since \(F_0>0\), some term has a positive coefficient, and that term is strictly decreasing; hence \(F\) is strictly decreasing and positive. Each term tends to zero and the tails are uniformly small, so \(F(s)\to0\).
Integrating each exponential gives
\[ \int_0^\infty k(s)\,\dif s=\frac\lr2\sum_i\frac{\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}{2\mathfrak d_i}=\bar k . \]
The scalar inequality \(be^{-bs}\le1/(es)\), valid for \(b,s>0\), applied with \(b=2\mathfrak d_i\), gives
\[ k(s)=\sum_i\frac{\lr\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}{4\mathfrak d_i}\,(2\mathfrak d_i)e^{-2\mathfrak d_is}\le\frac{\bar k}{es}. \]
For bounded \(f\), the convolution with the \(i\)-th term of \(k\) is
\[ \frac{\lr\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}2\,e^{-2\mathfrak d_is}\int_0^se^{2\mathfrak d_iv}f(v)\,\dif v , \]
which is continuous and vanishes at \(0\). The tail of the sum of these convolutions beyond the index \(M\) is at most \(\sup\abs f\cdot\frac\lr2\sum_{i>M}\mu_i\mathfrak v_i/(2\mathfrak d_i)\), which tends to zero. Uniform convergence gives continuity of \(k*f\), and the same bound for the full sum gives (SI.5).
□
