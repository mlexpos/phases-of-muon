Proposition SJ.1 (The batch constant)
For every integer \(B\ge1\), the constant (SJ.4) satisfies \(\mathcal N_1=2/\pi\) and
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.7} \max\Big\{\frac2\pi\sqrt B,\ \sqrt B-\frac4{\sqrt B}\Big\}\le\mathcal N_B\le\sqrt B,\qquad 0\le1-\frac{\mathcal N_B}{\sqrt B}\le\frac4B . \end{equation}
In particular \(\mathcal N_B/\sqrt B\to1\) as \(B\to\infty\).
The batch constant is the drift coefficient of SignSGD: the main text writes the isotropic drift as \(\mathfrak d=\mathcal N_B/\sqrt\pi\) and then uses \(\mathcal N_B\approx\sqrt B\) throughout, which gives the tabulated drift \(\mu_i\sqrt{B/(\pi\bar\mu)}\) of Table 1. The proposition justifies this replacement with an explicit error: \(\mathcal N_B\) differs from \(\sqrt B\) by a factor in \([1-4/B,1]\), and it is never smaller than \(\frac2\pi\sqrt B\), a bound attained at \(B=1\).
Proof sketch.
The constant is the mean of the square root of a sum \(T_B=\sum_bx_b^2y_b^2\) of \(B\) independent terms with mean \(1\) and second moment \((\EE x^4)(\EE y^4)=9\), so \(T_B/B\) has mean \(1\) and variance \(8/B\). At \(B=1\) independence gives \(\mathcal N_1=(\EE\abs g)^2=2/\pi\). Jensen’s inequality gives \(\mathcal N_B\le(\EE T_B)^{1/2}=\sqrt B\), and the Cauchy–Schwarz comparison \((\sum_bz_b^2)^{1/2}\ge B^{-1/2}\sum_b\abs{z_b}\) with \(z_b=x_by_b\) gives \(\mathcal N_B\ge\frac2\pi\sqrt B\).
The Jensen deficit is at most half the variance. Rationalizing \(\sqrt s-1\) gives the exact identity, for \(s\ge0\),
\[ \sqrt s=1+\frac{s-1}2-\frac{(s-1)^2}{2(\sqrt s+1)^2}, \]
whose last term lies between \(0\) and \((s-1)^2/2\). At \(s=T_B/B\) the linear term has mean zero, so \(0\le1-\EE\sqrt{T_B/B}\le\frac12\cdot\frac8B=\frac4B\), which is the second inequality of (SJ.7) and the bound \(\mathcal N_B\ge\sqrt B-4/\sqrt B\).
