Proposition SG.8 (No-momentum endpoints)
Let \(\bmom=0\), let \(\alpha>0\) be fixed, and let \(N,B\to\infty\) with \(\gamma=N/B\to\infty\), \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\), \(\Nout=\Nin=N\) and \(\mu_i=i^{-\alpha}\). For \(\gamma>1\) let \(\resscale\) and \(i_\#=\resscale^{1/\alpha}\) be as in Proposition SG.6. At each such dimension the limits \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak d_i(\ridge)\) and \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) exist, and:
  1. (i)
    for every \(i\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)=\frac{\resscale\mu_i}{1+\resscale\mu_i}=\frac1{1+(i/i_\#)^\alpha},\qquad \sum_{i=1}^N\mathfrak v_i(0)=B ; \]
  2. (ii)
    for every row sequence \(1\le i=i_N\le N\),
    \[ \frac{i}{i_\#}\to0\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}{2\gamma}},\qquad \frac{i}{i_\#}\to\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathfrak d_i(0)\sim A_\alpha\,\mu_i\sqrt{\frac{\resscale}{2\gamma}} . \]
Proof.
The participation coordinate applies at \(\bmom=0\). The computation of Proposition SG.1(ii),(iii) uses only the equations of the branch, so with \(m(s)\in(0,1)\) defined by \(s_N(m(s))=s\),
\[ \volraw_i(\ridge)=f_i\big(m(\ridge)\big),\qquad \mathfrak d_i(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\infty f_i\big(m(\ridge+u^2)\big)\,\dif u . \]
The proof of Proposition SG.1(i) shows that \(s_N\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,1)\); it tends to \(\infty\) as \(m\downarrow0\), and to \(0\) as \(m\uparrow1\), because then \(Q_m\to\infty\) while \(\Lambda_N(m)\to\Lambda_N(1)=\resscale\) and \(\gamma-m\to\gamma-1>0\). Hence \(m(s)\to1\) as \(s\downarrow0\), and \(\Lambda_N(m(s))\le\resscale\) for all \(s\).
Part (i) is the zero-lag coefficient. At \(\bmom=0\) all lag coefficients with \(\ell\ge1\) vanish (Theorem SD.7(iv)), so \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=\volraw_i(\ridge)=f_i(m(\ridge))\to\resscale\mu_i/(1+\resscale\mu_i)\), and \(\resscale\mu_i=(i_\#/i)^\alpha\). The sum is \(Bm_N(\resscale)=B\).
The drift has a zero-ridge limit. The function \(t\mapsto f_i(m(t))\) is nonincreasing with values in \([0,1]\), and \(f_i\le\Lambda_N\mu_i=\tilde s_1\mu_i/t\le\gamma\mu_i/t\) because \(\tilde s_1=(\gamma-m)\Jfun(Q_m)\le\gamma\). The integration by parts in the proof of Proposition SG.2(ii), with \(\Lw/\Fw=1\) and total variation at most one, gives
\[ \abs{\mathfrak d_i(\ridge)-\mathfrak d_i(0)}\le\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\sqrt\ridge,\qquad \mathfrak d_i(0)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\infty f_i\big(m(u^2)\big)\,\dif u . \]
The participation scale at small participation is a power of \(m\). Put \(\nu\defeq1\) for \(\alpha\le1\) and \(\nu\defeq\alpha\) for \(\alpha>1\). For fixed \(m\in(0,1)\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.40} \frac{\Lambda_N(m)}{\resscale}\longrightarrow m^\nu,\qquad m\frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}\longrightarrow\nu . \end{equation}
Indeed, by (SG.42), \(\Lambda_N(m)/\resscale=V_N(m/\gamma)/V_N(1/\gamma)\), and Lemma SG.6(i) gives \(S_\alpha(V_N(p))=p(1+O(1/(Bm)))\) at \(p=m/\gamma\); the small-\(V\) laws of Lemma SG.5(i),(iii) then give the first limit, and \(VS_N'(V)=\mathsf T(V)+O(N^{-1})\) with \(\mathsf T/S_\alpha\to1/\nu\) at small \(V\) gives the second, as in the proof of Lemma SG.6(iv). Moreover \(\Lambda_N(m)\le m\resscale\) for \(m\le1\), because \(\Lambda_N(m)/m\) is nondecreasing.
The resolved endpoint is the head computation of Lemma SG.11. Let \(i/i_\#\to0\), so \(\resscale\mu_i\to\infty\), and fix \(\delta\in(0,1)\). By (SG.40), \(\Lambda_N(\delta)\mu_i\ge c_\delta\resscale\mu_i\to\infty\), so \(s_N(\delta)/(\gamma\mu_i)\le C_\delta/(\Lambda_N(\delta)\mu_i)\to0\). On \(m\le\delta\), Lemma SG.1(i) at \(\bmom=0\) gives \(\tilde s_1/\gamma=(1-m/\gamma)\Jfun(Q_m)=1+O(\delta)+o(1)\), so that part of the integral is
\[ \int_{\sqrt{s_N(\delta)}}^\infty\frac{\tilde s_1\mu_i}{u^2+\tilde s_1\mu_i}\,\dif u=\frac\pi2\sqrt{\gamma\mu_i}\,\big(1+O(\delta)+o(1)\big), \]
and the part \(u<\sqrt{s_N(\delta)}\) is at most \(\sqrt{s_N(\delta)}=o(\sqrt{\gamma\mu_i})\). Multiplying by \(\sqrt2/(\pi\gamma)\) and letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) gives \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(2\gamma)}\).
The unresolved endpoint is linear in \(\mu_i\). Let \(i/i_\#\to\infty\), so \(\resscale\mu_i\to0\). Since \(\Lambda_N(m(t))\le\resscale\), \(f_i=\Lambda_N\mu_i\,(1+O(\resscale\mu_i))\) uniformly in \(t\), and the substitution \(u^2=s_N(m)\) of Lemma SG.8 gives
\[ \frac{\mathfrak d_i(0)}{\mu_i\sqrt{\resscale/(2\gamma)}}=\big(1+o(1)\big)\,\frac1\pi\int_0^1\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_N(m)}{\resscale}}\sqrt{1-\frac m\gamma}\,\sqrt{\frac m{Q_m}}\;\vartheta_N(m)\,\dif m . \]
The integrand converges for each \(m\in(0,1)\), by (SG.40), to \(m^{(\nu+1)/2}Q_m^{-1/2}\big(1/E(m)+(\nu-1)/m\big)\). It is bounded by \(m\,Q_m^{-1/2}\big(1/E(m)+C/m\big)\), using \(\Lambda_N(m)\le m\resscale\) and Lemma SG.7; this bound is \(O(m^{-1/2})\) near \(0\), where \(Q_m\asymp m\asymp E(m)\), and bounded near \(1\), where \(E(m)\sqrt{Q_m}\) stays bounded below because \(Q\,r_0'(Q)=\EE\,Qg^2(1+Qg^2)^{-2}\ge c\,Q^{-1/2}\) for \(Q\ge1\) (the Gaussian density is at least \(c\) on \(\abs g\le1\)). The integrals therefore converge to the integral of the limit, which in the variable \(q=Q_m\), with \(m=r_0(q)\), \(\dif m=r_0'(q)\,\dif q\) and \(E=qr_0'(q)\), is
\[ \frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\Big(r_0(q)^{\frac{\nu+1}2}q^{-3/2}+(\nu-1)\,r_0(q)^{\frac{\nu-1}2}r_0'(q)\,q^{-1/2}\Big)\dif q . \]
For \(\nu=1\) this is \(\pi^{-1}\int_0^\infty q^{-1/2}\Jfun(q)\,\dif q=\sqrt{2\pi}/\pi=c_0\), by the computation in the proof of Lemma SG.2. For \(\nu=\alpha>1\), the second term is \(\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{\alpha+1}\int q^{-1/2}\,\dif\big(r_0^{(\alpha+1)/2}\big)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}\int_0^\infty q^{-3/2}r_0^{(\alpha+1)/2}\,\dif q\), by integration by parts [92, Theorem 6.22] on \([\epsilon,T]\), whose boundary terms vanish as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), \(T\to\infty\) because \(r_0(q)\le\min(q,1)\); the total is \(\frac1\pi\big(1+\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}\big)\int_0^\infty q^{-3/2}r_0^{(\alpha+1)/2}\,\dif q=A_\alpha\).
□
