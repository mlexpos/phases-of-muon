Lemma SE.1 (The field)
There is \(C_{\mathsf F}\), depending only on \((\macrostep,\ridge,C_\Sigma,E_\star,r_0,r_1)\) and on \(m_\zeta\), such that for \(\chi,\chi'\in\mathfrak M\):
  1. (i)
    \(\abs{\mathsf F_B(\chi)(\mathbb K)}\le\norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le C_E\) and \(\frac12\big|\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\mathsf F_B(\chi)\big|\le C_{\cR}\);
  2. (ii)
    \(\norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)-\mathsf F_B(\chi')}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le C_{\mathsf F}\norm{\chi-\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\);
  3. (iii)
    \(\norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)-\mathsf F_B(\chi')}_\BL\le C_{\mathsf F}\norm{\chi-\chi'}_\BL\).
The same statements hold, with \(C_E\), \(C_{\cR}\), \(C_{\mathsf F}\) depending also on the bounds, on every set defined as \(\mathfrak M\) with other positive bounds on the mass and the risk.
Proof.
The mass bound is (SE.6). On \(\mathfrak M\),
\[ \norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}\,\chi(\mathbb K)}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}+\macrostep^2\zeta(\mathbb K)\le\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}(E_\star+1)}{\sqrt{r_0/2}}+\macrostep^2m_\zeta=C_E . \]
The risk bound uses \(\mathfrak d\le\mathfrak d_{\max}\) inside the risk integral. Since \(\int\mu\lambda\,\mathfrak d\,\dif\chi\le\mathfrak d_{\max}\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\chi=2\mathfrak d_{\max}\fRisk(\chi)\) and \(\mu\lambda\le C_\Sigma^2\) on \(\mathbb K\),
\[ \frac12\Big|\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\mathsf F_B(\chi)\Big|\le\frac{\macrostep}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}\,2\mathfrak d_{\max}\fRisk(\chi)+\frac{\macrostep^2C_\Sigma^2}{2}\zeta(\mathbb K) \le2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}\sqrt{r_1+1}+\frac{\macrostep^2C_\Sigma^2m_\zeta}{2}=C_{\cR} . \]
The Lipschitz bounds follow from one decomposition. The forcing cancels in the difference, and
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.12} \mathsf F_B(\chi)-\mathsf F_B(\chi')=-\frac{2\macrostep}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}\,\mathfrak d\,(\chi-\chi') -2\macrostep\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi')}}\Big)\mathfrak d\,\chi' . \end{equation}
For \(\fRisk,\fRisk'\ge r_0/2\) the mean value theorem gives
\[ \Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk'}}\Big|\le\frac12\Big(\frac{r_0}{2}\Big)^{-3/2}\abs{\fRisk-\fRisk'} . \]
In total variation, \(\norm{\mathfrak d(\chi-\chi')}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\mathfrak d_{\max}\norm{\chi-\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\) and \(\abs{\fRisk(\chi)-\fRisk(\chi')}\le\frac12C_\Sigma^2\norm{\chi-\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\), which with \(\norm{\mathfrak d\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\mathfrak d_{\max}(E_\star+1)\) proves (ii).
In the bounded-Lipschitz norm we use that multiplication by a bounded Lipschitz function maps the unit ball of test functions into a multiple of it. If \(\norm\phi_\infty\le1\) and \(\phi\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz for \(\abs\cdot_1\), then by (SE.6)
\[ \norm{\phi\,\mathfrak d}_\infty\le\mathfrak d_{\max},\qquad \abs{\phi\mathfrak d(x)-\phi\mathfrak d(y)}\le\Big(\mathfrak d_{\max}+\frac{C_\Sigma}{\sqrt{2\ridge}}\Big)\abs{x-y}_1 , \]
so \(\abs{\int\phi\,\mathfrak d\,\dif(\chi-\chi')}\le c_1\norm{\chi-\chi'}_\BL\) with \(c_1\defeq2\mathfrak d_{\max}+C_\Sigma/\sqrt{2\ridge}\). The function \(\mu\lambda\) has supremum \(C_\Sigma^2\) and \(\abs\cdot_1\)-Lipschitz constant \(C_\Sigma\) on \(\mathbb K\), so \(\abs{\fRisk(\chi)-\fRisk(\chi')}\le\frac12(2C_\Sigma^2+C_\Sigma)\norm{\chi-\chi'}_\BL\). Inserting these two bounds in (SE.12), with \(\abs{\int\phi\,\mathfrak d\,\dif\chi'}\le\mathfrak d_{\max}(E_\star+1)\), proves (iii). The last sentence is the same computation with the other bounds.
□
