Lemma SJ.4 (Decay of the characteristic function)
If \(\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F\ge F/2\), then \(\phi_Y(t)\defeq\EE e^{\mathrm i tY}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.12} \abs{\phi_Y(t)}\le\psi\big(\abs t/8\big)\le\min\big\{1,\ C_{\rm ch}\abs t^{-1/2}\big\}\qquad(t\ne0). \end{equation}
Proof.
The coordinate is conditionally Gaussian in \(z'\). Given \(\xi,\zeta,w'\), the terms of (SJ.10) containing \(z'\) are \(\xi\zeta\,z'^\T(\bar\Delta w'+\zeta q)\), a centered Gaussian of variance \(\xi^2\zeta^2\norm{\bar\Delta w'+\zeta q}^2\). Integrating \(z'\) first and taking absolute values gives
\[ \abs{\EE e^{\mathrm itX}}\le\EE\exp\Big(-\frac{t^2\xi^2\zeta^2}2\,\norm{\bar\Delta w'+\zeta q}^2\Big). \]
The integral over \(w'\) is bounded by the Frobenius norm of \(\bar\Delta\). For \(\lambda\ge0\) and a fixed vector \(h\), write \(\bar\Delta=\sum_l\sigma_l\,a_l\tensor b_l\) in singular value form. The coordinates \(b_l^\T w'\) are independent standard Gaussian, and completing the square gives, for one of them,
\[ \EE\,e^{-\lambda(\sigma_lg+c)^2/2}=(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2)^{-1/2}\,e^{-\lambda c^2/(2(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2))}\le(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2)^{-1/2}. \]
Directions orthogonal to the \(a_l\) contribute a factor at most \(1\), so
\[ \EE_{w'}e^{-\lambda\norm{\bar\Delta w'+h}^2/2}\le\prod_l(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2)^{-1/2}\le\big(1+\lambda\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F^2\big)^{-1/2}. \]
With \(\lambda=t^2\xi^2\zeta^2\) this gives
\[ \abs{\EE e^{\mathrm itX}}\le\psi\big(\abs t\,\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F\big). \]
Centering multiplies the characteristic function by a phase, so \(\abs{\phi_Y(t)}=\abs{\EE e^{\mathrm itX/s}}\le\psi(\abs t\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F/s)\). By Lemma SJ.3, \(\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F/s\ge(F/2)/(\sqrt8F)>1/8\), and \(\psi\) is nonincreasing, which gives the first inequality. The second follows from \((1+x^2)^{-1/2}\le x^{-1/2}\):
\[ \psi(x)\le x^{-1/2}\,\EE\abs\xi^{-1/2}\,\EE\abs\zeta^{-1/2}, \]
evaluated at \(x=\abs t/8\).
□
