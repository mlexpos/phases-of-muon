Proposition SD.4 (Recovery of the rows)
If \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\), then \(\zeta_\ridge=B^{-1}\sum_i\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\,\delta_{(\mu_i,1)}\), and \(\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)/\Nin\), so that \(\sum_j\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) for every \(i\).
The proposition is the relation \(\mathfrak v_i=\sum_j\mathfrak v_{ij}\) that the main text uses in the row recursion (14) of the half-anisotropic setting \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\). There the volatility of row \(i\) is the long-run variance of the whole row \({\mathsf S}^\T u_i\) of the update, and its deterministic counterpart \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) is defined directly from the row test. The proposition says that this row coefficient is the sum of the mode coefficients of the row, and that all \(\Nin\) modes of a row carry the same share \(\mathfrak v_i/\Nin\). It is an exact identity between deterministic quantities at every finite dimension; no limit is taken. With the closed form of Section G.3 it gives the explicit row coefficient \(\mathfrak v_i=(\Nin/B)\big(\mathfrak c_0(\mu_i,1;\ridge)+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathfrak c_\ell(\mu_i,1;\ridge)\big)\) ((SD.91)), whose same-step term is \(\volraw_i(\ridge)\) (Corollary SD.4), the deterministic equivalent of the expected row energy of the update (Theorem SD.1).
Proof sketch.
The deterministic covariances are linear in each of the two tests, so the mass of the forcing measure at a point is the sum of the row coefficients of the rows sitting there. The deterministic lag covariance \(\overline C_\ell[F,G]\) is a limit of expected traces \(B^{-1}\EE\Tr[F{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_tG\,{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c\,\T}_{t+\ell}]\), and in the pairing expansion of Section G.3 each test enters every tree through exactly one factor. Hence \(\overline C_\ell\) and its lag sum \(\overline\Gamma\) are linear in \(F\) and in \(G\). When \(\Sigmain=\Id\) the input side has the single eigenvalue \(1\), whose spectral projection is \(\Id\), with multiplicity \(\Nin\). The atom of \(\zeta_\ridge\) at \((\mu,1)\) is therefore, splitting the projection \(P_\mu\) onto the eigenvalue \(\mu\) of \(\Sigmaout\) into its rows,
\[ \zeta_\ridge\big(\{(\mu,1)\}\big)=\overline\Gamma[P_\mu,\Id](\ridge,\ridge)=\sum_{i:\,\mu_i=\mu}\overline\Gamma[u_i\tensor u_i,\Id](\ridge,\ridge)=\frac1B\sum_{i:\,\mu_i=\mu}\mathfrak v_i(\ridge), \]
where the last step is the definition of \(\mathfrak v_i\). This is the first claim, \(\zeta_\ridge=B^{-1}\sum_i\mathfrak v_i\,\delta_{(\mu_i,1)}\).
The row coefficient depends on the row only through its eigenvalue, which turns the atom into equal mode coefficients. With the test \(u_i\tensor u_i\), the row index enters every tree value only through one normalized trace \(B^{-1}\mu_i^p\) with \(p\ge1\), so \(\mathfrak v_i\) is a function of \(\mu_i\) alone (Theorem SD.7(iv)). The atom at \((\mu,1)\) is thus \(r_\mu\mathfrak v_i/B\) for any row \(i\) with \(\mu_i=\mu\), where \(r_\mu\) is the multiplicity of \(\mu\). The mode coefficient divides the atom equally among the \(r_\mu\Nin\) modes \((i,j)\) that sit at \((\mu,1)\) (Proposition SD.3), so
\[ \mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\frac{B}{r_\mu\Nin}\cdot\frac{r_\mu\,\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)}B=\frac{\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)}{\Nin},\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\mathfrak v_i(\ridge). \]
