In the setting of Proposition SI.2 the index set is finite and every row has the same drift, \(\mathfrak d_i=\mathfrak d>0\). The total volatility is \(\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}=\sum_i\mu_i\mathfrak v_i>0\), the volatility \(\mathfrak v\) of (8), and \(\mathcal S=\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}/(2\mathfrak d)\).
Theorem SI.4 (Matched floors)
In the setting of Proposition SI.2, let \(\epsilon>0\), \(T>1\) and \(F_0>T\epsilon\), and take the matched step \(\lr=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S=4\mathfrak d\sqrt\epsilon/\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}\). Put \(X\defeq\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}>\sqrt T\). Then the floor is \(\epsilon\) and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.13} t_{T\epsilon}=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}\Big[X-\sqrt T+\log\frac{X-1}{\sqrt T-1}\Big]. \end{equation}
If \(x\defeq1/X\le\frac12\), then
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.14} \Big|\frac{t_{T\epsilon}}{\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}}-1\Big|\le x\Big[\sqrt T+\abs{\log(\sqrt T-1)}+\log\frac1x+2x\Big]. \end{equation}
For two such models with the same \(F_0\), \(\epsilon\) and \(T\),
\[ \frac{t^{(1)}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{(2)}_{T\epsilon}}=\frac{\mathfrak v^{(1)}_{\rm tot}/(\mathfrak d^{(1)})^2}{\mathfrak v^{(2)}_{\rm tot}/(\mathfrak d^{(2)})^2} . \]
This theorem is the constant-learning-rate part of the main text’s statement, after (12), that the Muon speedup is also the ratio of the times to a common floor. With a common drift the model closes on the risk, as the recursion (8) in continuous time, and at the matched step the time to \(T\epsilon\) is explicit. Its leading term is \(\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}\) times \(\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}/(4\mathfrak d^2)\), half the inverse of the rate \(2\mathfrak d^2/\mathfrak v\), so the ratio of the times of two models is the inverse ratio of their rates, exactly and at every accuracy.
Proof sketch.
With a common drift the risk solves a scalar equation, which integrates in closed form: summing the row equations with weights \(\mu_i/2\) turns the drift term into \(-2\lr\mathfrak d\sqrt{\fRisk}\), so exactly (Proposition SI.2)
\[ \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif t}=-2\lr\mathfrak d\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\lr^2\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}2 . \]
In \(\mathsf u=\sqrt{\fRisk}\) this reads \(\mathsf u'=-a_\circ(\mathsf u-b_\circ)/\mathsf u\), with \(a_\circ=\lr\mathfrak d\) and \(b_\circ=\lr\mathcal S/2\). The difference \(\mathsf u-b_\circ\) keeps its sign, so for \(\sqrt{F_0}>b_\circ\) the risk decreases to \(b_\circ^2\) without reaching it, and separating variables gives ((SI.12))
\[ a_\circ t=\sqrt{F_0}-\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}+b_\circ\log\frac{\sqrt{F_0}-b_\circ}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}-b_\circ} . \]
At the matched step the closed form evaluated at the level \(T\epsilon\) is the time. There \(b_\circ=\sqrt\epsilon\) and \(a_\circ=4\mathfrak d^2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}\), and since \(F_0>T\epsilon>b_\circ^2\), setting \(\sqrt{\fRisk}=\sqrt{T\epsilon}\) gives (SI.13). With \(X-1=X(1-x)\) the exact relative error is
\[ \frac{t_{T\epsilon}}{\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}X}-1 =x\Big[-\sqrt T+\log\frac1x+\log(1-x)-\log(\sqrt T-1)\Big], \]
and \(\abs{\log(1-x)}\le2x\) for \(x\le\frac12\) gives (SI.14). The bracket in (SI.13) depends only on \(F_0\), \(\epsilon\) and \(T\), so it cancels in a ratio.
