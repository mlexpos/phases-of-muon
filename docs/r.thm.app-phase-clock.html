Theorem SI.2 (Clock and row equations)
Assume (M1) and let \(Q\) be the solution of Theorem SI.1. For \(i\in\mathcal I\) and \(s\ge0\) put
\[ E_i(s)\defeq\fQ_i(0)e^{-2\mathfrak d_is}+\lr\mathfrak v_i\int_0^se^{-2\mathfrak d_i(s-v)}\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v,\qquad t(s)\defeq\frac1\lr\int_0^s\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v, \]
and \(t_*\defeq\lim_{s\to\infty}t(s)\in(0,\infty]\). Then \(\frac12\sum_i\mu_iE_i(s)=Q(s)\) for every \(s\), and \(t\) is continuously differentiable and strictly increasing from \([0,\infty)\) onto \([0,t_*)\). Its inverse is the clock
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.8} \clock(t)=\int_0^t\frac{\lr}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t')}}\,\dif t', \end{equation}
where \(\fQ_i(t)\defeq E_i(\clock(t))\) and \(\fRisk(t)\defeq Q(\clock(t))\) solve (SI.2) on \([0,t_*)\) with continuous positive risk. Every solution of (SI.2) with continuous positive risk on an interval \([0,t_1)\) agrees with this one there, and \([0,t_*)\) is the maximal such interval. If \(\mathcal S>0\), then \(t_*=\infty\) and \(\fRisk(t)\to\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4\) as \(t\to\infty\). If \(\mathcal S=0\), then \(t_*=\lr^{-1}\int_0^\infty\sqrt{F(s)}\,\dif s\), and \(\fRisk(t)\to0\) as \(t\uparrow t_*\) when \(t_*<\infty\).
Proof.
Summing the formulas for \(E_i\) with weights \(\mu_i/2\) and using (SI.3) gives
\[ \frac12\sum_i\mu_iE_i(s)=F(s)+\int_0^sk(s-v)\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v=Q(s). \]
Each \(E_i\) is differentiable, with
\[ E_i'=-2\mathfrak d_iE_i+\lr\mathfrak v_i\sqrt Q . \]
Since \(Q\) is continuous and positive, \(t'=\sqrt Q/\lr>0\), so \(t\) is a continuously differentiable increasing bijection onto \([0,t_*)\). Its inverse satisfies \(\clock'(t)=\lr/\sqrt{Q(\clock(t))}\) and \(\clock(0)=0\), which is (SI.8). The chain rule applied to \(E_i\circ\clock\) gives
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif t}E_i(\clock(t))=\frac{\lr}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}}\big(-2\mathfrak d_iE_i(\clock(t))+\lr\mathfrak v_i\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}\big), \]
which is (SI.2).
Conversely, let \((\fQ_i)\) solve (SI.2) on \([0,t_1)\) with continuous positive risk. Define \(\clock\) by (SI.8); it is continuously differentiable with positive derivative. In the time \(s=\clock(t)\) the chain rule gives \(\frac{\dif}{\dif s}\fQ_i=-2\mathfrak d_i\fQ_i+\lr\mathfrak v_i\sqrt{\fRisk}\). Multiplying by \(e^{2\mathfrak d_is}\) and integrating gives the formula for \(E_i\) with \(\fRisk\) in place of \(Q\), and summing with weights \(\mu_i/2\) gives (SI.4) on the image interval. Uniqueness on compact intervals (Theorem SI.1) identifies the risk and every row with the construction.
If \(\mathcal S>0\), the positive limit of Theorem SI.1 gives \(\sqrt Q\ge\bar k/2\) for large \(s\), so \(t_*=\infty\), and \(\fRisk(t)=Q(\clock(t))\) converges to \(\bar k^2\). If \(\mathcal S=0\), then \(Q=F\) and \(t_*=\lr^{-1}\int_0^\infty\sqrt F\). When this is finite, \(\clock(t)\to\infty\) as \(t\uparrow t_*\) and \(\fRisk(t)=F(\clock(t))\to0\), so no solution with positive continuous risk extends to \(t_*\).
□
