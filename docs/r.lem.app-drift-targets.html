Lemma SC.9 (Candidate-centered targets)
Under Assumption S3, \(\vartheta_B(I)\to0\). For a sample of age \(j\) and \(s\in I\) define, with \(x_j\defeq\emaw j^2q(-s)\in[0,q_I]\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.33} \alpha_{1,j}^\star\defeq\emaw j\,f_{x_j}(d_j),\qquad \alpha_{2,j}^\star\defeq\emaw j^2\,\sigma(-s)\,\psi_{x_j}(d_j). \end{equation}
Then, with \(e_B\) the sequence of Lemma SB.12 and \(e_B^\star\defeq e_B+C_I\vartheta_B(I)\to0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.34} \norm{\alpha_{1,j}-\alpha_{1,j}^\star}_2\le\emaw j\,e_B^\star,\qquad \norm{\alpha_{2,j}-\alpha_{2,j}^\star}_2\le\emaw j^2\,e_B^\star \qquad(j\ge0,\ s\in I). \end{equation}
Proof.
The expected traces converge to the branch. By the last claim of Corollary SB.1, \(\EE\sup_{s\in I}\abs{\frac1B\Tr(\Sigmaout R(-s))-\sigma(-s)}^2\to0\), and since \(\sigma_B(s)=\EE\frac1B\Tr(\Sigmaout R(-s))\), Jensen’s inequality gives
\[ \sup_{s\in I}\abs{\sigma_B(s)-\sigma(-s)}\le\Big(\EE\sup_{s\in I}\Big|\frac1B\Tr(\Sigmaout R(-s))-\sigma(-s)\Big|^2\Big)^{1/2}\longrightarrow0 , \]
and likewise for \(\tilde\sigma_B\). The products are controlled by the bounds (SB.31) and (SB.6):
\[ \abs{q_B-q}=\abs{s\sigma_B(\tilde\sigma_B-\tilde\sigma)+s\tilde\sigma(\sigma_B-\sigma)}\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\,\vartheta_B(I). \]
The targets of (SB.33) and (SC.33) differ by the change of center. With \(\check x_j\defeq\emaw j^2q_B\), the mean value theorem and Lemma SC.7 give
\[ \abs{f_{\check x_j}(d)-f_{x_j}(d)}\le\emaw j^2\abs{q_B-q}\,\abs d^3,\qquad \abs{\sigma_B\psi_{\check x_j}(d)-\sigma\psi_{x_j}(d)}\le\abs{\sigma_B-\sigma}\,d^2+\sigma\,\emaw j^2\abs{q_B-q}\,d^4 . \]
Taking \(L^2\) norms with \(\norm d_6^3\le15\), \(\norm d_4^2\le3\) and \(\norm d_8^4\le105\) from Lemma SA.7(i), and \(\sigma\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star/s_-\),
\[ \norm{\check\alpha_{1,j}-\alpha_{1,j}^\star}_2\le15\,\emaw j^3\abs{q_B-q}\le C_I\emaw j\,\vartheta_B(I),\qquad \norm{\check\alpha_{2,j}-\alpha_{2,j}^\star}_2\le C_I\emaw j^2\,\vartheta_B(I). \]
The triangle inequality with (SB.34) proves (SC.34).
□
