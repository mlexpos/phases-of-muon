This section fixes the objects that every later section of the appendix uses and proves the exact finite-dimensional identities they start from. It restates the regression model of Section 1.1 and the algebra of its risk (Section SA.2); it defines the algorithm that the appendix analyzes, an exponential moving average of the minibatch gradients with momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) followed by the regularized SignSVD map at a fixed normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\) (Section SA.3); it proves the exact one-step recursion of the projected risks under this algorithm (Section SA.4); it introduces the frozen-state model, in which the buffer is a weighted sum of independent batches, and proves the exact laws of that buffer together with the filter estimates that later sections use (Section SA.5); and it states the regime assumption and the definition of a deterministic equivalent under which every asymptotic statement of the appendix is made (Section SA.6). The main statements are Proposition SA.1, Proposition SA.4 and Corollary SA.1 (exact identities for the algorithm), Lemma SA.5, Lemma SA.2 and Lemma SA.6 (the frozen buffer and the filter), and Assumption S3 and Definition SA.4 (the regime). Nothing in this section is asymptotic except Lemma SA.7(iii) and Definition SA.4.

SA.1 Conventions

\(\norm{X}\) is the operator norm, \(\norm{X}_\F\) the Frobenius norm and \(\norm{X}_*\) the nuclear norm of a matrix \(X\); \(\norm{x}\) is the Euclidean norm of a vector. Square roots of positive semidefinite matrices are the positive semidefinite roots, and \((\Id+K)^{-1/2}\) for positive definite \(\Id+K\) is the positive definite inverse root. For a random scalar \(\xi\) and \(p\ge1\), \(\norm{\xi}_p\defeq(\EE\abs{\xi}^p)^{1/p}\). For a sub-\(\sigma\)-field \(\mathcal G\) we write \(\EE_{\mathcal G}\defeq\EE[\,\cdot\mid\mathcal G]\) and \(\norm{\xi}_{2,\mathcal G}\defeq(\EE_{\mathcal G}\xi^2)^{1/2}\), and \(\EE_t\defeq\EE[\,\cdot\mid\cF_t]\) for the past \(\cF_t\) of Definition SA.1. Expectations of matrices are entrywise. A constant written \(C\) may change from line to line; its dependence is stated where it is introduced, and it never depends on the dimension unless the statement says so.

SA.2 Model and risk

Definition SA.1 (Regression model)
Let \(\Nout,\Nin,B\) be positive integers. The features \(\Xin\in\R^{\Nin}\) and \(\Xout\in\R^{\Nout}\) are independent random vectors with finite second moments, centered in every coordinate, with covariances
\[ \Sigmain\defeq\EE[\Xin\tensor\Xin],\qquad\Sigmaout\defeq\EE[\Xout\tensor\Xout], \]
and with eigendecompositions \(\Sigmaout=\sum_{i=1}^{\Nout}\mu_i\,u_i\tensor u_i\) and \(\Sigmain=\sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\lambda_j\,v_j\tensor v_j\) in orthonormal eigenbases, all eigenvalues included. For a parameter \(W\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) and a fixed teacher \(\Wstar\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), the single-sample loss and the population risk are
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.1} \cL(W;(\Xin,\Xout))\defeq\tfrac12\big\langle\Xout\tensor\Xin,\ W-\Wstar\big\rangle^2,\qquad \cR(W)\defeq\EE\big[\cL(W;(\Xin,\Xout))\big]. \end{equation}
For \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) we write
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.2} \begin{gathered} \langle V,W\rangle_\Sigma\defeq\Tr\big(\Sigmaout V\Sigmain W^\T\big),\qquad \norm{V}_\Sigma^2\defeq\langle V,V\rangle_\Sigma,\\ \CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta)\defeq(u_i^\T\Delta v_j)^2,\qquad \CMscr Q_i(\Delta)\defeq\norm{\Delta^\T u_i}^2 . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
The past at step \(t\) is denoted \(\cF_t\): it contains the parameter \(\Wt\) and everything used to compute it, but not the minibatch of step \(t\), which is a family \((\Xin^{(a)},\Xout^{(a)})_{a=1}^B\) of independent copies of \((\Xin,\Xout)\) independent of the past. We write
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.3} \begin{gathered} \Dt\defeq\Wt-\Wstar,\qquad \cR(t)\defeq\cR(\Wt),\qquad d_a^\circ\defeq(\Xout^{(a)})^\T\Dt\Xin^{(a)},\\ \Gt^\circ\defeq\frac1B\sum_{a=1}^Bd_a^\circ\,\Xout^{(a)}\tensor\Xin^{(a)} , \end{gathered} \end{equation}
so that \(\Gt^\circ\) is the minibatch gradient (2) at \(\Dt\); as in the main text we write \(\CMscr Q_{ij}(t)\defeq\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Dt)\) and \(\CMscr Q_i(t)\defeq\CMscr Q_i(\Dt)\). The data matrices and the residual diagonal of the batch are \(X\defeq[\Xin^{(1)}\cdots\Xin^{(B)}]\in\R^{\Nin\times B}\), \(Y\defeq[\Xout^{(1)}\cdots\Xout^{(B)}]\in\R^{\Nout\times B}\) and \(\vec D^\circ\defeq\diag(d_1^\circ,\dots,d_B^\circ)\). The superscript \(^\circ\) marks physical objects, as opposed to the normalized objects of (SA.10).
In (SA.3), \(\cR(t)\) is the function \(\cR\) evaluated at the random matrix \(\Wt\); by Proposition SA.1(iii) it is the conditional expected loss of a fresh sample given \(\cF_t\). The main text takes the features Gaussian, and all random-matrix statements of the appendix do so; the identities of this subsection need only second moments.
Assumption S1 (Data-target normalization)
The input and output features are independent and Gaussian, \(\Xin\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigmain)\) and \(\Xout\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigmaout)\), with positive semidefinite covariances \(\Sigmain\in\R^{\Nin\times\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nout}\). They are represented in whitened form as \(\Xin=\Sigmain^{1/2}w\) and \(\Xout=\Sigmaout^{1/2}z\) with \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) and \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) independent; the whitened columns of a batch are written \((w_a,z_a)_{a\le B}\).
Proposition SA.1 (Residual and gradient moments)
In Definition SA.1, \(\Sigmain\) and \(\Sigmaout\) are symmetric and positive semidefinite, and the following hold.
  1. (i)
    For deterministic \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) the residual \(d\defeq\Xout^\T\Delta\Xin\) is centered, and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.4} \EE d^2=\Var(d)=\Tr\big(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain\Delta^\T\big)=\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma^2, \qquad \cR(W)=\tfrac12\norm{W-\Wstar}_\Sigma^2 . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    For deterministic \(\Delta\) and a batch of \(B\) independent copies, the gradient
    \[ G^\circ\defeq\frac1B\sum_ad_a^\circ\,\Xout^{(a)}\tensor\Xin^{(a)} \]
    satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.5} G^\circ=\frac1B\,Y\vec D^\circ X^\T,\qquad \EE G^\circ=\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain . \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    For the state \(\Dt\) at step \(t\) with \(\EE\norm{\Dt}_\F^2<\infty\) and the fresh batch of step \(t\), the residuals and the gradient of (SA.3) satisfy
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.6} \EE_t\,d_a^\circ=0,\qquad \EE_t\,(d_a^\circ)^2=2\cR(t),\qquad \EE_t\,\Gt^\circ=\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The covariances are symmetric, and \(v^\T\Sigmain v=\EE[(v^\T\Xin)^2]\ge0\) for every \(v\in\R^{\Nin}\); the same holds for \(\Sigmaout\).
Mixed fourth-order moments factorize by independence: for coordinates \(i,k\le\Nout\) and \(j,l\le\Nin\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.7} \EE\big[(\Xout)_i(\Xout)_k(\Xin)_j(\Xin)_l\big]=(\Sigmaout)_{ik}(\Sigmain)_{jl}. \end{equation}
The second moment of the residual is the trace. Expanding
\[ d^2=\sum_{i,k\le\Nout}\sum_{j,l\le\Nin}\Delta_{ij}\Delta_{kl}\,(\Xout)_i(\Xout)_k(\Xin)_j(\Xin)_l \]
and taking expectations term by term with (SA.7) gives
\[ \EE d^2=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\Delta_{ij}\Delta_{kl}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}(\Sigmain)_{jl}=\Tr\big(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain\Delta^\T\big), \]
the last equality by expanding the trace as \(\sum_{i,k,j,l}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}\Delta_{kl}(\Sigmain)_{lj}\Delta_{ij}\) and using the symmetry of \(\Sigmain\). The residual is centered because \(\EE[(\Xout)_i(\Xin)_j]=\EE(\Xout)_i\,\EE(\Xin)_j=0\) by independence, and hence \(\Var(d)=\EE d^2\). The formula for \(\cR\) is (SA.1) with \(\Delta=W-\Wstar\). This proves (i).
The matrix form of the gradient is finite algebra. Matrix multiplication gives \((Y\vec D^\circ X^\T)_{ij}=\sum_ad_a^\circ(\Xout^{(a)})_i(\Xin^{(a)})_j\), which is \(B\) times the \((i,j)\) entry of \(G^\circ\). For one sample, the entry \(d\,(\Xout)_i(\Xin)_j=\sum_{k,l}\Delta_{kl}(\Xout)_k(\Xout)_i(\Xin)_l(\Xin)_j\) has expectation \(\sum_{k,l}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}\Delta_{kl}(\Sigmain)_{lj}=(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain)_{ij}\) by (SA.7); identical distribution of the samples gives \(\EE G^\circ\). This proves (ii).
Part (iii) follows from (i) and (ii) because the batch of step \(t\) is independent of the past: conditionally on \(\cF_t\) the state \(\Dt\) is fixed and the batch has its unconditional law, so the fixed-state identities of (i) and (ii) at \(\Delta=\Dt\) are the three conditional expectations.
□
Lemma SA.1 (Mode and row decomposition)
For every \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.8} \CMscr Q_i(\Delta)=\sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta),\qquad \tfrac12\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma^2=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\,\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta), \end{equation}
and if \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) then \(\frac12\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma^2=\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\CMscr Q_i(\Delta)\). Moreover \(\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma=0\) if and only if \(u_i^\T\Delta v_j=0\) for every pair \((i,j)\) with \(\mu_i\lambda_j>0\).
Proof
Proof.
The row identity is Parseval’s identity in the basis \((v_j)\): \(\Delta^\T u_i=\sum_j(u_i^\T\Delta v_j)\,v_j\), and the squared norm of the right side is \(\sum_j(u_i^\T\Delta v_j)^2\).
The risk identity is the eigenexpansion of the trace:
\[ \Tr\big(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain\Delta^\T\big)=\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\Tr\big((u_i\tensor u_i)\Delta(v_j\tensor v_j)\Delta^\T\big)=\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j(u_i^\T\Delta v_j)^2 . \]
When \(\Sigmain=\Id\) all \(\lambda_j\) equal \(1\) and the row identity applies. The last claim holds because a finite sum of nonnegative terms vanishes exactly when each term does.
□
Whitening and the normalized state. Under Assumption S1 the residual and the gradient are functions of the rotated error \(\At\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Dt\Sigmain^{1/2}\):
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.9} d_a^\circ=z_a^\T\At w_a,\qquad \Gt^\circ=\frac1B\sum_{a=1}^Bd_a^\circ\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z_a\tensor w_a)\Sigmain^{1/2},\qquad \cR(t)=\tfrac12\norm{\At}_\F^2 , \end{equation}
the last by (SA.4) and \(\Tr(\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain\Dt^\T)=\Tr(\At\At^\T)\). When \(\cR(t)>0\) we define the normalized state, residuals and gradient
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.10} \begin{gathered} \Ahat\defeq\frac{\At}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}},\qquad d_a\defeq\frac{d_a^\circ}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}}=z_a^\T\Ahat w_a,\\ \Gt\defeq\frac{\Gt^\circ}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}}=\frac1B\sum_{a=1}^Bd_a\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z_a\tensor w_a)\Sigmain^{1/2}. \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Proposition SA.2 (Normalization)
Assume Assumption S1.
  1. (i)
    If \(\Dt=\Delta\) is deterministic with \(\cR(t)>0\), then \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\), the normalized residuals are centered with unit variance, and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.11} \EE\,d_a=0,\qquad \EE\,d_a^2=1,\qquad \EE\,\Gt=\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Ahat\Sigmain^{1/2}=\frac{\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}} . \end{equation}
    For every \(c>0\), replacing \(\Delta\) by \(c\Delta\) with the same batch multiplies \(\cR(t)\) by \(c^2\) and leaves \(\Ahat\), \(d_a\) and \(\Gt\) unchanged.
  2. (ii)
    For the state \(\Dt\) at step \(t\) and the fresh batch of step \(t\), on the event \(\{\cR(t)>0\}\) the identities (SA.11) hold with \(\EE_t\) in place of \(\EE\) and \(\Dt\) in place of \(\Delta\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is Proposition SA.1(i),(ii) applied to \(\Delta/\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma\). Indeed \(\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma=\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\) by (SA.4), so \(d_a\) is the residual and \(\Gt\) the gradient of the state \(\Delta/\norm\Delta_\Sigma\), whose \(\Sigma\)-norm is \(1\); the first identity of (SA.11) is the centering, the second is (SA.4), and the third is (SA.5) together with \(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain=\Sigmaout^{1/2}\At\Sigmain^{1/2}\). The identity \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\) is (SA.9). Scaling \(\Delta\) by \(c\) scales \(\At\) and every \(d_a^\circ\) by \(c\) and \(\cR(t)\) by \(c^2\), so the three quotients are unchanged.
Part (ii) follows from (i) because the batch of step \(t\) is independent of the past.
□
Every random-matrix statement of the appendix concerns the normalized objects (SA.10). The risk re-enters only through the inverse relations \(\Gt^\circ=\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\Gt\) and \(\Gt^\circ\Gt^{\circ\T}=2\cR(t)\,\Gt\Gt^\T\), and through the ridge conversion of Proposition SA.3.

SA.3 The algorithm

The regularized spectral map. For \(K\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) with singular value decomposition \(K=\sum_ks_k\,p_k\tensor r_k\) (\(s_k>0\), orthonormal families \((p_k)\), \((r_k)\)) and \(\ridge\ge0\), the regularized SignSVD map is
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.12} \Qridge{\ridge}(K)\defeq\sum_k\frac{s_k}{\sqrt{s_k^2+\ridge}}\,p_k\tensor r_k, \qquad\text{so that}\qquad \Qridge{\ridge}(K)=\big(KK^\T+\ridge\Id_{\Nout}\big)^{-1/2}K\quad(\ridge>0). \end{equation}
The sum does not depend on the choice of the decomposition, since \(\Qridge\ridge(K)=K\,\phi_\ridge(K^\T K)\) with \(\phi_\ridge(x)=(x+\ridge)^{-1/2}\) on \((0,\infty)\) and \(\phi_\ridge(0)\defeq0\) applied through the spectral theorem to \(K^\T K\). At \(\ridge=0\) the map is the polar factor \(\Qridge0(K)=\sum_kp_k\tensor r_k\), which is the exact SignSVD update (4). In the language of (3), \(\Qridge\ridge\) is the spectral update with \(\varphi_\ridge(x)=(x+\ridge)^{-1/2}\). A Newton–Schulz polynomial approximation of \(x^{-1/2}\) recovers Muon (Section 1.2); no statement of this appendix concerns that approximation.
Lemma SA.2 (Filter bounds and Lipschitz estimate)
Let \(K,L\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\).
  1. (i)
    For \(\ridge\ge0\): \(\norm{\Qridge\ridge(K)}\le1\) and \(\norm{\Qridge\ridge(K)}_\F^2\le\rank K\le\min(\Nout,\Nin)\), with equality \(\norm{\Qridge0(K)}_\F^2=\rank K\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(\ridge\ge0\), if \(P\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nout}\) and \(P'\in\R^{\Nin\times\Nin}\) are orthogonal projections with \(K=PKP'\), then \(\Qridge\ridge(K)=P\Qridge\ridge(K)P'\).
  3. (iii)
    For \(\ridge>0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.13} \norm{\Qridge\ridge(K)-\Qridge\ridge(L)}_\F\le\ridge^{-1/2}\norm{K-L}_\F . \end{equation}
  4. (iv)
    \(\Qridge\ridge(K)\to\Qridge0(K)\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) reads off the singular values. By (SA.12) the singular values of \(\Qridge\ridge(K)\) are \(s_k/\sqrt{s_k^2+\ridge}\in(0,1]\), one for each positive singular value of \(K\), so the operator norm is at most \(1\) and the squared Frobenius norm, the sum of their squares, is at most the number of positive singular values, with equality at \(\ridge=0\).
Part (ii) holds because the singular vectors lie in the ranges of the projections. If \(K=PKP'\) and \(Kr_k=s_kp_k\) with \(s_k>0\), then \(p_k=s_k^{-1}PKP'r_k=Pp_k\), and likewise \(r_k=s_k^{-1}P'K^\T Pp_k=P'r_k\), so each term \(p_k\tensor r_k\) of (SA.12) equals \(P(p_k\tensor r_k)P'\).
Part (iii) is a divided-difference bound for a Lipschitz spectral function. Let \(H,T\) be real symmetric matrices of the same size with orthonormal eigenbases \(Hh_i=x_ih_i\), \(Tt_j=y_jt_j\), and let \(f:\R\to\R\) be \(L_f\)-Lipschitz. Then
\[ h_i^\T\big(f(H)-f(T)\big)t_j=\big(f(x_i)-f(y_j)\big)\,h_i^\T t_j,\qquad h_i^\T(H-T)t_j=(x_i-y_j)\,h_i^\T t_j , \]
so \(\abs{h_i^\T(f(H)-f(T))t_j}\le L_f\abs{h_i^\T(H-T)t_j}\) for every pair, including pairs with \(x_i=y_j\), where both sides vanish. Summing the squares over \(i,j\), which computes the Frobenius norms in the two orthonormal bases, gives
\[ \norm{f(H)-f(T)}_\F\le L_f\norm{H-T}_\F . \]
We apply this to \(f(x)\defeq x/\sqrt{x^2+\ridge}\), whose derivative is
\[ f'(x)=\frac{\ridge}{(x^2+\ridge)^{3/2}}\le\ridge^{-1/2}, \]
so \(f\) is \(\ridge^{-1/2}\)-Lipschitz by the mean value theorem, and to the symmetric dilations
\[ \mathcal D(K)\defeq\begin{pmatrix}0&K\\K^\T&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad f(\mathcal D(K))=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Qridge\ridge(K)\\ \Qridge\ridge(K)^\T&0\end{pmatrix}. \]
The second display holds because, for each positive singular pair, the vectors \((p_k;\pm r_k)/\sqrt2\) are eigenvectors of \(\mathcal D(K)\) with eigenvalues \(\pm s_k\), the remaining eigenvalue is \(0\) with \(f(0)=0\), and \(f\) is odd, so that
\[ \begin{aligned} f(\mathcal D(K))&=\sum_kf(s_k)\,\frac{(p_k;r_k)\tensor(p_k;r_k)-(p_k;-r_k)\tensor(p_k;-r_k)}{2}\\ &=\sum_k\frac{s_k}{\sqrt{s_k^2+\ridge}}\begin{pmatrix}0&p_k\tensor r_k\\ r_k\tensor p_k&0\end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned} \]
Since \(\norm{\mathcal D(E)}_\F^2=2\norm E_\F^2\) for every rectangular \(E\), the divided-difference bound for \(\mathcal D(K)\) and \(\mathcal D(L)\) is (SA.13) after cancelling the factor \(\sqrt2\) on both sides.
Part (iv) holds because each coefficient \(s_k/\sqrt{s_k^2+\ridge}\) in (SA.12) increases to \(1\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\).

Lemma SA.3 (Integral representation)
For every \(K\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) and \(\ridge>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.14} \Qridge{\ridge}(K)=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\big(KK^\T+(\ridge+u^2)\Id_{\Nout}\big)^{-1}K\,du , \end{equation}
and \(\int_0^\infty\norm{(KK^\T+(\ridge+u^2)\Id)^{-1}K}_*\,du\le\tfrac\pi2\min(\Nout,\Nin)\).
Proof
Proof.
The identity is a scalar integral applied to each singular value. In the singular value decomposition of \(K\) the integrand is
\[ \big(KK^\T+(\ridge+u^2)\Id_{\Nout}\big)^{-1}K=\sum_k\frac{s_k}{s_k^2+\ridge+u^2}\,p_k\tensor r_k , \]
and for \(b>0\) the substitution \(u=\sqrt b\,v\) gives
\[ \int_0^\infty\frac{du}{b+u^2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt b}\int_0^\infty\frac{dv}{1+v^2}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt b}. \]
Applying this with \(b=s_k^2+\ridge\) to each term and summing over \(k\) gives (SA.14). The nuclear norm of the integrand is the sum of its coefficients, so
\[ \int_0^\infty\norm{(KK^\T+(\ridge+u^2)\Id)^{-1}K}_*\,du=\frac\pi2\sum_{k:\,s_k>0}\frac{s_k}{\sqrt{s_k^2+\ridge}}\le\frac\pi2\min(\Nout,\Nin). \qedhere \]
□
Proposition SA.3 (Scaling, physical ridge and heavy ball)
Let \(K\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), \(\ridge\ge0\) and \(a>0\).
  1. (i)
    \(\Qridge{\ridge}(aK)=\Qridge{\ridge/a^2}(K)\).
  2. (ii)
    If \(\cR>0\) and \(\ridgephys\defeq2\cR\,\ridge\), then \(\Qridge{\ridgephys}\big(\sqrt{2\cR}\,K\big)=\Qridge{\ridge}(K)\).
  3. (iii)
    Let \((G_{t+1})_{t\ge0}\) be any sequence of matrices, \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), and let \(M_t=\bmom M_{t-1}+(1-\bmom)G_{t+1}\) and \(H_t=\bmom H_{t-1}+G_{t+1}\) for \(t\ge0\) with \(H_{-1}=M_{-1}/(1-\bmom)\). Then \(H_t=M_t/(1-\bmom)\) for all \(t\ge-1\), and \(\Qridge{\ridge}(H_t)=\Qridge{(1-\bmom)^2\ridge}(M_t)\).
Proof
Proof.
The singular values of \(aK\) are \(as_k\), with the same singular vectors as \(K\), so the singular-value filter of \(\Qridge{\ridge}(aK)\) is
\[ \frac{as_k}{\sqrt{a^2s_k^2+\ridge}}=\frac{s_k}{\sqrt{s_k^2+\ridge/a^2}}, \]
which is the filter of \(\Qridge{\ridge/a^2}(K)\); this proves (i). Part (ii) is (i) with \(a=\sqrt{2\cR}\) and \(\ridgephys\) in place of \(\ridge\), since \(\ridgephys/a^2=\ridge\).
Part (iii) is an induction. If \(H_{t-1}=M_{t-1}/(1-\bmom)\) then
\[ H_t=\bmom\frac{M_{t-1}}{1-\bmom}+G_{t+1}=\frac{\bmom M_{t-1}+(1-\bmom)G_{t+1}}{1-\bmom}=\frac{M_t}{1-\bmom}, \]
and the ridge conversion is (i) with \(a=(1-\bmom)^{-1}\).

Definition SA.2 (The algorithm)
Fix a momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), a normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\), a deterministic step sequence \((\lr_t)_{t\ge0}\) with \(\lr_t\ge0\), an initial parameter \(W_0\) and an initial buffer \(M_{-1}^\circ\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), both given before the first batch is drawn (the default is \(M_{-1}^\circ=0\)). Let \(t_{\mathrm{stop}}\defeq\inf\{t\ge0:\cR(t)=0\}\in\{0,1,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}\). The algorithm is, for \(t\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.15} \begin{gathered} M_t^\circ=\bmom\,M_{t-1}^\circ+(1-\bmom)\,\Gt^\circ,\qquad W_{t+1}=\Wt-\lr_t\,\Gtilde^\circ,\\ \Gtilde^\circ\defeq\begin{cases}\Qridge{\ridgephys_t}(M_t^\circ),&t<t_{\mathrm{stop}},\\ 0,&t\ge t_{\mathrm{stop}},\end{cases} \qquad \ridgephys_t\defeq2\cR(t)\,\ridge . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
The stopping time matters only for \(\bmom>0\). At \(\bmom=0\) zero risk is absorbing, because the update at a zero-risk state vanishes by Proposition SA.5. For \(\bmom>0\) the buffer retains past gradients, so the update at a zero-risk state need not vanish, and the algorithm is stopped at \(t_{\mathrm{stop}}\). The normalized objects (SA.10) are defined at positive risk, so they exist at every time before \(t_{\mathrm{stop}}\).
Whether \(t<t_{\mathrm{stop}}\) is decided by the past \(\cF_t\). At \(\bmom=0\) the buffer is the current gradient, \(M_t^\circ=\Gt^\circ\), and by Proposition SA.3(ii) the update is
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.16} \Gtilde^\circ=\Qridge{2\cR(t)\ridge}(\Gt^\circ)=\Qridge{\ridge}(\Gt) \qquad(t<t_{\mathrm{stop}}), \end{equation}
which is the spectral update (3) with \(\varphi(x)=(x+2\cR(t)\ridge)^{-1/2}\), that is, \(\varphi_\ridge\) in the normalized spectral variable \(x/(2\cR(t))\) up to the factor \((2\cR(t))^{-1/2}\) absorbed by \(\Gt=\Gt^\circ/\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\). Unrolling the buffer recursion gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.17} M_t^\circ=\bmom^{t+1}M_{-1}^\circ+\sum_{j=0}^t\emaw{j}\,G^\circ_{t+1-j},\qquad \emaw{j}\defeq(1-\bmom)\bmom^j , \end{equation}
where \(G^\circ_{t+1-j}\) is the gradient computed at the earlier error \(\Delta_{t-j}\). The EMA weights satisfy, with the convention \(\bmom^0=1\) at \(\bmom=0\) (where \(\emaw0=1\) and all other weights vanish),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.18} \sum_{j\ge0}\emaw{j}=1,\qquad \sw{p}\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw{j}^p=\frac{(1-\bmom)^p}{1-\bmom^p}\quad(p\ge2),\qquad \sw{2}=\frac{1-\bmom}{1+\bmom}. \end{equation}
Remark SA.1 (Fixed normalized versus fixed physical ridge)
The algorithm applies the regularized map at the physical ridge \(\ridgephys_t=2\cR(t)\ridge\), which decreases with the risk, so that in the normalized variables of (SA.10) the ridge is the constant \(\ridge\); at \(\bmom=0\) this is (SA.16). An implementation with a fixed physical ridge \(\ell>0\) applies \(\Qridge{\ell}(M_t^\circ)=\Qridge{\ell/(2\cR(t))}(M_t^\circ/\sqrt{2\cR(t)})\), whose normalized ridge \(\ell/(2\cR(t))\) varies with the risk. The results of the appendix are stated for a normalized ridge in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\) (Assumption S3); they cover a fixed physical ridge exactly on the time ranges where \(\ell/(2\cR(t))\) stays in such a subset, and no statement is made as the normalized ridge tends to \(0\) or to \(\infty\). The normalized convention is the one under which the frozen-state laws of Section SA.5 do not depend on the risk, which is what makes the deterministic equivalents of the following sections risk-free.
Remark SA.2 (Heavy-ball momentum)
The momentum ablation appendix and the Muon implementation it describes keep the heavy-ball buffer \(H_t=\bmom H_{t-1}+\Gt^\circ\) and orthogonalize \(H_t\). By Proposition SA.3(iii), with matched initialization, \(H_t=M_t^\circ/(1-\bmom)\) and \(\Qridge{\ridge'}(H_t)=\Qridge{(1-\bmom)^2\ridge'}(M_t^\circ)\). Every statement of the appendix is for the normalized average \(M_t^\circ\); the heavy-ball version at ridge \(\ridge'\) is the same statement at ridge \((1-\bmom)^2\ridge'\), and at \(\ridge=0\) the two updates coincide.

SA.4 The exact risk recursion

Proposition SA.4 (Conditional recursion of the projected risks)
In Definition SA.1, fix \(t\ge0\), let \(\Dt\) be the state at step \(t\) with \(\EE\norm{\Dt}_\F^2<\infty\), let \({\mathsf S}\) be a random matrix in \(\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) with \(\EE\norm {\mathsf S}_\F^2<\infty\) (the update, determined by the past and the batch of step \(t\)), let \(\lr_t\ge0\) be deterministic, and put \(\Delta_{t+1}\defeq\Dt-\lr_t{\mathsf S}\). Define
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.19} \cD_{ij}(t)\defeq(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)\,\EE_t\big[u_i^\T {\mathsf S}v_j\big],\qquad \cV_{ij}(t)\defeq\EE_t\big[(u_i^\T {\mathsf S}v_j)^2\big]. \end{equation}
Then
\begin{align} \EE_t\big[\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta_{t+1})\big]&=\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Dt)-2\lr_t\,\cD_{ij}(t)+\lr_t^2\,\cV_{ij}(t),\tag{SA.20}\\ \EE_t\big[\CMscr Q_{i}(\Delta_{t+1})\big]&=\CMscr Q_{i}(\Dt)-2\lr_t\sum_j\cD_{ij}(t)+\lr_t^2\sum_j\cV_{ij}(t),\tag{SA.21}\\ \EE_t\big[\tfrac12\norm{\Delta_{t+1}}_\Sigma^2\big]&=\tfrac12\norm{\Dt}_\Sigma^2-\lr_t\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\cD_{ij}(t)+\frac{\lr_t^2}2\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\cV_{ij}(t),\tag{SA.22} \end{align}
with the contractions in the last line equal to
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.23} \sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\cD_{ij}(t)=\big\langle\Dt,\EE_t{\mathsf S}\big\rangle_\Sigma,\qquad \sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\cV_{ij}(t)=\EE_t\norm{{\mathsf S}}_\Sigma^2 . \end{equation}
The quantity \(\cV_{ij}(t)\) is a conditional second moment, not a centered conditional variance; the distinction matters for momentum and is the subject of the volatility section.
Proof
Proof.
Write \(a_{ij}\defeq u_i^\T\Dt v_j\) and \(b_{ij}\defeq u_i^\T {\mathsf S}v_j\); the coefficient \(a_{ij}\) is determined by the past. The mode identity is the expansion of a square. Pointwise,
\[ \CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta_{t+1})=(a_{ij}-\lr_tb_{ij})^2=a_{ij}^2-2\lr_ta_{ij}b_{ij}+\lr_t^2b_{ij}^2 , \]
and taking \(\EE_t\) term by term gives (SA.20).
The row and total identities are finite weighted sums of the mode identity. Summing (SA.20) over \(j\) with weight \(1\), and over \((i,j)\) with weight \(\mu_i\lambda_j/2\), and applying Lemma SA.1 to \(\Delta_{t+1}\) and to \(\Dt\), gives (SA.21) and (SA.22).
The contraction identities are the eigenexpansion of the pairing. For any \(V,W\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\),
\[ \langle V,W\rangle_\Sigma=\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\,(u_i^\T Vv_j)(u_i^\T Wv_j), \]
by the computation in the proof of Lemma SA.1 with \(V\) and \(W\) in the two slots. Taking \((V,W)=(\Dt,{\mathsf S})\) and \(({\mathsf S},{\mathsf S})\) and applying \(\EE_t\) gives (SA.23).
□
Corollary SA.1 (Recursion of the algorithm)
In Definition SA.2, assume \(\EE\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2<\infty\). Then for every \(t\ge0\) the identities (SA.20), (SA.21) and (SA.22) hold with \({\mathsf S}=\Gtilde^\circ\), with \(0\le\cV_{ij}(t)\le1\). In particular
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.24} \EE_t\big[\cR(t+1)\big]-\cR(t)=-\lr_t\big\langle\Dt,\EE_t\Gtilde^\circ\big\rangle_\Sigma+\frac{\lr_t^2}2\,\EE_t\norm{\Gtilde^\circ}_\Sigma^2 . \end{equation}
On \(\{t<t_{\mathrm{stop}}\}\) the conditional expectations in (SA.19) and (SA.24) are obtained by integrating the fresh batch of step \(t\) with \((\Dt,M_{t-1}^\circ)\) held fixed, because the batch is independent of the past; on \(\{t\ge t_{\mathrm{stop}}\}\) both sides of (SA.24) vanish.
Proof
Proof.
The update satisfies \(\norm{\Gtilde^\circ}\le1\) and \(\norm{\Gtilde^\circ}_\F^2\le\min(\Nout,\Nin)\) by Lemma SA.2(i), so \(\EE\norm{\Dt}_\F^2<\infty\) for every \(t\) and Proposition SA.4 applies at every \(t\). The identity (SA.24) is (SA.22) rewritten with (SA.23) and (SA.4). The bound \(\cV_{ij}(t)\le1\) follows from \(\abs{u_i^\T\Gtilde^\circ v_j}\le\norm{\Gtilde^\circ}\le1\). On \(\{t<t_{\mathrm{stop}}\}\) the update is a function of \((\Dt,M_{t-1}^\circ)\) and of the batch of step \(t\); on \(\{t\ge t_{\mathrm{stop}}\}\) the update vanishes and \(\Delta_{t+1}=\Dt\).

Proposition SA.5 (Support event and zero risk)
In Definition SA.1 let \(P_{\mathrm{out}}\defeq\sum_{\mu_i>0}u_i\tensor u_i\) and \(P_{\mathrm{in}}\defeq\sum_{\lambda_j>0}v_j\tensor v_j\). There is an event \(\Omega_t\) of probability one, determined by the batch of step \(t\), on which
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.25} \Xout^{(a)}=P_{\mathrm{out}}\Xout^{(a)},\qquad \Xin^{(a)}=P_{\mathrm{in}}\Xin^{(a)}\quad(a\le B),\qquad \Gt^\circ=P_{\mathrm{out}}\Gt^\circ P_{\mathrm{in}} \end{equation}
for every value of \(\Dt\). On \(\Omega_t\), if \(\cR(t)=0\) then \(d_a^\circ=0\) for every \(a\) and \(\Gt^\circ=0\). If, in Definition SA.2, \(M_{-1}^\circ=P_{\mathrm{out}}M_{-1}^\circ P_{\mathrm{in}}\), then on \(\bigcap_t\Omega_t\) every buffer and every update satisfy \(M_t^\circ=P_{\mathrm{out}}M_t^\circ P_{\mathrm{in}}\) and \(\Gtilde^\circ=P_{\mathrm{out}}\Gtilde^\circ P_{\mathrm{in}}\), and \(\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Dt)\) is constant in \(t\) for every pair with \(\mu_i\lambda_j=0\).
Proof
Proof.
A zero eigenvalue is a direction of zero variance. If \(\mu_i=0\) then \(\EE(u_i^\T\Xout^{(a)})^2=u_i^\T\Sigmaout u_i=0\), so \(u_i^\T\Xout^{(a)}=0\) almost surely, and likewise \(v_j^\T\Xin^{(a)}=0\) almost surely when \(\lambda_j=0\). Let \(\Omega_t\) be the complement of the finite union of these null events over the zero modes and the samples. On \(\Omega_t\) each \(\Xout^{(a)}\tensor\Xin^{(a)}\) equals \(P_{\mathrm{out}}(\Xout^{(a)}\tensor\Xin^{(a)})P_{\mathrm{in}}\), which gives the last identity of (SA.25) for every \(\Dt\).
Zero risk forces zero residuals on \(\Omega_t\). By Lemma SA.1, \(\cR(t)=0\) exactly when \(P_{\mathrm{out}}\Dt P_{\mathrm{in}}=0\), since \(P_{\mathrm{out}}\Dt P_{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_{\mu_i\lambda_j>0}(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)\,u_i\tensor v_j\). On \(\Omega_t\) each residual is \(d_a^\circ=(\Xout^{(a)})^\T P_{\mathrm{out}}\Dt P_{\mathrm{in}}\Xin^{(a)}\), which vanishes, and then \(\Gt^\circ=0\).
The support property propagates through the algorithm by induction. If \(M_{t-1}^\circ=P_{\mathrm{out}}M_{t-1}^\circ P_{\mathrm{in}}\) then \(M_t^\circ=\bmom M_{t-1}^\circ+(1-\bmom)\Gt^\circ\) has the same property on \(\Omega_t\), and Lemma SA.2(ii) transfers it to \(\Qridge{\ridgephys_t}(M_t^\circ)\). For a pair with \(\mu_i=0\) or \(\lambda_j=0\) this gives \(u_i^\T\Gtilde^\circ v_j=0\), hence \(u_i^\T\Delta_{t+1}v_j=u_i^\T\Dt v_j\).

SA.5 The frozen-state model

In the algorithm, the gradient \(G^\circ_{t+1-j}\) of age \(j\) in (SA.17) was computed at the earlier error \(\Delta_{t-j}\), whose risk differs from \(\cR(t)\). The frozen-state model replaces every past gradient by a gradient recomputed at the current error, with its own independent batch, and removes the initial buffer; it is the model in which the deterministic equivalents of the following sections are computed. Its relation to the algorithm is the subject of the dynamics section.
Definition SA.3 (Frozen-state model)
Assume Assumption S1. Fix \(t\), condition on \(\cF_t\), and assume \(\cR(t)>0\), so that \(\Dt\) and \(\Ahat\) are deterministic. Let \((z_{ja},w_{ja})\), \(j\ge0\), \(1\le a\le B\), be independent standard Gaussian vectors in \(\R^{\Nout}\times\R^{\Nin}\), and put
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.26} \begin{gathered} d_{ja}\defeq z_{ja}^\T\Ahat w_{ja},\qquad G_{t+1-j}\defeq\frac1B\sum_{a=1}^Bd_{ja}\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z_{ja}\tensor w_{ja})\Sigmain^{1/2},\\ \Mt\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw{j}\,G_{t+1-j}. \end{gathered} \end{equation}
The frozen-state model is the model in which the normalized buffer at time \(t\) is \(\Mt\) and the update applied at step \(t\) is \(\Gtilde\defeq\Qridge{\ridge}(\Mt)\).
By Proposition SA.3(ii), \(\Qridge{\ridge}(\Mt)=\Qridge{\ridgephys_t}(\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\Mt)\), so the frozen-state update is the update (SA.15) with \(M_t^\circ\) replaced by \(\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\Mt\). The gradients \(G_{t+1-j}\), \(j\ge0\), are independent and identically distributed copies of the normalized gradient \(\Gt\) of (SA.10). When lagged buffers \(M_{t+\ell}\) are needed, the samples are indexed by time instead of age: independent standard Gaussian pairs \((z_{sa},w_{sa})\), \(s\in\mathbb Z\), \(a\le B\), with \(G_s\) built from the samples of time \(s\) by (SA.26), \(M_{t'}\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw jG_{t'+1-j}\) for every integer \(t'\), and the age-\(j\) sample of \(\Mt\) being the sample of time \(t+1-j\). The series defining \(\Mt\) converges by Lemma SA.5. The letters \(\Gt\) and \(\EE\Gt\) in this subsection refer to the frozen-state model.
Lemma SA.4 (Gaussian covariance of a batch)
Let \(A\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic with \(\norm A_\F=1\), let \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) and \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) be independent, and let \(X\defeq(z^\T Aw)\,z\tensor w\). Then \(\EE X=A\), and its covariance tensor \(\mathcal C_{ij,kl}\defeq\Cov(X_{ij},X_{kl})\) is
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.27} \mathcal C_{ij,kl}=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+2(AA^\T)_{ik}\delta_{jl}+2\delta_{ik}(A^\T A)_{jl}+A_{ij}A_{kl}+2A_{il}A_{kj}. \end{equation}
Consequently, in the frozen-state model with \(A=\Ahat\), the batches \(G_{t+1-j}\) are independent and identically distributed,
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.28} \begin{gathered} \EE\Gt=\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Ahat\Sigmain^{1/2},\\ \Cov\big((\Gt)_{ij},(\Gt)_{kl}\big)=\frac1B\sum_{p,q,r,s}(\Sigmaout^{1/2})_{ip}(\Sigmain^{1/2})_{jq}\,\mathcal C_{pq,rs}\,(\Sigmaout^{1/2})_{kr}(\Sigmain^{1/2})_{ls}, \end{gathered} \end{equation}
and the single-batch Frobenius variance is
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.29} \begin{aligned} \mathsf V_B&\defeq\EE\norm{\Gt-\EE\Gt}_\F^2\\ &=\frac1B\Big\{\Tr\Sigmaout\Tr\Sigmain+2\Tr(\Sigmaout\Ahat\Ahat^\T)\Tr\Sigmain+2\Tr\Sigmaout\Tr(\Sigmain\Ahat^\T\Ahat)+3\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F^2\Big\}. \end{aligned} \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The mean is computed by independence of \(z\) and \(w\) and the second moments of standard normals:
\[ \EE X_{ij}=\sum_{p,q}A_{pq}\,\EE[z_iz_p]\,\EE[w_jw_q]=A_{ij}. \]
The second moment is a sum over nine Wick pairings. Expanding the product,
\[ \EE[X_{ij}X_{kl}]=\sum_{p,q,r,s}A_{pq}A_{rs}\,\EE[z_iz_kz_pz_r]\,\EE[w_jw_lw_qw_s], \]
and the fourth moments of independent standard normal coordinates are
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.30} \EE[z_iz_kz_pz_r]=\delta_{ik}\delta_{pr}+\delta_{ip}\delta_{kr}+\delta_{ir}\delta_{kp}, \end{equation}
and likewise for \(w\); this identity holds because an index appearing an odd number of times gives zero, two distinct pairs give \(1\), and four equal indices give \(\EE g^4=3\), which is the number of pairings. The product of the two three-term identities has nine terms. The term pairing \((i,k)\) with \((j,l)\) contributes \(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\sum_{p,q}A_{pq}^2=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\). The two terms pairing \((i,k)\) in \(z\) with a crossed pairing in \(w\) contribute
\[ \delta_{ik}\sum_pA_{pj}A_{pl}+\delta_{ik}\sum_pA_{pl}A_{pj}=2\delta_{ik}(A^\T A)_{jl}, \]
and symmetrically the two terms with \((j,l)\) paired in \(w\) and a crossed pairing in \(z\) contribute \(2(AA^\T)_{ik}\delta_{jl}\). The four terms with crossed pairings in both families contribute \(A_{ij}A_{kl}+A_{il}A_{kj}+A_{kj}A_{il}+A_{kl}A_{ij}=2A_{ij}A_{kl}+2A_{il}A_{kj}\). Subtracting the product of the means \(A_{ij}A_{kl}\) gives (SA.27).
The batch statements follow from independence of the samples. The batch \(\Gt\) is the average over \(a\) of the independent, identically distributed matrices \(\Sigmaout^{1/2}X_a\Sigmain^{1/2}\), where \(X_a\) is built from \((z_a,w_a)\) and \(A=\Ahat\). Its mean is \(\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Ahat\Sigmain^{1/2}\), and its covariance is \(1/B\) times the covariance of one transformed sample, which is (SA.28) by the bilinearity of the covariance. Different batches use disjoint sets of samples and are therefore independent.
The Frobenius variance is a contraction of the tensor. For one sample,
\[ \EE\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}(X-A)\Sigmain^{1/2}}_\F^2=\EE\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(X-A)\Sigmain(X-A)^\T\big) =\sum_{i,j,k,l}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}(\Sigmain)_{jl}\,\mathcal C_{ij,kl}, \]
using the symmetry \(\mathcal C_{ij,kl}=\mathcal C_{kl,ij}\) and of the covariances. The five terms of (SA.27) contract to
\[ \begin{gathered} \Tr\Sigmaout\Tr\Sigmain,\qquad 2\Tr(\Sigmaout AA^\T)\Tr\Sigmain,\qquad 2\Tr\Sigmaout\Tr(\Sigmain A^\T A),\\ \Tr(\Sigmaout A\Sigmain A^\T),\qquad 2\Tr(\Sigmaout A\Sigmain A^\T), \end{gathered} \]
where the last two use \(\sum_{i,j,k,l}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}(\Sigmain)_{jl}A_{ij}A_{kl}=\sum_{i,k}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}(A\Sigmain A^\T)_{ik}\) and the same value for \(A_{il}A_{kj}\) after exchanging the roles of \(j\) and \(l\). Since \(\Tr(\Sigmaout A\Sigmain A^\T)=\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}A\Sigmain^{1/2}}_\F^2=\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F^2\) and \(\mathsf V_B\) is \(1/B\) times the one-sample variance, (SA.29) follows.
□
Lemma SA.5 (Stationary EMA law)
In the frozen-state model, index the samples by time as above, and write \(\Sigma_G\defeq\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Gt)\) for the batch covariance (SA.28).
  1. (i)
    The series \(\Mt=\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw{j}G_{t+1-j}\) and the sample series \(\sum_{j,a}\emaw{j}\abs{d_{ja}}\norm{z_{ja}}\norm{w_{ja}}\) converge absolutely.
  2. (ii)
    For every integer \(t\), \(\Mt=\bmom M_{t-1}+(1-\bmom)\Gt\), and the sequence \((\Mt)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) is strictly stationary.
  3. (iii)
    \(\EE\Mt=\EE\Gt\), \(\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt)=\sw2\,\Sigma_G\), and \(\EE\norm{\Mt-\EE\Gt}_\F^2=\sw2\,\mathsf V_B\).
  4. (iv)
    For every lag \(\ell\ge0\), \(\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt,\operatorname{vec}M_{t+\ell})=\bmom^\ell\,\sw2\,\Sigma_G\).
  5. (v)
    For every positive integer \(T\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.31} \Cov\Big(\sum_{t=1}^T\operatorname{vec}(\Mt-\EE\Gt)\Big) =\sw2\Big[T+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)\bmom^\ell\Big]\Sigma_G =\Big[T-\frac{2\bmom(1-\bmom^T)}{1-\bmom^2}\Big]\Sigma_G , \end{equation}
    the two-sided lag sum \(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt,\operatorname{vec}M_{t+\ell})\) converges absolutely and equals \(\Sigma_G\), and \(T^{-1}\Cov(\sum_{t\le T}\operatorname{vec}(\Mt-\EE\Gt))\to\Sigma_G\) as \(T\to\infty\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) holds because \(\sum_j\emaw j=1\) and the batches are identically distributed.
The recursion is a reindexing of the series:
\[ \bmom M_{t-1}+(1-\bmom)\Gt=\sum_{j\ge0}(1-\bmom)\bmom^{j+1}G_{t-j}+(1-\bmom)G_{t+1} =\sum_{j\ge1}\emaw{j}G_{t+1-j}+\emaw0G_{t+1}=\Mt . \]
Strict stationarity holds because the batches are independent and identically distributed, so that a common shift of the times is a relabeling of the batches. This proves (ii).
The moments follow from the orthogonality of the centered batches. For \(k\le l\), independence and centering give
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.32} \EE\Big\|\sum_{j=k}^l\emaw{j}\big(G_{t+1-j}-\EE\Gt\big)\Big\|_\F^2=\mathsf V_B\sum_{j=k}^l\emaw{j}^2 . \end{equation}
The mean is \(\sum_j\emaw j\,\EE\Gt=\EE\Gt\) by (SA.18); the covariance is \(\sum_j\emaw j^2\,\Sigma_G=\sw2\Sigma_G\) by the same orthogonality; and the Frobenius variance is (SA.32) with \(k=0\), \(l\to\infty\). This proves (iii).
The lag covariance follows from the decomposition of a later buffer into the past and a new part. By the reindexing of the series, for \(\ell\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.33} M_{t+\ell}=\bmom^\ell\,\Mt+\sum_{r=0}^{\ell-1}\emaw{r}\,G_{t+\ell+1-r}, \end{equation}
where the finite sum involves only batches of times larger than \(t+1\) and is therefore independent of \(\Mt\). Centering and bilinearity of the covariance give \(\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt,\operatorname{vec}M_{t+\ell})=\bmom^\ell\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt)=\bmom^\ell\sw2\Sigma_G\). This proves (iv).
The block covariance is a sum over the \(T^2\) pairs of times. By (iii) and (iv), and the symmetry of the covariance under exchanging the two times,
\[ \Cov\Big(\sum_{t=1}^T\operatorname{vec}(\Mt-\EE\Gt)\Big)=\sum_{t,t'=1}^T\bmom^{\abs{t-t'}}\sw2\Sigma_G =\sw2\Big[T+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)\bmom^\ell\Big]\Sigma_G , \]
which is the first form of (SA.31). The scalar identity
\[ \sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)\bmom^\ell=\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^k\bmom^\ell=\frac{\bmom}{1-\bmom}\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}(1-\bmom^k) =\frac{T\bmom}{1-\bmom}-\frac{\bmom(1-\bmom^T)}{(1-\bmom)^2} \]
follows from writing \(T-\ell\) as the number of \(k\) with \(\ell\le k\le T-1\) and exchanging the order of summation, and inserting it with \(\sw2=(1-\bmom)/(1+\bmom)\) gives the second form. The two-sided lag sum converges absolutely by the geometric majorant \(\bmom^{\abs\ell}\) and equals
\[ \sw2\Big(1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\bmom^\ell\Big)\Sigma_G=\frac{1-\bmom}{1+\bmom}\cdot\frac{1+\bmom}{1-\bmom}\,\Sigma_G=\Sigma_G , \]
and the limit of \(T^{-1}\) times (SA.31) is the same value. This proves (v).

Proposition SA.6 (Startup and bias correction)
In the frozen-state model let \(K\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic and let \((M_t^K)_{t\ge-1}\) be the recursion \(M_t^K=\bmom M_{t-1}^K+(1-\bmom)G_{t+1}\) started from \(M_{-1}^K=K\). Then for \(t\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.34} \begin{gathered} M_t^K=\bmom^{t+1}K+\sum_{j=0}^t\emaw{j}G_{t+1-j},\qquad \EE M_t^K=\bmom^{t+1}K+(1-\bmom^{t+1})\,\EE\Gt,\\ \Cov(\operatorname{vec}M_t^K)=\sw2\big(1-\bmom^{2(t+1)}\big)\Sigma_G . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
The bias-corrected zero-start buffer \(\widetilde M_t\defeq M_t^0/(1-\bmom^{t+1})\) has mean \(\EE\Gt\) and covariance \(\sw2\,\frac{1+\bmom^{t+1}}{1-\bmom^{t+1}}\Sigma_G\). If \(M_t^K\) and the stationary buffer \(\Mt\) are built from the same batches, then
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.35} M_t^K-\Mt=\bmom^{t+1}(K-M_{-1}),\qquad \EE\norm{M_t^K-\Mt}_\F^2=\bmom^{2(t+1)}\big(\norm{K-\EE\Gt}_\F^2+\sw2\mathsf V_B\big). \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The explicit form is an induction on \(t\), the case \(t=0\) being \(M_0^K=\bmom K+(1-\bmom)G_1\). The mean and covariance follow from the finite geometric sums
\[ \sum_{j=0}^t\emaw j=1-\bmom^{t+1},\qquad \sum_{j=0}^t\emaw j^2=(1-\bmom)^2\,\frac{1-\bmom^{2(t+1)}}{1-\bmom^2}=\sw2\big(1-\bmom^{2(t+1)}\big), \]
and the independence of the batches. Dividing by \(1-\bmom^{t+1}>0\) gives the bias-corrected moments, using \((1-\bmom^{2(t+1)})/(1-\bmom^{t+1})^2=(1+\bmom^{t+1})/(1-\bmom^{t+1})\).
The coupling follows by subtracting the two recursions, whose difference is \(M_t^K-\Mt=\bmom(M_{t-1}^K-M_{t-1})\), and iterating down to \(t=-1\). Since \(\EE M_{-1}=\EE\Gt\) and \(\EE\norm{M_{-1}-\EE\Gt}_\F^2=\sw2\mathsf V_B\) by Lemma SA.5(iii), expanding the square gives the second identity of (SA.35).
□
Remark SA.3 (The linear buffer and the filtered update)
By Lemma SA.5(iii), the raw covariance of the buffer is that of a single batch of size \(B/\sw2=B(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)\), and by (v) the two-sided sum of its lag covariances is exactly the covariance of one batch. Both statements concern the linear buffer \(\Mt\) and say nothing about the nonlinear filtered update \(\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\): the update is not linear in the batches, its lag covariances are not \(\bmom^\ell\) times its covariance, and no effective batch size reproduces its law (the deterministic-equivalent section shows that the weighted fixed-point system depends on the whole weight sequence). The volatility coefficient of the dynamics is the integrated centered lag covariance of the filtered update, which the volatility section identifies.
Lemma SA.6 (History truncation and sample sensitivity)
In the frozen-state model let \(M^{(J)}\defeq\sum_{j=0}^J\emaw jG_{t+1-j}\) for \(J\ge0\), and let \(\ridge>0\).
  1. (i)
    \(\EE\big(\Mt-M^{(J)}\big)=\bmom^{J+1}\,\EE\Gt\) and \(\EE\norm{\Mt-M^{(J)}}_\F^2=\bmom^{2(J+1)}\big(\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F^2+\sw2\mathsf V_B\big)\).
  2. (ii)
    \(\EE\norm{\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)-\Qridge\ridge(M^{(J)})}_\F^2\le\ridge^{-1}\bmom^{2(J+1)}\big(\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F^2+\sw2\mathsf V_B\big)\).
  3. (iii)
    Let \(F:\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\to\R\) be Lipschitz in Frobenius norm with constant \(L_F\), and let \(\Mt^{[ja]}\) denote the buffer with the sample \((z_{ja},w_{ja})\) replaced by an independent copy. Then
    \[ \EE\big|F(\Mt)-F(\Mt^{[ja]})\big|^2\le\frac{2L_F^2\,\emaw j^2\,\mathsf V_B}{B},\qquad \Var F(\Mt)\le L_F^2\,\sw2\,\mathsf V_B . \]
  4. (iv)
    If \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) then \(\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\mathsf V_B\le C_\Sigma^2\,(\Nout\Nin+2\Nout+2\Nin+3)/B\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is the tail of the weight sums. The tail \(\Mt-M^{(J)}=\sum_{j>J}\emaw jG_{t+1-j}\) has mean \((\sum_{j>J}\emaw j)\EE\Gt=\bmom^{J+1}\EE\Gt\), and by (SA.32) its second moment is
\[ \Big(\sum_{j>J}\emaw j\Big)^2\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F^2+\mathsf V_B\sum_{j>J}\emaw j^2 =\bmom^{2(J+1)}\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F^2+\mathsf V_B\,\sw2\,\bmom^{2(J+1)} , \]
since \(\sum_{j>J}\emaw j^2=(1-\bmom)^2\bmom^{2(J+1)}/(1-\bmom^2)\).
Part (ii) is (i) combined with the Lipschitz bound (SA.13).
Part (iii) is the Efron–Stein inequality applied to a truncated history. With \(X_{ja}\defeq d_{ja}\,z_{ja}\tensor w_{ja}\) and \(X_{ja}'\) the same expression in the independent copy, replacing the sample \((j,a)\) changes \(\Mt\) by \(\frac{\emaw j}{B}\Sigmaout^{1/2}(X_{ja}-X_{ja}')\Sigmain^{1/2}\); by Lemma SA.4 the two transformed samples are independent and identically distributed with Frobenius variance \(B\mathsf V_B\) each, so
\[ \EE\norm{\Mt-\Mt^{[ja]}}_\F^2=\frac{\emaw j^2}{B^2}\cdot2B\mathsf V_B=\frac{2\emaw j^2\mathsf V_B}{B}, \]
and the Lipschitz property of \(F\) gives the first bound. For the variance, fix \(J\) and regard \(F(M^{(J)})\) as a function of the \(B(J+1)\) independent samples of ages \(0,\dots,J\). The Efron–Stein inequality [84, Theorem 3.1] and the first bound, which holds verbatim for \(M^{(J)}\), give
\[ \Var F(M^{(J)})\le\frac12\sum_{j=0}^J\sum_{a=1}^B\EE\big|F(M^{(J)})-F(M^{(J)[ja]})\big|^2 \le\frac12\sum_{j=0}^JB\,\frac{2L_F^2\emaw j^2\mathsf V_B}{B}\le L_F^2\,\sw2\,\mathsf V_B . \]
Letting \(J\to\infty\), with \(\norm{F(\Mt)-F(M^{(J)})}_2\le L_F\big(\EE\norm{\Mt-M^{(J)}}_\F^2\big)^{1/2}\to0\) by (i), gives the bound for \(\Mt\).
Part (iv) bounds the traces in (SA.29). We have \(\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F\le\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}}\norm{\Ahat}_\F\norm{\Sigmain^{1/2}}\le C_\Sigma\), \(\Tr\Sigmaout\le C_\Sigma\Nout\), \(\Tr\Sigmain\le C_\Sigma\Nin\), \(\Tr(\Sigmaout\Ahat\Ahat^\T)\le C_\Sigma\norm{\Ahat}_\F^2=C_\Sigma\) and \(\Tr(\Sigmain\Ahat^\T\Ahat)\le C_\Sigma\); inserting these in (SA.29) gives the bound.
□
Lemma SA.7 (Residual law)
Let \(A\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic with \(\norm{A}_\F=1\), let \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) and \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) be independent, and put \(d=z^\T Aw\). Let \(\alpha_1\ge\alpha_2\ge\dots\ge0\) be the singular values of \(A\) and \(g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).
  1. (i)
    \(d\) is symmetric, \(\EE d^2=1\), \(\EE d^4=3+6\sum_k\alpha_k^4\le3+6\norm{A}^2\), and \(\EE\abs{d}^{2p}\le\big((2p-1)!!\big)^2\) for every integer \(p\ge1\).
  2. (ii)
    \(\EE e^{i\theta d}=\prod_k(1+\theta^2\alpha_k^2)^{-1/2}\) for real \(\theta\), and \(0\le\log\EE e^{i\theta d}+\theta^2/2\le\theta^4\norm{A}^2/4\).
  3. (iii)
    Along any sequence of such \(A=A_B\) with \(\norm{A_B}\to0\), \(d\Rightarrow g\) in distribution, and \(\EE\,\psi(d)\to\EE\,\psi(g)\) for every continuous \(\psi\) with \(\abs{\psi(x)}\le1+x^2\).
Proof
Proof.
The residual reduces to a weighted sum of products of independent normals. Write \(A=U\diag(\alpha)V^\T\) with \(U,V\) orthogonal. Since \(U^\T z\) and \(V^\T w\) are again independent standard Gaussian vectors, \(d\) has the law of
\[ d\overset{\mathrm{law}}{=}\sum_k\alpha_kU_kV_k,\qquad U_k,V_k\ \text{independent standard normal}. \]
Negating all \(U_k\) proves symmetry.
The second and fourth moments follow by expansion. Independence gives \(\EE d^2=\sum_k\alpha_k^2=1\). Each product \(U_kV_k\) has fourth moment \(9\), and expanding the fourth power of the sum leaves only the multiplicities \(4\) and \(2+2\), so
\[ \EE d^4=9\sum_k\alpha_k^4+6\sum_{k<l}\alpha_k^2\alpha_l^2=3\Big(\sum_k\alpha_k^2\Big)^2+6\sum_k\alpha_k^4=3+6\sum_k\alpha_k^4 . \]
The bound \(\sum_k\alpha_k^4\le\alpha_1^2\sum_k\alpha_k^2=\norm A^2\) gives the fourth-moment bound.
The \(2p\)-th moment is bounded by conditioning on \(w\). Given \(w\), the variable \(d\) is centered Gaussian with variance \(\norm{Aw}^2\), so
\[ \EE\big[\abs d^{2p}\mid w\big]=(2p-1)!!\,\norm{Aw}^{2p}. \]
In the coordinates above \(\norm{Aw}^2=\sum_k\alpha_k^2V_k^2\) is a convex combination of the \(V_k^2\), so Jensen’s inequality for \(x\mapsto x^p\) gives
\[ \EE\norm{Aw}^{2p}\le\sum_k\alpha_k^2\,\EE V_k^{2p}=(2p-1)!! , \]
and (i) follows.
The characteristic function is a product. Integrating first in \(V_k\) and then in \(U_k\),
\[ \EE e^{i\theta\alpha U_kV_k}=\EE e^{-\theta^2\alpha^2U_k^2/2}=(1+\theta^2\alpha^2)^{-1/2}, \]
and independence gives the product formula of (ii). For \(x\ge0\),
\[ 0\le x-\log(1+x)=\int_0^x\frac{u}{1+u}\,du\le\frac{x^2}{2}; \]
summing at \(x=\theta^2\alpha_k^2\) and using \(\sum_k\alpha_k^2=1\) and \(\sum_k\alpha_k^4\le\norm A^2\) gives the bound of (ii).
Part (iii) is Lévy’s continuity theorem: by (ii) the characteristic functions converge pointwise to \(e^{-\theta^2/2}\), hence \(d\Rightarrow g\) [82, Theorem 26.3], and the convergence of \(\EE\psi(d)\) for \(\abs{\psi(x)}\le1+x^2\) follows from the bound \(\EE d^4\le9\) of (i).
□

SA.6 Gram resolvents, the regime, and deterministic equivalents

Gram resolvents of the buffer. In the frozen-state model, for \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\) define
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.36} R(z)\defeq\big(\Mt\Mt^\T-z\,\Id_{\Nout}\big)^{-1},\qquad \widetilde R(z)\defeq\big(\Mt^\T\Mt-z\,\Id_{\Nin}\big)^{-1}. \end{equation}
At \(\bmom=0\) one has \(\Mt=\Gt\) and these are the resolvents of Section 2 and Figure 2. By Lemma SA.3, the regularized update is an integral of the resolvent along the negative axis,
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.37} \Qridge{\ridge}(\Mt)=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty R(-\ridge-u^2)\,\Mt\,du , \end{equation}
which is why every deterministic equivalent of the appendix is stated on the negative axis, \(z=-s\) with \(s>0\). The two resolvents are related by the following identities, which we keep in the form of the main text.
Lemma SA.8 (Rectangular resolvent intertwining)
Let \(\vec{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_1\times n_2}\) and \(\vec{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_2\times n_1}\). For \(z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\sigma(\vec{A}\vec{B})\cup\sigma(\vec{B}\vec{A}))\), define
\[ R_{AB}(z)\defeq(\vec{A}\vec{B}-z\,\Id_{n_1})^{-1},\qquad R_{BA}(z)\defeq(\vec{B}\vec{A}-z\,\Id_{n_2})^{-1}. \]
Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.38} R_{AB}\vec{A}=\vec{A}R_{BA},\qquad \vec{B}R_{AB}=R_{BA}\vec{B}, \end{equation}
and consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.39} \vec{A}R_{BA}\vec{B}=\Id_{n_1}+z\,R_{AB},\qquad \vec{B}R_{AB}\vec{A}=\Id_{n_2}+z\,R_{BA}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The identities
\[ (\vec{A}\vec{B}-z\Id_{n_1})\vec{A}=\vec{A}(\vec{B}\vec{A}-z\Id_{n_2}),\qquad \vec{B}(\vec{A}\vec{B}-z\Id_{n_1})=(\vec{B}\vec{A}-z\Id_{n_2})\vec{B} \]
are immediate. Multiplying the first on the left by \(R_{AB}\) and on the right by \(R_{BA}\) gives \(R_{AB}\vec{A}=\vec{A}R_{BA}\); multiplying the second similarly gives \(\vec{B}R_{AB}=R_{BA}\vec{B}\). Then
\[ \vec{A}R_{BA}\vec{B}=(R_{AB}\vec{A})\vec{B}=R_{AB}(\vec{A}\vec{B}) =\Id_{n_1}+zR_{AB}, \]
and similarly
\[ \vec{B}R_{AB}\vec{A}=R_{BA}(\vec{B}\vec{A})=\Id_{n_2}+zR_{BA}. \]
□
Applied with \((\vec A,\vec B)=(\Mt,\Mt^\T)\), whose products have the same nonzero spectrum, the lemma gives for every \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\)
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.40} R(z)\Mt=\Mt\widetilde R(z),\qquad \Mt^\T R(z)\Mt=\Id_{\Nin}+z\widetilde R(z),\qquad \Mt\widetilde R(z)\Mt^\T=\Id_{\Nout}+zR(z). \end{equation}
Spectral measures. The covariances enter the deterministic equivalents only through the two spectral measures
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.41} \nu_{\mathrm{out}}\defeq\frac1B\sum_{i=1}^{\Nout}\delta_{\mu_i},\qquad \nu_{\mathrm{in}}\defeq\frac1B\sum_{k=1}^{\Nin}\delta_{\lambda_k}, \end{equation}
so that \(\frac1B\Tr\,\phi(\Sigmaout)=\int\phi\,d\nu_{\mathrm{out}}\) for every function \(\phi\) on \([0,\infty)\), and likewise for \(\Sigmain\).
The regime. We state the proportional regime of the main text and then the regime under which the random-matrix results of the appendix are proved.
Assumption S2 (Proportional regime)
The dimensions \(\Nin\), \(\Nout\), \(B\) tend to infinity jointly, with aspect ratios converging to constants:
\[ \frac{\Nin}{B} \to \gin \in (0,\infty), \qquad \frac{\Nout}{B} \to \gout \in (0,\infty). \]
The covariance operator norms remain uniformly bounded: there exists a constant \(C > 0\), independent of \(\Nin\) and \(\Nout\), such that
\[ \|\Sigmain\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\leq\; C, \qquad \|\Sigmaout\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\leq\; C. \]
We refer to the joint limit as the proportional regime.
Assumption S3 (Spectrally diffuse proportional regime)
Assumption S1 and Assumption S2 hold, and we fix \(\gamma_\star<\infty\) and \(C_\Sigma\ge1\) with \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), \(\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\norm{\Sigmaout}\le C_\Sigma\) along the whole sequence of dimensions; the covariances are deterministic. The momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) is fixed, and the normalized ridge satisfies \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\) for fixed \(0<\ridge_-\le\ridge_+<\infty\). The state is frozen as in Definition SA.3, with \(\cR(t)>0\), and the normalized state is spectrally diffuse: \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\).
The two conditions \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\) and \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\) say that no single singular direction of the error carries a nonvanishing fraction of the residual variance; by Lemma SA.7(iii) they are exactly what makes the normalized residuals asymptotically standard Gaussian. No lower bound on the covariances, no rank condition and no rate for \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\) is assumed. Under Assumption S3 the spectral measures (SA.41) are positive measures on \([0,C_\Sigma]\) of total mass at most \(\gamma_\star\). All limits of the appendix are along a sequence of dimensions satisfying Assumption S3, and all constants are uniform in \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\) unless a statement says otherwise; a statement for a fixed ridge is the case \(\ridge_-=\ridge_+\).
Deterministic equivalents. We make the underlying convergence mode precise.
Definition SA.4 (Deterministic equivalent)
Let \(M_N(z)\) be a sequence of random matrices indexed by the dimension parameter \(N\) and depending on a spectral parameter \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\). A deterministic matrix family \(\overline M_N(z)\) of the same size is a deterministic equivalent of \(M_N(z)\) in the proportional regime (Assumption S2) if, for every sequence of deterministic test matrices \(T_N\) with \(\norm{T_N}\) bounded uniformly in \(N\),
\[ \frac{1}{N}\,\Tr\!\big[(M_N(z) - \overline M_N(z))\,T_N\big] \;\xrightarrow{\;\Pr\;}\; 0 \qquad \text{as } N \to \infty. \]
Equivalently, \(\overline M_N(z)\) matches \(M_N(z)\) in the normalized trace pairing against any bounded test matrix, up to a vanishing error in probability. All deterministic-equivalent claims of the appendix are statements of this form, with \(N\) the appropriate dimension (\(\Nin\), \(\Nout\) or \(B\)) determined by context; the deterministic-equivalent section proves them in the stronger form of convergence of the expected matrix in operator norm together with convergence of the trace pairing in mean square, uniformly on compact subsets of the negative axis. Two display-equation symbols make the convergence mode explicit: \(A\DEequiv\overline A\) (read “\(\overline A\) is a deterministic equivalent of \(A\)”) stands for the matrix-valued statement of Definition SA.4, and \(a\Peq b\) (read “\(a\) equals \(b\) up to \(o_{\Pr}(1)\)”) stands for \(a=b+o_{\Pr}(1)\) for scalars normalized so that both sides are \(O(1)\). A bare “\(=\)” between random and deterministic quantities means exact equality, algebraic or with probability one.
