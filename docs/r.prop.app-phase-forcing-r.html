Proposition SI.8 (Forcing integrals below the critical exponent)
Let \(p>1\) and \(q>0\) with \(r\defeq r_q<1\), and write \(C=C_{p,q}\) and \(A=A_p\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(N\ge1\), \(J_N(p,q)\le2\Gamma(1-r)\,C\,N^{q(1-r)}\).
  2. (ii)
    For every \(N\) with \((N/4)^{q(1-r)}\ge2\),
    \[ J_N(p,q)\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{2(1-r)}\,4^{-q(1-r)}N^{q(1-r)} . \]
  3. (iii)
    As \(N\to\infty\), \(J_N(p,q)/N^{q(1-r)}\to J_\circ(p,q)\), and \(0<J_\circ(p,q)\le2\Gamma(1-r)\,C\).
  4. (iv)
    \(\mathcal K(\frac12)=\sqrt{2\pi}\); consequently \(J_\circ(q+1,q)=\frac12\sqrt{\pi/q}\).
Proof of Proposition SI.8.
The idea is to rescale time by the slowest rate \(N^{-q}\). The rescaled forcing is a Riemann sum, and Lemma SI.19 supplies a majorant that does not depend on \(N\).
For (i), Lemma SI.19 gives \(\sqrt{f_{N,q}(u)}\le2^rCu^{-r}e^{-u/(2N^q)}\), and
\[ \int_0^\infty u^{-r}e^{-u/(2N^q)}\,\dif u=(2N^q)^{1-r}\,\Gamma(1-r) . \]
The product of the constants is \(2\Gamma(1-r)CN^{q(1-r)}\).
For (ii), put \(V\defeq(N/4)^q\). The hypothesis gives \(N>4\) and \(V^{1-r}\ge2\). The lower envelope of (SI.23) on \([0,V]\) gives
\[ J_N(p,q)\ge\sqrt A\int_0^V(1+u)^{-r}\,\dif u\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{1-r}\big(V^{1-r}-1\big)\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{2(1-r)}\,V^{1-r} , \]
and \(V^{1-r}=4^{-q(1-r)}N^{q(1-r)}\).
For (iii), substitute \(u=N^qz\). Since \(N^q\cdot N^{-(p-1)/2}=N^{q(1-r)}\),
\[ \frac{J_N(p,q)}{N^{q(1-r)}}=\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\Phi_N(z)/2}\,\dif z,\qquad \Phi_N(z)\defeq2N^{p-1}f_{N,q}(N^qz)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\varphi_z(i/N), \]
where \(\varphi_z(t)\defeq t^{-p}e^{-2zt^{-q}}\) for \(0<t\le1\) and \(\varphi_z(0)\defeq0\). For fixed \(z>0\) the function \(\varphi_z\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), so the right Riemann sums converge:
\[ \Phi_N(z)\longrightarrow\Phi(z)\defeq\int_0^1\varphi_z(t)\,\dif t . \]
Lemma SI.19 at \(u=N^qz\), with \(2qr=p-1\), gives the majorant
\[ \frac{\Phi_N(z)}2\le N^{p-1}e^{-z}C^2\,2^{2r}(N^qz)^{-2r}=4^rC^2z^{-2r}e^{-z} . \]
Its square root \(2^rCz^{-r}e^{-z/2}\) does not depend on \(N\) and has integral \(2\Gamma(1-r)C\) over \((0,\infty)\), finite because \(r<1\). Hence the integrals converge,
\[ \frac{J_N(p,q)}{N^{q(1-r)}}\longrightarrow\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\Phi(z)/2}\,\dif z\le2\Gamma(1-r)\,C . \]
To evaluate the limit, substitute \(t=w^{-1/q}\) with \(w\in[1,\infty)\). Then \(\dif t=q^{-1}w^{-1/q-1}\dif w\), \(t^{-p}=w^{p/q}\) and \((p-1)/q=2r\), so
\[ \Phi(z)=\frac1q\int_1^\infty w^{2r-1}e^{-2zw}\,\dif w=\frac{(2z)^{-2r}}q\,\Gamma(2r,2z) . \]
The substitution \(y=2z\) then gives
\[ \int_0^\infty\sqrt{\Phi(z)/2}\,\dif z=(2q)^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty(2z)^{-r}\Gamma(2r,2z)^{1/2}\,\dif z=(8q)^{-1/2}\,\mathcal K(r)=J_\circ(p,q) , \]
which is positive because the integrand is.
For (iv), \(\Gamma(1,y)=e^{-y}\), so
\[ \mathcal K(\tfrac12)=\int_0^\infty y^{-1/2}e^{-y/2}\,\dif y=\sqrt2\,\Gamma(\tfrac12)=\sqrt{2\pi} . \]
If \(p=q+1\), then \(r=\frac12\) and \(J_\circ(p,q)=\sqrt{2\pi}/\sqrt{8q}=\frac12\sqrt{\pi/q}\).
□
