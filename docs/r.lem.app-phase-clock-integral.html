Lemma SI.10 (The clock integral)
Fix \(p>1\), \(q>0\) and \(T\ge4\), and let \(N\ge4\) and \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0(p,q,T)\). Let \(Y\) be continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with \(Y\ge f_{N,q}\), and suppose that its first \(T\epsilon\) hit \(\hat u\) is attained, \(0<\hat u<\infty\) with \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\). Let \(H\ge\hat u\) and \(\mathcal E\defeq\int_0^{\hat u}(\sqrt Y-\sqrt{f_{N,q}})\,\dif u\).
  1. (i)
    If \(r<1\) and \(H\le(N/4)^q\), then
    \[ \frac{\sqrt A}{2(1-r)}\Big(\frac AT\Big)^{(1-r)/(2r)}\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)}\le\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\le C\,I_r(H)+\mathcal E . \]
  2. (ii)
    If \(r=1\) and \(H\le(N/4)^q\), then \(\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\ge\frac{\sqrt A}4\log\frac1\epsilon\), and
    \[ \Big|\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u-\frac{a_q}2\log\frac1\epsilon\Big|\le K_{p,q,T}+a_q\log_+\big((1+H)\sqrt\epsilon\big)+\frac{HN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}+\mathcal E , \]
    where \(K_{p,q,T}\defeq C+a_q\log2+qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}/a_q+\frac{a_q}2\log(T/A)\).
  3. (iii)
    If \(r>1\), then \(\frac1{2\sqrt2}\le\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\le J(p,q)+\mathcal E\), and
    \begin{multline*} \Big|\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u-J(p,q)\Big|\\ \le\Big[\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}+\frac C{r-1}\Big]N^{-q(r-1)}+\frac C{r-1}\Big(1+\frac12\log\frac1{2T\epsilon}\Big)^{1-r}+\mathcal E . \end{multline*}
Proof.
Write \(f=f_{N,q}\). By definition,
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.52} \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u=\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u+\mathcal E . \end{equation}
The level at the hit bounds the hit from below: \(f(\hat u)\le Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\).
For (i) and (ii), \(\hat u\le H\le(N/4)^q\), so the lower envelope of (SI.23) applies at \(\hat u\) and on \([0,\hat u]\). At \(\hat u\) it gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.53} 1+\hat u\ge\Big(\frac A{T\epsilon}\Big)^{1/(2r)} . \end{equation}
For \(r<1\), integrating the lower envelope gives
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u\ge\sqrt A\,I_r(\hat u)\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{1-r}\Big[\Big(\frac A{T\epsilon}\Big)^{(1-r)/(2r)}-1\Big]\ge\frac{\sqrt A}{2(1-r)}\Big(\frac AT\Big)^{(1-r)/(2r)}\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)} , \]
where the last step uses \((A/(T\epsilon))^{(1-r)/(2r)}\ge2\), which is the last entry of \(\epsilon_0\). The upper envelope gives \(\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\le C\,I_r(H)\), and (SI.52) proves (i).
For \(r=1\), integrating the lower envelope and using (SI.53) gives
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u\ge\sqrt A\log(1+\hat u)\ge\frac{\sqrt A}2\log\frac A{T\epsilon}\ge\frac{\sqrt A}4\log\frac1\epsilon , \]
where the last step uses \(\epsilon\le(A/T)^2\). For the constant, \(r=1\) means \(p=2q+1\), so \(d(p,q)=2\) and \(\Gamma(2)=1\). Lemma SI.6 with \(2u\) in place of \(a\), divided by \(2\), gives \(\abs{f_{\infty,q}(u)-a_q^2u^{-2}}\le M_{p,q}(2u)^{-p/q}\). Dividing by \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}+a_q/u\ge a_q/u\) gives, for \(u\ge1\),
\[ \Big|\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}(u)}-\frac{a_q}u\Big|\le\frac{M_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q}\,u^{-1-1/q} . \]
The right side has integral \(qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}/a_q\) over \([1,\infty)\), and \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\le C\) on \([0,1]\). Together with \(0\le\log(1+z)-\log z\le\log2\) for \(z\ge1\), this gives, for every \(z\ge0\),
\[ \Big|\int_0^z\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\,\dif u-a_q\log(1+z)\Big|\le C+a_q\log2+\frac{qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q} . \]
The finite sum differs from the infinite one by \(0\le f_{\infty,q}-f\le N^{1-p}/(2(p-1))\) (Lemma SI.6), and \(N^{(1-p)/2}=N^{-q}\), so
\[ 0\le\int_0^{\hat u}\big(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}-\sqrt f\big)\,\dif u\le\frac{\hat uN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}\le\frac{HN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}} . \]
Finally (SI.53) and \(\hat u\le H\) give
\[ -\frac12\log\frac TA\le\log(1+\hat u)-\frac12\log\frac1\epsilon\le\log\big((1+H)\sqrt\epsilon\big) . \]
Combining the last three displays with (SI.52) proves (ii).
For (iii), \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\le C(1+u)^{-r}\) is integrable and \(J(p,q)>0\). Since \(f\ge\frac12e^{-2u}\), the level at the hit gives \(\hat u\ge l_\epsilon\defeq\frac12\log\frac1{2T\epsilon}\), and \(l_\epsilon\ge\log2\) because \(\epsilon\le1/(8T)\). Hence
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\ge\int_0^{\log2}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt2}\,\dif u=\frac1{2\sqrt2} . \]
The upper bound follows from (SI.52) and \(f\le f_{\infty,q}\). For the limit, split
\[ J(p,q)-\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u=\big(J(p,q)-J_N(p,q)\big)+\int_{\hat u}^\infty\sqrt f\,\dif u . \]
On \([0,N^q]\) the pointwise bound \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}-\sqrt f\le N^{(1-p)/2}/\sqrt{2(p-1)}=N^{-qr}/\sqrt{2(p-1)}\) holds, and on \([N^q,\infty)\) the integrand is at most \(C(1+u)^{-r}\); hence
\[ 0\le J(p,q)-J_N(p,q)\le\Big[\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}+\frac C{r-1}\Big]N^{-q(r-1)} . \]
The upper envelope and \(\hat u\ge l_\epsilon\) give
\[ 0\le\int_{\hat u}^\infty\sqrt f\,\dif u\le\frac C{r-1}(1+l_\epsilon)^{1-r} . \]
With (SI.52) this proves (iii).
□
