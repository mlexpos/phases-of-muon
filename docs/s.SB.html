SB Deterministic equivalents for the EMA Gram resolvent

  1. SB.1 The weighted fixed-point system
  2. SB.2 Finite identities
  3. SB.3 The Gram closure
This section proves the first random-matrix input of the appendix. In the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3, the exponential moving average \(\Mt\) of the normalized minibatch gradients is a weighted sum of independent batches, and the resolvent of its Gram matrix \(\Mt\Mt^\T\) on the negative real axis has a deterministic equivalent
\[ \Rbar(z)=\big(\tilde s_1(z)\,\Sigmaout-z\,\Id_{\Nout}\big)^{-1},\qquad z=-s<0, \]
where \(\tilde s_1(z)\) is determined by the weighted fixed-point system of Definition SB.1. At \(\bmom=0\) the system is the one displayed in Section 2. The section has three main results. Theorem SB.1 shows that the weighted system has exactly one nonnegative solution and that this solution is continuous in the spectral data and stable under approximate roots. Theorem SB.2 shows that the expected Gram resolvents converge to \(\Rbar\) and \(\Rtbar\) in operator norm, uniformly on compact subsets of the negative axis. Corollary SB.1 gives the normalized-trace form of the closure, with bounded deterministic tests, in expected squared compact supremum.
The section works in the setting of Section SA: its conventions (Section SA.1), the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3 under Assumption S3, the normalized state \(\Ahat\) of (SA.10), the EMA weights of (SA.18), the Gram resolvents \(R(z)\) and \(\widetilde R(z)\) of (SA.36), and the spectral measures of (SA.41). From that section it also takes the convergence of the buffer series (Lemma SA.5(i)), its truncation bound (Lemma SA.6(i)) and the residual law (Lemma SA.7).

SB.1 The weighted fixed-point system

Scalar functions. Let \(w=(w_j)_{j\ge0}\) be a sequence of nonnegative numbers, not all zero, with \(\sum_jw_j<\infty\); write \(\sw{p}=\sum_jw_j^p\) for \(p\ge2\). The EMA weights of (SA.18) are the case of interest, and for them we write \(\Fw\) and \(\Lw\) for the functions \(F_w\) and \(L_w\) defined now. For \(g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) and \(x,q\ge0\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.1} \Jfun(x)\defeq\EE\frac{g^2}{1+xg^2},\qquad F_w(q)\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}w_j^2\,\Jfun(w_j^2q),\qquad L_w(q)\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}w_j\,\Jfun(w_j^2q). \end{equation}
Lemma SB.1 (Properties of the scalar functions)
Let \(w\) be nonnegative, summable and not all zero.
  1. (i)
    \(\Jfun\) is continuous and strictly decreasing on \([0,\infty)\) with values in \((0,1]\), it is \(C^\infty\) on \((0,\infty)\) with one-sided derivatives at \(0\), and
    \[ \Jfun(0)=1,\qquad \Jfun'(0)=-3 . \]
  2. (ii)
    The series defining \(F_w\) and \(L_w\) converge uniformly on \([0,\infty)\); \(F_w\) and \(L_w\) are continuous, \(C^\infty\) on \((0,\infty)\) with one-sided derivatives at \(0\), and
    \[ F_w(0)=\sw2,\qquad F_w'(0)=-3\sw4,\qquad L_w(0)=\sum_jw_j,\qquad L_w'(0)=-3\sw3 . \]
  3. (iii)
    The function
    \[ r_w(q)\defeq qF_w(q)=\sum_j\EE\frac{w_j^2qg^2}{1+w_j^2qg^2} \]
    is strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\), with \(r_w(0)=0\) and
    \[ r_w'(q)=\sum_jw_j^2\,\EE\frac{g^2}{(1+w_j^2qg^2)^2}>0 , \]
    and \(r_w(q)\uparrow\#\{j:w_j>0\}\in\{1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}\) as \(q\to\infty\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is differentiation under the Gaussian integral. The integrand \(g^2/(1+xg^2)\) is bounded by \(g^2\) and strictly decreasing in \(x\) on \(\{g\ne0\}\), and its \(x\)-derivatives satisfy
\[ \Big|\frac{\partial^k}{\partial x^k}\frac{g^2}{1+xg^2}\Big|=\frac{k!\,g^{2k+2}}{(1+xg^2)^{k+1}}\le k!\,g^{2k+2}\qquad(x\ge0), \]
and differentiation under the expectation, dominated by these bounds, gives continuity, strict monotonicity, smoothness on \((0,\infty)\), one-sided derivatives at \(0\), and
\[ \Jfun(0)=\EE g^2=1,\qquad \Jfun'(0)=-\EE g^4=-3 . \]
Part (ii) is uniform convergence of the series and of their derivatives. The \(j\)-th term of \(F_w\) is bounded by \(w_j^2\), that of \(L_w\) by \(w_j\), and their \(k\)-th derivatives in \(q\) are bounded, by the chain rule and part (i), by
\[ k!\,\EE g^{2k+2}\,w_j^{2k+2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad k!\,\EE g^{2k+2}\,w_j^{2k+1}. \]
Since \(\sum_jw_j<\infty\) and \(w_j\le\sum_kw_k\), all these series converge uniformly on \([0,\infty)\), which gives continuity and smoothness. The values at \(0\) follow from \(\Jfun(0)=1\) and \(\Jfun'(0)=-3\):
\[ \begin{gathered} F_w(0)=\sum_jw_j^2=\sw2,\qquad F_w'(0)=-3\sum_jw_j^4=-3\sw4,\\ L_w(0)=\sum_jw_j,\qquad L_w'(0)=-3\sum_jw_j^3=-3\sw3 . \end{gathered} \]
Part (iii) is monotone convergence. Termwise,
\[ qF_w(q)=\sum_j\EE\frac{w_j^2qg^2}{1+w_j^2qg^2},\qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{xg^2}{1+xg^2}=\frac{g^2}{(1+xg^2)^2}>0\ \text{on }\{g\ne0\}, \]
which gives \(r_w(0)=0\) and the displayed derivative, positive because at least one \(w_j\) is positive. As \(q\to\infty\) each term with \(w_j>0\) increases to \(\Pr(g\ne0)=1\) by monotone convergence, and so does the sum.

Definition SB.1 (Weighted fixed-point system)
Let \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\) be finite positive Borel measures on \([0,\infty)\) with compact support, let \(w\) be as above, and let \(z=-s\) with \(s>0\). The weighted fixed-point system at \(z\) is the system of equations in the four unknowns \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)\in[0,\infty)^4\)
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.2} \begin{aligned} \sigma&=\int\frac{x}{\tilde s_1x-z}\,\nu(dx),\qquad& \tilde\sigma&=\int\frac{x}{s_1x-z}\,\tilde\nu(dx),\\ q&=-z\,\sigma\tilde\sigma,\qquad& \tilde s_1&=-z\,\tilde\sigma\,F_w(q),\qquad s_1=-z\,\sigma\,F_w(q). \end{aligned} \end{equation}
For the spectral measures of (SA.41) the first two equations read
\[ \sigma=\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(\tilde s_1\Sigmaout-z\Id_{\Nout})^{-1}\big),\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmain(s_1\Sigmain-z\Id_{\Nin})^{-1}\big), \]
and we define the deterministic equivalents
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.3} \Rbar(z)\defeq\big(\tilde s_1(z)\,\Sigmaout-z\,\Id_{\Nout}\big)^{-1},\qquad \Rtbar(z)\defeq\big(s_1(z)\,\Sigmain-z\,\Id_{\Nin}\big)^{-1}, \end{equation}
where \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)(z)\) is the solution given by Theorem SB.1.
In this appendix \(\sigma(z)\), \(\tilde\sigma(z)\), \(\tilde s_1(z)\) and \(s_1(z)\) always denote the solution of (SB.2); the random traces are written out as \(\frac1B\Tr(\Sigmaout R(z))\). The letters are those of Section 2.
Multiplying the last two equations of (SB.2) by \(\sigma\) and \(\tilde\sigma\) gives the coupling
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.4} \tilde s_1\,\sigma=s_1\,\tilde\sigma=qF_w(q)=r_w(q), \end{equation}
which at \(\bmom=0\) is the third line of the system of Section 2 (Proposition SB.1).
Under Assumption S3 the pair \((\nu_{\mathrm{out}},\nu_{\mathrm{in}})\) of (SA.41) belongs to the class
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.5} \mathcal K\defeq\big\{(\nu,\tilde\nu):\ \nu,\tilde\nu\text{ finite positive Borel measures on }[0,C_\Sigma],\ \nu([0,C_\Sigma])\le\gamma_\star,\ \tilde\nu([0,C_\Sigma])\le\gamma_\star\big\}. \end{equation}
Theorem SB.1 (The weighted branch)
Let \(w\) be nonnegative, summable and not all zero, and let \(C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star>0\).
  1. (i)
    (Existence and uniqueness.) For every \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\) and every \(s>0\) the system (SB.2) at \(z=-s\) has exactly one solution in \([0,\infty)^4\). It satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SB.6} \sigma,\tilde\sigma\le\frac{\gamma_\star C_\Sigma}{s},\qquad \tilde s_1,s_1\le\gamma_\star C_\Sigma\,\sw2,\qquad q\le\frac{(\gamma_\star C_\Sigma)^2}{s}. \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    (Continuity.) Equip \(\mathcal K\) with the topology of weak convergence against \(C([0,C_\Sigma])\) in each coordinate. The solution map \((s,\nu,\tilde\nu)\mapsto(\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\times\mathcal K\), and for every compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) it is uniformly continuous in \(s\in I\), uniformly over \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\).
  3. (iii)
    (Stability under approximate roots.) Let \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) be compact with \(s_-\defeq\min I\). For every \(\epsilon>0\) there is \(\delta>0\), depending only on \((\epsilon,I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star,w)\), with the following property. If \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\), \(s\in I\), and \((\sigma',\tilde\sigma',a',c')\in[0,\gamma_\star C_\Sigma/s_-]^2\times[0,\gamma_\star C_\Sigma\sw2]^2\) satisfy, with \(q'=s\sigma'\tilde\sigma'\),
    \[ \begin{gathered} \Big|\sigma'-\int\frac{x}{s+a'x}\,\nu(dx)\Big|\le\delta,\qquad \Big|\tilde\sigma'-\int\frac{x}{s+c'x}\,\tilde\nu(dx)\Big|\le\delta,\\ \abs{a'-s\tilde\sigma'F_w(q')}\le\delta,\qquad \abs{c'-s\sigma'F_w(q')}\le\delta, \end{gathered} \]
    then \(\abs{\sigma'-\sigma}+\abs{\tilde\sigma'-\tilde\sigma}+\abs{a'-\tilde s_1}+\abs{c'-s_1}\le\epsilon\), where \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)\) is the solution of (SB.2) at \(z=-s\) for \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\).
  4. (iv)
    (Smoothness in \(s\).) Fix \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\) with \(\int x\,\nu(dx)>0\) and \(\int x\,\tilde\nu(dx)>0\). Then \(s\mapsto(\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)(-s)\) is \(C^\infty\) on \((0,\infty)\), and \(\abs{q'(s)}\le q(s)/s\).
The proof uses a compactness lemma for the class \(\mathcal K\).
Lemma SB.2 (Compactness of the spectral class)
Every sequence in \(\mathcal K\) has a subsequence that converges, weakly against \(C([0,C_\Sigma])\) in each coordinate, to an element of \(\mathcal K\). If \(\nu_n\to\nu\) weakly in this sense, \(s_n\to s>0\) and \(a_n\to a\ge0\), then \(\int\frac{x}{s_n+a_nx}\,\nu_n(dx)\to\int\frac{x}{s+ax}\,\nu(dx)\).
Proof
Proof.
By the Riesz representation theorem [93, Theorem 6.19], a finite positive Borel measure \(\nu\) on \([0,C_\Sigma]\) is the same as a positive bounded linear functional on \(C([0,C_\Sigma])\), with norm \(\nu([0,C_\Sigma])\). The closed ball of radius \(\gamma_\star\) in the dual of \(C([0,C_\Sigma])\) is weak-\(*\) compact by the Banach–Alaoglu theorem [94, Theorem 3.15] and, since \(C([0,C_\Sigma])\) is separable, weak-\(*\) metrizable [94, Theorem 3.16], hence weak-\(*\) sequentially compact. Positivity of a functional \(\ell\) and the bound \(\ell(1)\le\gamma_\star\) are preserved under weak-\(*\) limits, so the limit is again in \(\mathcal K\).
The second claim is continuity of the integrand in its parameters. The function \(\phi_{s,a}(x)\defeq x/(s+ax)\) is continuous on \([0,C_\Sigma]\), with parameter derivatives
\[ \partial_s\phi_{s,a}(x)=-\frac{x}{(s+ax)^2},\qquad \partial_a\phi_{s,a}(x)=-\frac{x^2}{(s+ax)^2}. \]
For \(s_n,s\ge s_\circ>0\) and \(a_n,a\ge0\) the mean value theorem in \((s,a)\) therefore gives
\[ \sup_{x\in[0,C_\Sigma]}\abs{\phi_{s_n,a_n}(x)-\phi_{s,a}(x)}\le\frac{C_\Sigma\abs{s_n-s}+C_\Sigma^2\abs{a_n-a}}{s_\circ^2}. \]
Splitting the difference of the integrals into a change of integrand and a change of measure,
\[ \Big|\int\phi_{s_n,a_n}\,d\nu_n-\int\phi_{s,a}\,d\nu\Big| \le\gamma_\star\,\norm{\phi_{s_n,a_n}-\phi_{s,a}}_\infty+\Big|\int\phi_{s,a}\,d\nu_n-\int\phi_{s,a}\,d\nu\Big|\longrightarrow0 , \]
the first term by the displayed bound and the second by weak convergence.

Proof
Proof of Theorem SB.1.
Throughout, \(z=-s\), and we write \(r=r_w\) from Lemma SB.1(iii). Set \(p\defeq\nu((0,C_\Sigma])\) and \(\tilde p\defeq\tilde\nu((0,C_\Sigma])\).
Degenerate cases. If \(p=0\) the system has the unique solution
\[ \sigma=0,\qquad q=0,\qquad s_1=0,\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac1s\int x\,\tilde\nu(dx),\qquad \tilde s_1=s\tilde\sigma F_w(0)=s\tilde\sigma\,\sw2 . \]
Indeed \(\nu\) is then a multiple of \(\delta_0\), so the first equation forces \(\sigma=0\) whatever \(\tilde s_1\) is, and the remaining equations determine the other unknowns in the displayed order. The case \(\tilde p=0\) is symmetric. Assume now \(p>0\) and \(\tilde p>0\).
Reduction to one scalar equation. The two trace equations are inverted through the functions
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.7} H(x)\defeq\int\frac{x\lambda}{s+x\lambda}\,\nu(d\lambda),\qquad \widetilde H(x)\defeq\int\frac{x\lambda}{s+x\lambda}\,\tilde\nu(d\lambda)\qquad(x\ge0). \end{equation}
The function \(H\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\), since \(p>0\) and the integrand is strictly increasing in \(x\) for \(\lambda>0\), with
\[ H(0)=0,\qquad H(x)\uparrow p\ \ (x\to\infty) \]
by monotone convergence. Its quotient
\[ \frac{H(x)}{x}=\int\frac{\lambda}{s+x\lambda}\,\nu(d\lambda) \]
is strictly decreasing in \(x>0\) and tends to \(m_1/s\) as \(x\downarrow0\), where \(m_1\defeq\int\lambda\,\nu(d\lambda)>0\). Let \(X:[0,p)\to[0,\infty)\) be the inverse of \(H\). Then \(X\) is continuous and strictly increasing, \(X(\rho)\to\infty\) as \(\rho\uparrow p\), and
\[ \frac{X(\rho)}{\rho}=\Big(\frac{H(X(\rho))}{X(\rho)}\Big)^{-1} \]
is strictly increasing on \((0,p)\) with limit \(s/m_1\) at \(0^+\). The same holds for \(\widetilde H\), \(\widetilde X\) and \(\tilde m_1\).
Every nonnegative solution is determined by \(q\). Let \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)\) be a solution in \([0,\infty)^4\). Since \(p>0\), the first equation gives \(\sigma>0\); likewise \(\tilde\sigma>0\); hence \(q>0\), \(F_w(q)>0\) and \(\tilde s_1,s_1>0\). The coupling (SB.4) reads
\[ H(\tilde s_1)=\tilde s_1\sigma=r(q),\qquad \widetilde H(s_1)=s_1\tilde\sigma=r(q), \]
so \(r(q)<\min(p,\tilde p)\) and
\[ \tilde s_1=X(r(q)),\qquad s_1=\widetilde X(r(q)),\qquad \sigma=\frac{r(q)}{\tilde s_1},\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac{r(q)}{s_1}. \]
Substituting into \(q=s\sigma\tilde\sigma\) gives the scalar equation
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.8} \Phi(q)\defeq\frac{X(r(q))}{r(q)}\cdot\frac{\widetilde X(r(q))}{r(q)}-\frac{s}{q}=0, \qquad q\in Q\defeq\{q>0:\ r(q)<\min(p,\tilde p)\}. \end{equation}
Conversely, every solution of the scalar equation yields a solution of the system. If \(q\in Q\) solves (SB.8), define \(\tilde s_1\), \(s_1\), \(\sigma\), \(\tilde\sigma\) by the last display. The first equation of the system holds because
\[ \sigma=\frac{H(\tilde s_1)}{\tilde s_1}=\int\frac{\lambda}{s+\tilde s_1\lambda}\,\nu(d\lambda), \]
and likewise the second. The crossing equation gives
\[ s\sigma\tilde\sigma=\frac{s\,r(q)^2}{\tilde s_1s_1}=q , \]
which is the third equation. Finally, the crossing equation and \(r=qF_w\) give
\[ \tilde s_1s_1=\frac{s\,r(q)^2}{q}=sqF_w(q)^2, \]
so
\[ \tilde s_1=\frac{sqF_w(q)^2}{s_1}=s\,\frac{r(q)}{s_1}\,F_w(q)=s\tilde\sigma F_w(q), \]
and symmetrically \(s_1=s\sigma F_w(q)\). Hence nonnegative solutions of (SB.2) are in bijection with solutions of (SB.8).
Exactly one crossing. The scalar equation has exactly one solution because \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing and changes sign. Since \(r\) is continuous and strictly increasing from \(0\), the set \(Q\) is an interval \((0,q_\dagger)\) with \(q_\dagger\in(0,\infty]\). On \(Q\) the first term of \(\Phi\) is positive, continuous and nondecreasing, as a product of two positive nondecreasing functions of \(r(q)\), while \(s/q\) is continuous and strictly decreasing; so \(\Phi\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \(Q\). At the left endpoint,
\[ \lim_{q\downarrow0}\frac{X(r(q))}{r(q)}\cdot\frac{\widetilde X(r(q))}{r(q)}=\frac{s^2}{m_1\tilde m_1}<\infty,\qquad \lim_{q\downarrow0}\frac sq=\infty, \]
so \(\Phi(q)\to-\infty\) as \(q\downarrow0\). At the right endpoint there are two cases. If \(q_\dagger<\infty\) then \(r(q)\uparrow\min(p,\tilde p)\) as \(q\uparrow q_\dagger\), so \(X(r(q))\) or \(\widetilde X(r(q))\) tends to infinity while the other factor and \(s/q\) stay bounded, and \(\Phi(q)\to+\infty\). If \(q_\dagger=\infty\) then \(s/q\to0\) while the first term stays at least \(s^2/(m_1\tilde m_1)>0\), so \(\liminf_{q\to\infty}\Phi(q)>0\). In both cases the intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity give exactly one zero. This proves existence and uniqueness.
The a priori bounds (SB.6) follow from the three inequalities
\[ \frac{x}{s+ax}\le\frac xs\le\frac{C_\Sigma}{s}\ \ (x\in[0,C_\Sigma],\ a\ge0),\qquad F_w\le\sw2,\qquad q=s\sigma\tilde\sigma , \]
the second by Lemma SB.1, applied to the two trace equations, to \(\tilde s_1=s\tilde\sigma F_w(q)\), and to \(q\).
Continuity and stability. We prove (iii) by compactness and uniqueness. Part (ii) is the special case \(\delta=0\) of the same argument with varying \((s,\nu,\tilde\nu)\). The modulus in \(s\in I\) uniform over \(\mathcal K\) follows from continuity by the same compactness: sequences \(s_n,s_n'\in I\) with \(\abs{s_n-s_n'}\to0\) and \((\nu_n,\tilde\nu_n)\in\mathcal K\) have a subsequence along which \((s_n,\nu_n,\tilde\nu_n)\) and \((s_n',\nu_n,\tilde\nu_n)\) converge to the same point of \(I\times\mathcal K\). Suppose (iii) fails for some \(\epsilon>0\). Then there are sequences \((\nu_n,\tilde\nu_n)\in\mathcal K\), \(s_n\in I\) and tuples \((\sigma_n',\tilde\sigma_n',a_n',c_n')\) in the stated box whose four residuals are at most \(\delta_n\to0\), but with
\[ \abs{\sigma_n'-\sigma_n}+\abs{\tilde\sigma_n'-\tilde\sigma_n}+\abs{a_n'-\tilde s_{1,n}}+\abs{c_n'-s_{1,n}}>\epsilon \]
for the exact solutions \((\sigma_n,\tilde\sigma_n,\tilde s_{1,n},s_{1,n})\).
All sequences have convergent subsequences. By Lemma SB.2 and the compactness of \(I\) and of the box, in which the exact solutions also lie by (SB.6), we may pass to a subsequence along which
\[ \begin{gathered} (\nu_n,\tilde\nu_n)\to(\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\ \text{weakly},\qquad s_n\to s\in I,\\ (\sigma_n',\tilde\sigma_n',a_n',c_n')\to(\sigma',\tilde\sigma',a',c'),\qquad (\sigma_n,\tilde\sigma_n,\tilde s_{1,n},s_{1,n})\to(\sigma,\tilde\sigma,a,c). \end{gathered} \]
Both limits solve the limit system. By Lemma SB.2 and the continuity of \(F_w\), each of the four equations of (SB.2) passes to the limit along the subsequence, with residuals \(\delta_n\to0\) for the approximate tuples and residuals \(0\) for the exact ones. Hence both limit tuples solve the system at \(z=-s\) for \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\). By uniqueness they coincide, so the displayed distance tends to \(0\) along the subsequence, which contradicts its being larger than \(\epsilon\).
Smoothness in \(s\). Here \(p,\tilde p>0\) by hypothesis and the measures are fixed. The dependence on \(s\) is removed from the inverse functions by scaling. Write \(H(x,s)\) for the function (SB.7) at the spectral parameter \(s\) and \(X(\rho,s)\) for its inverse. Since
\[ H(x,s)=\int\frac{(x/s)\lambda}{1+(x/s)\lambda}\,\nu(d\lambda)=H(x/s,1), \]
one has \(X(\rho,s)=sX(\rho,1)\). Put \(\mathsf A(\rho)\defeq X(\rho,1)\) and \(\widetilde{\mathsf A}(\rho)\defeq\widetilde X(\rho,1)\). The implicit function theorem applied to \(H(x,1)=\rho\), whose \(x\)-derivative
\[ \partial_xH(x,1)=\int\frac{\lambda}{(1+x\lambda)^2}\,\nu(d\lambda) \]
is positive and smooth in \(x\), shows that \(\mathsf A\) is \(C^\infty\) on \((0,p)\), and likewise \(\widetilde{\mathsf A}\).
The crossing equation becomes an implicit equation with a positive \(q\)-derivative. Write \(r\) for \(r(q)=qF_w(q)\). Then (SB.8) reads
\[ \frac{s^2\mathsf A(r)\widetilde{\mathsf A}(r)}{r^2}=\frac sq , \]
that is, \(\mathsf G(q,s)=0\) with
\[ \mathsf G(q,s)\defeq\log s+\log\mathsf A(r(q))+\log\widetilde{\mathsf A}(r(q))-\log q-2\log F_w(q). \]
Since \(\mathsf A(\rho)/\rho\) is nondecreasing, \(\mathsf A'/\mathsf A\ge1/\rho\), and likewise for \(\widetilde{\mathsf A}\). With \(r'=F_w+qF_w'\) this gives
\[ \partial_q\mathsf G=\Big(\frac{\mathsf A'}{\mathsf A}+\frac{\widetilde{\mathsf A}'}{\widetilde{\mathsf A}}\Big)r'-\frac1q-\frac{2F_w'}{F_w} \ \ge\ \frac{2r'}{r}-\frac1q-\frac{2F_w'}{F_w}=\frac2q+\frac{2F_w'}{F_w}-\frac1q-\frac{2F_w'}{F_w}=\frac1q>0 , \]
where the inequality uses \(\mathsf A'/\mathsf A\ge1/r\) and \(r'>0\), and the first equality uses \(r'/r=1/q+F_w'/F_w\).
The implicit function theorem concludes. Since \(\partial_s\mathsf G=1/s\) and \(\mathsf G\) is \(C^\infty\), the function \(q(s)\) is \(C^\infty\) with
\[ q'(s)=-\frac{\partial_s\mathsf G}{\partial_q\mathsf G},\qquad \abs{q'(s)}=\frac{1/s}{\partial_q\mathsf G}\le\frac{q}{s}. \]
The remaining coordinates are the \(C^\infty\) compositions
\[ \tilde s_1=s\,\mathsf A(r(q(s))),\qquad s_1=s\,\widetilde{\mathsf A}(r(q(s))),\qquad \sigma=\frac{r(q(s))}{\tilde s_1},\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac{r(q(s))}{s_1}. \qedhere \]

The case \(\bmom=0\). At \(\bmom=0\) the only weight is \(\emaw0=1\), so that
\[ F_0=L_0=\Jfun,\qquad \tilde s_1\sigma=s_1\tilde\sigma=q\Jfun(q), \]
the second identity being the coupling (SB.4). The next proposition identifies \(q\Jfun(q)\) with the closure function \(f\) of Section 2.
Proposition SB.1 (No-momentum closure)
For \(q>0\) put \(\xi\defeq(2q)^{-1/2}\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.9} q\Jfun(q)=\EE\frac{qg^2}{1+qg^2}=\EE\frac{g^2}{g^2+2\xi^2} =1-\sqrt\pi\,\xi\,e^{\xi^2}\operatorname{erfc}(\xi). \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The first two equalities are the definition of \(\Jfun\) and \(q=1/(2\xi^2)\).
The third equality is a Laplace-transform computation. Since
\[ \EE\frac{qg^2}{1+qg^2}=1-\EE\frac{1}{1+qg^2}, \]
it suffices to compute the last expectation. It is computed from the Laplace representation and the Gaussian integral
\[ \frac{1}{1+qg^2}=\int_0^\infty e^{-u(1+qg^2)}\,du,\qquad \EE e^{-uqg^2}=(1+2uq)^{-1/2}, \]
which give
\[ \EE\frac{1}{1+qg^2}=\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,\EE e^{-uqg^2}\,du =\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\Big(1+\frac{u}{\xi^2}\Big)^{-1/2}du . \]
The substitution \(u=\rho^2-\xi^2\) with \(\rho\in[\xi,\infty)\) turns the last integral into
\[ \int_\xi^\infty e^{-(\rho^2-\xi^2)}\,\frac{\xi}{\rho}\,2\rho\,d\rho =2\xi e^{\xi^2}\int_\xi^\infty e^{-\rho^2}\,d\rho=\sqrt\pi\,\xi\,e^{\xi^2}\operatorname{erfc}(\xi). \qedhere \]

Remark SB.1 (Dictionary to the no-momentum system)
At \(\bmom=0\), with \(z=-s\), the system (SB.2) for the spectral measures (SA.41) is

Proposition SB.2 (Normalization checks)
Let \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\) and \(s>0\).
  1. (i)
    (One weight.) Let \(w=(\varpi,0,0,\dots)\) with \(\varpi>0\), and let \((\sigma_1,\tilde\sigma_1,\tilde s_{1,1},s_{1,1})(-s')\) denote the solution for the unit weight \((1,0,0,\dots)\) at the spectral parameter \(s'=s/\varpi^2\). Then the solution for \(w\) at \(s\) is

    and \(\Rbar_w(-s)=\varpi^{-2}\Rbar_1(-s')\).
  2. (ii)
    (\(K\) equal weights.) Let \(w_j=1/K\) for \(0\le j<K\) and \(w_j=0\) otherwise. Then \(F_w(q)=K^{-1}\Jfun(q/K^2)\), and the solution for \(w\) at \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\) is related to the solution \((\sigma',\tilde\sigma',\tilde s_1',s_1')\) for the unit weight at the measures \((\nu/K,\tilde\nu/K)\) by

Proof
Proof.
In both parts the displayed tuple is checked against the four equations of (SB.2) and then identified with the solution by the uniqueness in Theorem SB.1(i).
For (i), the weight function is \(F_w(q)=\varpi^2\Jfun(\varpi^2q)\). The first trace equation holds at \(s\) because, for every \(x\ge0\),
\[ \frac{x}{s+\varpi^2\tilde s_{1,1}x}=\varpi^{-2}\,\frac{x}{s'+\tilde s_{1,1}x}, \]
and the second likewise. The equation for \(q\) holds because
\[ s\sigma\tilde\sigma=\varpi^2s'\cdot\varpi^{-4}\sigma_1\tilde\sigma_1=\varpi^{-2}q_1 . \]
The equation for \(\tilde s_1\) holds because
\[ s\tilde\sigma F_w(q)=\varpi^2s'\cdot\varpi^{-2}\tilde\sigma_1\cdot\varpi^2\Jfun(\varpi^2\varpi^{-2}q_1)=\varpi^2\,s'\tilde\sigma_1\Jfun(q_1)=\varpi^2\tilde s_{1,1}, \]
and the equation for \(s_1\) symmetrically. The resolvent relation follows from
\[ \big(s\Id+\varpi^2\tilde s_{1,1}\Sigmaout\big)^{-1}=\varpi^{-2}\big(s'\Id+\tilde s_{1,1}\Sigmaout\big)^{-1}. \]

and the second likewise. The equation for \(q\) holds because
\[ s\sigma\tilde\sigma=K^2s\sigma'\tilde\sigma'=K^2q', \]
and the equation for \(\tilde s_1\) because
\[ s\tilde\sigma F_w(q)=s\,K\tilde\sigma'\,K^{-1}\Jfun(q')=s\tilde\sigma'\Jfun(q')=\tilde s_1' , \]
with the equation for \(s_1\) symmetric.

Remark SB.2 (No scalar effective batch for \(\bmom>0\))
By Lemma SB.1(ii), the value and the slope of \(F_w\) at \(0\) are \(\sw2\) and \(-3\sw4\). For \(K\) equal weights, \(\sw4/\sw2^{\,3}=1\), so a uniform average over \(K\) batches, equivalently a batch of size \(KB\) by Proposition SB.2(ii), is determined to first order at \(q=0\) by \(\sw2\) alone. For the EMA weights with \(\bmom\in(0,1)\),
\[ \frac{\sw4}{\sw2^{\,3}}=\frac{(1+\bmom)^2}{1+\bmom^2}>1,\qquad \frac{\sw3}{\sw2^{\,2}}=\frac{(1+\bmom)^2}{1+\bmom+\bmom^2}>1 , \]
so no choice of an effective batch size \(B_{\mathrm{eff}}=B/\sw2\) reproduces the function \(F_{\bmom}\), nor \(L_{\bmom}\), beyond the value at \(q=0\). This is why the system is stated with the full weight sequence.

SB.2 Finite identities

The closure theorem is proved by deleting one sample from the buffer. This subsection collects the exact finite-dimensional identities and bounds that the proof uses. Throughout, \(s>0\) and \(M\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) is a deterministic or random matrix; we write
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.10} R_s(M)\defeq(MM^\T+s\Id_{\Nout})^{-1},\quad \widetilde R_s(M)\defeq(M^\T M+s\Id_{\Nin})^{-1},\quad \mathsf X_s(M)\defeq R_s(M)M . \end{equation}
The dynamics section also needs these objects at complex parameters in the sector
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.11} \mathbb S\defeq\{z\in\mathbb C:\ \Ree z>0,\ \abs{\Img z}\le\Ree z/2\}, \end{equation}
which contains \((0,\infty)\). For \(z\in\mathbb S\) we define \(R_z(M)\), \(\widetilde R_z(M)\) and \(\mathsf X_z(M)\) by (SB.10) with \(z\) in place of \(s\); the inverses exist by Lemma SB.3. Transposes of complex matrices are not conjugated.
Lemma SB.3 (Resolvent identities)
For every \(M\) and \(s>0\),
\[ \begin{gathered} \norm{R_s(M)}\le\frac1s,\qquad \norm{\widetilde R_s(M)}\le\frac1s,\qquad \norm{\mathsf X_s(M)}\le\frac{1}{2\sqrt s},\\ R_s(M)M=M\widetilde R_s(M),\qquad M^\T R_s(M)M=\Id_{\Nin}-s\widetilde R_s(M),\\ R_s(M)MM^\T=\Id_{\Nout}-sR_s(M), \end{gathered} \]
and for \(s,s'>0\),
\[ \norm{R_s(M)-R_{s'}(M)}\le\frac{\abs{s-s'}}{ss'}. \]
For \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\defeq\Ree z\), the matrices \(MM^\T+z\Id_{\Nout}\) and \(M^\T M+z\Id_{\Nin}\) are invertible, the three identities hold with \(z\) in place of \(s\), and
\[ \norm{R_z(M)}\le\frac1x,\qquad \norm{\widetilde R_z(M)}\le\frac1x,\qquad \norm{\mathsf X_z(M)}\le\frac{1}{2\sqrt x},\qquad \frac{\abs z}{x}\le\frac{\sqrt5}{2}. \]
Proof
Proof.
The norm bounds follow from a singular value decomposition \(M=\sum_ks_k\,p_k\tensor r_k\). In it, the three matrices are
\[ \begin{gathered} R_s(M)=\sum_k\frac{p_k\tensor p_k}{s_k^2+s}+\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}}{s},\qquad \widetilde R_s(M)=\sum_k\frac{r_k\tensor r_k}{s_k^2+s}+\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}}{s},\\ \mathsf X_s(M)=\sum_k\frac{s_k}{s_k^2+s}\,p_k\tensor r_k , \end{gathered} \]
where \(\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}\) are the orthogonal projections onto the left and right null spaces of \(M\). The coefficients satisfy
\[ \frac{1}{s_k^2+s}\le\frac1s,\qquad \frac{s_k}{s_k^2+s}\le\frac{1}{2\sqrt s}, \]
the second by the inequality \(s_k^2+s\ge2s_k\sqrt s\) between arithmetic and geometric means.
The intertwining identity follows from the elementary identity
\[ (MM^\T+s\Id_{\Nout})M=M(M^\T M+s\Id_{\Nin}). \]
Multiplying it by \(R_s(M)\) on the left and by \(\widetilde R_s(M)\) on the right gives \(R_s(M)M=M\widetilde R_s(M)\).
The second identity follows from the first. Multiplying \(R_s(M)M=M\widetilde R_s(M)\) on the left by \(M^\T\) gives
\[ M^\T R_s(M)M=M^\T M\,\widetilde R_s(M)=(M^\T M+s\Id_{\Nin}-s\Id_{\Nin})\widetilde R_s(M)=\Id_{\Nin}-s\widetilde R_s(M). \]
The third identity is the same computation with \(MM^\T\):
\[ R_s(M)MM^\T=R_s(M)(MM^\T+s\Id_{\Nout}-s\Id_{\Nout})=\Id_{\Nout}-sR_s(M). \]
The Lipschitz bound is the resolvent identity. For \(s,s'>0\),
\[ R_s(M)-R_{s'}(M)=R_s(M)\big((MM^\T+s'\Id)-(MM^\T+s\Id)\big)R_{s'}(M)=(s'-s)\,R_s(M)R_{s'}(M), \]
and the norm bound follows from \(\norm{R_s(M)}\le1/s\) and \(\norm{R_{s'}(M)}\le1/s'\).
The sector statement is the same computation with \(z\) in place of \(s\). In the singular value decomposition, \(MM^\T+z\Id\) and \(M^\T M+z\Id\) have the real orthonormal eigenvectors \(p_k\), \(r_k\) and the null-space vectors, with eigenvalues \(s_k^2+z\) and \(z\), whose real parts are at least \(x>0\); so both matrices are invertible, and \(R_z(M)\), \(\widetilde R_z(M)\), \(\mathsf X_z(M)\) are the displayed sums with \(s\) replaced by \(z\). Since \(R_z(M)\) and \(\widetilde R_z(M)\) are diagonal in real orthonormal bases, their norms are the largest moduli of their eigenvalues, and the singular values of \(\mathsf X_z(M)\) are \(s_k/\abs{s_k^2+z}\). The bound \(\abs{s_k^2+z}\ge\Ree(s_k^2+z)=s_k^2+x\) gives
\[ \frac{1}{\abs{s_k^2+z}}\le\frac1x,\qquad \frac{s_k}{\abs{s_k^2+z}}\le\frac{s_k}{s_k^2+x}\le\frac{1}{2\sqrt x}. \]
The identities are algebraic and hold verbatim. Finally \(\abs z^2=x^2+(\Img z)^2\le\frac54x^2\).

Lemma SB.4 (Rank-two deletion)
Let \(M=M_0+\mathsf h\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v\) with \(M_0\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), \(\mathsf h\in\R\), \(\mathsf u\in\R^{\Nout}\), \(\mathsf v\in\R^{\Nin}\), and \(s>0\). Write \(R_0=R_s(M_0)\), \(\widetilde R_0=\widetilde R_s(M_0)\), \(\mathsf X_0=\mathsf X_s(M_0)\), \(R=R_s(M)\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.12} \mathsf U\defeq\mathsf u^\T R_0\mathsf u,\qquad \mathsf V\defeq\mathsf v^\T\widetilde R_0\mathsf v,\qquad \mathsf C\defeq\mathsf u^\T\mathsf X_0\mathsf v,\qquad \Lambda\defeq(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2+s\mathsf h^2\,\mathsf U\mathsf V . \end{equation}
Then \(\Lambda>0\), and with
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.13} \alpha_1\defeq\frac{\mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)}{\Lambda},\qquad \alpha_2\defeq\frac{\mathsf h^2\,\mathsf U}{\Lambda},\qquad \alpha_3\defeq-\frac{s\mathsf h^2\,\mathsf V}{\Lambda}, \end{equation}
one has
\begin{align} R-R_0&=\alpha_3\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(R_0\mathsf u)-\alpha_1\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v) -\alpha_1\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(R_0\mathsf u)+\alpha_2\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v) , \tag{SB.14}\\ \mathsf h\,RM\mathsf v&=-\alpha_3\,R_0\mathsf u+\alpha_1\,\mathsf X_0\mathsf v . \tag{SB.15} \end{align}
Moreover, if \(\Lambda'\) denotes the quantity (SB.12) computed from \(M\) in place of \(M_0\) and \(-\mathsf h\) in place of \(\mathsf h\), then \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\), and consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.16} \Lambda^{-1}\le\Big(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt s}\,\abs{\mathsf h}\,\mathsf N\Big)^2,\qquad \mathsf N\defeq\norm{\mathsf u}^2+\norm{\mathsf v}^2 . \end{equation}
The same holds at \(z\in\mathbb S\) in place of \(s\), with \(x\defeq\Ree z\), in the following form. Define \(R_0,\widetilde R_0,\mathsf X_0,R\) and \(\mathsf U,\mathsf V,\mathsf C,\Lambda,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) by the formulas above with \(z\) in place of \(s\); they are complex. Then \(\Lambda\ne0\), (SB.14) and (SB.15) hold, \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.17} \abs\Lambda^{-1}\le\Big(1+\frac{\abs{\mathsf h}\,\mathsf N}{\sqrt x}\Big)^2 . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
For \(K\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) let
\[ \mathcal L(K)\defeq\begin{pmatrix}s\Id_{\Nout}&K\\K^\T&-\Id_{\Nin}\end{pmatrix}. \]
Block inversion with the Schur complement \(s\Id+KK^\T\) of the block \(-\Id\) gives, for every \(K\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.18} \mathcal L(K)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}R_s(K)&\mathsf X_s(K)\\ \mathsf X_s(K)^\T&-\Id_{\Nin}+K^\T R_s(K)K\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}R_s(K)&\mathsf X_s(K)\\ \mathsf X_s(K)^\T&-s\widetilde R_s(K)\end{pmatrix}, \end{equation}
where the second form uses Lemma SB.3. The rank-one change of \(M\) is a rank-two change of \(\mathcal L\):
\[ \mathcal L(M)=\mathcal L(M_0)+\mathsf h\big(\tilde{\mathsf u}\tensor\tilde{\mathsf v}+\tilde{\mathsf v}\tensor\tilde{\mathsf u}\big)=\mathcal L(M_0)+PSP^\T, \]
with
\[ \tilde{\mathsf u}\defeq\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf u\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \tilde{\mathsf v}\defeq\begin{pmatrix}0\\ \mathsf v\end{pmatrix},\qquad P\defeq\big[\tilde{\mathsf u}\ \ \tilde{\mathsf v}\big],\qquad S\defeq\mathsf h\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \]
Assume first \(\mathsf h\ne0\). By (SB.18),
\[ W\defeq P^\T\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}P=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf U&\mathsf C\\ \mathsf C&-s\mathsf V\end{pmatrix},\qquad S^{-1}+W=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf U&\mathsf C+\mathsf h^{-1}\\ \mathsf C+\mathsf h^{-1}&-s\mathsf V\end{pmatrix}. \]
The determinant of \(S^{-1}+W\) is expressed through \(\Lambda\):
\[ \det(S^{-1}+W)=-s\mathsf U\mathsf V-\big(\mathsf C+\mathsf h^{-1}\big)^2 =-\frac{s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V+(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2}{\mathsf h^2}=-\frac{\Lambda}{\mathsf h^2}. \]
Positivity of \(\Lambda\) follows from the positive definiteness of \(R_0\) and \(\widetilde R_0\). Indeed \(\mathsf U\ge0\) and \(\mathsf V\ge0\), with \(\mathsf U=0\) only if \(\mathsf u=0\) and \(\mathsf V=0\) only if \(\mathsf v=0\). If \(\mathsf u=0\) or \(\mathsf v=0\), then \(\mathsf C\) vanishes and \(\Lambda=1\); otherwise \(\Lambda\ge s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V>0\). In particular \(S^{-1}+W\) is invertible, and the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury formula [87, Section 2.1.4] gives
\[ \mathcal L(M)^{-1}=\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}-\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}P\,\Theta\,P^\T\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}, \]
where
\[ \Theta\defeq(S^{-1}+W)^{-1}=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\begin{pmatrix}s\mathsf h^2\mathsf V&\mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)\\ \mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)&-\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_3&\alpha_1\\ \alpha_1&-\alpha_2\end{pmatrix}. \]
The two identities are read off from the blocks of the Woodbury identity. By (SB.18), the columns of \(\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}P\) are
\[ \mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}\tilde{\mathsf u}=\begin{pmatrix}R_0\mathsf u\\ \mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}\tilde{\mathsf v}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf X_0\mathsf v\\ -s\widetilde R_0\mathsf v\end{pmatrix}. \]
The upper-left block of the Woodbury identity is therefore
\[ R-R_0=-\big[R_0\mathsf u\ \ \mathsf X_0\mathsf v\big]\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_3&\alpha_1\\ \alpha_1&-\alpha_2\end{pmatrix}\big[R_0\mathsf u\ \ \mathsf X_0\mathsf v\big]^\T , \]
which, expanded, is (SB.14). The upper-right block is
\[ \begin{aligned} RM={}&\mathsf X_0+\alpha_3\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u)+s\alpha_1\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\widetilde R_0\mathsf v)\\ &-\alpha_1\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(\mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u)-s\alpha_2\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(\widetilde R_0\mathsf v) . \end{aligned} \]
Multiplying on the right by \(\mathsf v\) and using the definitions of \(\mathsf C\) and \(\mathsf V\) in (SB.12) gives
\[ RM\mathsf v=R_0\mathsf u\,(\alpha_3\mathsf C+s\alpha_1\mathsf V)+\mathsf X_0\mathsf v\,(1-\alpha_1\mathsf C-s\alpha_2\mathsf V). \]
The two scalar coefficients simplify by the definitions (SB.13):
\[ \alpha_3\mathsf C+s\alpha_1\mathsf V=\frac{s\mathsf h\mathsf V}{\Lambda}\big(-\mathsf h\mathsf C+1+\mathsf h\mathsf C\big)=\frac{s\mathsf h\mathsf V}{\Lambda}=-\frac{\alpha_3}{\mathsf h}, \]
\[ 1-\alpha_1\mathsf C-s\alpha_2\mathsf V=\frac{\Lambda-\mathsf h\mathsf C(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)-s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V}{\Lambda}=\frac{(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2-\mathsf h\mathsf C(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)}{\Lambda}=\frac{1+\mathsf h\mathsf C}{\Lambda}=\frac{\alpha_1}{\mathsf h}. \]
Multiplying \(RM\mathsf v\) by \(\mathsf h\) gives (SB.15). If \(\mathsf h=0\), then \(M=M_0\), so \(R=R_0\) and all three coefficients vanish; both identities hold.
The relation \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\) is a determinant identity. We factor
\[ \mathcal L(M)=\mathcal L(M_0)\big(\Id+\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}PSP^\T\big). \]
Sylvester’s identity \(\det(\Id+XY)=\det(\Id+YX)\) [88, Section 0.8.5], applied to the second factor, gives
\[ \begin{aligned} \det\mathcal L(M)&=\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,\det(\Id_2+SW) =\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,\det(S)\det(S^{-1}+W)\\ &=\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,(-\mathsf h^2)\Big(-\frac{\Lambda}{\mathsf h^2}\Big) =\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,\Lambda . \end{aligned} \]
Both determinants are nonzero, because the block inverse (SB.18) exists for every \(K\). Exchanging the roles of \(M\) and \(M_0\) and replacing \(\mathsf h\) by \(-\mathsf h\) is the same situation, because \(M_0=M-\mathsf h\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v\); it gives
\[ \det\mathcal L(M_0)=\det\mathcal L(M)\,\Lambda' , \]
and the two relations together give \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\).
The inverse bound follows from the norm bounds of Lemma SB.3 applied to \(M\). The primed quantities satisfy
\[ 0\le\mathsf U'\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf u}^2}{s},\qquad 0\le\mathsf V'\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf v}^2}{s},\qquad \abs{\mathsf C'}\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}}{2\sqrt s}\le\frac{\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}, \]
the last step by \(2\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}\le\mathsf N\). Using \((1+x)^2+y^2\le(1+x+y)^2\) for \(x,y\ge0\) in the last step,
\[ \Lambda^{-1}=\Lambda'=(1-\mathsf h\mathsf C')^2+s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U'\mathsf V' \le\Big(1+\frac{\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N}{2\sqrt s}\Big)^2 \le\Big(1+\frac{3\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}\Big)^2 . \]
At \(z\in\mathbb S\) the argument is the same with \(s\) replaced by \(z\), except for the nonvanishing of \(\Lambda\) and the inverse bound. The block inverse (SB.18) holds with \(z\) in place of \(s\), since \(z\Id+KK^\T\) is invertible by Lemma SB.3. Sylvester’s identity is used first: directly from the definitions,
\[ \Id_2+SW=\begin{pmatrix}1+\mathsf h\mathsf C&-z\mathsf h\mathsf V\\ \mathsf h\mathsf U&1+\mathsf h\mathsf C\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det(\Id_2+SW)=\Lambda , \]
so \(\det\mathcal L(M)=\Lambda\det\mathcal L(M_0)\) with both determinants nonzero, and \(\Lambda\ne0\). For \(\mathsf h\ne0\) this gives \(\det(S^{-1}+W)=-\Lambda/\mathsf h^2\ne0\), and the Woodbury step, the reading of the blocks and the relation \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\) are unchanged. For the inverse bound, Lemma SB.3 at \(z\) applied to \(M\) gives \(\abs{\mathsf U'}\le\norm{\mathsf u}^2/x\), \(\abs{\mathsf V'}\le\norm{\mathsf v}^2/x\) and \(\abs{\mathsf C'}\le\mathsf N/(4\sqrt x)\), and with \(\abs z\le\frac{\sqrt5}{2}x\) and \(\norm{\mathsf u}^2\norm{\mathsf v}^2\le\mathsf N^2/4\), writing \(t\defeq\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N/\sqrt x\),
\[ \abs\Lambda^{-1}=\abs{\Lambda'}\le\abs{1-\mathsf h\mathsf C'}^2+\abs z\,\mathsf h^2\abs{\mathsf U'}\abs{\mathsf V'} \le\Big(1+\frac t4\Big)^2+\frac{\sqrt5}{8}\,t^2\le(1+t)^2 , \]
the last step by \((1+a)^2+b^2\le(1+a+b)^2\) with \(a=t/4\), \(b=(\sqrt5/8)^{1/2}t\le\frac34t\).

Lemma SB.5 (Coefficient bounds)
Let \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) be compact with \(s_-=\min I\) and put \(C_I\defeq\max(1,s_-^{-1})\big(1+\tfrac{3}{4\sqrt{s_-}}\big)^3\). In the setting of Lemma SB.4 with \(\mathsf h=\varpi\,d\), \(0\le\varpi\le1\), \(d\in\R\), and \(s\in I\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.19} \abs{\alpha_1}\le C_I\,\varpi\,(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3,\qquad \abs{\alpha_2}+\abs{\alpha_3}\le C_I\,\varpi^2(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3 , \end{equation}
and the same bounds hold for \(\alpha_1/\varpi\) and \((\alpha_2,\alpha_3)/\varpi^2\) with the factors \(\varpi\), \(\varpi^2\) removed. Moreover \(R-R_0\) has rank at most two and \(\norm{R-R_0}_*\le4/s\).
Proof
Proof.
The three scalars of (SB.12) are bounded by the norm bounds of Lemma SB.3 applied to \(M_0\):
\[ 0\le\mathsf U\le\frac{\mathsf N}{s},\qquad 0\le s\mathsf V\le\mathsf N,\qquad \abs{\mathsf C}\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}}{2\sqrt s}\le\frac{\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}. \]
We also use \(\abs{\mathsf h}\le\abs d\), which holds because \(\varpi\le1\), the inverse bound (SB.16), and the elementary inequality
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.20} 1+c\abs d\mathsf N\le(1+c)(1+\abs d)(1+\mathsf N)\qquad(c\ge0). \end{equation}
For \(\alpha_1\) these give
\[ \abs{\alpha_1}=\frac{\abs{\mathsf h}\,\abs{1+\mathsf h\mathsf C}}{\Lambda} \le \varpi\abs d\Big(1+\frac{\abs d\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{3\abs d\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}\Big)^2 \le \varpi\abs d\Big(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt{s_-}}\Big)^3(1+\abs d)^3(1+\mathsf N)^3 , \]
the first inequality by (SB.16) and \(\abs{\mathsf C}\le\mathsf N/(4\sqrt s)\), the second by (SB.20) applied to each factor with \(s\ge s_-\). For \(\alpha_2\) and \(\alpha_3\) the two numerators are bounded together. Since \(s\mathsf U\le\norm{\mathsf u}^2\) and \(s\mathsf V\le\norm{\mathsf v}^2\),
\[ \mathsf U+s\mathsf V\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf u}^2}{s}+\norm{\mathsf v}^2\le\max(1,s_-^{-1})\,\mathsf N . \]
Since \(\alpha_2\ge0\ge\alpha_3\), the first inequality below follows from this bound, (SB.16) and \(\abs{\mathsf h}\le\abs d\), and the second from (SB.20):
\[ \begin{aligned} \abs{\alpha_2}+\abs{\alpha_3}&=\frac{\mathsf h^2(\mathsf U+s\mathsf V)}{\Lambda}\\ &\le\max(1,s_-^{-1})\,\varpi^2d^2\,\mathsf N\Big(1+\frac{3\abs d\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}\Big)^2\\ &\le\max(1,s_-^{-1})\Big(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt{s_-}}\Big)^2\varpi^2(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3 . \end{aligned} \]
Since \(\abs d\le1+\abs d\) and \(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt{s_-}}\ge1\), both right sides are at most \(C_I\) times \(\varpi(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3\), respectively \(\varpi^2(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3\), with the stated \(C_I\). The bounds for the normalized coefficients are the same inequalities with the factor \(\varpi\) or \(\varpi^2\) of \(\mathsf h\) or \(\mathsf h^2\) cancelled before bounding \(\abs{\mathsf h}\) by \(\abs d\).
The rank claim follows from (SB.14), whose right side has range in the span of \(R_0\mathsf u\) and \(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v\). For a matrix of rank at most two the nuclear norm is at most twice the operator norm, so
\[ \norm{R-R_0}_*\le2\norm{R-R_0}\le2\big(\norm R+\norm{R_0}\big)\le\frac4s . \qedhere \]

Lemma SB.6 (Gaussian moment bounds)
Let \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\), \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) be independent, let \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), let \(\Ahat\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) with \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\), and put \(\mathsf u=\Sigmaout^{1/2}z/\sqrt B\), \(\mathsf v=\Sigmain^{1/2}w/\sqrt B\), \(\mathsf N=\norm{\mathsf u}^2+\norm{\mathsf v}^2\) and \(d=z^\T\Ahat w\). Then for every integer \(p\ge1\)
\[ \begin{gathered} \norm{\mathsf N}_p\le2C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\big((2p-1)!!\big)^{1/p},\qquad \norm{d}_{2p}\le\big((2p-1)!!\big)^{1/p},\\ \big\|\abs d\,\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}\big\|_2\le 15^{2/3}\,C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\le 7\,C_\Sigma\gamma_\star . \end{gathered} \]
Also, for any symmetric \(A\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nout}\),
\[ \EE[z^\T Az]=\Tr A,\qquad \Var(z^\T Az)=2\norm{A}_\F^2 . \]
Proof
Proof.
The bound on \(\mathsf N\) reduces to the moments of \(\norm z^2\). The covariance bound gives
\[ \norm{\mathsf u}^2=\frac{z^\T\Sigmaout z}{B}\le\frac{C_\Sigma\norm z^2}{B}, \]
and Jensen’s inequality for the convex function \(x\mapsto x^p\), applied to the average of the \(\Nout\) coordinates, gives
\[ \EE\Big(\frac{\norm z^2}{\Nout}\Big)^p\le\frac1{\Nout}\sum_{i=1}^{\Nout}\EE z_i^{2p}=(2p-1)!! . \]
Together,
\[ \norm{\norm{\mathsf u}^2}_p\le C_\Sigma\,\frac{\Nout}{B}\,\big((2p-1)!!\big)^{1/p}\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\big((2p-1)!!\big)^{1/p}, \]
the same bound holds for \(\norm{\mathsf v}^2\), and the triangle inequality in \(L^p\) gives the bound on \(\mathsf N\).
The bound on \(d\) is Lemma SA.7(i).
The bound on the product is the generalized Hölder inequality with three exponents equal to \(3\):
\[ \EE\big[d^2\norm{\mathsf u}^2\norm{\mathsf v}^2\big]\le\norm{d^2}_3\,\norm{\norm{\mathsf u}^2}_3\,\norm{\norm{\mathsf v}^2}_3 \le15^{2/3}\cdot\big(15^{1/3}C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\big)^2=15^{4/3}(C_\Sigma\gamma_\star)^2 , \]
where the second inequality uses the bounds on \(d\) and on \(\norm{\mathsf u}^2\), \(\norm{\mathsf v}^2\) with \(p=3\):
\[ \norm{d^2}_3=\norm d_6^2\le(5!!)^{2/3},\qquad \norm{\norm{\mathsf u}^2}_3,\ \norm{\norm{\mathsf v}^2}_3\le(5!!)^{1/3}C_\Sigma\gamma_\star . \]
Taking the square root gives the bound \(15^{2/3}C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\), which is at most \(7C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\).
The quadratic form is diagonalized by an orthogonal \(O\) with \(A=O\diag(a)O^\T\):
\[ z^\T Az=\sum_ia_i\,(O^\T z)_i^2 , \]
a sum of independent terms with means \(a_i\) and variances \(2a_i^2\), since \(O^\T z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) and a standard Gaussian square has variance \(2\). Summing over \(i\) gives
\[ \EE[z^\T Az]=\sum_ia_i=\Tr A,\qquad \Var(z^\T Az)=2\sum_ia_i^2=2\norm A_\F^2 . \qedhere \]

Lemma SB.7 (Bessel bounds)
Let \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) and \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) be independent of each other and of a \(\sigma\)-field \(\mathcal G\), and let \(e\) be a real or complex random variable depending on \(z\), \(w\) and \(\mathcal G\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.21} \begin{gathered} \norm{\EE_{\mathcal G}[(z\tensor w)\,e]}_\F\le\norm{e}_{2,\mathcal G},\qquad \norm{\EE_{\mathcal G}[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,e]}_\F\le\sqrt2\,\norm{e}_{2,\mathcal G},\\ \norm{\EE_{\mathcal G}[(w\tensor w-\Id)\,e]}_\F\le\sqrt2\,\norm{e}_{2,\mathcal G}. \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Since \((z,w)\) is independent of \(\mathcal G\), it suffices to prove the inequalities with \(\EE\) in place of \(\EE_{\mathcal G}\), in the \(L^2\) space of \((z,w)\).
The inequalities are Bessel’s inequality for two orthonormal families. By the Gaussian fourth-moment identity (SA.30) and the independence of \(z\) and \(w\), the products \(z_iw_l\) (\(i\le\Nout\), \(l\le\Nin\)) form an orthonormal family in \(L^2\), and the variables \((z_i^2-1)/\sqrt2\) (\(i\le\Nout\)) and \(z_iz_k\) (\(i<k\)) form another orthonormal family.
Bessel’s inequality for the first family gives the first bound:
\[ \norm{\EE[(z\tensor w)\,e]}_\F^2=\sum_{i,l}\abs{\EE[z_iw_le]}^2\le\EE\abs e^2 . \]
Bessel’s inequality for the second family gives
\[ \sum_{i<k}\abs{\EE[z_iz_ke]}^2+\sum_i\frac{\abs{\EE[(z_i^2-1)e]}^2}{2}\le\EE\abs e^2 . \]
Each off-diagonal entry of the symmetric matrix \(\EE[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,e]\) occurs twice in its squared Frobenius norm, so
\[ \norm{\EE[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,e]}_\F^2=2\sum_{i<k}\abs{\EE[z_iz_ke]}^2+\sum_i\abs{\EE[(z_i^2-1)e]}^2\le2\,\EE\abs e^2 , \]
which is the second bound. The third bound is the second with \(w\) in place of \(z\).

Lemma SB.8 (Gaussian response)
Let \(A\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic, \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\), \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) independent, and \(d=z^\T Aw\). Let \(f\in C^3(\R)\) and \(\psi\in C^2(\R)\) have polynomially bounded derivatives up to the stated orders. Then
\begin{align} \EE[(z\tensor w)\,f(d)]&=A\,\EE f'(d)+A\,\EE[(w\tensor w)\,f'''(d)]\,A^\T A, \tag{SB.22}\\ \norm{\EE[(z\tensor w)\,f(d)]-A\,\EE f'(d)}_\F&\le\norm{f'''}_\infty\norm A^2\norm A_\F, \notag\\ \EE[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,\psi(d)]&=A\,\EE[(w\tensor w)\,\psi''(d)]\,A^\T, \tag{SB.23}\\ \norm{\EE[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,\psi(d)]}_\F&\le\norm{\psi''}_\infty\norm A\norm A_\F, \notag \end{align}
and both (SB.22) and (SB.23) hold with \((z,w,A)\) replaced by \((w,z,A^\T)\).
Proof
Proof.
The tool is Stein’s lemma [96, Lemma 1]: for \(g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) and an absolutely continuous \(\phi\) with \(\EE\abs{\phi'(g)}<\infty\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.24} \EE[g\,\phi(g)]=\EE\phi'(g). \end{equation}
We apply it to one coordinate of \(z\) or \(w\) at a time, conditionally on all other coordinates. The partial derivatives of \(d=z^\T Aw\) are
\[ \partial_{z_i}d=(Aw)_i,\qquad \partial_{w_l}d=(A^\T z)_l . \]
The first identity is obtained by three applications of (SB.24). Applying it to the coordinate \(z_i\) gives
\[ \EE[z_iw_lf(d)]=\EE\big[w_l\,\partial_{z_i}f(d)\big]=\EE\big[w_l\,(Aw)_i\,f'(d)\big]. \]
Applying it to the coordinate \(w_l\), with the product rule, gives
\[ \EE\big[w_l\,(Aw)_i\,f'(d)\big]=\EE\big[\partial_{w_l}\big((Aw)_if'(d)\big)\big] =A_{il}\,\EE f'(d)+\EE\big[(Aw)_i\,(A^\T z)_l\,f''(d)\big]. \]
In matrix form the two steps read
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.25} \EE[(z\tensor w)\,f(d)]=A\,\EE f'(d)+A\,\EE[(w\tensor z)\,f''(d)]\,A . \end{equation}
Applying (SB.24) to the coordinate \(z_l\) inside the remaining expectation gives
\[ \EE[w_kz_lf''(d)]=\EE\big[w_k\,(Aw)_l\,f'''(d)\big],\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad \EE[(w\tensor z)\,f''(d)]=\EE[(w\tensor w)\,f'''(d)]\,A^\T . \]
Substituting this into (SB.25) proves (SB.22).
The bound for the first identity has two steps. The matrix \(N\defeq\EE[(w\tensor w)\,f'''(d)]\) satisfies
\[ \norm N=\sup_{\norm x=\norm y=1}\big|\EE\big[(x^\T w)(y^\T w)f'''(d)\big]\big| \le\norm{f'''}_\infty\sup_{\norm x=\norm y=1}\EE\big[\abs{x^\T w}\,\abs{y^\T w}\big]\le\norm{f'''}_\infty , \]
the last step by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and \(\EE(x^\T w)^2=\norm x^2\). Then
\[ \norm{A\,N\,A^\T A}_\F\le\norm A\,\norm N\,\norm{A^\T A}_\F\le\norm A\,\norm N\,\norm A\,\norm A_\F \le\norm{f'''}_\infty\norm A^2\norm A_\F , \]
using \(\norm{XY}_\F\le\norm X\norm Y_\F\) twice.
The second identity is obtained by two applications of (SB.24). Applying it to the coordinate \(z_i\) of \(z_i\cdot z_k\psi(d)\) gives
\[ \EE[z_iz_k\psi(d)]=\EE\big[\partial_{z_i}\big(z_k\psi(d)\big)\big] =\delta_{ik}\,\EE\psi(d)+\EE\big[z_k\,(Aw)_i\,\psi'(d)\big], \]
that is, in matrix form,
\[ \EE[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,\psi(d)]=A\,\EE[(w\tensor z)\,\psi'(d)]. \]
The step from \(\EE[(w\tensor z)\,f''(d)]\) to \(\EE[(w\tensor w)\,f'''(d)]A^\T\) above, applied with \(\psi'\) in place of \(f''\), gives \(\EE[(w\tensor z)\,\psi'(d)]=\EE[(w\tensor w)\,\psi''(d)]A^\T\), which proves (SB.23). Its bound is the same two-step estimate with \(\norm{\psi''}_\infty\) in place of \(\norm{f'''}_\infty\) and one factor of \(\norm A\) fewer:
\[ \norm{A\,\EE[(w\tensor w)\,\psi''(d)]\,A^\T}_\F\le\norm A\,\norm{\psi''}_\infty\,\norm{A^\T}_\F=\norm{\psi''}_\infty\norm A\norm A_\F . \]
The last claim follows from the symmetry \(d=w^\T A^\T z\): the same computations with the roles of \(z\) and \(w\) exchanged and \(A\) replaced by \(A^\T\) give both identities and both bounds for \((w,z,A^\T)\).

Lemma SB.9 (Nuclear-norm Lipschitz bound)
For \(K,K'\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) and \(s>0\), \(\norm{R_s(K)-R_s(K')}_*\le2s^{-3/2}\norm{K-K'}_*\).
Proof
Proof.
The resolvent \(R_s(K)\) is a block of the resolvent of a symmetric matrix at a point at distance \(\sqrt s\) from the real axis. Let
\[ \mathcal D(K)\defeq\begin{pmatrix}0&K\\K^\T&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathcal Y(K)\defeq\big(\mathcal D(K)-i\sqrt s\,\Id\big)^{-1}. \]
Since \(\mathcal D(K)\) is real symmetric, its spectrum is real, and \(\norm{\mathcal Y(K)}\le s^{-1/2}\). Block inversion with the Schur complement of the lower-right block \(-i\sqrt s\,\Id\) shows that the upper-left \(\Nout\times\Nout\) block of \(\mathcal Y(K)\) is
\[ \Big(-i\sqrt s\,\Id-K\big(-i\sqrt s\big)^{-1}K^\T\Big)^{-1} =\Big(-\frac{i}{\sqrt s}\big(KK^\T+s\Id\big)\Big)^{-1}=i\sqrt s\,R_s(K). \]
The difference of the two dilated resolvents is controlled by the resolvent identity:
\[ \mathcal Y(K)-\mathcal Y(K')=\mathcal Y(K)\,\mathcal D(K'-K)\,\mathcal Y(K'). \]
The nuclear norm is unitarily invariant, so the ideal property \(\norm{XYZ}_*\le\norm X\,\norm Y_*\,\norm Z\) [81, Proposition IV.2.4 and (IV.40)] applies; the singular values of \(\mathcal D(E)\) are those of \(E\), each taken twice, so \(\norm{\mathcal D(E)}_*=2\norm E_*\). Hence
\[ \norm{\mathcal Y(K)-\mathcal Y(K')}_*\le\norm{\mathcal Y(K)}\,\norm{\mathcal D(K'-K)}_*\,\norm{\mathcal Y(K')} \le\frac{2}{s}\,\norm{K-K'}_* . \]
Compression to the upper-left block does not increase the nuclear norm, by the same ideal property with the orthogonal projection \(P\) onto the first \(\Nout\) coordinates in place of \(X\) and \(Z\). Therefore
\[ \sqrt s\,\norm{R_s(K)-R_s(K')}_*=\norm{P\big(\mathcal Y(K)-\mathcal Y(K')\big)P}_*\le\frac2s\,\norm{K-K'}_* , \]
which is the claim after division by \(\sqrt s\).

SB.3 The Gram closure

In the frozen-state model the buffer is a series over samples,
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.26} \Mt=\sum_{j\ge0}\sum_{a=1}^B\mathsf h_{ja}\,\mathsf u_{ja}\tensor\mathsf v_{ja},\qquad \mathsf h_{ja}\defeq\emaw{j}d_{ja},\quad \mathsf u_{ja}\defeq\frac{\Sigmaout^{1/2}z_{ja}}{\sqrt B},\quad \mathsf v_{ja}\defeq\frac{\Sigmain^{1/2}w_{ja}}{\sqrt B}, \end{equation}
and, with \(z=-s\) and the notation (SB.10), the Gram resolvents are
\[ R(-s)=R_s(\Mt),\qquad \widetilde R(-s)=\widetilde R_s(\Mt). \]
The results of this subsection are stated for a fixed weight sequence \(w=(\emaw j)_{j\ge0}\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.27} 0\le\emaw j\le1\quad(j\ge0),\qquad \sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j\le1,\qquad \emaw j>0\ \text{for some }j , \end{equation}
where the frozen-state model with weights \(w\) is Definition SA.3 with \(\emaw j\) in place of the EMA weight of age \(j\) in (SA.26). The EMA weights satisfy (SB.27) by (SA.18), and the truncated weights of the drift section do as well. Under (SB.27) one has \(\sw2\le1\) and \(\sw3\le1\), and these two sums are the only way the weights enter the constants below.
Theorem SB.2 (Gram closure)
Under Assumption S3, in the frozen-state model with a weight sequence \(w\) satisfying (SB.27), let \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)(z)\) be the solution of the weighted system (SB.2) for the weights \(w\) and the spectral measures (SA.41), and let \(\Rbar,\Rtbar\) be as in (SB.3). Then for every compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.28} \sup_{s\in I}\Big(\norm{\EE R(-s)-\Rbar(-s)}+\norm{\EE\widetilde R(-s)-\Rtbar(-s)}\Big)\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
Corollary SB.1 (Trace closure with bounded tests)
Under the hypotheses of Theorem SB.2, let \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) be compact and let \(T=T_B\in\mathbb{C}^{\Nout\times\Nout}\) and \(\widetilde T=\widetilde T_B\in\mathbb{C}^{\Nin\times\Nin}\) be deterministic with \(\norm{T_B},\norm{\widetilde T_B}\le C_T\) for all \(B\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.29} \EE\sup_{s\in I}\Big|\frac1{\Nout}\Tr\big[T\big(R(-s)-\Rbar(-s)\big)\big]\Big|^2\longrightarrow0,\qquad \EE\sup_{s\in I}\Big|\frac1{\Nin}\Tr\big[\widetilde T\big(\widetilde R(-s)-\Rtbar(-s)\big)\big]\Big|^2\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
In particular, in mean square and uniformly on compact \(s\)-intervals,
\[ \frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout R(-s)\big)\longrightarrow\sigma(-s),\qquad \frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmain\widetilde R(-s)\big)\longrightarrow\tilde\sigma(-s). \]
The proof of Theorem SB.2 occupies the rest of the subsection. We fix a compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\), write \(s_-=\min I\), and let \(C_I\) denote constants depending only on \((I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\). Lemma SB.10, Lemma SB.11 and Lemma SB.12 are finite-dimensional: they hold at every dimension in the frozen-state model with a weight sequence in the class (SB.27), \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\), \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), with no condition on \(\norm{\Ahat}\), and their constants \(C_I\) depend on nothing else. For a sample \((j,a)\) we write \(M_{0}^{(ja)}\defeq\Mt-\mathsf h_{ja}\,\mathsf u_{ja}\tensor\mathsf v_{ja}\) for the buffer with that sample deleted, \(R_0^{(ja)}=R_s(M_0^{(ja)})\), \(\widetilde R_0^{(ja)}\), \(\mathsf X_0^{(ja)}\) accordingly, and \(\mathsf U_{ja},\mathsf V_{ja},\mathsf C_{ja},\Lambda_{ja},\alpha_{1,ja},\alpha_{2,ja},\alpha_{3,ja}\) for the quantities of Lemma SB.4 with \((M_0,\mathsf h,\mathsf u,\mathsf v)=(M_0^{(ja)},\mathsf h_{ja},\mathsf u_{ja},\mathsf v_{ja})\). The deleted buffer is independent of \((z_{ja},w_{ja})\), and \(\mathcal G_{ja}\) denotes the \(\sigma\)-field generated by all samples other than \((j,a)\). Since samples of the same age are exchangeable, the law of \((M_0^{(ja)},z_{ja},w_{ja})\) does not depend on \(a\), and we drop \(a\) from the notation when only the law matters. Finally we define the expected resolvents and traces
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.30} \begin{gathered} \mathcal P(s)\defeq\EE R(-s),\qquad \mathcal P_j(s)\defeq\EE R_0^{(j1)},\\ \sigma_B(s)\defeq\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout\mathcal P(s)\big),\qquad \tilde\sigma_B(s)\defeq\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmain\,\EE\widetilde R(-s)\big), \end{gathered} \end{equation}
and \(q_B(s)\defeq s\sigma_B(s)\tilde\sigma_B(s)\). Since \(\norm{R(-s)}\le1/s\) and \(\Tr\Sigmaout\le C_\Sigma\Nout\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.31} 0\le\sigma_B,\tilde\sigma_B\le\frac{C_\Sigma\gamma_\star}{s},\qquad 0\le q_B(s)\le q_I\defeq\frac{(C_\Sigma\gamma_\star)^2}{s_-}\qquad(s\in I). \end{equation}
Lemma SB.10 (Variance of normalized traces)
Let \(T\in\mathbb{C}^{\Nout\times\Nout}\) be deterministic with \(\norm T\le C_T\) and put \(Y(s)\defeq\frac1B\Tr\big(TR(-s)\big)\). Then \(\Var Y(s)\le C_IC_T^2\,\sw2/B\) for \(s\in I\). The same bound holds for \(\frac1B\Tr(\widetilde TR_s(\Mt^\T))\) with \(\norm{\widetilde T}\le C_T\), and for the traces of the resolvents of any buffer with one sample deleted.
Proof
Proof.
The variance is controlled by the Efron–Stein inequality for a truncated history, and the truncation is removed by an \(L^2\) limit. For \(J\ge0\) let
\[ M^{(J)}\defeq\sum_{j\le J}\sum_{a=1}^B\mathsf h_{ja}\,\mathsf u_{ja}\tensor\mathsf v_{ja},\qquad Y^{(J)}\defeq\frac1B\Tr\big(TR_s(M^{(J)})\big), \]
so that \(Y^{(J)}\) is a function of the \(B(J+1)\) independent samples of ages \(0,\dots,J\).
The effect of one replaced sample is bounded through the nuclear norm. Replacing the sample \((j,a)\) by an independent copy \((d',\mathsf u',\mathsf v')\) produces \(Y^{(J),(ja)'}\) and changes \(M^{(J)}\) by the rank-two matrix \(\emaw j(d\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v-d'\,\mathsf u'\tensor\mathsf v')\), whose nuclear norm is at most \(\emaw j(\abs d\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}+\abs{d'}\norm{\mathsf u'}\norm{\mathsf v'})\). By trace duality \(\abs{\Tr(TX)}\le\norm T\norm X_*\) and Lemma SB.9,
\[ \big|Y^{(J)}-Y^{(J),(ja)'}\big|\le\frac{C_T}{B}\cdot\frac{2}{s^{3/2}}\,\emaw j\big(\abs d\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}+\abs{d'}\norm{\mathsf u'}\norm{\mathsf v'}\big), \]
and by Lemma SB.6 and the triangle inequality in \(L^2\),
\[ \big\|Y^{(J)}-Y^{(J),(ja)'}\big\|_2\le\frac{C_T}{B}\cdot\frac{2}{s_-^{3/2}}\,\emaw j\cdot2\cdot7C_\Sigma\gamma_\star=\frac{C_IC_T\,\emaw j}{B}. \]
The Efron–Stein inequality [84, Theorem 3.1] then gives
\[ \Var Y^{(J)}\le\frac12\sum_{j\le J}\sum_{a=1}^B\EE\big[(Y^{(J)}-Y^{(J),(ja)'})^2\big] \le\frac12\sum_{j\le J}B\,\frac{C_I^2C_T^2\emaw j^2}{B^2}\le\frac{C_I^2C_T^2\sw2}{2B}. \]
The truncation is removed at fixed dimension. By Lemma SB.9, trace duality, and \(\norm E_*\le\sqrt{\min(\Nout,\Nin)}\norm E_\F\),
\[ \big|Y-Y^{(J)}\big|\le\frac{C_T}{B}\cdot\frac{2}{s^{3/2}}\sqrt{\min(\Nout,\Nin)}\,\norm{\Mt-M^{(J)}}_\F , \]
and \(\EE\norm{\Mt-M^{(J)}}_\F^2\to0\) as \(J\to\infty\). Indeed, the batches of different ages are independent with the common mean \(\EE\Gt\) and the common Frobenius variance \(\mathsf V_B\) of Lemma SA.4, so
\[ \EE\norm{\Mt-M^{(J)}}_\F^2=\Big(\sum_{j>J}\emaw j\Big)^2\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F^2+\sum_{j>J}\emaw j^2\,\mathsf V_B , \]
and both tail sums vanish as \(J\to\infty\) because \(\sum_j\emaw j\le1\). (For the EMA weights this is Lemma SA.6(i).) Hence \(Y^{(J)}\to Y\) in \(L^2\), and
\[ \Var Y=\lim_{J\to\infty}\Var Y^{(J)}\le\frac{C_I^2C_T^2\sw2}{2B}. \]
The input side is the same argument for \(\Mt^\T=\sum_{j,a}\mathsf h_{ja}\,\mathsf v_{ja}\tensor\mathsf u_{ja}\), and deleting one sample removes one term from the Efron–Stein sum.

Lemma SB.11 (Concentration of the deleted-sample scalars)
For every age \(j\) and \(s\in I\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.32} \norm{\mathsf U_{j}-\sigma_B(s)}_2+\norm{\mathsf V_{j}-\tilde\sigma_B(s)}_2+\norm{\mathsf C_{j}}_2\le\frac{C_I}{\sqrt B}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Write \(R_0=R_0^{(j1)}\), \(\mathsf u=\mathsf u_{j1}\), and so on. Decompose
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathsf U-\sigma_B={}&\Big(\mathsf u^\T R_0\mathsf u-\frac{\Tr(\Sigmaout R_0)}{B}\Big) +\frac{\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(R_0-R(-s))\big)}{B}\\ &+\Big(\frac{\Tr(\Sigmaout R(-s))}{B}-\EE\frac{\Tr(\Sigmaout R(-s))}{B}\Big). \end{aligned} \]
The first term is a centered Gaussian quadratic form given \(\mathcal G_{j1}\). Indeed
\[ \mathsf u^\T R_0\mathsf u=z^\T\Big(\frac{\Sigmaout^{1/2}R_0\Sigmaout^{1/2}}{B}\Big)z . \]
Since \(R_0\) is built from the samples other than \((j,1)\), of which \(z\) is independent, Lemma SB.6 gives the conditional mean \(\Tr(\Sigmaout R_0)/B\) and the conditional variance
\[ \frac{2\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}R_0\Sigmaout^{1/2}}_\F^2}{B^2}\le\frac{2\Nout\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}R_0\Sigmaout^{1/2}}^2}{B^2} \le\frac{2\Nout C_\Sigma^2}{B^2s^2}\le\frac{2\gamma_\star C_\Sigma^2}{Bs_-^2}, \]
using \(\norm X_\F^2\le\Nout\norm X^2\), \(\norm{R_0}\le1/s\) and \(\Nout\le\gamma_\star B\).
The second term is a rank-two correction. By trace duality and Lemma SB.5,
\[ \frac{\big|\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(R_0-R(-s))\big)\big|}{B}\le\frac{\norm{\Sigmaout}\,\norm{R_0-R(-s)}_*}{B}\le\frac{4C_\Sigma}{Bs_-}. \]

Adding the three bounds gives \(\norm{\mathsf U-\sigma_B}_2\le C_I/\sqrt B\). The bound for \(\mathsf V\) is the same argument with \(\Sigmain\), \(\widetilde R_0\) and the input-side statements of Lemma SB.5 and Lemma SB.10.
The bound for \(\mathsf C\) is a conditional second-moment computation. We write
\[ \mathsf C=\mathsf u^\T\mathsf X_0\mathsf v=\frac{z^\T\big(\Sigmaout^{1/2}\mathsf X_0\Sigmain^{1/2}\big)w}{B}. \]
The matrix \(\mathsf X_0\) is built from the samples other than \((j,1)\), and \(\EE[(z^\T Aw)^2]=\norm A_\F^2\) for deterministic \(A\). With \(\norm A_\F^2\le\operatorname{rank}(A)\norm A^2\) and the bound \(\norm{\mathsf X_0}\le(2\sqrt s)^{-1}\) of Lemma SB.3, this gives
\[ \EE_{\mathcal G_{j1}}\mathsf C^2=\frac{\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\mathsf X_0\Sigmain^{1/2}}_\F^2}{B^2} \le\frac{C_\Sigma^2\min(\Nout,\Nin)\norm{\mathsf X_0}^2}{B^2}\le\frac{C_\Sigma^2\gamma_\star}{4Bs_-}. \]
Taking expectations and square roots gives \(\norm{\mathsf C}_2\le C_I/\sqrt B\).

Lemma SB.12 (Replacement of the deletion coefficients)
For \(x\ge0\) put \(f_x(d)\defeq\frac{d}{1+xd^2}\) and \(\psi_x(d)\defeq\frac{d^2}{1+xd^2}\), and for a sample of age \(j\) and \(s\in I\) define the target coefficients
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.33} \check\alpha_{1,j}\defeq\emaw j\,f_{\emaw j^2q_B}(d_j),\qquad \check\alpha_{2,j}\defeq\emaw j^2\,\sigma_B\,\psi_{\emaw j^2q_B}(d_j),\qquad \check\alpha_{3,j}\defeq-s\,\emaw j^2\,\tilde\sigma_B\,\psi_{\emaw j^2q_B}(d_j). \end{equation}
There is a sequence \(e_B\to0\), depending only on \((I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\), such that in every frozen-state model with the hypotheses stated before Lemma SB.10, whatever its weights, its dimension and its normalized state \(\Ahat\), for every age \(j\) and every \(s\in I\)
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.34} \norm{\alpha_{1,j}-\check\alpha_{1,j}}_2\le\emaw j\,e_B,\qquad \norm{\alpha_{2,j}-\check\alpha_{2,j}}_2\le\emaw j^2\,e_B,\qquad \norm{\alpha_{3,j}-\check\alpha_{3,j}}_2\le\emaw j^2\,e_B . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Fix \(j\) with \(\emaw j>0\) (for \(\emaw j=0\) both sides of (SB.34) vanish) and \(s\in I\), and abbreviate \(\varpi=\emaw j\), \(d=d_j\), \(\mathsf h=\varpi d\) and
\[ \check D\defeq1+\varpi^2q_Bd^2\ge1 . \]
We treat \(\alpha_1\) in full; the other two coefficients are handled at the end by the same argument with different numerators.
The deviation of the deleted-sample scalars from their expected values is collected in one random variable,
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.35} \Xi\defeq s\sigma_B\abs{\tilde\sigma_B-\mathsf V}+s\mathsf V\abs{\sigma_B-\mathsf U}+(1+q_B)\abs{\mathsf C}+\mathsf C^2 , \end{equation}
whose expectation is small. Indeed \(s\sigma_B\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\) by (SB.31), and \(\norm{s\mathsf V}_2\le\norm{\norm{\mathsf v}^2}_2\le C_I\) by Lemma SB.3 and Lemma SB.6; so the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Lemma SB.11 give
\[ \EE\,\Xi\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\norm{\tilde\sigma_B-\mathsf V}_2+\norm{s\mathsf V}_2\norm{\sigma_B-\mathsf U}_2+(1+q_I)\norm{\mathsf C}_2+\norm{\mathsf C}_2^2\le\frac{C_I}{\sqrt B}. \]
The numerators of the differences are linear in \(\Xi\) on bounded residuals. Using
\[ q_B-s\mathsf U\mathsf V=s\sigma_B(\tilde\sigma_B-\mathsf V)+s\mathsf V(\sigma_B-\mathsf U), \]
direct expansion of \(\Lambda=(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2+s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V\) gives
\begin{align} (1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)\check D-\Lambda &=\varpi^2d^2\,(q_B-s\mathsf U\mathsf V)+\varpi^3d^3\mathsf Cq_B-\varpi d\mathsf C-\varpi^2d^2\mathsf C^2, \tag{SB.36}\\ \Lambda-\check D&=2\varpi d\mathsf C+\varpi^2d^2\mathsf C^2+\varpi^2d^2\,(s\mathsf U\mathsf V-q_B). \tag{SB.37} \end{align}
Fix a level \(\mathsf m>0\), which is sent to infinity at the end of the argument. Since \(\varpi\le1\) and \(q_B\le q_I\), on the event \(\{\abs d\le\mathsf m\}\) both right sides are bounded in absolute value by \((1+\mathsf m)^3\,\Xi\).
The normalized difference is small in probability. Let
\[ Z\defeq\frac{\alpha_1}{\varpi}-\frac{\check\alpha_1}{\varpi} =\frac{d(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)}{\Lambda}-\frac{d}{\check D} =\frac{d\,\big[(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)\check D-\Lambda\big]}{\Lambda \check D}, \]
and let \(E\defeq\{\abs d\le\mathsf m\}\cap\{\Lambda\ge1/2\}\). On \(E\), since \(\check D\ge1\) and by (SB.36),
\[ \abs Z\le\frac{\abs d\,\abs{(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)\check D-\Lambda}}{\Lambda}\le2\mathsf m(1+\mathsf m)^3\,\Xi . \]
On the complement, either \(\abs d>\mathsf m\), or \(\abs d\le\mathsf m\) and \(\Lambda<1/2\le \check D-1/2\), so that \(\abs{\Lambda-\check D}>1/2\). Chebyshev’s inequality with \(\EE d^2=1\), Markov’s inequality and (SB.37) give
\[ \Pr(E^c)\le\Pr(\abs d>\mathsf m)+\Pr\big(\abs d\le\mathsf m,\ \abs{\Lambda-\check D}>1/2\big) \le\mathsf m^{-2}+2(1+\mathsf m)^3\,\EE\,\Xi . \]
Combining the two bounds, for every \(a>0\),
\[ \Pr(\abs Z>a)\le\Pr(E^c)+\Pr\big(E,\ 2\mathsf m(1+\mathsf m)^3\Xi>a\big) \le\mathsf m^{-2}+2(1+\mathsf m)^3\,\EE\,\Xi+\frac{2\mathsf m(1+\mathsf m)^3\,\EE\,\Xi}{a}. \]
Convergence in probability is upgraded to \(L^2\) by a uniform fourth moment. By Lemma SB.5 with the factor \(\varpi\) removed and Lemma SB.6, \(\norm{\alpha_1/\varpi}_4\le C_I\), and \(\norm{\check\alpha_1/\varpi}_4\le\norm d_4\le C_I\); hence \(\norm Z_4\le C_I\). Splitting at level \(a\) and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the second piece,
\[ \begin{aligned} \EE Z^2&=\EE\big[Z^2\mathbf 1_{\abs Z\le a}\big]+\EE\big[Z^2\mathbf 1_{\abs Z>a}\big] \le a^2+\norm Z_4^2\,\Pr(\abs Z>a)^{1/2}\\ &\le a^2+C_I\Big(\mathsf m^{-2}+C_I(1+\mathsf m)^4\big(1+a^{-1}\big)B^{-1/2}\Big)^{1/2}, \end{aligned} \]
where the last step uses \(\EE\,\Xi\le C_IB^{-1/2}\) and \(\mathsf m\le1+\mathsf m\). The right side does not depend on \(j\) or on \(s\in I\). Taking first \(B\to\infty\), then \(\mathsf m\to\infty\) and then \(a\downarrow0\) shows that
\[ \sup_{j,\ s\in I}\frac{\norm{\alpha_{1,j}-\check\alpha_{1,j}}_2}{\emaw j}\longrightarrow0 . \]
The other two coefficients follow by the same argument. The normalized differences are
\[ Z_2\defeq\frac{\alpha_2-\check\alpha_2}{\varpi^2}=\frac{d^2\big[\mathsf U\check D-\sigma_B\Lambda\big]}{\Lambda \check D},\qquad Z_3\defeq\frac{\alpha_3-\check\alpha_3}{\varpi^2}=-\frac{s\,d^2\big[\mathsf V\check D-\tilde\sigma_B\Lambda\big]}{\Lambda \check D}, \]
whose numerators satisfy
\[ \mathsf U\check D-\sigma_B\Lambda=(\mathsf U-\sigma_B)\check D+\sigma_B(\check D-\Lambda),\qquad \mathsf V\check D-\tilde\sigma_B\Lambda=(\mathsf V-\tilde\sigma_B)\check D+\tilde\sigma_B(\check D-\Lambda). \]
On \(\{\abs d\le\mathsf m\}\) these are bounded by \((1+q_I\mathsf m^2)\abs{\mathsf U-\sigma_B}+C_I(1+\mathsf m)^3\Xi\), respectively the same expression with \((\mathsf V,\tilde\sigma_B)\), and their expectations are at most \(C_I(1+\mathsf m)^3B^{-1/2}\) by Lemma SB.11. The fourth moments of \(Z_2\) and \(Z_3\) are bounded by Lemma SB.5 with the factor \(\varpi^2\) removed, together with \(\norm{d^2}_4\le C_I\). The event decomposition and the \(L^2\) upgrade are then identical.
The sequence is defined from these bounds, not from the model. Every constant \(C_I\) above depends only on \((I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\), since Lemma SB.11, Lemma SB.5 and Lemma SB.6 involve neither \(\norm{\Ahat}\) nor the weights beyond (SB.27). Let \(\epsilon_B(\mathsf m,a)\) be the largest of the three right sides obtained for \(\EE Z^2\), \(\EE Z_2^2\) and \(\EE Z_3^2\), and put
\[ e_B\defeq\inf_{\mathsf m\ge1,\ 0<a\le1}\epsilon_B(\mathsf m,a)^{1/2}. \]
Then \(e_B\) depends only on \((I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\) and \(B\), it bounds the three normalized errors for every model, every age and every \(s\in I\), and \(e_B\to0\) by the order of limits used for \(\alpha_1\).

Lemma SB.13 (Uniform Gaussian limit of the residual functional)
With \(\psi_x\) as in Lemma SB.12 and \(d=z^\T\Ahat w\),
\[ r_B\defeq\sup_{0\le x\le q_I}\abs{\EE\,\psi_x(d)-\Jfun(x)}\longrightarrow0 . \]
Proof
Proof.
Pointwise convergence holds at each \(x\). The function \(\psi_x\) is continuous with \(0\le\psi_x(d)\le d^2\), so Lemma SA.7(iii) gives
\[ \EE\,\psi_x(d)\longrightarrow\EE\,\psi_x(g)=\Jfun(x). \]
The family is uniformly Lipschitz in \(x\). For \(x,x'\in[0,q_I]\),
\[ \abs{\psi_x(d)-\psi_{x'}(d)}=\frac{d^4\,\abs{x-x'}}{(1+xd^2)(1+x'd^2)}\le\abs{x-x'}\,d^4 , \]
so, by \(\EE d^4\le9\) from Lemma SA.7(i) and \(\EE g^4=3\),
\[ \abs{\EE\psi_x(d)-\EE\psi_{x'}(d)}\le9\abs{x-x'},\qquad \abs{\Jfun(x)-\Jfun(x')}\le3\abs{x-x'}. \]
Pointwise convergence of a uniformly Lipschitz family on a compact interval is uniform. Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose a finite \(\epsilon/36\)-net of \([0,q_I]\) and then \(B\) so large that the pointwise error is below \(\epsilon/3\) at every point of the net. \[ \abs{\EE\psi_x(d)-\Jfun(x)}\le9\abs{x-x'}+\abs{\EE\psi_{x'}(d)-\Jfun(x')}+3\abs{x-x'}\le\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{12}<\epsilon . \qedhere \]

\[ \norm{\EE\widetilde R(-s)-(s\Id+c_B(s)\Sigmain)^{-1}}\le\frac{\norm{\widetilde E_B(s)}}{s_-}, \qquad \sup_{s\in I}\norm{\widetilde E_B(s)}\longrightarrow0 . \]
Step 5: identification of the branch. The tuple \((\sigma_B,\tilde\sigma_B,a_B,c_B)(s)\) is nonnegative and lies in the box of Theorem SB.1(iii) by (SB.31) and \(\Fw\le\sw2\). Its third and fourth residuals vanish by (SB.43), and its first residual is
\[ \begin{aligned} \Big|\sigma_B-\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1}\big)\Big| &=\Big|\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(\mathcal P-(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1})\big)\Big|\\ &\le\frac{\Tr\Sigmaout}{B}\,\frac{\norm{E_B(s)}}{s_-}\le\frac{C_\Sigma\gamma_\star}{s_-}\norm{E_B(s)}, \end{aligned} \]
with the same bound for the second residual in terms of \(\widetilde E_B\). Both residuals tend to zero uniformly in \(s\in I\). By Theorem SB.1(iii), applied with \((\nu,\tilde\nu)=(\nu_{\mathrm{out}},\nu_{\mathrm{in}})\in\mathcal K\),
\[ \sup_{s\in I}\Big(\abs{\sigma_B-\sigma}+\abs{\tilde\sigma_B-\tilde\sigma}+\abs{a_B-\tilde s_1}+\abs{c_B-s_1}\Big)(-s)\longrightarrow0 . \]
The conclusion follows from the resolvent identity. For every \(s\in I\),
\[ \begin{aligned} &\norm{(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1}-(s\Id+\tilde s_1\Sigmaout)^{-1}}\\ &\qquad=\norm{(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1}\,(\tilde s_1-a_B)\Sigmaout\,(s\Id+\tilde s_1\Sigmaout)^{-1}} \le\frac{C_\Sigma\abs{a_B-\tilde s_1}}{s_-^2}, \end{aligned} \]
and together with (SB.45) and the triangle inequality,
\[ \sup_{s\in I}\norm{\EE R(-s)-\Rbar(-s)}\le\sup_{s\in I}\Big(\frac{\norm{E_B(s)}}{s_-}+\frac{C_\Sigma\abs{a_B(s)-\tilde s_1(-s)}}{s_-^2}\Big)\longrightarrow0 . \]
The input side is identical.

Proof
Proof of Corollary SB.1.
Put
\[ \varepsilon(s)\defeq\frac1{\Nout}\Tr\big[T\big(R(-s)-\Rbar(-s)\big)\big]. \]
At a fixed \(s\in I\) the error splits into a fluctuation and a bias. We use the trace bound
\[ \frac{\abs{\Tr(TX)}}{\Nout}\le\frac{\norm T\,\norm X_*}{\Nout}\le\norm T\,\norm X , \]
and \(\Nout\ge B\gout/2\) for all large \(B\). By \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\), Lemma SB.10 rescaled from \(B\) to \(\Nout\), and Theorem SB.2,
\[ \begin{aligned} \EE\abs{\varepsilon(s)}^2&\le2\Var\Big(\frac{\Tr(TR(-s))}{\Nout}\Big)+2\,\Big|\frac{\Tr[T(\mathcal P(s)-\Rbar(-s))]}{\Nout}\Big|^2\\ &\le\frac{8C_IC_T^2\sw2}{\gout^2B}+2C_T^2\norm{\mathcal P(s)-\Rbar(-s)}^2\longrightarrow0 . \end{aligned} \]
The error is equicontinuous in \(s\), uniformly in the dimension and the realization. By Lemma SB.3, for \(s,s'\in I\),
\[ \norm{R(-s)-R(-s')}\le\frac{\abs{s-s'}}{s_-^2}, \]
and by the resolvent identity applied to \(\Rbar\),
\[ \norm{\Rbar(-s)-\Rbar(-s')}\le\frac{C_\Sigma\abs{\tilde s_1(-s)-\tilde s_1(-s')}+\abs{s-s'}}{s_-^2}. \]
By Theorem SB.1(ii) the map \(s\mapsto\tilde s_1(-s)\) has a modulus of continuity \(\omega_I\) on \(I\) that is uniform over \(\mathcal K\), hence over the dimension. With the trace bound this gives a deterministic modulus \(\varkappa\), uniform in the dimension and the realization, such that
\[ \abs{\varepsilon(s)-\varepsilon(s')}\le\varkappa(\abs{s-s'})\qquad(s,s'\in I). \]
The supremum is reduced to a finite net. Let \(I_\eta\subset I\) be a finite \(\eta\)-net. Then
\[ \sup_{s\in I}\abs{\varepsilon(s)}\le\max_{s\in I_\eta}\abs{\varepsilon(s)}+\varkappa(\eta), \]
and squaring, bounding the maximum by the sum and taking expectations,
\[ \EE\sup_{s\in I}\abs{\varepsilon(s)}^2\le2\sum_{s\in I_\eta}\EE\abs{\varepsilon(s)}^2+2\varkappa(\eta)^2 . \]
Taking first the limit in the dimension at fixed \(\eta\) and then \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the first claim. The input side is identical.
The last claim is the case \(T=\Sigmaout\,B/\Nout\) and \(\widetilde T=\Sigmain\,B/\Nin\), admissible since \(B/\Nout\le2/\gout\) for large \(B\), for which
\[ \frac1{\Nout}\Tr\big(T\Rbar(-s)\big)=\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout\Rbar(-s)\big)=\sigma(-s) \]
by Definition SB.1.
