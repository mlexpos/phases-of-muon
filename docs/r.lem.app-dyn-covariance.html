Lemma SE.6 (Covariance inequality)
Let \(F,G\) be square-integrable functions of countably many independent random coordinates \((\xi_l)_{l\ge1}\), and let \(F^{[l]}\), \(G^{[l]}\) denote \(F\), \(G\) with \(\xi_l\) replaced by an independent copy. Then
\[ \abs{\Cov(F,G)}\le\sum_{l\ge1}\norm{F-F^{[l]}}_2\,\norm{G-G^{[l]}}_2 . \]
Proof.
Let \(\mathcal A_l\) be generated by \(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_l\) and \(D_lF\defeq\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_l]-\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_{l-1}]\). By Lévy’s upward theorem [74, Theorem 14.2], \(\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_l]\to F\) in \(L^2\), so \(F-\EE F=\sum_lD_lF\) in \(L^2\), and likewise for \(G\). Distinct martingale differences are orthogonal, so \(\Cov(F,G)=\sum_l\EE[D_lF\,D_lG]\). Since the copy of \(\xi_l\) is independent, \(\EE[F^{[l]}\mid\mathcal A_l]=\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_{l-1}]\), hence \(D_lF=\EE[F-F^{[l]}\mid\mathcal A_l]\) and \(\norm{D_lF}_2\le\norm{F-F^{[l]}}_2\) by Jensen’s inequality. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to each term proves the claim.
□
