Proposition SA.1 (Residual and gradient moments)
In Definition SA.1, \(\Sigmain\) and \(\Sigmaout\) are symmetric and positive semidefinite, and the following hold.
  1. (i)
    For deterministic \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) the residual \(d\defeq\Xout^\T\Delta\Xin\) is centered, and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.4} \EE d^2=\Var(d)=\Tr\big(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain\Delta^\T\big)=\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma^2, \qquad \cR(W)=\tfrac12\norm{W-\Wstar}_\Sigma^2 . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    For deterministic \(\Delta\) and a batch of \(B\) independent copies, the gradient \(G^\circ\defeq\frac1B\sum_ad_a^\circ\,\Xout^{(a)}\tensor\Xin^{(a)}\) satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.5} G^\circ=\frac1B\,Y\vec D^\circ X^\T,\qquad \EE G^\circ=\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain . \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    For the state \(\Dt\) at step \(t\) with \(\EE\norm{\Dt}_\F^2<\infty\) and the fresh batch of step \(t\), the residuals and the gradient of (SA.3) satisfy
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.6} \EE_t\,d_a^\circ=0,\qquad \EE_t\,(d_a^\circ)^2=2\cR(t),\qquad \EE_t\,\Gt^\circ=\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain . \end{equation}
Proof.
The covariances are symmetric, and \(v^\T\Sigmain v=\EE[(v^\T\Xin)^2]\ge0\) for every \(v\in\R^{\Nin}\); the same holds for \(\Sigmaout\).
Mixed fourth-order moments factorize by independence: for coordinates \(i,k\le\Nout\) and \(j,l\le\Nin\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.7} \EE\big[(\Xout)_i(\Xout)_k(\Xin)_j(\Xin)_l\big]=(\Sigmaout)_{ik}(\Sigmain)_{jl}. \end{equation}
The second moment of the residual is the trace. Expanding
\[ d^2=\sum_{i,k\le\Nout}\sum_{j,l\le\Nin}\Delta_{ij}\Delta_{kl}\,(\Xout)_i(\Xout)_k(\Xin)_j(\Xin)_l \]
and taking expectations term by term with (SA.7) gives
\[ \EE d^2=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\Delta_{ij}\Delta_{kl}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}(\Sigmain)_{jl}=\Tr\big(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain\Delta^\T\big), \]
the last equality by expanding the trace as \(\sum_{i,k,j,l}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}\Delta_{kl}(\Sigmain)_{lj}\Delta_{ij}\) and using the symmetry of \(\Sigmain\). The residual is centered because \(\EE[(\Xout)_i(\Xin)_j]=\EE(\Xout)_i\,\EE(\Xin)_j=0\) by independence, and hence \(\Var(d)=\EE d^2\). The formula for \(\cR\) is (SA.1) with \(\Delta=W-\Wstar\). This proves (i).
The matrix form of the gradient is finite algebra. Matrix multiplication gives \((Y\vec D^\circ X^\T)_{ij}=\sum_ad_a^\circ(\Xout^{(a)})_i(\Xin^{(a)})_j\), which is \(B\) times the \((i,j)\) entry of \(G^\circ\). For one sample, the entry \(d\,(\Xout)_i(\Xin)_j=\sum_{k,l}\Delta_{kl}(\Xout)_k(\Xout)_i(\Xin)_l(\Xin)_j\) has expectation \(\sum_{k,l}(\Sigmaout)_{ik}\Delta_{kl}(\Sigmain)_{lj}=(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain)_{ij}\) by (SA.7); identical distribution of the samples gives \(\EE G^\circ\). This proves (ii).
Part (iii) follows from (i) and (ii) because the batch of step \(t\) is independent of the past: conditionally on \(\cF_t\) the state \(\Dt\) is fixed and the batch has its unconditional law, so the fixed-state identities of (i) and (ii) at \(\Delta=\Dt\) are the three conditional expectations.
□
