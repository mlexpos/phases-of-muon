This section computes the half-anisotropic drift and volatility coefficients of Section SC and Section SD for the power-law output covariance \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\), in the thin-batch regime in which \(N\), \(B\) and the aspect ratio \(\gamma=N/B\) all tend to infinity, at a fixed momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\). For each finite dimension the coefficients are evaluated at zero normalized ridge, as limits of the positive-ridge coefficients of those sections. The main result, Theorem SG.1, gives explicit approximations of the drift coefficient \(\mathfrak d_i\) and of the integrated volatility coefficient \(\mathfrak v_i\), with relative errors tending to zero uniformly over all rows \(n_N\le i\le N\) for every sequence \(n_N\to\infty\). Both coefficients have a crossover between a resolved window, where
\[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i\sim\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}{\gamma\sw2}},\qquad \mathfrak v_i\sim\frac1{\sw2}, \]
and an unresolved window, where both are governed by explicit bulk profiles; Theorem SG.2 and Theorem SG.3 and Corollary SG.1 locate the crossovers and give the endpoint laws, and Proposition SG.6 compares the crossover indices with the resolution threshold \(i_\#\) of (15).
Two conventions of notation are used. The letter \(\resscale\) denotes only the resolution scalar of the main text’s half-anisotropic table, defined in Proposition SG.6. The target exponent \(\texp\) of the main text does not appear in this section, because every statement here concerns the coefficients and none concerns the initial data.
Every statement of the section is a statement about deterministic coefficient functions at a finite dimension and about their asymptotics. None is a statement about the random algorithm at zero ridge or in the thin-batch regime; Remark SG.3 explains how the deterministic model of the phase-comparison section uses the profiles.

SG.1 Standing setup

Dimensions and covariances. Throughout the section \(\Nout=\Nin=N\), the input covariance is \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\), and the output covariance is \(\Sigmaout=\sum_{i=1}^N\mu_i\,u_i\tensor u_i\) with \(\mu_1\ge\dots\ge\mu_N>0\). The aspect ratio is \(\gamma\defeq N/B\). In Section SG.2 the spectrum is an arbitrary positive one; from Section SG.3 on it is the power law \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\). The momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) is fixed, the weights are \(\emaw j=(1-\bmom)\bmom^j\), and
\[ \sw p=\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j^p=\frac{(1-\bmom)^p}{1-\bmom^p}\quad(p\ge2),\qquad \sw2=\frac{1-\bmom}{1+\bmom},\qquad c_0\defeq\EE\abs g=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}, \]
with \(g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\). The no-momentum case \(\bmom=0\) is treated in Remark SG.1.
The coefficients at a finite dimension. At a fixed dimension, the objects of Section SB, Section SC and Section SD are deterministic functions of the spectrum, of \(B\), of \(\bmom\) and of the normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\). They are the branch \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)(-s)\) of Theorem SB.1 for the measures \(\nu=B^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{\mu_i}\) and \(\tilde\nu=\gamma\,\delta_1\) of (SA.41); the drift coefficient
\[ \mathfrak d_i(\ridge)\defeq\mathfrak d(\mu_i,1) \]
of (SC.4), which is the row coefficient of Corollary SC.2; the lag kernels \(\mathfrak k_\ell\) of Theorem SD.3; the row lag coefficients \(\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)\defeq\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge,\ridge)\) and the integrated row coefficient \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\defeq\mathfrak v_i(\ridge,\ridge)\) of (SD.75); and the raw coefficient \(\volraw_i(\ridge)\) of (SD.22), which equals \(\overline C_{i,0}(\ridge)\) by Theorem SD.7(iv). The constructions of Section SD.6 are made at a fixed finite dimension and use the size conditions only through constants \(\gamma_\star\ge\gamma\) and \(C_\Sigma\ge\mu_1\) of that dimension, along its replications (Definition SD.3). The row lag kernel is
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.1} \mathfrak k_{i,\ell}(s,s')\defeq B\,\mathfrak k_\ell[u_i\tensor u_i,\Id_N](s,s')\qquad(s,s'>0). \end{equation}
By Theorem SD.4(i) with \(F=u_i\tensor u_i\) and \(G=\Id_N\), multiplied by \(B\), at the fixed dimension and with \(T\to\infty\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.2} \overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac4{\pi^2}\int_0^T\!\!\int_0^T\mathfrak k_{i,\ell}(\ridge+x^2,\ridge+y^2)\,\dif x\,\dif y, \qquad \mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=\overline C_{i,0}(\ridge)+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge). \end{equation}
Scalar functions. The functions \(\Jfun\), \(\Fw\) and \(\Lw\) are those of (SB.1) for the EMA weights, and \(r_\bmom(q)\defeq q\Fw(q)\) is the function of Lemma SB.1(iii). Since infinitely many weights are positive, \(r_\bmom\) increases strictly from \(0\) to \(\infty\), and we put
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.3} Q_m\defeq r_\bmom^{-1}(m),\qquad E(m)\defeq Q_m\,r_\bmom'(Q_m)\qquad(m>0). \end{equation}
Asymptotic conventions. Asymptotic statements are made along sequences of dimensions indexed by \(N\), with \(B=B_N\), \(\gamma=\gamma_N\) and \(c_N\) depending on \(N\); a row sequence is a sequence of integers \(i=i_N\in\{1,\dots,N\}\). We write \(a\sim b\) when \(a/b\to1\), and \(a\asymp b\) when \(a/b\) is bounded above and below by positive constants. Unless a statement says otherwise, constants \(c,C\) depend only on \((\alpha,\bmom)\) and may change from line to line.

SG.2 The coefficients at finite spectrum and zero ridge

In this subsection the spectrum \(\mu_1\ge\dots\ge\mu_N>0\) and the dimension are fixed. The half-anisotropic branch is parametrized by one real coordinate, the participation \(m\in(0,\gamma)\), in which the drift becomes a single integral and the two-time volatility kernel becomes explicit. Both coefficients then extend to zero normalized ridge.
Definition SG.1 (Participation coordinates)
The participation function and its inverse are
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.4} m_N(\Lambda)\defeq\frac1B\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\Lambda\mu_i}{1+\Lambda\mu_i}\quad(\Lambda\ge0),\qquad \Lambda_N\defeq m_N^{-1}:(0,\gamma)\to(0,\infty). \end{equation}
For \(0<m<\gamma\) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.5} \begin{gathered} f_i(m)\defeq\frac{\Lambda_N(m)\mu_i}{1+\Lambda_N(m)\mu_i},\qquad s_N(m)\defeq\frac{(\gamma-m)\,m}{Q_m\,\Lambda_N(m)},\\ \vartheta_N(m)\defeq\frac1{E(m)}+\frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}+\frac1{\gamma-m}-\frac1m . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
The function \(m_N\) is strictly increasing from \(0\) to \(\gamma\), because each of its \(N\) terms is, so \(\Lambda_N\) is well defined, increasing and smooth.
Proposition SG.1 (The branch in participation coordinates)
  1. (i)
    The function \(s_N\) is a smooth strictly decreasing bijection of \((0,\gamma)\) onto \((0,\infty)\), and \(-\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log s_N(m)=\vartheta_N(m)>0\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(s>0\) let \(m=m(s)\in(0,\gamma)\) be the solution of \(s_N(m)=s\). Then
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.6} \begin{gathered} q(-s)=Q_m,\qquad \tilde s_1(-s)=s\,\Lambda_N(m),\qquad s_1(-s)=\frac{s\,m}{\gamma-m},\\ \sigma(-s)=\frac{m}{s\,\Lambda_N(m)},\qquad \tilde\sigma(-s)=\frac{\gamma-m}{s},\qquad \frac{\tilde s_1(-s)\mu_i}{s+\tilde s_1(-s)\mu_i}=f_i(m). \end{gathered} \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    For every \(\ridge>0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.7} \mathfrak d_i(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\infty \frac{\Lw(Q_{m(\ridge+u^2)})}{\Fw(Q_{m(\ridge+u^2)})}\,f_i\big(m(\ridge+u^2)\big)\,\dif u, \qquad \volraw_i(\ridge)=f_i\big(m(\ridge)\big). \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) rests on the monotonicity of \(\Lambda_N(m)/m\). Since \(m_N(\Lambda)/\Lambda\) is decreasing in \(\Lambda\), its inverse relation makes \(\Lambda_N(m)/m\) nondecreasing, so that
\[ \frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}\ge\frac1m . \]
The logarithmic derivative of \(s_N\) is, by \(\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log Q_m=1/E(m)\),
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log s_N(m)=-\frac1{\gamma-m}+\frac1m-\frac1{E(m)}-\frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}=-\vartheta_N(m), \]
and the preceding inequality gives \(\vartheta_N(m)\ge1/(\gamma-m)+1/E(m)>0\). At the left endpoint, \(\Lambda_N(m)\sim Bm/\sum_i\mu_i\) and \(Q_m\sim m/\sw2\) as \(m\downarrow0\), by Lemma SB.1(ii), so that
\[ s_N(m)\sim\frac{\gamma\,\sw2}{m}\cdot\frac1B\sum_i\mu_i\longrightarrow\infty . \]
At the right endpoint, \(\Lambda_N(m)\to\infty\) and \(Q_m\to Q_\gamma<\infty\), so \(s_N(m)\to0\).
Part (ii) uses the coupling (SB.4). Let \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)\) be the branch at \(-s\) and put \(m\defeq\tilde s_1\sigma=s_1\tilde\sigma=r_\bmom(q)\), so that \(q=Q_m\). The second trace equation of (SB.2) for \(\tilde\nu=\gamma\delta_1\) reads \(\tilde\sigma=\gamma/(s+s_1)\), hence
\[ s\tilde\sigma=\gamma-s_1\tilde\sigma=\gamma-m,\qquad \tilde s_1=s\tilde\sigma\,\Fw(q)=(\gamma-m)\,\Fw(Q_m). \]
Put \(\Lambda\defeq\tilde s_1/s\). The first trace equation gives
\[ m=\tilde s_1\sigma=\frac1B\sum_i\frac{\tilde s_1\mu_i}{s+\tilde s_1\mu_i}=m_N(\Lambda), \]
so \(\Lambda=\Lambda_N(m)\), and, with \(\Fw(Q_m)=m/Q_m\),
\[ s=\frac{\tilde s_1}{\Lambda}=\frac{(\gamma-m)\Fw(Q_m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}=s_N(m). \]
By (i) this determines \(m=m(s)\). The remaining identities of (SG.6) are \(s_1=m/\tilde\sigma\), \(\sigma=m/\tilde s_1\) and \(\tilde s_1\mu_i/(s+\tilde s_1\mu_i)=\Lambda\mu_i/(1+\Lambda\mu_i)\).
Part (iii) is a computation in these coordinates. By (ii),
\[ s+s_1=\frac{s\gamma}{\gamma-m},\qquad \frac{\mu_i}{s+\tilde s_1\mu_i}=\frac{f_i(m)}{\tilde s_1}=\frac{f_i(m)}{(\gamma-m)\Fw(Q_m)}, \]
so the integrand of (SC.4) at \((\mu,\lambda)=(\mu_i,1)\) is
\[ \frac{s\,\Lw(q)\,\mu_i}{(s+\tilde s_1\mu_i)(s+s_1)}=\frac{\Lw(Q_m)}{\gamma\,\Fw(Q_m)}\,f_i(m),\qquad m=m(s), \]
which gives the first identity with \(s=\ridge+u^2\). The second is the last identity of (SG.6) at \(s=\ridge\), compared with (SD.22).

The weight functions. The analysis of the participation integrals uses the behaviour of the EMA weight functions at small and large arguments. For \(Q>0\) let
\[ n(Q)\defeq\#\{j\ge0:\ \emaw j^2Q\ge1\} \]
be the number of ages whose weight is resolved at the scale \(Q\); it is \(0\) when \((1-\bmom)^2Q<1\), and otherwise \(n(Q)=\lfloor\log((1-\bmom)^2Q)/(2\abs{\log\bmom})\rfloor+1\).
Lemma SG.1 (Weight functions)
  1. (i)
    For \(q\ge0\),
    \[ \begin{gathered} \Fw(q)=\sw2-3\sw4q+R_F(q),\quad 0\le R_F\le15\sw6q^2,\\ \Lw(q)=1-3\sw3q+R_L(q),\quad 0\le R_L\le15\sw5q^2 . \end{gathered} \]
    Consequently \(r_\bmom(q)=\sw2q+O(q^2)\), and \(Q_m\sim m/\sw2\) and \(E(m)\sim m\) as \(m\downarrow0\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(q>0\), \(\abs{q\Fw'(q)}\le\Fw(q)\) and \(\abs{q\Lw'(q)}\le\Lw(q)\). For \(m>0\), \(E(m)\le m\).
  3. (iii)
    There are \(0<c_\bmom\le C_\bmom<\infty\) such that, for \(Q\ge1\),
    \[ \abs{r_\bmom(Q)-n(Q)}\le C_\bmom,\qquad c_\bmom\le\sqrt Q\,\Lw(Q)\le C_\bmom,\qquad c_\bmom\le Q\,r_\bmom'(Q)\le C_\bmom . \]
    Consequently \(E(m)\asymp\min(m,1)\) for all \(m>0\), and \(\abs{\log Q_m-2\abs{\log\bmom}\,m}\le C_\bmom\) for \(m\ge1\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is a second-order expansion. With \(t=xg^2\) and \((1+t)^{-1}=1-t+t^2/(1+t)\), the Gaussian moments \(\EE g^2=1\), \(\EE g^4=3\), \(\EE g^6=15\) give
\[ \Jfun(x)=1-3x+\rho(x),\qquad 0\le\rho(x)=\EE\frac{x^2g^6}{1+xg^2}\le15x^2 . \]
Inserting this in \(\Fw(q)=\sum_j\emaw j^2\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\) and \(\Lw(q)=\sum_j\emaw j\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\), with \(\sum_j\emaw j=1\), gives the two expansions. The consequences follow from \(r_\bmom(q)=q\Fw(q)\) and \(r_\bmom'(q)\to\sw2\) as \(q\downarrow0\).
Part (ii) is a termwise bound. Since \(\Jfun'(x)=-\EE\,g^4(1+xg^2)^{-2}\) and \(xg^4(1+xg^2)^{-2}\le g^2(1+xg^2)^{-1}\),
\[ \abs{x\Jfun'(x)}\le\Jfun(x)\qquad(x>0). \]
Each term of \(q\Fw'(q)\) and \(q\Lw'(q)\) is \(\emaw j^2\cdot(\emaw j^2q)\Jfun'(\emaw j^2q)\), respectively \(\emaw j\cdot(\emaw j^2q)\Jfun'(\emaw j^2q)\), which proves the two bounds. Since \(\Fw'\le0\),
\[ q\,r_\bmom'(q)=r_\bmom(q)+q^2\Fw'(q)\le r_\bmom(q), \]
and at \(q=Q_m\) this is \(E(m)\le m\).
Part (iii) compares each sum with the count of resolved ages. Put \(\mathsf f(x)\defeq x\Jfun(x)=\EE\,xg^2(1+xg^2)^{-1}\), so that \(r_\bmom(Q)=\sum_j\mathsf f(\emaw j^2Q)\). Then \(\mathsf f(x)\le\min(x,1)\), and the bound \((2\pi)^{-1/2}\) on the Gaussian density gives
\[ 1-\mathsf f(x)=\EE\frac{1}{1+xg^2}\le\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_\R\frac{\dif y}{1+xy^2}=\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x^{-1/2}. \]
The ages \(j<n(Q)\) have \(\emaw j\sqrt Q\ge1\), and the numbers \((\emaw j\sqrt Q)^{-1}\) for \(j=n(Q)-1,n(Q)-2,\dots\) are at most \(1,\bmom,\bmom^2,\dots\); the ages \(j\ge n(Q)\) have \(\emaw j^2Q<1\), and the numbers \(\emaw j^2Q\) for \(j=n(Q),n(Q)+1,\dots\) are at most \(1,\bmom^2,\dots\). Hence
\[ \abs{r_\bmom(Q)-n(Q)}\le\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\sum_{j<n(Q)}\frac1{\emaw j\sqrt Q}+\sum_{j\ge n(Q)}\emaw j^2Q \le\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,\frac1{1-\bmom}+\frac{1}{1-\bmom^2}. \]
The same splitting bounds the other two sums from above. Indeed \(\sqrt Q\Lw(Q)=\sum_j\phi(\emaw j^2Q)\) with \(\phi(x)=\sqrt x\,\Jfun(x)\le\min(\sqrt x,x^{-1/2})\), using \(\Jfun\le1\) and \(\Jfun(x)\le1/x\); and \(Qr_\bmom'(Q)=\sum_j\mathsf k(\emaw j^2Q)\) with \(\mathsf k(x)=\EE\,xg^2(1+xg^2)^{-2}\le\min(\mathsf f(x),1-\mathsf f(x))\le\min(x,\sqrt{\pi/2}\,x^{-1/2})\). For the lower bounds, note that for \(Q\ge1\) some age has \(\emaw j^2Q\in[(1-\bmom)^2,\bmom^{-2}]\): the age \(0\) if \((1-\bmom)^2Q<1\), and the age \(n(Q)-1\) otherwise. The continuous positive functions \(\phi\) and \(\mathsf k\) are bounded below on this compact interval, which gives \(c_\bmom\).
The consequences follow. For \(m\ge1\) one has \(Q_m\ge1\), because \(r_\bmom(1)\le\sw2<1\), so \(E(m)=Q_mr_\bmom'(Q_m)\asymp1\). For \(0<m\le1\) the ratio \(E(m)/m=Q_mr_\bmom'(Q_m)/r_\bmom(Q_m)\) is continuous and positive, with limit \(1\) at \(m=0\) by (i), so \(E(m)\asymp m\). Finally \(r_\bmom(Q_m)=m\) and the first bound of (iii) give \(\abs{n(Q_m)-m}\le C_\bmom\), and the explicit form of \(n\) gives the bound on \(\log Q_m\).

The large-argument behaviour of \(\sqrt q\,\Lw(q)\) oscillates with the geometric spacing of the weights and has no limit. Its average in the participation coordinate has one, and this is the form in which it enters the drift.
Lemma SG.2 (Averaging in the participation coordinate)
Put \(\mathcal A(m)\defeq\sqrt{Q_m}\,\Lw(Q_m)/E(m)\) for \(m>0\). For every \(m_0>0\), \(\mathcal A\asymp1\) on \([m_0,\infty)\) with constants depending on \((\bmom,m_0)\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.8} \sup_{t\ge m_0}\Big|\int_{m_0}^t\big(\mathcal A(m)-\sqrt{2\pi}\big)\,\dif m\Big|\le C_{\bmom,m_0}. \end{equation}
Consequently, for every \([a,c]\subset[m_0,\infty)\) and every continuously differentiable \(W\) on \([a,c]\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.9} \Big|\int_a^c\big(\mathcal A(m)-\sqrt{2\pi}\big)W(m)\,\dif m\Big|\le C_{\bmom,m_0}\Big(\abs{W(a)}+\abs{W(c)}+\int_a^c\abs{W'(m)}\,\dif m\Big). \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The two-sided bound on \(\mathcal A\) is Lemma SG.1(iii) for \(m\ge1\) and continuity and positivity on \([m_0,1]\).
The average is computed in the variable \(q\). Since \(\dif m=r_\bmom'(q)\,\dif q\) at \(m=r_\bmom(q)\) and \(E(m)=qr_\bmom'(q)\),
\[ \int_{m_0}^t\mathcal A(m)\,\dif m=\int_{q_0}^{Q_t}\sqrt q\,\Lw(q)\,\frac{\dif q}{q} =\sum_{j\ge0}\int_{\emaw j^2q_0}^{\emaw j^2Q_t}x^{-1/2}\Jfun(x)\,\dif x,\qquad q_0\defeq Q_{m_0}, \]
by the substitution \(x=\emaw j^2q\) in the \(j\)-th term of \(\Lw\). The full integral of each term is
\[ \int_0^\infty x^{-1/2}\Jfun(x)\,\dif x=\EE\Big[\abs g\int_0^\infty\frac{y^{-1/2}}{1+y}\,\dif y\Big]=\pi\,\EE\abs g=\sqrt{2\pi}, \]
by the substitution \(y=xg^2\) and the Beta integral \(\int_0^\infty y^{-1/2}(1+y)^{-1}\dif y=\pi\) [85, (5.12.3)]. The incomplete terms are controlled by \(x^{-1/2}\Jfun(x)\le\min(x^{-1/2},x^{-3/2})\): for \(j<n(Q_t)\) the missing parts are at most \(2\emaw j\sqrt{q_0}\) and \(2(\emaw j\sqrt{Q_t})^{-1}\), and for \(j\ge n(Q_t)\) the whole term is at most \(2\emaw j\sqrt{Q_t}\). Summing these geometric bounds as in the proof of Lemma SG.1(iii),
\[ \Big|\int_{m_0}^t\mathcal A(m)\,\dif m-\sqrt{2\pi}\,n(Q_t)\Big|\le2\sqrt{q_0}+\frac{4}{1-\bmom}. \]
For \(t\ge1\), Lemma SG.1(iii) replaces \(n(Q_t)\) by \(r_\bmom(Q_t)=t\) up to \(C_\bmom\), and for \(m_0\le t\le1\) both integrals are bounded. This proves (SG.8).
The weighted bound is an integration by parts. With \(\Phi_0(v)\defeq\int_{m_0}^v(\mathcal A-\sqrt{2\pi})\,\dif m\),
\[ \int_a^c\big(\mathcal A-\sqrt{2\pi}\big)W\,\dif m=\Phi_0(c)W(c)-\Phi_0(a)W(a)-\int_a^c\Phi_0W'\,\dif m \]
[92, Theorem 6.22], and \(\abs{\Phi_0}\le C_{\bmom,m_0}\) by (SG.8).

The drift at zero ridge. By Proposition SG.1(i), the participation \(m(s)\) tends to \(\gamma\) as \(s\downarrow0\). Put
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.10} \begin{gathered} F_\star\defeq\Fw(Q_\gamma),\qquad z_1\defeq s_N\big(m_N(1)\big),\qquad D_i\defeq\frac1{\mu_i}\sqrt{\frac{1+\mu_1}{\gamma F_\star}},\\ \mathcal G_i(s)\defeq\frac{\Lw(Q_{m(s)})}{\Fw(Q_{m(s)})}\,f_i\big(m(s)\big). \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Proposition SG.2 (Drift at zero ridge)
  1. (i)
    As \(s\downarrow0\), \(Q_{m(s)}\to Q_\gamma<\infty\) and \(s\,\Lambda_N(m(s))^2\to F_\star\,B^{-1}\sum_i\mu_i^{-1}\). For \(0<s\le z_1\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.11} 1-\volraw_i(s)=\frac1{1+\Lambda_N(m(s))\mu_i}\le D_i\sqrt s . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    The integral
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.12} \mathfrak d_i(0)\defeq\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\infty\mathcal G_i(u^2)\,\dif u \end{equation}
    is finite, and for every \(\ridge>0\)
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.13} \max_{1\le i\le N}\abs{\mathfrak d_i(\ridge)-\mathfrak d_i(0)}\le\frac{C_\bmom}{\gamma}\sqrt\ridge\,\big(1+\sqrt{Q_\gamma}\big), \end{equation}
    with \(C_\bmom\) depending only on \(\bmom\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) reads the endpoint off the participation coordinate. As \(s\downarrow0\), \(m(s)\to\gamma\) and \(\Lambda\defeq\Lambda_N(m(s))\to\infty\), and
\[ \gamma-m_N(\Lambda)=\frac1B\sum_i\frac{1}{1+\Lambda\mu_i},\qquad \Lambda\big(\gamma-m_N(\Lambda)\big)\longrightarrow\frac1B\sum_i\frac1{\mu_i}. \]
Since \(s\Lambda=(\gamma-m)\Fw(Q_m)\) by (SG.5), the product \(s\Lambda^2\) has the stated limit. For the deficit, \(\Fw\) is decreasing, so \(\Fw(Q_m)\ge F_\star\), and \(\gamma-m_N(\Lambda)\ge\gamma/(1+\Lambda\mu_1)\). If \(s\le z_1\) then \(\Lambda\ge1\), and
\[ s=\frac{(\gamma-m)\Fw(Q_m)}{\Lambda}\ge\frac{\gamma F_\star}{\Lambda(1+\Lambda\mu_1)}\ge\frac{\gamma F_\star}{(1+\mu_1)\Lambda^2}. \]
Hence \(1/(1+\Lambda\mu_i)\le1/(\Lambda\mu_i)\le D_i\sqrt s\), and the identity in (SG.11) is (SG.7).
Part (ii) bounds the variation of \(\mathcal G_i\). As \(s\) decreases from \(\infty\) to \(0\), \(q=Q_{m(s)}\) increases from \(0\) to \(Q_\gamma\), and \(\mathcal G_i=\mathsf R(q)\,f_i(r_\bmom(q))\) with \(\mathsf R\defeq\Lw/\Fw\). The factor \(f_i(r_\bmom(q))\) increases from \(0\) to \(1\), so
\[ \int_0^\infty\Big|\frac{\dif\mathcal G_i}{\dif s}\Big|\,\dif s\le\sup_{[0,Q_\gamma]}\mathsf R+\int_0^{Q_\gamma}\abs{\mathsf R'(q)}\,\dif q . \]
On \([0,1]\), \(\mathsf R\) and \(\mathsf R'\) are bounded by constants depending on \(\bmom\), because \(\Fw\ge\Fw(1)>0\), \(\Lw\le1\), \(\abs{\Fw'}\le3\sw4\) and \(\abs{\Lw'}\le3\sw3\) (Lemma SB.1). On \([1,\infty)\), Lemma SG.1(ii),(iii) and \(r_\bmom(q)\ge r_\bmom(1)\) give
\[ \mathsf R(q)=\frac{\sqrt q\cdot\sqrt q\Lw(q)}{r_\bmom(q)}\le C_\bmom\sqrt q,\qquad \abs{\mathsf R'(q)}\le\frac{\abs{\Lw'}}{\Fw}+\frac{\Lw\abs{\Fw'}}{\Fw^2}\le\frac{2\Lw(q)}{q\Fw(q)}=\frac{2\sqrt q\Lw(q)}{\sqrt q\,r_\bmom(q)}\le\frac{C_\bmom}{\sqrt q}. \]
Integrating, the variation of \(\mathcal G_i\) is at most \(C_\bmom(1+\sqrt{Q_\gamma})\).
The ridge shift is a pairing of this variation with a bounded function. Lemma SG.3 (Two ridges and the lag envelope)
For \(0<\eta\le\ridge\) and every \(\ell\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.18} \abs{\overline C_{i,\ell}(\eta)-\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)}\le2\sqrt{1-\volraw_i(\ridge)},\qquad \abs{\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)}\le1, \end{equation}
and for every \(\ell\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.19} \abs{\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)}\le\frac{A_N\,\bmom^\ell}{\sqrt\ridge}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
All three bounds are read from the independent model along the replications. At the \(n\)-fold replication of Definition SD.3, let \(M^{\mathrm g}_t\) be the buffer of the independent model of Definition SD.2, and for the \(n\) copies \(u_i\tensor e_k\) of the row \(u_i\) put
\[ a_k(\ridge)\defeq\Qridge\ridge(M^{\mathrm g}_t)^\T(u_i\tensor e_k),\qquad b_k(\ridge)\defeq\Qridge\ridge(M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell})^\T(u_i\tensor e_k),\]
\[ \mathsf a^{(n)}(\ridge,\ridge')\defeq\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n\EE\langle a_k(\ridge),b_k(\ridge')\rangle . \]
The independent updates have mean zero, so \(\mathsf a^{(n)}(\ridge,\ridge')\) is \(B\) times the independent covariance \(C^{\mathrm g}_\ell[F,\Id]\) at order \(n\), for the replicated test \(F=(u_i\tensor u_i)\tensor\Id_n\) of norm one. By (SD.52) and the replication invariance of Theorem SD.3,
\[ \mathsf a^{(n)}(\ridge,\ridge)\longrightarrow\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)\qquad(n\to\infty), \]
and at \(\ell=0\) the limit is \(\overline C_{i,0}(\ridge)=\volraw_i(\ridge)\).
Each row of a regularized update has norm at most one, because \(\Qridge\ridge(K)\Qridge\ridge(K)^\T\preceq\Id\); this gives the second bound of (SG.18). For the first, \(0\le\omega_\ridge\le\omega_\eta\le1\) on \([0,\infty)\) for the filters of (SD.2), so
\[ (\omega_\eta-\omega_\ridge)^2\le(1-\omega_\ridge)^2\le1-\omega_\ridge^2 , \]
and functional calculus gives \((\Qridge\eta-\Qridge\ridge)(\Qridge\eta-\Qridge\ridge)^\T(K)\preceq\Id-\Qridge\ridge(K)\Qridge\ridge(K)^\T\). Hence \(\EE\norm{a_k(\eta)-a_k(\ridge)}^2\le1-\EE\norm{a_k(\ridge)}^2\), and the same holds for \(b_k\), which has the same law. Writing \(\langle a(\eta),b(\eta)\rangle-\langle a(\ridge),b(\ridge)\rangle=\langle a(\eta)-a(\ridge),b(\eta)\rangle+\langle a(\ridge),b(\eta)-b(\ridge)\rangle\) and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the average over \(k\),
\[ \abs{\mathsf a^{(n)}(\eta,\eta)-\mathsf a^{(n)}(\ridge,\ridge)}\le2\Big(1-\frac1n\sum_k\EE\norm{a_k(\ridge)}^2\Big)^{1/2}. \]
The average in the bracket is \(\mathsf a^{(n)}(\ridge,\ridge)\) at \(\ell=0\), which tends to \(\volraw_i(\ridge)\), and the first bound follows as \(n\to\infty\).
The envelope is the coupling of the proof of Theorem SD.2(ii). Let \(M^*\) be built from an independent past, so that \(M^*\) is independent of \(M^{\mathrm g}_t\), has the law of \(M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell}\), and \(M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell}-M^*=\bmom^\ell(M^{\mathrm g}_t-M'_t)\) with \(M'_t\) an independent copy of \(M^{\mathrm g}_t\). The term with \(M^*\) has zero mean, so the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality over the copies and (SA.13) give
\[ \abs{\mathsf a^{(n)}(\ridge,\ridge)}\le\frac1n\Big(n\,\EE\norm{\Qridge\ridge(M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell})-\Qridge\ridge(M^*)}_\F^2\Big)^{1/2} \le\frac{\bmom^\ell}{\sqrt{n\ridge}}\Big(\EE\norm{M^{\mathrm g}_t-M'_t}_\F^2\Big)^{1/2}. \]
At order \(n\) the last expectation is \(2\sw2(nB)^{-1}\Tr(\Sigmaout\tensor\Id_n)\,nN=2n\gamma\sw2\sum_j\mu_j=nA_N^2\), which gives (SG.19) in the limit.

Proposition SG.4 (Volatility at zero ridge)
Let \(0<\bar z\le z_1\) and \(E_i\defeq A_N\bar z^{-1/2}+2\sqrt{D_i}\,\bar z^{1/4}\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(\ell\ge0\) the limit \(\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)\) exists, \(\overline C_{i,0}(0)=1\), and \(\abs{\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)}\le E_i\,\bmom^{\ell/3}\) for \(0\le\ridge\le\bar z\) and \(\ell\ge1\).
  2. (ii)
    The series \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\defeq1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\) converges absolutely, \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\to\mathfrak v_i(0)\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\), and for every integer \(H\ge0\) and \(0<\ridge\le\bar z\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.21} \abs{\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)-\mathfrak v_i(0)}\le2(1+2H)\sqrt{D_i}\,\ridge^{1/4}+\frac{4E_i\,\bmom^{(H+1)/3}}{1-\bmom^{1/3}} . \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    For every \(\ell\ge0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.22} \overline C_{i,\ell}(0)=\frac4{\pi^2}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty\mathfrak k_{i,\ell}(x^2,y^2)\,\dif x\,\dif y =\frac1{\pi^2}\int_0^\gamma\!\!\int_0^\gamma W_i(m_1)W_i(m_2)\,\frac{\mathsf D_\ell}{\Delta_\ell^N}\,\dif m_1\,\dif m_2 , \end{equation}
    all terms are nonnegative, and \(\mathfrak v_i(0)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\ge1\) with \(\overline C_{i,-\ell}(0)\defeq\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\), which is the integral (SG.22) with the kernels of the lag \(-\ell\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) combines the two bounds of Lemma SG.3 with the deficit (SG.11). For \(0<\eta\le\ridge\le z_1\),
\[ \abs{\overline C_{i,\ell}(\eta)-\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)}\le2\sqrt{D_i}\,\ridge^{1/4}, \]
so the family is Cauchy as \(\ridge\downarrow0\), uniformly in \(\ell\), and the limit satisfies \(\abs{\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)-\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)}\le2\sqrt{D_i}\,\ridge^{1/4}\). At \(\ell=0\) the limit is \(\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\volraw_i(\ridge)=1\), by (SG.11). For the envelope fix \(\ell\ge1\) and put \(\ridge_\ell\defeq\bar z\,\bmom^{4\ell/3}\). If \(\ridge\le\ridge_\ell\), comparison with \(\ridge_\ell\) and (SG.19) give
\[ \abs{\overline C_{i,\ell}(\ridge)}\le\frac{A_N\bmom^\ell}{\sqrt{\ridge_\ell}}+2\sqrt{D_i}\,\ridge_\ell^{1/4} =\big(A_N\bar z^{-1/2}+2\sqrt{D_i}\,\bar z^{1/4}\big)\bmom^{\ell/3}=E_i\bmom^{\ell/3}. \]
If \(\ridge_\ell<\ridge\le\bar z\), (SG.19) alone gives \(A_N\bmom^\ell\ridge^{-1/2}\le A_N\bmom^\ell\ridge_\ell^{-1/2}\le E_i\bmom^{\ell/3}\).
Part (ii) splits the lag sum at \(H\). The first \(1+2H\) terms of \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)-\mathfrak v_i(0)\) are bounded by the comparison of part (i), and the remaining ones by the envelope:
\[ \abs{\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)-\mathfrak v_i(0)}\le(1+2H)\,2\sqrt{D_i}\,\ridge^{1/4}+4E_i\sum_{\ell>H}\bmom^{\ell/3}, \]
which is (SG.21). Letting \(\ridge\downarrow0\) and then \(H\to\infty\) gives the convergence.
Part (iii) compares the regularized filter with a truncated resolvent filter at zero ridge. For \(0<d<T\) let \(f_{d,T}(x)\defeq\frac2\pi\int_d^T\iota_{y^2}(x)\,\dif y\), an odd function with \(0\le f_{d,T}\le1\) on \([0,\infty)\) and Lipschitz constant at most \(2/(\pi d)\). For \(x>0\),
\[ \begin{gathered} 0\le1-f_{d,T}(x)=\frac2\pi\arctan\frac dx+\frac2\pi\arctan\frac xT\le\frac2\pi\arctan\frac dx+\frac{2x}{\pi T},\\ \Big(\frac2\pi\arctan\frac dx\Big)^2\le\frac{2d^2}{x^2+d^2}=2\big(1-\omega_{d^2}(x)^2\big), \end{gathered} \]
the last inequality by the bound \(1\) when \(x\le d\) and \(\arctan(d/x)\le d/x\) when \(x>d\). Both \(\omega_\eta\) and \(f_{d,T}\) vanish at \(x=0\), and on \(x>0\), \(\abs{\omega_\eta-f_{d,T}}\le(1-\omega_\eta)+(1-f_{d,T})\). Squaring and using \((1-\omega_\eta)^2\le1-\omega_\eta^2\), functional calculus gives
\[ \begin{aligned} &\big(\Qridge\eta(K)-f_{d,T}^\sharp(K)\big)\big(\Qridge\eta(K)-f_{d,T}^\sharp(K)\big)^\T\\ &\qquad\preceq2\big(\Id-\Qridge\eta(K)\Qridge\eta(K)^\T\big)+8\big(\Id-\Qridge{d^2}(K)\Qridge{d^2}(K)^\T\big)+\frac{16}{\pi^2T^2}KK^\T . \end{aligned} \]
The argument of Lemma SG.3 applies to the pair of filters \((\omega_\eta,f_{d,T})\): the closure (SD.52) holds for the fixed odd bounded Lipschitz filter \(f_{d,T}\) by Remark SD.2, and the independent model has the exact row moment \(\EE\,(M^{\mathrm g}_tM^{\mathrm g\,\T}_t)_{ii}=\gamma\sw2\mu_i\). In the limit,
\[ \Big|\overline C_{i,\ell}(\eta)-B\,\mathfrak R_\ell[u_i\tensor u_i,\Id](f_{d,T},f_{d,T})\Big| \le2\Big(2\big(1-\volraw_i(\eta)\big)+8\big(1-\volraw_i(d^2)\big)+\frac{16\gamma\sw2\mu_i}{\pi^2T^2}\Big)^{1/2}, \]
where \(\mathfrak R_\ell\) is the response of (SD.49). By the bilinearity and the sup-norm bound of the response (Theorem SD.3), and the uniform continuity of \(y\mapsto\iota_{y^2}\) in the supremum norm on \([d,T]\), the response of \(f_{d,T}\) is the integral of the responses of the resolvent filters,
\[ B\,\mathfrak R_\ell[u_i\tensor u_i,\Id](f_{d,T},f_{d,T})=\frac4{\pi^2}\int_d^T\!\!\int_d^T\mathfrak k_{i,\ell}(x^2,y^2)\,\dif x\,\dif y . \]
Let \(\eta\downarrow0\), then \(d\downarrow0\) and \(T\to\infty\). The right side of the error bound tends to zero by (SG.11), the left side tends to \(\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\) by (i), and the double integrals of the nonnegative kernel (Proposition SG.3) increase to the integral over \((0,\infty)^2\). This proves the first equality of (SG.22).
The second equality is the change of variables \(x^2=s_N(m_1)\), \(y^2=s_N(m_2)\). By Proposition SG.1(i), \(\dif x=\frac12\sqrt{s_N(m_1)}\,\vartheta_N(m_1)\,\dif m_1\) in absolute value, so
\[ \begin{aligned} &\frac4{\pi^2}\int\!\!\int\mathfrak k_{i,\ell}(x^2,y^2)\,\dif x\,\dif y\\ &\qquad=\frac1{\pi^2}\int_0^\gamma\!\!\int_0^\gamma\mathfrak k_{i,\ell}(s_1',s_2')\sqrt{s_1's_2'}\,\vartheta_N(m_1)\vartheta_N(m_2)\,\dif m_1\,\dif m_2,\qquad s_k'\defeq s_N(m_k). \end{aligned} \]
In the coordinates of (SG.6) at \(s=s_N(m)\), writing \(\Lambda=\Lambda_N(m)\) and using \(\tilde s_1=s\Lambda=(\gamma-m)m/Q_m\),
\[ \frac{s}{s+s_1}=\frac{\gamma-m}{\gamma},\qquad \frac{\sqrt{\mu_i}\,\sqrt s}{s+\tilde s_1\mu_i}=\frac{\sqrt{\mu_i}}{\sqrt s\,(1+\Lambda\mu_i)} =\sqrt{\frac{Q_m}{\gamma-m}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\mu_i\Lambda}}{\sqrt m\,(1+\Lambda\mu_i)} . \]
Inserting these in (SG.16), multiplied by \(\sqrt{ss'}\), gives
\[ \mathfrak k_{i,\ell}(s_1',s_2')\sqrt{s_1's_2'}\,\vartheta_N(m_1)\vartheta_N(m_2)=W_i(m_1)W_i(m_2)\,\frac{\sqrt{q_1q_2}\,b_\ell}{\Delta_\ell}. \]
The scalars of \(\Delta_\ell\) transform in the same way. Since \(\mu_j/(s+\tilde s_1\mu_j)=f_j(m)/\tilde s_1\), \(s\tilde\sigma=\gamma-m\) and \(\sigma=m/\tilde s_1=Q_m/(\gamma-m)\),
\[ ss'\,\mathsf U\tilde\sigma\tilde\sigma'=\frac{\mathsf T_N\,q_1q_2}{m_1m_2},\qquad ss'\,\mathsf V\sigma\sigma'=\frac{q_1q_2}{\gamma},\qquad ss'\,\mathsf U\mathsf V=\frac{\mathsf T_N\,q_1q_2}{\gamma\,m_1m_2}. \]
With \(\mathsf D_\ell=\sqrt{q_1q_2}\,b_\ell\) and \(\mathsf M_\ell=q_1q_2e_\ell\) this gives \(\Delta_\ell=\Delta_\ell^N\), which proves the second equality.
For the lag sum, the negative lags are defined by the transposed kernels, whose integrals over the symmetric square equal those of the positive lags. Absolute convergence and the bound \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\ge\overline C_{i,0}(0)=1\) follow from (i) and the nonnegativity of every term.

Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
The exponent \(\alpha>0\) and the momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) are fixed. Along a sequence of positive integers \(N\) and \(B=B_N\),
\[ N\to\infty,\qquad B\to\infty,\qquad \gamma=\frac NB\to\infty, \]

Constants. Under Assumption S4 put
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.23} \begin{gathered} a_N\defeq\sqrt{c_N}\,N^{-\alpha/2},\qquad K_\alpha\defeq\frac{\pi}{\alpha\sin(\pi/\alpha)},\qquad i_{\mathrm c}\defeq\frac{B}{K_\alpha},\\ b_N\defeq\sqrt{c_N}\,\big(\gamma\,i_{\mathrm c}^{\alpha}\big)^{-1/2},\qquad d_\alpha\defeq\frac{c_0\,\pi}{\alpha\cos(\pi/(2\alpha))}, \end{gathered} \end{equation}

Definition SG.3 (Profiles)
Let \(S_\alpha(V)\defeq\int_0^1V(V+t^\alpha)^{-1}\dif t\) for \(V>0\), a strictly increasing bijection onto \((0,1)\), and \(V_\alpha\defeq S_\alpha^{-1}\). For \(0<x\le1\) and \(0<p<1\) put
\[ f_x(p)\defeq\frac{V_\alpha(p)}{V_\alpha(p)+x^\alpha},\qquad U_\alpha(p)\defeq\frac{V_\alpha(p)}{p(1-p)} . \]
The bulk profiles are
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.24} \begin{gathered} \Psi_\alpha(x)\defeq c_0\int_0^1\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p\,V_\alpha(p)}}\;f_x(p)\,\dif p,\\ C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\defeq\int_0^{1-\tau}\frac{f_x(p+\tau)-f_x(p)}{\tau}\sqrt{\frac{U_\alpha(p)}{U_\alpha(p+\tau)}}\,\dif p\quad(0<\tau<1),\\ \Gamma_\alpha(x)\defeq2\int_0^1C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\,\dif\tau . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
For \(\alpha>1\), the crossover profiles are, for \(y,X>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.25} \begin{aligned} \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\defeq\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty&\sqrt q\,\Lw(q)\,r_\bmom(q)^{-\frac{\alpha+1}2}\\ &\times\frac{y\,r_\bmom(q)^\alpha}{1+y\,r_\bmom(q)^\alpha}\Big(1+(\alpha-1)\frac{q\,r_\bmom'(q)}{r_\bmom(q)}\Big)\frac{\dif q}{q}, \end{aligned} \end{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.26} \begin{aligned} G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\defeq\frac1{\pi^2}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty &\frac{\sqrt{\zeta_X(u)\big(1-\zeta_X(u)\big)\zeta_X(v)\big(1-\zeta_X(v)\big)}}{\sqrt{uv}}\\ &\times\frac{\mathsf D_\ell(u,v)\,\vartheta_\infty(u)\vartheta_\infty(v)}{\Delta_\ell^\infty(u,v)}\,\dif u\,\dif v, \end{aligned} \end{equation}
where \(\mathsf D_\ell\) and \(\mathsf M_\ell\) are the functions of (SG.20), which depend on \((m_1,m_2)=(u,v)\) only through \((Q_u,Q_v)\), and
\[ \zeta_X(u)\defeq\frac{u^\alpha}{X^\alpha+u^\alpha},\qquad \vartheta_\infty(u)\defeq\frac1{E(u)}+\frac{\alpha-1}u,\]

The participation coordinate applies at \(\bmom=0\). The computation of Proposition SG.1(ii),(iii) uses only the equations of the branch, so with \(m(s)\in(0,1)\) defined by \(s_N(m(s))=s\),

Part (i) is the zero-lag coefficient. At \(\bmom=0\) all lag coefficients with \(\ell\ge1\) vanish (Theorem SD.7(iv)), so \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=\volraw_i(\ridge)=f_i(m(\ridge))\to\resscale\mu_i/(1+\resscale\mu_i)\), and \(\resscale\mu_i=(i_\#/i)^\alpha\). The sum is \(Bm_N(\resscale)=B\).

Remark SG.2 (Why the reference scale is not the no-momentum threshold)
For \(\alpha\le1\), along rows with \(i/i_\#\to x\in(0,\infty)\), the momentum drift satisfies \(\sqrt{\gamma\resscale}\,\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\to\infty\). Indeed \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to\infty\) and \(i/N\to0\) by Proposition SG.6, so Theorem SG.2(i) gives \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\log\gamma\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(\sim d\,a_N(\log\gamma)^{3/2}\) for \(\alpha=1\), while the no-momentum normalization is \((\gamma\resscale)^{-1/2}\sim a_N/\sqrt{1-\alpha}\), respectively \(a_N(\log\gamma)^{1/2}\), by (SG.36). The extra logarithm comes from the growth \(\log Q_m\sim2\abs{\log\bmom}\,m\) of the momentum participation (Lemma SG.1(iii)). A fixed effective batch size multiplies \(i_\#\) and \((\gamma\resscale)^{-1/2}\) by fixed factors and cannot remove a logarithm, which is why the crossover indices of Theorem SG.2 are not effective-batch versions of \(i_\#\). For \(\alpha>1\) the logarithm is absent and the reference scale \(i_{\mathrm c}=B/K_\alpha\) is asymptotic to \(i_\#\).
Remark SG.3 (Scope for the dynamics)
The comparison of Theorem SE.1 and of its row form Corollary SE.1 holds at a fixed positive normalized ridge and in proportional dimensions, with the restrictions of Remark SE.1. The profiles of this section concern zero (or negligible, Proposition SG.7) ridge and a diverging aspect ratio, which are outside those hypotheses. The deterministic model of the phase-comparison section inserts the coefficient pair of Theorem SG.1, with the exact covariance \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\), into the row equations (SE.13) as adopted coefficients; that insertion is a modeling assumption and not a consequence of Section SE. For nonnegative aggregates such as the noise constant of that model, Corollary SG.2 transfers the relative accuracy of Theorem SG.1 to weighted sums over rows \(i\ge n_N\); it makes no statement about the rows \(i<n_N\) or about trajectories.

SG.4 Power-law participation

From here on Assumption S4 holds. The participation function of a power law is a Riemann sum of the continuum function \(S_\alpha\) of Definition SG.3. Put
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.41} S_N(V)\defeq\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\frac{V}{V+(j/N)^\alpha},\qquad V_N\defeq S_N^{-1},\qquad \mathsf T(V)\defeq VS_\alpha'(V)=\int_0^1\frac{Vt^\alpha}{(V+t^\alpha)^2}\,\dif t . \end{equation}
With \(V=c_N\Lambda N^{-\alpha}\) one has \(\Lambda\mu_j=V(j/N)^{-\alpha}\), hence \(m_N(\Lambda)=\gamma S_N(V)\) and
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.42} \Lambda_N(m)=\frac{N^\alpha}{c_N}\,V_N\Big(\frac m\gamma\Big)\qquad(0<m<\gamma). \end{equation}
Lemma SG.5 (Continuum participation)
  1. (i)
    As \(V\downarrow0\), \(S_\alpha(V)\sim V/(1-\alpha)\) for \(\alpha<1\), \(S_1(V)=V\log(1+1/V)\sim V\log(1/V)\), and \(S_\alpha(V)\sim K_\alpha V^{1/\alpha}\) for \(\alpha>1\). As \(V\to\infty\), \(1-S_\alpha(V)\sim((\alpha+1)V)^{-1}\).
  2. (ii)
    \(\mathsf T(V)\asymp S_\alpha(V)\big(1-S_\alpha(V)\big)\) for \(V>0\).
  3. (iii)
    As \(p\downarrow0\), \(V_\alpha(p)\sim(1-\alpha)p\) for \(\alpha<1\), \(V_1(p)\sim p/\log(1/p)\), and \(V_\alpha(p)\sim(p/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) for \(\alpha>1\); and \(V_\alpha(p)\asymp(1-p)^{-1}\) for \(p\ge1/2\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is a scaling of the integral. For \(\alpha<1\), \(V^{-1}S_\alpha(V)=\int_0^1(V+t^\alpha)^{-1}\dif t\) increases to \(\int_0^1t^{-\alpha}\dif t=(1-\alpha)^{-1}\) as \(V\downarrow0\). For \(\alpha=1\) the integral is explicit. For \(\alpha>1\) the substitution \(t=V^{1/\alpha}u\) gives
\[ S_\alpha(V)=V^{1/\alpha}\int_0^{V^{-1/\alpha}}\frac{\dif u}{1+u^\alpha},\qquad \int_0^\infty\frac{\dif u}{1+u^\alpha}=\frac1\alpha\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{1/\alpha-1}}{1+t}\,\dif t=\frac{\pi}{\alpha\sin(\pi/\alpha)}=K_\alpha, \]
by the Beta integral and the reflection formula [85, (5.12.3), (5.5.3)]. As \(V\to\infty\), \(1-S_\alpha(V)=\int_0^1t^\alpha(V+t^\alpha)^{-1}\dif t\) and \(V(1-S_\alpha(V))\to\int_0^1t^\alpha\dif t\).
Part (ii) compares the ratio at the two ends. The same substitutions give \(\mathsf T/S_\alpha\to1\) as \(V\downarrow0\) for \(\alpha\le1\), \(\mathsf T/S_\alpha\to1/\alpha\) for \(\alpha>1\), and \(\mathsf T/(1-S_\alpha)\to1\) as \(V\to\infty\). Both \(\mathsf T\) and \(S_\alpha(1-S_\alpha)\) are continuous and positive on \((0,\infty)\), so their ratio is bounded above and below.
Part (iii) inverts (i).

Lemma SG.6 (Finite participation)
  1. (i)
    \(0\le S_\alpha(V)-S_N(V)\le1/N\) and \(\abs{VS_N'(V)-\mathsf T(V)}\le1/(2N)\) for \(V>0\).
  2. (ii)
    There is \(C\) such that, for \(M\ge1\), \(B\ge2\) and \(M/\gamma\le p\le1-M/\gamma\),
    \[ \Big|\log\frac{V_N(p)}{V_\alpha(p)}\Big|\le\frac{C}{BM}. \]
  3. (iii)
    \(V_N(p)\asymp(1-p)^{-1}\) for \(p\ge1/2\), uniformly in \(N\).
  4. (iv)
    If \(\alpha>1\), \(Bm\to\infty\) and \(m/\gamma\to0\), then \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\sim(Bm/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) and \(m\Lambda_N'(m)/\Lambda_N(m)\to\alpha\), locally uniformly in \(m\) on compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\) when \(m\) is held fixed.
  5. (v)
    If \(\alpha>1\), then \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\asymp(Bm)^\alpha\) for \(1/B\le m\le\gamma/2\) and \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\asymp Bm\) for \(0<m\le1/B\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is a Riemann-sum comparison. The function \(t\mapsto V(V+t^\alpha)^{-1}\) decreases from \(1\) on \([0,1]\), so \(S_N(V)\) is a lower Riemann sum of \(S_\alpha(V)\) that differs from the upper one by at most \(1/N\). The function \(t\mapsto Vt^\alpha(V+t^\alpha)^{-2}\) vanishes at \(0\), increases to its maximum \(1/4\) and then decreases, so its total variation is at most \(1/2\), and its Riemann sum differs from its integral by at most \(1/(2N)\).
Part (ii) follows from (i) and Lemma SG.5(ii). By (i), \(S_\alpha(V_N(p))\in[p,p+1/N]\), so \(V_N(p)=V_\alpha(p')\) with \(p\le p'\le p+1/N\), and
\[ \log\frac{V_\alpha(p')}{V_\alpha(p)}=\int_p^{p'}\frac{\dif p''}{\mathsf T(V_\alpha(p''))}\le\frac1N\sup_{p''\in[p,p']}\frac{C}{p''(1-p'')}. \]
On the stated range, \(p''\ge M/\gamma\) and \(1-p''\ge M/\gamma-1/N\ge M/(2\gamma)\), so \(p''(1-p'')\ge M/(4\gamma)\), and the right side is at most \(4C\gamma/(NM)=4C/(BM)\).
Part (iii) bounds the deficit. Since \(1-S_N(V)=N^{-1}\sum_j(j/N)^\alpha(V+(j/N)^\alpha)^{-1}\) and \(N^{-1}\sum_j(j/N)^\alpha\) is bounded above and below, \(1-S_N(V)\asymp\min(1,1/V)\) for \(V\) bounded below, which inverts to the claim.
Part (iv) inverts (i) at small participation. With \(p=m/\gamma\), \(S_\alpha(V_N(p))=p(1+O(1/(Np)))=p(1+O(1/(Bm)))\), so \(V_N(p)\sim(p/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) by Lemma SG.5(iii), and (SG.42) gives \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\sim(Np/K_\alpha)^\alpha=(Bm/K_\alpha)^\alpha\). For the derivative, \(\Lambda_N'/\Lambda_N=\gamma^{-1}/(VS_N'(V))\) at \(V=V_N(p)\), and by (i) and Lemma SG.5(ii) at small \(V\), \(VS_N'(V)=\mathsf T(V)+O(N^{-1})=\alpha^{-1}p\,(1+o(1))\), so \(m\Lambda_N'(m)/\Lambda_N(m)\to\alpha\).
Part (v) compares the sum \(Bm_N(\Lambda)=\sum_jn^\alpha(n^\alpha+j^\alpha)^{-1}\), \(n=(c_N\Lambda)^{1/\alpha}\), with integrals. If \(n\le1\), comparison with \(\sum_jj^{-\alpha}<\infty\) gives \(Bm_N\asymp n^\alpha\). If \(1\le n\le N\), the terms \(j\le n\) give at least \(\lfloor n\rfloor/2\), and splitting the sum at \(n\) and comparing the terms \(j>n\) with \(\int_n^\infty n^\alpha t^{-\alpha}\dif t=n/(\alpha-1)\) gives at most \(C_\alpha n\). The case \(n>N\) does not occur when \(m\le\gamma/2\), because then every term would exceed \(1/2\). Since \(Bm_N=Bm\), these give \(c_N\Lambda\asymp Bm\) for \(Bm\le1\) and \(c_N\Lambda=n^\alpha\asymp(Bm)^\alpha\) for \(Bm\ge1\), the bounded transition region included.

Proof.

\[ Bm_N(\Lambda)=\sum_j\frac{u_j}{1+u_j},\qquad B\Lambda m_N'(\Lambda)=\sum_j\frac{u_j}{(1+u_j)^2}. \]
The upper bound holds term by term. For the lower bound put
\[ S\defeq\sum_j\frac{u_j}{1+u_j}=Bm_N,\qquad T\defeq\sum_j\frac1{1+u_j}=N-S,\qquad F\defeq\sum_j\frac{u_j}{(1+u_j)^2}=B\Lambda m_N', \]

\[ S\le2F+K,\qquad T\le2F+N-K . \]
Since \(S,T\le N\), it follows that \(ST/N\le\min(S,T)\le2F+\min(K,N-K)\).
A window count bounds \(\min(K,N-K)\) by \(F\). Put \(\delta\defeq2^\alpha/(1+2^\alpha)^2\). For \(2^{-\alpha}\le u\le2^\alpha\),
\[ \frac u{(1+u)^2}-\delta=\frac{(2^\alpha-u)(2^\alpha u-1)}{(1+u)^2(1+2^\alpha)^2}\ge0, \]

This is the lower bound with \(c_\alpha\defeq\delta/(2\delta+2)\).

The bound on \(\vartheta_N\) follows with \(E(m)\asymp\min(m,1)\) (Lemma SG.1(iii)), and \(E\vartheta_N\ge1\) because \(\Lambda_N'/\Lambda_N\ge1/m\).

Lemma SG.10 (Averaged tail)
There is \(C\) such that, for every fixed \(M\ge M_0(\alpha,\bmom)\),
\[ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{1\le i\le N}\Big|\frac{\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i^{>M}}{a_N\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(i/N)}-1\Big|\le\frac CM . \]

Proof.
The tail integrand is \(\mathcal A\mathcal B_i\cdot E\vartheta_N\), and the factor \(E\vartheta_N\) is close to one inside \([M,\gamma-M]\). Indeed
\[ E(m)\vartheta_N(m)-1=E(m)\Big(\frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}-\frac1m+\frac1{\gamma-m}\Big)\ge0, \]
and Lemma SG.7 and Lemma SG.1(iii) bound it by \(C(m^{-1}+(\gamma-m)^{-1})\le2C/M\) there. The function \(\mathcal B_i\) varies slowly there:
\[ \frac{\mathcal B_i'(m)}{\mathcal B_i(m)}=-\frac1{2(\gamma-m)}-\frac1{2m}+\Big(\frac12-f_i(m)\Big)\frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}, \qquad\Big|\frac{\mathcal B_i'}{\mathcal B_i}\Big|\le C\Big(\frac1m+\frac1{\gamma-m}\Big), \]
uniformly in \(i\).

\[ C\Big(\mathcal B_i(M)+\mathcal B_i(\gamma-M)+\int_M^{\gamma-M}\mathcal B_i(m)\Big(\frac1m+\frac1{\gamma-m}\Big)\dif m\Big). \]

Lemma SG.14 (Determinant)
For every lag and all \(m_1,m_2\in(0,\gamma)\),
\[ 0\le\mathsf M_\ell\le m_-,\qquad \mathsf D_\ell^2\le(m_1-\mathsf M_\ell)(m_2-\mathsf M_\ell),\]

Proof.

Lemma SG.23 (Supercritical volatility)
The function \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}\) is finite, positive and continuous on \((0,\infty)\) and satisfies the two endpoint laws of (SG.28). Under Assumption S4, for every row sequence \(i\to\infty\): if \(X\to X_0\in(0,\infty)\) then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X_0)\); if \(X\to0\) then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2\); if \(X\to\infty\) and \(i/N\to0\) then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac{4K_\alpha}{\alpha-1}X\). Proof.
The profile is dominated by a product. By Lemma SG.15(i),(iii), \(\sum_\ell\mathsf D_\ell(u,v)\le C\,\mathsf w(u)\mathsf w(v)\) with \(\mathsf w(u)\defeq\min(\sqrt u,1)\).
