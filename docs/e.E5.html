E5: Power-law and large-batch asymptotics

SJ.15 Power-law and large-batch asymptotics

This engine computes the half-anisotropic coefficients \(\mathfrak d_i\) and \(\mathfrak v_i\) when \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\) and \(\Nout=\Nin=N\), in two regimes: the thin batch \(N/B\to\infty\) with a power-law spectrum, and the oversampled batch \(B_{\rm eff}>3(1+\bmom)^2N\) with an arbitrary spectrum, where only the drift is computed. It takes in the scalar fixed-point system of the deterministic equivalent (Section G.1) and the integral formulas for the drift and the volatility (Section G.2 and Section G.3), and it produces explicit profiles. At each finite dimension the coefficients are deterministic functions of the spectrum, of \(B\) and of \(\bmom\); the zero-ridge coefficient is their limit as \(\ridge\downarrow0\), and nothing is claimed about the algorithm at zero ridge.
With an isotropic input side, the fixed-point system reduces to a single scalar, the participation \(m\). Put
\[ m_N(\Lambda)=\frac1B\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\Lambda\mu_i}{1+\Lambda\mu_i},\qquad \Lambda_N\defeq m_N^{-1},\qquad f_i(m)=\frac{\Lambda_N(m)\mu_i}{1+\Lambda_N(m)\mu_i}. \]
Each eigenvalue enters with a soft indicator of \(\mu_i\ge1/\Lambda\), so \(Bm_N(\Lambda)\) is a soft count of the output directions above the level \(1/\Lambda\), and \(\sum_if_i(m)=Bm\). On the branch at the spectral parameter \(-s\) the participation equals \(r_\bmom(q)\defeq q\Fw(q)\), which counts, up to a bounded error, the past batches that the normalized update whitens (Lemma SG.1(iii)), and \(s\) fixes \(m\) through the exact relation \(s=(\gamma-m)m/(Q_m\Lambda_N(m))\), with \(Q_m=r_\bmom^{-1}(m)\); this is a decreasing bijection of \((0,\gamma)\) onto \((0,\infty)\) (Proposition SG.1). In this coordinate the drift is a single integral and the zero-lag volatility is \(f_i\) itself ((SG.7)):
\[ \mathfrak d_i(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\infty \frac{\Lw(Q_{m(\ridge+u^2)})}{\Fw(Q_{m(\ridge+u^2)})}\,f_i\big(m(\ridge+u^2)\big)\,\dif u, \qquad \volraw_i(\ridge)=f_i\big(m(\ridge)\big). \]
The volatility is a double integral over two participations with an explicit lag kernel ((SG.22)). With momentum \(r_\bmom\) is unbounded, so \(m\to\gamma\) as \(s\downarrow0\), and at zero ridge the zero-lag coefficient of every row is one, \(\volraw_i(0)=1\) (Proposition SG.2 and Proposition SG.4).
The drift integral splits at participation of order one into a head, where few past batches are whitened, and a tail, where many are. In the head \(q\) is small, the weight functions take their small-argument values \(\Fw\approx\sw2\) and \(\Lw\approx1\), and the integrand is a Lorentzian in \(u\) of width \(\sqrt{\gamma\sw2\mu_i}\). When the row is resolved, that is \(m_N(1/\mu_i)\to0\), the head part of the drift is (Lemma SG.11)
\[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i^{\le M}\sim\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}{\gamma\sw2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_iB_{\rm eff}}N}. \]
In the tail the weight function \(\sqrt q\,\Lw(q)\) oscillates with the geometric spacing of the weights and has no limit, but its average in the participation is exact. With \(\mathcal A(m)\defeq\sqrt{Q_m}\Lw(Q_m)/E(m)\) and \(E(m)\defeq Q_mr_\bmom'(Q_m)\) (Lemma SG.2),
\[ \sup_{t\ge m_0}\Big|\int_{m_0}^t\big(\mathcal A(m)-\sqrt{2\pi}\big)\,\dif m\Big|\le C_{\bmom,m_0}. \]
In the variable \(q\) each past batch contributes \(\int_0^\infty x^{-1/2}\Jfun(x)\,\dif x=\pi\,\EE\abs g=\sqrt{2\pi}\) once it is whitened, and \(m\) counts the whitened batches up to a bounded error. An integration by parts then replaces \(\mathcal A\) by \(\sqrt{2\pi}\) against any slowly varying weight ((SG.9)). This is where the constant \(c_0=\sqrt{2\pi}/\pi=\EE\abs g\) of the profiles comes from.
For a power law the participation is a Riemann sum of a continuum function, and the small-argument law of that function separates the cases \(\alpha<1\), \(\alpha=1\) and \(\alpha>1\). With \(V=c_N\Lambda N^{-\alpha}\) one has \(m_N(\Lambda)=\gamma S_N(V)\), where \(S_N(V)=N^{-1}\sum_jV/(V+(j/N)^\alpha)\) is within \(1/N\) of \(S_\alpha(V)=\int_0^1V(V+t^\alpha)^{-1}\dif t\), so that \(\Lambda_N(m)=(N^\alpha/c_N)V_N(m/\gamma)\) with \(V_N=S_N^{-1}\) ((SG.42), Lemma SG.6). As \(V\downarrow0\) (Lemma SG.5),
\[ S_\alpha(V)\sim\frac V{1-\alpha}\ (\alpha<1),\qquad S_1(V)\sim V\log\frac1V,\qquad S_\alpha(V)\sim K_\alpha V^{1/\alpha}\ (\alpha>1), \]
with \(K_\alpha=\pi/(\alpha\sin(\pi/\alpha))\). For \(\alpha>1\) this says that a participation \(m\) resolves about \(Bm/K_\alpha\) rows: \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\sim(Bm/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) when \(Bm\to\infty\) and \(m/\gamma\to0\) (Lemma SG.6(iv)). The amplitude \(c_N\) multiplies the drift by \(\sqrt{c_N}\) and leaves the volatility unchanged (Lemma SG.4), so the proofs take \(c_N=1\). A uniform statement over the rows reduces, by passing to subsequences, to row sequences along which \(i/N\) and the relevant ratios converge.
In the oversampled regime the branch is the momentum-free profile equation up to a factor that stays in \([1-\epsilon_{\rm os},1]\), with \(\epsilon_{\rm os}=3(1+\bmom)^2N/B_{\rm eff}\). In the rescaled variables \(\hat s=s/(\sw2\gin)\) and \(b=\tilde s_1(-s)/(\sw2\gin)\), where \(\gin=N/B\), the branch satisfies exactly (Lemma SH.4)
\[ \frac b{\widehat F}+m_{\hat\nu}(\hat s/b)=1,\qquad \widehat F=\frac{\Fw(q)}{\sw2}\in(0,1],\qquad m_\nu(c)=\int\frac{x\,\nu(\dif x)}{c+x}, \]
where \(\hat\nu\) is the empirical spectral distribution of \(\Sigmaout\); this is the same soft count as above, since \(m_N(\Lambda)=\gamma\,m_{\hat\nu}(1/\Lambda)\). The coupling gives \(q\Fw(q)<\gin\), which bounds \(q\), and the Gaussian inequality \(\Jfun(x)\ge1/(1+3x)\) (Cauchy–Schwarz with \(\EE g^4=3\)) then keeps \(\widehat F\) and \(\Lw\) above \(1-\epsilon_{\rm os}\) for every \(s\) (Lemma SH.3). A monotone comparison places \(b\) between \(\widehat Fb_0\) and \(b_0\), where \(b_0\) is the root at \(\widehat F=1\), and hence the drift integrand within the factors \((1-\epsilon_{\rm os})^{\pm1}\) of the integrand of the profile \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}\) (Lemma SH.5, Theorem SH.1). The substitution \(c=\hat s/b_0\) turns the profile equation into \(\Upsilon_\nu(c)\defeq c(1-m_\nu(c))=\hat s\), and one integration by parts gives the exact representation (Lemma SH.8)
\[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)=\frac{\sqrt2}\pi\,\mu\int_0^\infty\frac{\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c)}}{(c+\mu)^2}\,\dif c =\sqrt{\frac\mu2}\;\chi_\nu(\mu),\qquad \chi_\nu(\mu)=\int_0^\infty\varpi(y)\sqrt{1-m_\nu(\mu y)}\,\dif y, \]
with \(\varpi(y)=\frac2\pi\sqrt y\,(1+y)^{-2}\). Here \(\varpi\) is a probability density and \(1-m_\nu(c)\) is a soft fraction of the spectrum below \(c\), so the efficiency \(\chi_\nu(\mu)\in(0,1]\) averages the square root of the fraction of the spectrum below the level of the mode, on a logarithmic scale around it (Remark SH.2). The results on head, packed and tail modes are bounds on this average.
