Lemma SD.11 (Removal of the cutoffs in the model)
Couple \((\mathsf K_1,\mathsf K_2)\) with \((M^{\mathrm g}_t,M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell})\) by building both from the same samples. Under the size conditions, with \(\delta_{J,R}\) of (SD.44),
\begin{equation}\tag{SD.48} \frac1B\EE\norm{M^{\mathrm g}_t-\mathsf K_1}_\F^2+\frac1B\EE\norm{M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell}-\mathsf K_2}_\F^2\le3C_\Sigma^2\gamma_\star^2\delta_{J,R}^2 , \end{equation}
and for odd bounded Lipschitz \(f_1,f_2\) and \(\norm F,\norm G\le1\),
\[ \begin{aligned} &\abs{\EE\,\mathcal K^{F,G}[f_1,f_2](M^{\mathrm g}_t,M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell})-\EE\,\mathcal K^{F,G}[f_1,f_2](\mathsf K_1,\mathsf K_2)}\\ &\qquad\le\sqrt3\,C_\Sigma\gamma_\star^{3/2}\big(\operatorname{Lip}(f_1)\norm{f_2}_\infty+\norm{f_1}_\infty\operatorname{Lip}(f_2)\big)\,\delta_{J,R}. \end{aligned} \]
Proof.
The differences are sums of independent centered sample terms. A sample term \(B^{-1}\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z\tensor w)\Sigmain^{1/2}\) has squared Frobenius norm with expectation \(B^{-2}\Tr\Sigmaout\Tr\Sigmain\le C_\Sigma^2\gamma_\star^2\). In \(M^{\mathrm g}_t-\mathsf K_1\) the samples of age \(j>J\) carry the weight \(\emaw jg\) and those of age \(j\le J\) carry \(\emaw jg\mathbf 1_{\abs g>R}\), so
\[ \frac1B\EE\norm{M^{\mathrm g}_t-\mathsf K_1}_\F^2\le C_\Sigma^2\gamma_\star^2\Big(\sum_{j>J}\emaw j^2+\sum_{j\le J}\emaw j^2\tau_R\Big)\le C_\Sigma^2\gamma_\star^2\delta_{J,R}^2 . \]
In \(M^{\mathrm g}_{t+\ell}-\mathsf K_2\) the old samples carry \(\bmom^\ell\) times the same weights, and the fresh samples carry \(\emaw{\ell-m}g\mathbf 1_{\abs g>R}\), whose squared sum is at most \(\tau_R\); the two groups are disjoint, which gives at most \(2C_\Sigma^2\gamma_\star^2\delta_{J,R}^2\). The contraction bound is (SD.9) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
□
