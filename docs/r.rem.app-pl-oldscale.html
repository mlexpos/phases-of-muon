Remark SG.2 (Why the reference scale is not the no-momentum threshold)
For \(\alpha\le1\), along rows with \(i/i_\#\to x\in(0,\infty)\), the momentum drift satisfies \(\sqrt{\gamma\resscale}\,\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\to\infty\). Indeed \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to\infty\) and \(i/N\to0\) by Proposition SG.6, so Theorem SG.2(i) gives \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\log\gamma\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(\sim d\,a_N(\log\gamma)^{3/2}\) for \(\alpha=1\), while the no-momentum normalization is \((\gamma\resscale)^{-1/2}\sim a_N/\sqrt{1-\alpha}\), respectively \(a_N(\log\gamma)^{1/2}\), by (SG.36). The extra logarithm comes from the growth \(\log Q_m\sim2\abs{\log\bmom}\,m\) of the momentum participation (Lemma SG.1(iii)). A fixed effective batch size multiplies \(i_\#\) and \((\gamma\resscale)^{-1/2}\) by fixed factors and cannot remove a logarithm, which is why the crossover indices of Theorem SG.2 are not effective-batch versions of \(i_\#\). For \(\alpha>1\) the logarithm is absent and the reference scale \(i_{\mathrm c}=B/K_\alpha\) is asymptotic to \(i_\#\).
