Lemma SA.1 (Mode and row decomposition)
For every \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.8} \CMscr Q_i(\Delta)=\sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta),\qquad \tfrac12\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma^2=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\,\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta), \end{equation}
and if \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) then \(\frac12\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma^2=\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\CMscr Q_i(\Delta)\). Moreover \(\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma=0\) if and only if \(u_i^\T\Delta v_j=0\) for every pair \((i,j)\) with \(\mu_i\lambda_j>0\).
Proof.
The row identity is Parseval’s identity in the basis \((v_j)\): \(\Delta^\T u_i=\sum_j(u_i^\T\Delta v_j)\,v_j\), and the squared norm of the right side is \(\sum_j(u_i^\T\Delta v_j)^2\).
The risk identity is the eigenexpansion of the trace:
\[ \Tr\big(\Sigmaout\Delta\Sigmain\Delta^\T\big)=\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\Tr\big((u_i\tensor u_i)\Delta(v_j\tensor v_j)\Delta^\T\big)=\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j(u_i^\T\Delta v_j)^2 . \]
When \(\Sigmain=\Id\) all \(\lambda_j\) equal \(1\) and the row identity applies. The last claim holds because a finite sum of nonnegative terms vanishes exactly when each term does.
□
