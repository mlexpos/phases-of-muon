Lemma SE.26 (Block residual)
Let \(b_c\) be as in Lemma SE.24 and \(\macrotime_c\defeq t_{b_c}/B\). For every \(T>0\) and all bounded \(f,g\) on \([0,C_\Sigma]\),
\[ \sup_{\macrotime\le T}\Big|\int f\tensor g\,\dif\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime\wedge\macrotime_c)-\int f\tensor g\,\dif\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(0) -\int_0^{\macrotime\wedge\macrotime_c}\!\!\int f\tensor g\,\dif\mathsf F_B\big(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\sigma)\big)\,\dif\sigma\Big|\longrightarrow0 \]
in probability. The process and the integral are stopped at the same time.
Proof.
We may assume \(\norm f_\infty,\norm g_\infty\le1\). Write \(X=X_{f,g}\), \(\mathcal W=\mathcal W_{f,g}\) and \(J\defeq\lceil TB/L\rceil\).
The block identity. Let \(t=t_b\) be a block start with \(\Dt\in\mathcal A\), write \(D=\Dt\), and let the chains at time \(t\) be as in Definition SE.2, with \(S=\sum_{r<L}V_r\) and \(\mathcal E=\sum_{r<L}{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-S\). The block form of (SE.26) and the decomposition \(\EE_t\langle S,\mathcal WS\rangle=L^2\langle\overline V,\mathcal W\overline V\rangle+\EE_t\langle S-L\overline V,\mathcal W(S-L\overline V)\rangle\), with \(\overline V=\overline V(D)\), give the exact identity
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.28} \begin{aligned} \EE_t\big[X_{f,g}(\Delta_{t+L})-X_{f,g}(D)\big]={}&-2\lr\Big\langle\mathcal WD,\sum_{r<L}\EE_t{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\Big\rangle +\lr^2L^2\langle\overline V,\mathcal W\overline V\rangle\\ &+\lr^2LB\int f\tensor g\,\dif\mathfrak F_L(D) +\lr^2\,\EE_t\big[2\langle S,\mathcal W\mathcal E\rangle+\langle\mathcal E,\mathcal W\mathcal E\rangle\big], \end{aligned} \end{equation}
where \(\mathfrak F_L(D)\) is the finite-time measure of Theorem SD.8(iv) at the state \(\widehat A(D)\) and \(T=L\). Indeed, in the eigenbases, \(\EE_t\langle S-L\overline V,\mathcal W(S-L\overline V)\rangle=\sum_{i,j}f(\mu_i)g(\lambda_j)\EE_t\big((S-L\overline V)_{ij}\big)^2\), which by Lemma SE.2(i) is \(LB\) times the integral of \(f\tensor g\) against \(\mathfrak F_L(D)\).
The diffuse cutoff. By Lemma SE.24 with \(T+1\) in place of \(T\), the maximum \(\mathsf X_B\) of \(\delta(\Dt)\) over \(t\le t_{b_c}\wedge(T+1)B\) tends to zero in probability. Choose a deterministic \(\delta_B\downarrow0\) with \(\Pr(\mathsf X_B>\delta_B)\to0\), which is possible because \(\Pr(\mathsf X_B>\epsilon)\to0\) for every \(\epsilon>0\). Let \(b_d\defeq\inf\{b:\delta(\Delta_{t_b})>\delta_B\}\), \(b_\star\defeq b_c\wedge b_d\) and \(I_b\defeq\mathbf 1_{b<b_\star}\). With probability tending to one \(b_\star\wedge(J+1)=b_c\wedge(J+1)\), so it suffices to prove the claim with \(\macrotime_\star\defeq t_{b_\star}/B\) in place of \(\macrotime_c\). Since \(\Delta_0\in\mathcal A\), and \(\delta(\Delta_0)\le\delta_B\) for large \(B\) because \(\delta(\Delta_0)\le\mathsf X_B\) is deterministic, we have \(b_\star\ge1\).
The drift of a block. For \(1\le b<J\) with \(I_b=1\), put \(t=t_b\), \(D=\Dt\) and
\[ \Lambda_b\defeq\EE_t\big[X(\Delta_{t+L})-X(D)\big]-\frac LB\int f\tensor g\,\dif\mathsf F_B\big(\chi(D)\big). \]
Since \((L/B)\mathsf F_B(\chi(D))=-2\lr L\cR(D)^{-1/2}\mathfrak d\chi(D)+\lr^2LB\zeta\), (SE.28) writes \(\Lambda_b\) as the sum of six terms:
\[ \begin{aligned} \Lambda_b={}&-2\lr\sum_{r<\mathsf q}\big\langle\mathcal WD,\EE_t{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\overline V\big\rangle -2\lr\sum_{r=\mathsf q}^{L-1}\big\langle\mathcal WD,\EE_t{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\overline V\big\rangle\\ &+\lr^2\,\EE_t\big[2\langle S,\mathcal W\mathcal E\rangle+\langle\mathcal E,\mathcal W\mathcal E\rangle\big] +\lr^2L^2\langle\overline V,\mathcal W\overline V\rangle\\ &-2\lr L\Big(\langle\mathcal WD,\overline V\rangle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cR(D)}}\int f\tensor g\,\mathfrak d\,\dif\chi(D)\Big) +\lr^2LB\int f\tensor g\,\dif\big(\mathfrak F_L(D)-\zeta\big). \end{aligned} \]
We bound \(\EE[I_b\abs\cdot]\) of each term, using \(t\ge L\ge\mathsf q\), \(I_b\le\mathbf 1_{\mathfrak E_t}\) for the event of Proposition SE.6, and \(\norm{\mathcal WD}_\F,\norm{\mathcal WD}\le\norm D_\F\le C\).
  1. (a)
    The prefix term is at most \(2\lr\EE[\mathbf 1_{t<\tau^+}\abs{\sum_{r<\mathsf q}\langle\mathcal WD,{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle}]+2\lr\mathsf q\norm D_\F\norm{\overline V}_\F\le C\mathsf q^2/B\), by Lemma SE.21(ii) and (SE.15).
  2. (b)
    The post-prefix term is at most \(2\lr L\rho_B\), by Proposition SE.6 with \(\Pi_t=\mathcal W\Dt\).
  3. (c)
    The quadratic response is at most \(\lr^2LB\,C\,\EE[I_b\,\mathsf r_B(a_b)]\), by Lemma SE.22, where \(\mathsf r_B(a)\) is its right side and \(a_b=1+\norm{M^\circ_{t-1}}_\F/\sqrt B\). By Lemma SE.15, \(\EE[I_b(a_b+a_b^2)]\le C\), so \(\EE[I_b\mathsf r_B(a_b)]\le C\bar{\mathsf r}_B\) with \(\bar{\mathsf r}_B\defeq L^{-1}+B^{-1/2}+L^2/B+L^{3/2}/\sqrt B+L/\sqrt B+L^3/B\to0\).
  4. (d)
    The coherent mean term is at most \(\lr^2L^2C=(L/B)\cdot C\macrostep^2L/B\).
  5. (e)
    The drift replacement is at most \(2\lr L\norm D_\F\,\bar e^{(1)}_B\). Indeed, by Lemma SE.2(i) and Corollary SC.1 at the state \(\widehat A(D)\), \(u_i^\T\overline Vv_j=\mathfrak d_{ij}(u_i^\T Dv_j)/\sqrt{\cR(D)}+(E_\ridge)_{ij}\), so \(\langle\mathcal WD,\overline V\rangle-\cR(D)^{-1/2}\int f\tensor g\,\mathfrak d\,\dif\chi(D)=\sum_{i,j}f(\mu_i)g(\lambda_j)(u_i^\T Dv_j)(E_\ridge)_{ij}\), which (SC.13) bounds by \(\norm D_\F e^{(1)}_B(\widehat A(D))\), and \(\delta(D)\le\delta_B\).
  6. (f)
    The forcing replacement is at most \(\lr^2LB\big(\norm{\mathfrak F_L(D)-\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{int}}(\ridge)}_{\mathrm{TV}}+\bar e^{(2)}_B\big)\), and the first norm is at most \(2\mathsf K_\star(\bmom^L/(1-\bmom)+\bmom/(L(1-\bmom)^2))\) by Theorem SD.8(iv).
With \(\lr L=\macrostep L/B\) and \(\lr^2LB=\macrostep^2L/B\), every bound is \(L/B\) times a quantity that tends to zero uniformly in \(b\), except (a), which is \((L/B)\) times \(C\mathsf q^2/L\). Summing over the at most \(TB/L\) blocks,
\[ \EE\sum_{1\le b<J}I_b\abs{\Lambda_b}\le C(T+1)\Big[\frac{\mathsf q^2}{L}+\rho_B+\bar{\mathsf r}_B+\frac LB+\bar e^{(1)}_B+\bar e^{(2)}_B+\bmom^L+\frac1L\Big]\longrightarrow0 . \]
Decomposition of the path. For \(1\le j\le J\),
\[ X(\Delta_{t_{j\wedge b_\star}})-X(\Delta_{t_1})=\sum_{1\le b<j}I_b\xi_b+\sum_{1\le b<j}I_b\Big[\frac LB\int f\tensor g\,\dif\mathsf F_B\big(\chi(\Delta_{t_b})\big)+\Lambda_b\Big], \]
with \(\xi_b\) as in Lemma SE.20, whose bound tends to zero. The Riemann sum is compared with the integral. For \(t_b\le t'<t_{b+1}\) with \(b<b_\star\), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality over the pairs \((i,j)\) and Lemma SE.14(i) give
\[ \norm{\chi(\Delta_{t'})-\chi(\Delta_{t_b})}_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sum_{i,j}\abs{\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta_{t'})-\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta_{t_b})} \le\norm{\Delta_{t'}-\Delta_{t_b}}_\F\big(\norm{\Delta_{t'}}_\F+\norm{\Delta_{t_b}}_\F\big)\le\frac{CL}{\sqrt B}. \]
By Lemma SE.1(ii) on the set of measures with the bounds of \(\mathcal A^+\), the integral of \(\int f\tensor g\,\dif\mathsf F_B(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\sigma))\) over \([t_b/B,t_{b+1}/B)\) differs from the Riemann term by at most \((L/B)CL/\sqrt B\), and the sum over the blocks by at most \(C(T+1)L/\sqrt B\).
The first block and intermediate times. No stop occurs in the first block, and by (SE.26), \(\abs{X(\Delta_{t_1})-X(\Delta_0)}\le(2\norm{\Delta_0}_\F+\norm{\Delta_L-\Delta_0}_\F)\norm{\Delta_L-\Delta_0}_\F\le C(L/\sqrt B+L^2/B)\), while the integral over \([0,L/B]\) is at most \(CL/B\) by Lemma SE.1(i). For a time \(\macrotime\) between two block starts, the same bounds control the change of both sides over at most \(L\) steps. Collecting the bounds proves the claim.
□
