Lemma SJ.7 (Haar sign matrix)
Let \(U\) be Haar on \(O(n)\), \(n\ge2\), let \(a\in\R^n\) be nonzero, and put \(Y\defeq U^\T\sign(Ua)\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.24} \EE_U\big[Y\tensor Y\big]=\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)\Id+\frac{2n}\pi\,\frac{a\tensor a}{\norm a^2}. \end{equation}
The same holds conditionally on a random nonzero \(a\) independent of \(U\).
Proof.
Put \(v=a/\norm a\), so that \(Y=U^\T\sign(Uv)\). The vector \(Uv\) is uniform on the sphere, so it has no zero coordinate almost surely, and \(\norm Y^2=n\).
Invariance reduces the second moment to two numbers. If \(Q\) is orthogonal with \(Qv=v\), then \(UQ\) is Haar and replacing \(U\) by \(UQ\) replaces \(Y\) by \(Q^\T Y\). Hence \(\EE[Y\tensor Y]\) commutes with every such \(Q\). In an orthonormal basis starting with \(v\), reflections of single other basis vectors make the off-diagonal entries vanish, and permutations of the other basis vectors make their diagonal entries equal. Therefore
\[ \EE[Y\tensor Y]=c_n\,v\tensor v+d_n(\Id-v\tensor v). \]
The two numbers follow from two scalar identities. First, \(v^\T Y=(Uv)^\T\sign(Uv)=\norm{Uv}_1\), so \(c_n=1+2(n-1)/\pi\) by (SJ.21). Second, the trace is \(\EE\norm Y^2=n\), so \(c_n+(n-1)d_n=n\). Solving gives \(d_n=1-2/\pi\) and \(c_n-d_n=2n/\pi\). Conditioning on an independent \(a\) gives the last claim.
□
