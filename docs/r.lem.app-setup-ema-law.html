Lemma SA.5 (Stationary EMA law)
In the frozen-state model, index the samples by time as above, and write \(\Sigma_G\defeq\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Gt)\) for the batch covariance (SA.28).
  1. (i)
    The series \(\Mt=\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw{j}G_{t+1-j}\) and the sample series \(\sum_{j,a}\emaw{j}\abs{d_{ja}}\norm{z_{ja}}\norm{w_{ja}}\) converge absolutely.
  2. (ii)
    For every integer \(t\), \(\Mt=\bmom M_{t-1}+(1-\bmom)\Gt\), and the sequence \((\Mt)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) is strictly stationary.
  3. (iii)
    \(\EE\Mt=\EE\Gt\), \(\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt)=\sw2\,\Sigma_G\), and \(\EE\norm{\Mt-\EE\Gt}_\F^2=\sw2\,\mathsf V_B\).
  4. (iv)
    For every lag \(\ell\ge0\), \(\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt,\operatorname{vec}M_{t+\ell})=\bmom^\ell\,\sw2\,\Sigma_G\).
  5. (v)
    For every positive integer \(T\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SA.31} \Cov\Big(\sum_{t=1}^T\operatorname{vec}(\Mt-\EE\Gt)\Big) =\sw2\Big[T+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)\bmom^\ell\Big]\Sigma_G =\Big[T-\frac{2\bmom(1-\bmom^T)}{1-\bmom^2}\Big]\Sigma_G , \end{equation}
    the two-sided lag sum \(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt,\operatorname{vec}M_{t+\ell})\) converges absolutely and equals \(\Sigma_G\), and \(T^{-1}\Cov(\sum_{t\le T}\operatorname{vec}(\Mt-\EE\Gt))\to\Sigma_G\) as \(T\to\infty\).
Proof.
Part (i) holds because \(\sum_j\emaw j=1\) and the batches are identically distributed.
The recursion is a reindexing of the series:
\[ \bmom M_{t-1}+(1-\bmom)\Gt=\sum_{j\ge0}(1-\bmom)\bmom^{j+1}G_{t-j}+(1-\bmom)G_{t+1} =\sum_{j\ge1}\emaw{j}G_{t+1-j}+\emaw0G_{t+1}=\Mt . \]
Strict stationarity holds because the batches are independent and identically distributed, so that a common shift of the times is a relabeling of the batches. This proves (ii).
The moments follow from the orthogonality of the centered batches. For \(k\le l\), independence and centering give
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.32} \EE\Big\|\sum_{j=k}^l\emaw{j}\big(G_{t+1-j}-\EE\Gt\big)\Big\|_\F^2=\mathsf V_B\sum_{j=k}^l\emaw{j}^2 . \end{equation}
The mean is \(\sum_j\emaw j\,\EE\Gt=\EE\Gt\) by (SA.18); the covariance is \(\sum_j\emaw j^2\,\Sigma_G=\sw2\Sigma_G\) by the same orthogonality; and the Frobenius variance is (SA.32) with \(k=0\), \(l\to\infty\). This proves (iii).
The lag covariance follows from the decomposition of a later buffer into the past and a new part. By the reindexing of the series, for \(\ell\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.33} M_{t+\ell}=\bmom^\ell\,\Mt+\sum_{r=0}^{\ell-1}\emaw{r}\,G_{t+\ell+1-r}, \end{equation}
where the finite sum involves only batches of times larger than \(t+1\) and is therefore independent of \(\Mt\). Centering and bilinearity of the covariance give \(\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt,\operatorname{vec}M_{t+\ell})=\bmom^\ell\Cov(\operatorname{vec}\Mt)=\bmom^\ell\sw2\Sigma_G\). This proves (iv).
The block covariance is a sum over the \(T^2\) pairs of times. By (iii) and (iv), and the symmetry of the covariance under exchanging the two times,
\[ \Cov\Big(\sum_{t=1}^T\operatorname{vec}(\Mt-\EE\Gt)\Big)=\sum_{t,t'=1}^T\bmom^{\abs{t-t'}}\sw2\Sigma_G =\sw2\Big[T+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)\bmom^\ell\Big]\Sigma_G , \]
which is the first form of (SA.31). The scalar identity
\[ \sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)\bmom^\ell=\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^k\bmom^\ell=\frac{\bmom}{1-\bmom}\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}(1-\bmom^k) =\frac{T\bmom}{1-\bmom}-\frac{\bmom(1-\bmom^T)}{(1-\bmom)^2} \]
follows from writing \(T-\ell\) as the number of \(k\) with \(\ell\le k\le T-1\) and exchanging the order of summation, and inserting it with \(\sw2=(1-\bmom)/(1+\bmom)\) gives the second form. The two-sided lag sum converges absolutely by the geometric majorant \(\bmom^{\abs\ell}\) and equals
\[ \sw2\Big(1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\bmom^\ell\Big)\Sigma_G=\frac{1-\bmom}{1+\bmom}\cdot\frac{1+\bmom}{1-\bmom}\,\Sigma_G=\Sigma_G , \]
and the limit of \(T^{-1}\) times (SA.31) is the same value. This proves (v).
□
