Theorem SG.3 (Volatility crossover and bulk profile)
Assume Assumption S4 and let \(i=i_N\) be a row sequence.
  1. (i)
    (Bulk.) If \(i/N\to x\in(0,1]\) and \(\ell=\ell_N\) are integers with \(\ell/\gamma\to\tau\in(0,1)\), then
    \[ \overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\longrightarrow C_{\alpha,x}(\tau),\qquad \frac{\mathfrak v_i(0)}{\gamma}\longrightarrow\Gamma_\alpha(x), \]
    locally uniformly in \(x\in(0,1]\) and \(\tau\in(0,1)\).
  2. (ii)
    Let \(0<\alpha\le1\), let \(i\to\infty\), and put
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.33} i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\defeq c_0^{2/\alpha}\,i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}=\begin{cases}N\big[(1-\alpha)/(\sw2\gamma\log^2\gamma)\big]^{1/\alpha},&\alpha<1,\\ 9B/(4\sw2\log^3\gamma),&\alpha=1.\end{cases} \end{equation}
    If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to y\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sw2\,\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1+2c_0y^{\alpha/2}+y^\alpha\), uniformly for \(y\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to0\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to\infty\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac{\gamma(i/N)^\alpha\big[\log((N/i)^\alpha)\big]^2}{1-\alpha}\quad(\alpha<1),\qquad \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac49\,\frac iB\,\big[\log(N/i)\big]^3\quad(\alpha=1). \]
  3. (iii)
    Let \(\alpha>1\) and \(i\to\infty\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to X_0\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X_0)\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to0\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to\infty\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac{4K_\alpha^2}{\alpha-1}\,\frac iB . \]

Definitions and assumptions used

Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
The exponent \(\alpha>0\) and the momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) are fixed. Along a sequence of positive integers \(N\) and \(B=B_N\),
\[ N\to\infty,\qquad B\to\infty,\qquad \gamma=\frac NB\to\infty, \]
and the spectrum is \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\) for \(1\le i\le N\), with amplitudes \(c_N>0\), with \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\) and \(\Nout=\Nin=N\).
This theorem gives the volatility column of Table 1 for \(\bmom>0\). On resolved rows \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2=B_{\rm eff}/B\): the row is fully whitened at every step, and (as the proof shows for \(\alpha>1\)) momentum correlates the updates \(\ell\) steps apart by \(\bmom^{\abs\ell}\). On unresolved rows \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), which grows with \(\gamma=N/B\), and part (i) identifies the correlation of updates \(\tau\gamma\) steps apart in a bulk row as \(C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\). For \(\alpha\le1\) the crossover profile \(1+2c_0y^{\alpha/2}+y^\alpha\) is not a sum of the two endpoint laws: it is the second moment of a sum of two correlated terms. For \(\alpha>1\) the crossover profile is \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}\). For \(\alpha\le1\) the crossover index differs from the drift index by the fixed factor \(c_0^{2/\alpha}\). This sketch also proves the volatility half of the uniform approximation behind Corollary SG.1.
Proof sketch.
The zero-ridge volatility is a positive double integral over two participations, summed over the lags (Proposition SG.4):
\[ \mathfrak v_i(0)=\frac1{\pi^2}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\int_0^\gamma\!\!\int_0^\gamma W_i(m_1)W_i(m_2)\,\frac{\mathsf D_\ell(m_1,m_2)}{\Delta^N_\ell(m_1,m_2)}\,\dif m_1\,\dif m_2 . \]
It is obtained from the closed-form lag kernel of Section G.3 by writing the regularized filter as an integral of resolvents and changing variables to the participation. Here \(W_i\) is the weight of the row, \(\mathsf D_\ell\) correlates two buffers \(\ell\) steps apart through the past batches they share, and the determinant \(\Delta^N_\ell\in(0,1]\) of the kernel is close to one when the smaller participation is much smaller than the larger one and than \(\gamma\) (Lemma SG.14).
In the bulk, the lag \(\ell\) couples the participation \(m\) to \(m+\ell\), because shifting the buffer by \(\ell\) steps moves every past batch by \(\ell\) places. Exactly, \(\mathsf D_\ell=(r_\bmom(q_1e^t)-r_\bmom(q_1))/(2\sinh(t/2))\) with \(t=\log(\bmom^{2\ell}q_2/q_1)\), and for large \(u\), \(r_\bmom(e^u)\) is within \(Ce^{-u/2}\) of a function that grows by exactly one when \(u\) grows by \(2\abs{\log\bmom}\). The kernel \(\mathsf D_\ell/(\pi^2E(m_1)E(m_2))\) therefore concentrates on the shell \(m_2=m_1+\ell+O(1)\) and carries mass one per unit of \(m_1\), by the identity (Lemma SG.22)
\[ \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{t}{2\sinh(t/2)}\,\dif t=\pi^2 . \]
In the coordinates \(m=\gamma p\) and \(\ell=\tau\gamma\) the row factors converge to the function \(A_x(p)\) and the determinant to its continuum limit \(\Delta_{\mathrm c}(p,p+\tau)\), both of the sketch for Proposition SG.5, so \(\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\to C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\) (the identity of Lemma SG.21). The lag sum is then a Riemann sum with mesh \(1/\gamma\); lags beyond \((1+\eta)\gamma\) contribute \(O(\gamma\bmom^{\ell-\gamma})\) each, because the later buffer then shares no whitened batch, and \(\mathfrak v_i(0)/\gamma\to2\int_0^1C_{\alpha,x}=\Gamma_\alpha(x)\).
For \(\alpha\le1\) the mesoscopic rows are governed by the coefficient with every determinant replaced by one, which is a perfect square. With one Gaussian \(g\) shared by the two buffers and \(\mathcal Z_g(q)=\sqrt q\sum_a\emaw a\,g/(1+\emaw a^2qg^2)\), the lag sum factorizes as \(\sum_\ell\mathsf D_\ell(m_1,m_2)=\EE[\mathcal Z_g(Q_{m_1})\mathcal Z_g(Q_{m_2})]\) (Lemma SG.15). The flat coefficient is therefore \(\EE\,\mathcal V_i(g)^2\) with \(\mathcal V_i(g)=\pi^{-1}\int_0^\gamma W_i\,\mathcal Z_g(Q_m)\,\dif m\), and it lies below \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\). The head of \(\mathcal V_i\) is linear in \(g\), and in the tail \(\mathcal Z_g/E\) averages to \(\pi\sign(g)\), as \(\mathcal A\) does for the drift, so (Lemma SG.18)
\[ \Big\|\mathcal V_i(g)-\frac g{\sqrt{\sw2}}-b\,\sign(g)\Big\|_{L^2(g)}=o(1+b),\qquad b=\frac{\sqrt\gamma\,x^{\alpha/2}\Psi_\alpha(x)}{c_0}. \]
With \(\EE g^2=\EE\sign(g)^2=1\) and \(\EE[g\sign(g)]=c_0\) the flat coefficient is \(1/\sw2+(2c_0/\sqrt{\sw2})\,b+b^2\) up to \(o((1+b)^2)\). The exact coefficient exceeds it by \(o((1+b)^2)\): off the diagonal the determinant is close to one, the strip \(m_1\asymp m_2\) carries little mass, and the top of the participation range contributes \(O(\gamma x^\alpha)\) (Lemma SG.19, Lemma SG.17 and Lemma SG.20). At \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to y\) one has \(b\to y^{\alpha/2}/\sqrt{\sw2}\), which gives \(\sw2\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1+2c_0y^{\alpha/2}+y^\alpha\).
For \(\alpha>1\) the rows near \(i_{\mathrm c}\) have a limit profile in which nothing is averaged. With \(X=i/i_{\mathrm c}\) and \(m\) of order one, the participation scaling of Section G.5 gives \(f_i(m)\to\zeta_X(m)\), the normalized sum \(\mathsf T_N\) becomes the integral \(t_\alpha(m_1,m_2)\), and \(\Delta^N_\ell\to\Delta^\infty_\ell\), so \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\) (Lemma SG.23). Along rows with \(X\to0\) or \(X\to\infty\), the rescalings that give the ends of \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}\) (Proposition SG.5(ii)), carried out with the dimension errors, give the resolved limit \(1/\sw2\) and the mesoscopic law \(4K_\alpha X/(\alpha-1)=4K_\alpha^2i/((\alpha-1)B)\).
