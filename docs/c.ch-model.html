Model, algorithm and standing assumptions

The model is the matrix linear regression of Section 1.1: a parameter \(W\) of size \(\Nout\times\Nin\), a teacher \(\Wstar\), independent Gaussian features with covariances \(\Sigmaout=\sum_i\mu_i\,u_i\tensor u_i\) and \(\Sigmain=\sum_j\lambda_j\,v_j\tensor v_j\), and the risk \(\cR(W)=\tfrac12\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}(W-\Wstar)\Sigmain^{1/2}}_\F^2=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\,\CMscr Q_{ij}\). The minibatch gradient (2) at the error \(\Dt=\Wt-\Wstar\) is written \(\Gt^\circ\); the superscript \(^\circ\) marks physical objects, as opposed to the normalized ones divided by \(\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\). For \(\ridge>0\) the regularized polar map is \(\Qridge\ridge(K)=(KK^\T+\ridge\Id)^{-1/2}K\) ((SA.12)). The three statements below are standing for the whole appendix: the first fixes the law of the data, the second the algorithm whose risk the dynamics tracks, and the third the limit in which every asymptotic statement is made. The random-matrix results add one condition on the state, that the error be spectrally diffuse, which the dynamical theorem shows persists, from a diffuse initial state, over the time window it covers.

Assumption S1 (Data-target normalization)
The input and output features are independent and Gaussian, \(\Xin\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigmain)\) and \(\Xout\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigmaout)\), with positive semidefinite covariances \(\Sigmain\in\R^{\Nin\times\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nout}\). They are represented in whitened form as \(\Xin=\Sigmain^{1/2}w\) and \(\Xout=\Sigmaout^{1/2}z\) with \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) and \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) independent; the whitened columns of a batch are written \((w_a,z_a)_{a\le B}\).
This is the data model of Section 1.1. In the whitened form the residual of a sample is \(d=z^\T\At w\) with the rotated error \(\At=\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Dt\Sigmain^{1/2}\), and \(\cR(t)=\tfrac12\norm{\At}_\F^2\) ((SA.9)). Every random-matrix statement uses the normalized residual \(d/\sqrt{2\cR(t)}=z^\T\Ahat w\), which has unit variance and is close to a standard Gaussian when the normalized error \(\Ahat\) has small operator norm (Lemma SA.7). Gaussianity is used for these computations (Wick pairings and Stein’s identity); the exact one-step risk recursion needs only second moments.
Definition SA.2 (The algorithm)
Fix a momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), a normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\), a deterministic step sequence \((\lr_t)_{t\ge0}\) with \(\lr_t\ge0\), an initial parameter \(W_0\) and an initial buffer \(M_{-1}^\circ\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), both given before the first batch is drawn (the default is \(M_{-1}^\circ=0\)). Let \(t_{\mathrm{stop}}\defeq\inf\{t\ge0:\cR(t)=0\}\in\{0,1,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}\). The algorithm is, for \(t\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SA.15} \begin{gathered} M_t^\circ=\bmom\,M_{t-1}^\circ+(1-\bmom)\,\Gt^\circ,\qquad W_{t+1}=\Wt-\lr_t\,\Gtilde^\circ,\\ \Gtilde^\circ\defeq\begin{cases}\Qridge{\ridgephys_t}(M_t^\circ),&t<t_{\mathrm{stop}},\\ 0,&t\ge t_{\mathrm{stop}},\end{cases} \qquad \ridgephys_t\defeq2\cR(t)\,\ridge . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
This is Muon\(_\varphi\) of Section 1.2 with the regularized polar map \(\Qridge\ridge\), applied at the physical ridge \(2\cR(t)\ridge\). Since \(\Qridge{2\cR\ridge}(\sqrt{2\cR}\,K)=\Qridge\ridge(K)\) (Proposition SA.3), the update is the regularized polar factor, at the fixed normalized ridge \(\ridge\), of the buffer divided by \(\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\); this is why the laws computed later do not depend on the risk. The stopping time only matters with momentum, where the buffer can be nonzero at a state of zero risk. A heavy-ball buffer at ridge \(\ridge'\) is the same algorithm at ridge \((1-\bmom)^2\ridge'\) (Remark SA.2), and no statement concerns \(\ridge\downarrow0\) or Newton–Schulz iterations.
Assumption S2 (Proportional regime)
The dimensions \(\Nin\), \(\Nout\), \(B\) tend to infinity jointly, with aspect ratios converging to constants:
\[ \frac{\Nin}{B} \to \gin \in (0,\infty), \qquad \frac{\Nout}{B} \to \gout \in (0,\infty). \]
The covariance operator norms remain uniformly bounded: there exists a constant \(C > 0\), independent of \(\Nin\) and \(\Nout\), such that
\[ \|\Sigmain\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\leq\; C, \qquad \|\Sigmaout\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\leq\; C. \]
We refer to the joint limit as the proportional regime.
This is the proportional limit of Theorem 2.1; the main text’s \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) is the case \(\gin=\gout\). No lower bound on the covariances and no rank condition is assumed. The random-matrix results of the supplement are proved under the stronger Assumption S3, for Gaussian data in the frozen-state model of Section C.1: explicit bounds \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\) and \(\norm{\Sigmain},\norm{\Sigmaout}\le C_\Sigma\) along the sequence, a normalized ridge in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), and a spectrally diffuse normalized error, \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\) with \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\).
