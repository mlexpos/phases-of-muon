Lemma SE.10 (Derivative coefficients)
Let \(s_\circ>0\) and \(p\ge1\). In the setting of Lemma SE.9, let \(M_0\) be fixed, \((\mathsf u,\mathsf v)=(\Sigmaout^{1/2}z/\sqrt B,\Sigmain^{1/2}w/\sqrt B)\) and \(d=z^\T\widehat Aw\) with \((z,w)\) standard Gaussian and \(\norm{\widehat A}_\F=1\). For \(s\ge s_\circ\) and \(\varpi\in[0,1]\),
\[ \begin{gathered} \norm{\partial_d\alpha_1-\varpi f_x'(d)}_p\le\frac{C\varpi}{\sqrt B},\qquad \norm{\partial_d\alpha_2-\varpi^2\sigma\psi_x'(d)}_p\le\frac{C\varpi^2}{s\sqrt B},\\ \norm{\partial_d\alpha_3+s\varpi^2\tilde\sigma\psi_x'(d)}_p\le\frac{C\varpi^2}{\sqrt B}, \end{gathered} \]
and \(\norm{\partial_d\alpha_1}_p\le C\varpi\), \(\norm{\partial_d\alpha_2}_p\le C\varpi^2/s\), \(\norm{\partial_d\alpha_3}_p\le C\varpi^2\), with \(C\) depending only on \((p,s_\circ,\gamma_\star,C_\Sigma)\).
Proof.
The scalars concentrate at the traces of the minor. Put \(\overline{\mathsf U}\defeq s\mathsf U\), \(\overline{\mathsf V}\defeq s\mathsf V\) and \(\overline{\mathsf C}\defeq\sqrt s\mathsf C\). Then \(\overline{\mathsf U}=z^\T F z\) with \(F\defeq s\Sigmaout^{1/2}R_s(M_0)\Sigmaout^{1/2}/B\), whose mean is \(\Tr F=s\sigma\) and whose Frobenius norm is at most \(C_\Sigma\sqrt{\Nout}/B\le C/\sqrt B\), since \(\norm{sR_s(M_0)}\le1\). The Hanson–Wright inequality [100, Theorem 6.2.1], integrated over the tail, gives \(\norm{\overline{\mathsf U}-s\sigma}_p\le C_p\norm F_\F\le C_p/\sqrt B\), and likewise \(\norm{\overline{\mathsf V}-s\tilde\sigma}_p\le C_p/\sqrt B\). The bilinear form \(\overline{\mathsf C}=z^\T F'w\) with \(F'\defeq\sqrt s\Sigmaout^{1/2}\mathsf X_s(M_0)\Sigmain^{1/2}/B\) is, given \(w\), centered Gaussian with variance \(\norm{F'w}^2\), so \(\norm{\overline{\mathsf C}}_p\le C_p\norm{F'}_\F\le C_p/\sqrt B\), using \(\norm{\sqrt s\mathsf X_s}\le1/2\) from Lemma SB.3.
The coefficients are rational in the scaled scalars, with denominators bounded below along an interpolation. Put, for \(\vartheta\in[0,1]\),
\[ (\mathsf U_\vartheta,\mathsf V_\vartheta,\mathsf C_\vartheta)\defeq\big((1-\vartheta)\mathsf U+\vartheta\sigma,\ (1-\vartheta)\mathsf V+\vartheta\tilde\sigma,\ (1-\vartheta)\mathsf C\big),\qquad \Lambda_\vartheta\defeq(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C_\vartheta)^2+s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U_\vartheta\mathsf V_\vartheta . \]
Since all four of \(\mathsf U,\mathsf V,\sigma,\tilde\sigma\) are nonnegative, \(\mathsf U_\vartheta\mathsf V_\vartheta\ge(1-\vartheta)^2\mathsf U\mathsf V\), so \(\Lambda_\vartheta\) is at least the quantity \(\Lambda\) computed with the residual \((1-\vartheta)d\), and (SB.16) gives \(\Lambda_\vartheta^{-1}\le(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt{s_\circ}}\abs d\mathsf N)^2\). After division by their leading scales \(\varpi\), \(\varpi^2/s\) and \(\varpi^2\), the three expressions (SE.20) are rational functions of \((\overline{\mathsf U},\overline{\mathsf V},\overline{\mathsf C})\) and \(d\), for instance \(\partial_d\alpha_1/\varpi=[(1+\varpi d\overline{\mathsf C}/\sqrt s)^2-\varpi^2d^2\overline{\mathsf U}\,\overline{\mathsf V}/s]/\Lambda^2\). Along the interpolation their partial derivatives in the three scaled scalars are bounded by a fixed polynomial in \((1+\abs d)(1+\mathsf N)\), because \(\abs{\overline{\mathsf U}_\vartheta},\abs{\overline{\mathsf V}_\vartheta}\le\mathsf N+C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\), \(\abs{\overline{\mathsf C}_\vartheta}\le\mathsf N/4\) and \(s^{-1/2}\le s_\circ^{-1/2}\). At \(\vartheta=1\) the denominator is \(\Lambda_1=1+xd^2\), and the three normalized expressions equal \(f_x'(d)\), \(s\sigma\psi_x'(d)\) and \(-s\tilde\sigma\psi_x'(d)\), since \(f_x'(d)=(1-xd^2)/(1+xd^2)^2\) and \(\psi_x'(d)=2d/(1+xd^2)^2\). The mean value theorem along \(\vartheta\), Hölder’s inequality, the concentration bounds of the first paragraph and Lemma SB.6 prove the three differences. The same polynomial bounds at \(\vartheta=0\) prove the last three bounds.
□
