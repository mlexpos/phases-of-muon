Lemma SG.12 (Cutoff estimates)
Let \(0<\alpha\le1\), \(c_N=1\), and let \(i\to\infty\) with \(x=i/N\to0\). Put \(\nu\defeq1\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(\nu\defeq3/2\) for \(\alpha=1\), and
\[ T\defeq\log(x^{-\alpha})\ (\alpha<1),\quad T\defeq\log(1/x)\ (\alpha=1),\qquad W\defeq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}}{a_N}=\sw2^{-1/2}e^{(T-\log\gamma)/2}. \]
Then \(\Psi_\alpha(x)\sim dT^\nu\) with \(d\) of (SG.32), and for fixed \(M\):
  1. (i)
    if \(W/(\log\gamma)^\nu\to z\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\sim d(\log\gamma)^\nu\sim\Psi_\alpha(x)\);
  2. (ii)
    if \(W/(\log\gamma)^\nu\to\infty\), then \(\Psi_\alpha(x)+\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)=o(W)\);
  3. (iii)
    if \(T\le\log\gamma+C_1\log\log\gamma\) for a constant \(C_1\), then \(\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\sim\Psi_\alpha(x)\); this applies when \(W/(\log\gamma)^\nu\to0\), and then \(W=o(\Psi_\alpha(x))\).
Proof.
The equivalence \(\Psi_\alpha(x)\sim dT^\nu\) is Lemma SG.9. By Lemma SG.5(iii), on a fixed small interval \(0<p<p_0\) the integrand of \(\Psi_\alpha\), without the factor \(c_0\), is comparable, uniformly in \(0<x<p_0\), to
\[ \frac1{p+x^\alpha}\quad(\alpha<1),\qquad\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal L(p)}}{p+x\mathcal L(p)}\quad(\alpha=1),\qquad\mathcal L(p)\defeq1+\log(1/p), \]
and above \(p_0\) all integrals are bounded uniformly in \(x\).
For (i), \(W\asymp(\log\gamma)^\nu\) gives \(T=\log\gamma+2\nu\log\log\gamma+O(1)\). Then \(x^\alpha\), and for \(\alpha=1\) also \(x\mathcal L(p)\) for \(p\ge M/\gamma\), is \(o(M/\gamma)\), so on \(p\ge M/\gamma\) the integrand of \(\Psi_\alpha\) is that of \(\Psi_\alpha\) at \(x=0\) to leading order, and its integral from \(M/\gamma\) is \(d(\log\gamma)^\nu(1+o(1))\), as in the proof of Lemma SG.9. Since \(T\sim\log\gamma\), this is also \(\Psi_\alpha(x)(1+o(1))\).
For (ii), \(T=\log\gamma+2\log W+\log\sw2\), so \(T^\nu\le C((\log\gamma)^\nu+(\log W)^\nu)=o(W)\), and \(\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}\le\Psi_\alpha\le C(1+T^\nu)\).
For (iii), let \(R_{\mathrm{rm}}\defeq\Psi_\alpha(x)-\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\) be the removed part, with \(\eta\defeq M/\gamma\). For \(\alpha<1\) the majorant gives
\[ R_{\mathrm{rm}}\le C\log\big(1+\eta x^{-\alpha}\big)=C\log\big(1+Me^{T-\log\gamma}\big). \]
For \(\alpha=1\) put \(s_x\defeq x(1+T)\), which is comparable to the solution of \(p=x\mathcal L(p)\). If \(\eta\le s_x\), the bound \(\sqrt{\mathcal L}/(x\mathcal L)\le1/(x\sqrt{\mathcal L(\eta)})\) gives \(R_{\mathrm{rm}}\le C\eta/(x\sqrt{\mathcal L(\eta)})\le C(1+T)/\sqrt{\mathcal L(\eta)}\). If \(\eta>s_x\), the part below \(s_x\) is at most \(C\sqrt{1+T}\), and the rest is at most \(C[\mathcal L(s_x)^{3/2}-\mathcal L(\eta)^{3/2}]\). Two cases remain. If \(T<\frac12\log\gamma\), then \(\Psi_\alpha(x)\ge c\) and the bounds give \(R_{\mathrm{rm}}\le CM\gamma^{-1/2}\). If \(T\ge\frac12\log\gamma\), the hypothesis gives \(T\asymp\log\gamma\) and \(T-\log\gamma\le C_1\log\log\gamma\), and the bounds give \(R_{\mathrm{rm}}=O_M(1+\log\log\gamma)\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(R_{\mathrm{rm}}=O_M(\sqrt{\log\gamma}\,(1+\log\log\gamma))\) for \(\alpha=1\), using \(\mathcal L(s_x)-\mathcal L(\eta)\le T-\log\gamma+\log M\). Both are \(o(T^\nu)\). Finally, if \(W/(\log\gamma)^\nu\to0\), then \(e^{(T-\log\gamma)/2}=o((\log\gamma)^\nu)\), which is the hypothesis with \(C_1=2\nu\), and \(W=o((\log\gamma)^\nu)\) is \(o(\Psi_\alpha(x))\) when \(T\ge\frac12\log\gamma\), while \(W\le Ce^{-\log\gamma/4}\to0\) and \(\Psi_\alpha(x)\ge c\) when \(T<\frac12\log\gamma\).
□
