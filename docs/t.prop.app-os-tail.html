Power-law spectra and the large-batch regime · cited in the main text
Proved in LeanTheorem numbering in the paper: Proposition H.5
For a probability measure \(\nu\), \(\ell\ge0\), \(\mu>0\) and \(\Gamma\ge0\) put
\[ \mathcal I_\nu(\ell)=\int_{(\ell,\infty)}\frac{\nu(\dif x)}x,\qquad \mathcal I_\nu=\mathcal I_\nu(0),\qquad \mathcal E(\mu,\Gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}\pi\,\mu\int_0^\infty\frac{\min\{\sqrt c,\,c\sqrt\Gamma\}}{(c+\mu)^2}\,\dif c, \]
and let \(C_*=\frac12+\frac{2\log2}\pi\) (Lemma SH.12 and Theorem SH.2).
Proposition SH.2 (Tail modes)
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure and \(\mu>0\).
  1. (i)
    (Split bound.) For every \(\ell\ge0\) with \(\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)<\infty\),
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\le\sqrt{\nu([0,\ell])}\,\sqrt{\frac\mu2}+\mathcal E\big(\mu,\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)\big). \]
  2. (ii)
    (Gradient-descent-like upper bound.) If \(\nu(\{0\})=0\) and \(\mathcal I_\nu<\infty\), then
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\le\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\mathcal I_\nu}\;\mu\Big[\log\Big(1+\frac1{\mu\mathcal I_\nu}\Big)+2\Big]. \]
  3. (iii)
    (Asymptotics.) If \(\nu(\{0\})=0\) and \(\mathcal I_\nu<\infty\), then, for fixed \(\nu\),
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\sim\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\mathcal I_\nu}\;\mu\log\frac1\mu\qquad(\mu\downarrow0). \]
  4. (iv)
    (Counts.) For every \(K>0\),
    \[ C_*\sqrt{\frac{\nu([0,K\mu])}{1+K}}\le\chi_\nu(\mu)\le\sqrt{\nu([0,K\mu])}+\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt K}\big[\log(1+K)+2\big]. \]
  5. (v)
    (Universal lower bound.) If \(\nu([0,X])=1\) for some \(X>0\), then
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\ge\frac1\pi\,\frac{\mu}{\sqrt X}\Big[\log\Big(1+\frac X\mu\Big)-\frac{X}{X+\mu}\Big]\ge\frac1{2\pi}\,\frac{\mu}{\sqrt X}\,\log\frac X\mu . \]
  6. (vi)
    (Sequences.) For probability measures \(\nu_N\) and numbers \(\mu_N>0\) put \(\chi_N\defeq\chi_{\nu_N}(\mu_N)\). Then \(\liminf_N\chi_N>0\) if and only if \(\liminf_N\nu_N([0,K\mu_N])>0\) for some \(K>0\); and \(\chi_N\to0\) if and only if \(\nu_N([0,K\mu_N])\to0\) for every \(K>0\).
This proposition supports the last sentence of Section 4.1: a mode that is not packed, such as an isolated small eigenvalue, can be as slow as under gradient descent, up to a logarithm. If the spectrum has a finite mean inverse \(\mathcal I_\nu\) and no atom at zero, the profile of a small mode is of order \(\mu\log(1/\mu)\) instead of \(\sqrt\mu\), by (ii) and (iii); by (v) it is never smaller than that for a bounded spectrum. Part (iv) brackets the efficiency by the fraction of the spectrum in \([0,K\mu]\), and (vi) turns this into the criterion along sequences of spectra.
Proof sketch.
All bounds come from the first form of the representation of Section C.5,
\[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)=\frac{\sqrt2}\pi\,\mu\int_0^\infty\frac{\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c)}}{(c+\mu)^2}\,\dif c,\qquad \Upsilon_\nu(c)=c\big(1-m_\nu(c)\big)=c^2\int\frac{\nu(\dif x)}{c+x}, \]
in which the mode enters only through the kernel \(\mu(c+\mu)^{-2}\), which concentrates near \(c=\mu\). Splitting the spectrum at \(\ell\) and using \(c/(c+x)\le c/x\) above \(\ell\) gives \(1-m_\nu(c)\le\nu([0,\ell])+c\,\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)\), hence \(\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c)}\le\sqrt{c\,\nu([0,\ell])}+\min\{\sqrt c,c\sqrt{\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)}\}\), and the two terms give the two terms of (i). At \(\ell=0\) the first term vanishes, and splitting the remaining integral at \(c=1/\mathcal I_\nu\), where the minimum changes branch, gives the logarithm of (ii) (Lemma SH.12).
The matching lower bounds use that \(\Upsilon_\nu(c)/c^2\) is nonincreasing in \(c\). Below a fixed \(c_1\) this gives \(\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c)}\ge c\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c_1)}/c_1\), and the integral of \(c(c+\mu)^{-2}\) over \((0,c_1)\) is at least \(\log(1+c_1/\mu)-1\); letting \(\mu\downarrow0\) and then \(c_1\downarrow0\), so that \(\Upsilon_\nu(c_1)/c_1^2\uparrow\mathcal I_\nu\), gives (iii). If the spectrum lies in \([0,X]\), then \(\Upsilon_\nu(c)\ge c^2/(2X)\) for \(c\le X\), and the elementary inequality \(\log(1+k)-k/(1+k)\ge\frac12\log k\) gives (v) (Lemma SH.13). Part (iv) combines the square-root law of Corollary SH.2 (Theorem SH.2) with (i) at \(\ell=K\mu\), where \(\mathcal I_\nu(K\mu)\le1/(K\mu)\), and (vi) follows from (iv) by choosing \(K\) large.
