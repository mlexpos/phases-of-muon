Theorem SB.1 (The weighted branch)
Let \(w\) be nonnegative, summable and not all zero, and let \(C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star>0\).
  1. (i)
    (Existence and uniqueness.) For every \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\) and every \(s>0\) the system (SB.2) at \(z=-s\) has exactly one solution in \([0,\infty)^4\). It satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SB.6} \sigma,\tilde\sigma\le\frac{\gamma_\star C_\Sigma}{s},\qquad \tilde s_1,s_1\le\gamma_\star C_\Sigma\,\sw2,\qquad q\le\frac{(\gamma_\star C_\Sigma)^2}{s}. \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    (Continuity.) Equip \(\mathcal K\) with the topology of weak convergence against \(C([0,C_\Sigma])\) in each coordinate. The solution map \((s,\nu,\tilde\nu)\mapsto(\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\times\mathcal K\), and for every compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) it is uniformly continuous in \(s\in I\), uniformly over \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\).
  3. (iii)
    (Stability under approximate roots.) Let \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) be compact with \(s_-\defeq\min I\). For every \(\epsilon>0\) there is \(\delta>0\), depending only on \((\epsilon,I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star,w)\), with the following property. If \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\), \(s\in I\), and \((\sigma',\tilde\sigma',a',c')\in[0,\gamma_\star C_\Sigma/s_-]^2\times[0,\gamma_\star C_\Sigma\sw2]^2\) satisfy, with \(q'=s\sigma'\tilde\sigma'\),
    \[ \begin{gathered} \Big|\sigma'-\int\frac{x}{s+a'x}\,\nu(dx)\Big|\le\delta,\qquad \Big|\tilde\sigma'-\int\frac{x}{s+c'x}\,\tilde\nu(dx)\Big|\le\delta,\\ \abs{a'-s\tilde\sigma'F_w(q')}\le\delta,\qquad \abs{c'-s\sigma'F_w(q')}\le\delta, \end{gathered} \]
    then \(\abs{\sigma'-\sigma}+\abs{\tilde\sigma'-\tilde\sigma}+\abs{a'-\tilde s_1}+\abs{c'-s_1}\le\epsilon\), where \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)\) is the solution of (SB.2) at \(z=-s\) for \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\).
  4. (iv)
    (Smoothness in \(s\).) Fix \((\nu,\tilde\nu)\in\mathcal K\) with \(\int x\,\nu(dx)>0\) and \(\int x\,\tilde\nu(dx)>0\). Then \(s\mapsto(\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)(-s)\) is \(C^\infty\) on \((0,\infty)\), and \(\abs{q'(s)}\le q(s)/s\).
The fixed-point system of Figure 2 has exactly one nonnegative solution on the negative axis, for every momentum and every spectrum, so the kernels built from it are well defined. Since \(\mathcal K\) contains the spectral measures at every dimension, continuity uniform over \(\mathcal K\) makes the dimension dependence of the kernels harmless, and (iii) identifies the limit of the random traces in Section G.1.
Proof sketch.
The system reduces exactly to one equation in \(q\). If \(\nu\) or \(\tilde\nu\) has no mass on \((0,C_\Sigma]\) the solution is explicit, so assume both have. Put \(H(x)=\int x\lambda/(s+x\lambda)\,\nu(d\lambda)\), which increases from \(0\) to the mass \(p\) of \(\nu\) on \((0,C_\Sigma]\), and let \(X\) be its inverse; define \(\widetilde H,\widetilde X,\tilde p\) from \(\tilde\nu\) in the same way. The coupling reads \(H(\tilde s_1)=\widetilde H(s_1)=r(q)\) with \(r(q)=qF_w(q)\), which is strictly increasing from \(0\). Hence
\[ \tilde s_1=X(r(q)),\qquad s_1=\widetilde X(r(q)),\qquad \sigma=\frac{r(q)}{\tilde s_1},\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac{r(q)}{s_1}, \]
and the equation \(q=s\sigma\tilde\sigma\) becomes ((SB.8))
\[ \Phi(q)\defeq\frac{X(r(q))}{r(q)}\cdot\frac{\widetilde X(r(q))}{r(q)}-\frac sq=0,\qquad r(q)<\min(p,\tilde p); \]
conversely every root of \(\Phi\) gives a solution. Since \(H(x)/x\) decreases, \(X(\rho)/\rho\) increases, so \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing. It tends to \(-\infty\) as \(q\downarrow0\), where the product stays bounded, and it becomes positive at the right end, where either one factor blows up or \(s/q\to0\). So \(\Phi\) has exactly one root. The bounds follow from \(x/(s+ax)\le C_\Sigma/s\) and \(F_w\le\sw2\).
Continuity and stability follow from uniqueness by compactness. The class \(\mathcal K\) is weakly compact (Lemma SB.2), so a sequence of approximate roots that stays away from the exact roots would have a limit solving the limit system and differing from its unique solution. This is why (iii) has no rate. The implicit function theorem, applied to the root equation written in logarithmic form, whose \(q\)-derivative is at least \(1/q\), gives (iv).
