Lemma SI.13 (Local mass)
Let \(g\ge0\) be continuous and nonincreasing on \([0,\infty)\), and put \(m_g(u)\defeq\int_0^ug(v)\,\dif v\). Let \(C,D>0\), \(0<r<1\) and \(h(u)\defeq C(1+u/D)^{-r}\). Then
\[ (g*h)(u)\le\frac{m_g(u)}{1-r}\,h(u)\qquad(u\ge0). \]
Proof.
At \(u=0\) both sides vanish. Fix \(u>0\). On \([0,u]\) the function \(g\) is nonincreasing and \(v\mapsto h(u-v)\) is nondecreasing, so
\[ \int_0^u\!\!\int_0^u\big(g(v)-g(v')\big)\big(h(u-v)-h(u-v')\big)\,\dif v\,\dif v'\le0 . \]
Expanding the product gives
\[ u\,(g*h)(u)\le m_g(u)\int_0^uh(v)\,\dif v . \]
Direct integration gives
\[ \frac1{u\,h(u)}\int_0^uh(v)\,\dif v=\frac{D+u-D(1+u/D)^r}{(1-r)\,u}\le\frac1{1-r} , \]
since \((1+u/D)^r\ge1\).
□
