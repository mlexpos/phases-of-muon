Theorem SG.2 (Drift crossover)
Assume Assumption S4 and let \(i=i_N\to\infty\) be a row sequence.
  1. (i)
    Let \(0<\alpha\le1\) and put
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.32} \begin{gathered} d\defeq\frac{c_0}{\sqrt{1-\alpha}},\quad i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\defeq\Big[\frac{BN^{\alpha-1}}{\sw2d^2\log^2\gamma}\Big]^{1/\alpha},\quad a_{\mathfrak d}\defeq d\,a_N\log\gamma\quad(\alpha<1);\\ d\defeq\frac23c_0,\quad i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\defeq\frac{B}{\sw2d^2\log^3\gamma},\quad a_{\mathfrak d}\defeq d\,a_N(\log\gamma)^{3/2}\quad(\alpha=1). \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/a_{\mathfrak d}\to1+x^{-\alpha/2}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to0\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to\infty\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\log\big((N/i)^\alpha\big)\ \ (\alpha<1),\qquad \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\big[\log(N/i)\big]^{3/2}\ \ (\alpha=1). \]
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\alpha>1\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/b_N\to\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(x^{-\alpha})\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to0\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to\infty\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d_\alpha\sqrt{c_NK_\alpha}\,N^{-1/2}\,i^{-(\alpha-1)/2}. \]
Proof of the drift part of Theorem SG.1 and of Theorem SG.2(i).
By Lemma SG.4, \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\) and \(\widehat{\mathfrak d}_i\) both scale by \(\sqrt{c_N}\) when the amplitude is changed, and the indices of Theorem SG.2 do not depend on \(c_N\); so we take \(c_N=1\). Let \(\alpha\le1\). If the supremum in (SG.31) for the drift did not tend to zero, there would be a row sequence \(i\ge n_N\), hence \(i\to\infty\), along which the ratio stays away from one; so it suffices to prove \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\widehat{\mathfrak d}_i\) along every row sequence \(i\to\infty\), and we may pass to subsequences.
Suppose \(x=i/N\to x_0\in(0,1]\). The head is \(O_M(\sqrt{\mu_i/\gamma})=o(a_N)\) by Lemma SG.11, the tail is \(a_N\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)(1+O(1/M)+o(1))\) by Lemma SG.10, and \(\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\to\Psi_\alpha(x_0)>0\). Letting \(M\to\infty\) gives \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(x)\sim\sqrt2\,\widehat{\mathfrak d}_i\).
Suppose \(x\to0\), and pass to a subsequence along which \(W/(\log\gamma)^\nu\to z\in[0,\infty]\), in the notation of Lemma SG.12; note \(\sqrt2\,\widehat{\mathfrak d}_i=a_N(W+\Psi_\alpha(x))\). If \(z=0\), the head is \(O_M(a_NW)=o(a_N\Psi_\alpha(x))\), and the tail is \(a_N\Psi_\alpha(x)(1+O(1/M)+o(1))\) by Lemma SG.10 and Lemma SG.12(iii). If \(z>0\), then \(m_N(1/\mu_i)\to0\). Indeed \(m_N(1/\mu_i)=\gamma S_N(x^\alpha)\le\gamma S_\alpha(x^\alpha)\), which is \(\asymp\gamma x^\alpha=1/(\sw2W^2)\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(\sim\gamma x\log(1/x)=T/(\sw2W^2)\) for \(\alpha=1\), and both tend to zero. Hence the head is \(a_NW(1+o(1))\) by Lemma SG.11, and the tail is \(a_N\Psi_\alpha(x)(1+O(1/M)+o(1))\) if \(z<\infty\) and \(o(a_NW)\) if \(z=\infty\), by Lemma SG.10 and Lemma SG.12(i),(ii). In all cases \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)=a_N(W+\Psi_\alpha(x))(1+O(1/M)+o(1))\), and \(M\to\infty\) concludes.
For Theorem SG.2(i), the definitions (SG.32) give, at \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to x_0\in(0,\infty)\),
\[ W=d(\log\gamma)^\nu x_0^{-\alpha/2}(1+o(1)),\qquad T=\log\gamma+O(\log\log\gamma), \]
so \(\Psi_\alpha(x)\sim d(\log\gamma)^\nu\) and \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/a_{\mathfrak d}\to1+x_0^{-\alpha/2}\). If the ratio tends to zero, \(W/(\log\gamma)^\nu\to\infty\) and Lemma SG.12(ii) makes the head term dominant. If it tends to infinity, \(W/(\log\gamma)^\nu\to0\) along the mesoscopic rows, Lemma SG.12(iii) makes the tail term dominant, and for bulk rows the first case of this proof applies; Lemma SG.9 gives the explicit forms.
□
Proof of the drift part of Theorem SG.1 and of Theorem SG.2(ii).
Let \(\alpha>1\) and \(c_N=1\), and take a row sequence \(i\to\infty\) along which \(i/N\) converges. If the limit is zero, Lemma SG.13 applies, and the second factor of (SG.30) tends to one by Lemma SG.9. If the limit \(x_0\) is positive, then \(y=(i_{\mathrm c}/i)^\alpha\to0\), the head is \(o(a_N)\) by Lemma SG.11, and Lemma SG.10 gives \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(x_0)\); by Lemma SG.13, \(b_N\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\sim b_Nd_\alpha y^{p_\alpha}=a_Nd_\alpha\sqrt{K_\alpha}(i/N)^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\), so the approximant is \(a_N\Psi_\alpha(x_0)(1+o(1))\) as well. The sequential argument of the case \(\alpha\le1\) gives the uniform statement. The endpoint statements of Theorem SG.2(ii) follow from Lemma SG.13 and Lemma SG.9, the case \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to\infty\) through the uniform statement and \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\sim d_\alpha y^{p_\alpha}\).
□
