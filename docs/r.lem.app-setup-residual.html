Lemma SA.7 (Residual law)
Let \(A\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic with \(\norm{A}_\F=1\), let \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nout})\) and \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,\Id_{\Nin})\) be independent, and put \(d=z^\T Aw\). Let \(\alpha_1\ge\alpha_2\ge\dots\ge0\) be the singular values of \(A\) and \(g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).
  1. (i)
    \(d\) is symmetric, \(\EE d^2=1\), \(\EE d^4=3+6\sum_k\alpha_k^4\le3+6\norm{A}^2\), and \(\EE\abs{d}^{2p}\le\big((2p-1)!!\big)^2\) for every integer \(p\ge1\).
  2. (ii)
    \(\EE e^{i\theta d}=\prod_k(1+\theta^2\alpha_k^2)^{-1/2}\) for real \(\theta\), and \(0\le\log\EE e^{i\theta d}+\theta^2/2\le\theta^4\norm{A}^2/4\).
  3. (iii)
    Along any sequence of such \(A=A_B\) with \(\norm{A_B}\to0\), \(d\Rightarrow g\) in distribution, and \(\EE\,\psi(d)\to\EE\,\psi(g)\) for every continuous \(\psi\) with \(\abs{\psi(x)}\le1+x^2\).
Proof.
The residual reduces to a weighted sum of products of independent normals. Write \(A=U\diag(\alpha)V^\T\) with \(U,V\) orthogonal. Since \(U^\T z\) and \(V^\T w\) are again independent standard Gaussian vectors, \(d\) has the law of
\[ d\overset{\mathrm{law}}{=}\sum_k\alpha_kU_kV_k,\qquad U_k,V_k\ \text{independent standard normal}. \]
Negating all \(U_k\) proves symmetry.
The second and fourth moments follow by expansion. Independence gives \(\EE d^2=\sum_k\alpha_k^2=1\). Each product \(U_kV_k\) has fourth moment \(9\), and expanding the fourth power of the sum leaves only the multiplicities \(4\) and \(2+2\), so
\[ \EE d^4=9\sum_k\alpha_k^4+6\sum_{k<l}\alpha_k^2\alpha_l^2=3\Big(\sum_k\alpha_k^2\Big)^2+6\sum_k\alpha_k^4=3+6\sum_k\alpha_k^4 . \]
The bound \(\sum_k\alpha_k^4\le\alpha_1^2\sum_k\alpha_k^2=\norm A^2\) gives the fourth-moment bound.
The \(2p\)-th moment is bounded by conditioning on \(w\). Given \(w\), the variable \(d\) is centered Gaussian with variance \(\norm{Aw}^2\), so
\[ \EE\big[\abs d^{2p}\mid w\big]=(2p-1)!!\,\norm{Aw}^{2p}. \]
In the coordinates above \(\norm{Aw}^2=\sum_k\alpha_k^2V_k^2\) is a convex combination of the \(V_k^2\), so Jensen’s inequality for \(x\mapsto x^p\) gives
\[ \EE\norm{Aw}^{2p}\le\sum_k\alpha_k^2\,\EE V_k^{2p}=(2p-1)!! , \]
and (i) follows.
The characteristic function is a product. Integrating first in \(V_k\) and then in \(U_k\),
\[ \EE e^{i\theta\alpha U_kV_k}=\EE e^{-\theta^2\alpha^2U_k^2/2}=(1+\theta^2\alpha^2)^{-1/2}, \]
and independence gives the product formula of (ii). For \(x\ge0\),
\[ 0\le x-\log(1+x)=\int_0^x\frac{u}{1+u}\,du\le\frac{x^2}{2}; \]
summing at \(x=\theta^2\alpha_k^2\) and using \(\sum_k\alpha_k^2=1\) and \(\sum_k\alpha_k^4\le\norm A^2\) gives the bound of (ii).
Part (iii) is Lévy’s continuity theorem: by (ii) the characteristic functions converge pointwise to \(e^{-\theta^2/2}\), hence \(d\Rightarrow g\) [9, Theorem 26.3], and the convergence of \(\EE\psi(d)\) for \(\abs{\psi(x)}\le1+x^2\) follows from the bound \(\EE d^4\le9\) of (i).
□
