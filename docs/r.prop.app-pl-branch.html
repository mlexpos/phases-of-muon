Proposition SG.1 (The branch in participation coordinates)
  1. (i)
    The function \(s_N\) is a smooth strictly decreasing bijection of \((0,\gamma)\) onto \((0,\infty)\), and \(-\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log s_N(m)=\vartheta_N(m)>0\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(s>0\) let \(m=m(s)\in(0,\gamma)\) be the solution of \(s_N(m)=s\). Then
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.6} \begin{gathered} q(-s)=Q_m,\qquad \tilde s_1(-s)=s\,\Lambda_N(m),\qquad s_1(-s)=\frac{s\,m}{\gamma-m},\\ \sigma(-s)=\frac{m}{s\,\Lambda_N(m)},\qquad \tilde\sigma(-s)=\frac{\gamma-m}{s},\qquad \frac{\tilde s_1(-s)\mu_i}{s+\tilde s_1(-s)\mu_i}=f_i(m). \end{gathered} \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    For every \(\ridge>0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.7} \mathfrak d_i(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\infty \frac{\Lw(Q_{m(\ridge+u^2)})}{\Fw(Q_{m(\ridge+u^2)})}\,f_i\big(m(\ridge+u^2)\big)\,\dif u, \qquad \volraw_i(\ridge)=f_i\big(m(\ridge)\big). \end{equation}
Proof.
Part (i) rests on the monotonicity of \(\Lambda_N(m)/m\). Since \(m_N(\Lambda)/\Lambda\) is decreasing in \(\Lambda\), its inverse relation makes \(\Lambda_N(m)/m\) nondecreasing, so that
\[ \frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}\ge\frac1m . \]
The logarithmic derivative of \(s_N\) is, by \(\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log Q_m=1/E(m)\),
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log s_N(m)=-\frac1{\gamma-m}+\frac1m-\frac1{E(m)}-\frac{\Lambda_N'(m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}=-\vartheta_N(m), \]
and the preceding inequality gives \(\vartheta_N(m)\ge1/(\gamma-m)+1/E(m)>0\). At the left endpoint, \(\Lambda_N(m)\sim Bm/\sum_i\mu_i\) and \(Q_m\sim m/\sw2\) as \(m\downarrow0\), by Lemma SB.1(ii), so that
\[ s_N(m)\sim\frac{\gamma\,\sw2}{m}\cdot\frac1B\sum_i\mu_i\longrightarrow\infty . \]
At the right endpoint, \(\Lambda_N(m)\to\infty\) and \(Q_m\to Q_\gamma<\infty\), so \(s_N(m)\to0\).
Part (ii) uses the coupling (SB.4). Let \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)\) be the branch at \(-s\) and put \(m\defeq\tilde s_1\sigma=s_1\tilde\sigma=r_\bmom(q)\), so that \(q=Q_m\). The second trace equation of (SB.2) for \(\tilde\nu=\gamma\delta_1\) reads \(\tilde\sigma=\gamma/(s+s_1)\), hence
\[ s\tilde\sigma=\gamma-s_1\tilde\sigma=\gamma-m,\qquad \tilde s_1=s\tilde\sigma\,\Fw(q)=(\gamma-m)\,\Fw(Q_m). \]
Put \(\Lambda\defeq\tilde s_1/s\). The first trace equation gives
\[ m=\tilde s_1\sigma=\frac1B\sum_i\frac{\tilde s_1\mu_i}{s+\tilde s_1\mu_i}=m_N(\Lambda), \]
so \(\Lambda=\Lambda_N(m)\), and, with \(\Fw(Q_m)=m/Q_m\),
\[ s=\frac{\tilde s_1}{\Lambda}=\frac{(\gamma-m)\Fw(Q_m)}{\Lambda_N(m)}=s_N(m). \]
By (i) this determines \(m=m(s)\). The remaining identities of (SG.6) are \(s_1=m/\tilde\sigma\), \(\sigma=m/\tilde s_1\) and \(\tilde s_1\mu_i/(s+\tilde s_1\mu_i)=\Lambda\mu_i/(1+\Lambda\mu_i)\).
Part (iii) is a computation in these coordinates. By (ii),
\[ s+s_1=\frac{s\gamma}{\gamma-m},\qquad \frac{\mu_i}{s+\tilde s_1\mu_i}=\frac{f_i(m)}{\tilde s_1}=\frac{f_i(m)}{(\gamma-m)\Fw(Q_m)}, \]
so the integrand of (SC.4) at \((\mu,\lambda)=(\mu_i,1)\) is
\[ \frac{s\,\Lw(q)\,\mu_i}{(s+\tilde s_1\mu_i)(s+s_1)}=\frac{\Lw(Q_m)}{\gamma\,\Fw(Q_m)}\,f_i(m),\qquad m=m(s), \]
which gives the first identity with \(s=\ridge+u^2\). The second is the last identity of (SG.6) at \(s=\ridge\), compared with (SD.22).
□
