Lemma SG.20 (Mesoscopic crossover)
Let \(0<\alpha\le1\). Along every row sequence \(i\to\infty\) with \(i/N\to0\),
\[ \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac1{\sw2}+\frac{2c_0}{\sqrt{\sw2}}\,b_{i,N}+b_{i,N}^2 . \]
Proof.
The flat coefficient has the claimed form. By Lemma SG.18, \(\EE g^2=1\), \(\EE\sign(g)^2=1\) and \(\EE[g\sign(g)]=c_0\),
\[ \mathfrak v^{\mathrm{flat}}_i=\frac1{\sw2}+\frac{2c_0}{\sqrt{\sw2}}\,b+b^2+o\big((1+b)^2\big). \]
It remains to show that the nonnegative difference \(\mathfrak v_i(0)-\mathfrak v^{\mathrm{flat}}_i\) is \(o((1+b)^2)\); the quadratic is bounded above and below by multiples of \((1+b)^2\), which then gives the relative equivalence.
Fix \(0<\delta<1<M\), \(R>1\) and \(0<\eta<1/2\), and split \((0,\gamma)\) into \(I_1\defeq(0,\delta)\), \(I_2\defeq[\delta,M]\), \(I_3\defeq(M,\eta\gamma)\) and \(I_4\defeq[\eta\gamma,\gamma)\). The square \(I_4\times I_4\) is at most \(C_\eta\gamma x^\alpha\) by Lemma SG.17, which is \(o((1+b)^2)\) by (SG.47). On \((0,\eta\gamma)^2\), Lemma SG.14 bounds \(1/\Delta^N_\ell\) by \(C_\eta\), so the exact square there is at most \(C_\eta\) times the flat square, which is \(O((1+b)^2)\) by the estimates of Lemma SG.18 restricted to \((0,\eta\gamma)\). Lemma SG.16 then makes every term meeting \(I_4\) equal to \(o((1+b)^2)\). In the same way the flat norm over \(I_2\) is \(o(1+b)\) by Lemma SG.18, and the terms meeting \(I_2\) are \(o((1+b)^2)\).
The remaining products compare the exact and flat kernels. On \(I_1\times I_1\), \(1/\Delta^N_\ell-1\le C_\bmom\delta\) by Lemma SG.14, and the flat square is bounded by (SG.48), so the change is at most \(C\delta\). On \(I_1\times I_3\) and its transpose, \(1-\Delta^N_\ell\le\delta/M+2\delta/\gamma\), so the change is at most \(C\delta M^{-1}(1+b)^2+o((1+b)^2)\). On \(I_3\times I_3\), the strip of Lemma SG.19 is \(o((1+b)^2)\), and off the strip \(1-\Delta^N_\ell\le R^{-1}+2\eta\), which for \(R^{-1}+2\eta<1/2\) changes the integral by at most \(C(R^{-1}+\eta)\) times the flat total, that is by \(O((R^{-1}+\eta)(1+b)^2)\). Letting \(N\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\), \(M\to\infty\), \(R\to\infty\) and \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the claim.
□
