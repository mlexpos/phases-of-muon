The isotropic case

In this chapter the data are isotropic, \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), as in Section 3; \(\gin=\Nin/B\) and \(\gout=\Nout/B\) are the two aspect ratios, \(\gamma_\wedge\) and \(\gamma_\vee\) their minimum and maximum, and \(\gamma=N/B\) in the square case. Every eigenvalue is one, so every mode has the same drift kernel \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) and the same share \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)/(\Nout\Nin)\) of the volatility. The fixed-point system of Figure 2 at \(z=-s\) reduces to one scalar equation for its root \(q=q(-s)\) (Proposition SF.1):
\[ \begin{gathered} s\,q=\big(\gout-r_\bmom(q)\big)\big(\gin-r_\bmom(q)\big),\\ r_\bmom(q)\defeq q\Fw(q)=\sum_{j\ge0}\Phi(\emaw j^2q),\qquad \Phi(v)\defeq\EE\frac{vg^2}{1+vg^2}\quad(g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)). \end{gathered} \]
The value \(\psi(s)\defeq r_\bmom(q(-s))<\gamma_\wedge\) is the participation level: \(B\psi(\ridge)\) is the deterministic equivalent of the squared Frobenius norm of the update \(\Qridge\ridge(M_t)\), which for an exact polar factor would be its rank (Theorem SF.2(i)). The drift is the integral (SF.10) of \(q\Lw(q)\) along the root, written \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge;\bmom,\gin,\gout)\) when the arguments matter; its value at zero ridge is
\[ C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\defeq\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty q(-u^2)\,\Lw\big(q(-u^2)\big)\,\dif u,\qquad C_\bmom(\gamma)\defeq C_\bmom(\gamma,\gamma),\qquad C\defeq C_0, \]
and \(\sw k\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j^k\), which is \((1-\bmom)^k/(1-\bmom^k)\) for \(\bmom>0\), so that \(B/\sw2=B_{\rm eff}\). The chapter closes the dynamics on the risk, computes the two coefficients of (8) at zero ridge at large and small batch, and ends with a small ridge at small batch.

Proposition SF.2 (Zero-ridge isotropic volatility without momentum)
Let \(\bmom=0\) and \(m_\gamma\defeq\min\{1,\gin,\gout\}\). Then \(\psi(\ridge)\to m_\gamma\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\), and
\[ \lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\,m_\gamma=\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}. \]
Without momentum the zero-ridge volatility is \(\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}\), the rank of one minibatch gradient. This is the value \(\mathfrak v_0=\min\{B,N\}\) of Figure 4, and it gives the learning-rate ratio \(N/\sqrt B\) at \(\bmom=0\) and \(B\le N\) in (11). For an exact polar factor the squared Frobenius norm is the rank, \(\norm{\Qridge0(\Gt)}_\F^2=\rank\Gt\) (Lemma SA.2(i)), so the value is the expected one; the proposition shows that the deterministic coefficients of the regularized algorithm have this limit as \(\ridge\downarrow0\). No statement is made about the algorithm at zero ridge.
Proof sketch.
Without momentum the volatility is the same-time value \(B\psi(\ridge)\), exactly. The updates of two different steps are built from independent batches, so the lag covariances of the volatility vanish (Theorem SD.4(ii)), and the integrated coefficient is the expected squared Frobenius norm of one update. Its deterministic equivalent is read off the anchors of the scalar equation ((SF.18)):
\[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\,\psi(\ridge)=B\,\Phi\big(q(-\ridge)\big)=B\big(\gout-\ridge\,\sigma(-\ridge)\big). \]
At \(\bmom=0\) there is a single weight \(\emaw0=1\), so the participation level is one Gaussian average, \(\psi=\Phi(q)\).
The participation level has two ceilings. Both factors on the right of the scalar equation are positive, so \(\psi<\min(\gin,\gout)\) (Proposition SF.1(ii)). And \(\Phi<1\), because the integrand \(vg^2/(1+vg^2)\) is below one (Lemma SF.1(ii)): one batch contributes at most one unit of participation. Hence \(\psi(\ridge)<m_\gamma=\min\{1,\gin,\gout\}\).
As the ridge vanishes, the level reaches the lower of the two ceilings. Suppose that \(\psi(\ridge_k)\le m_\gamma-\delta\) along a sequence \(\ridge_k\downarrow0\). Then both factors of the scalar equation stay at least \(\delta\), and the root is forced to infinity:
\[ q(-\ridge_k)=\frac{\big(\gout-\psi(\ridge_k)\big)\big(\gin-\psi(\ridge_k)\big)}{\ridge_k}\ge\frac{\delta^2}{\ridge_k}\longrightarrow\infty . \]
But \(\Phi(v)\to1\) as \(v\to\infty\), so \(\psi(\ridge_k)=\Phi(q(-\ridge_k))\to1\ge m_\gamma\), a contradiction. Hence \(\psi(\ridge)\to m_\gamma\), and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\to B\,m_\gamma=\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}\) because \(B\gin=\Nin\) and \(B\gout=\Nout\). With momentum the ceiling one disappears, since \(r_\bmom\) is unbounded, and the same-time total tends to \(\min\{\Nin,\Nout\}\) instead (Proposition SF.3(i)).

Definition SF.1 (Zero-ridge isotropic volatility)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) and let \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) be the number of Theorem SF.2(ii) at momentum \(\bmom\). The zero-ridge isotropic volatility at momentum \(\bmom\) is
\[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge), \]
the limit being taken at this fixed \(\bmom\), whenever it exists.
The definition names the volatility \(\mathfrak v_\bmom\) of the main text’s scalar recursion (8) at zero ridge, written \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\) here. It is the limit of the deterministic coefficient of Theorem SF.2(ii) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\) at a fixed momentum, it depends on \(B\), \(\Nin\) and \(\Nout\), and it says nothing about the algorithm at zero ridge. At \(\bmom=0\) the limit is \(\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}\) (Proposition SF.2). For \(\bmom>0\) the supplement proves that the limit exists in the square case, where it equals \(N(1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0))\) with \(\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\) the correlation of two updates \(\ell\) steps apart (Proposition SF.3(ii)).
Proposition SF.4 (Scalar reduction)
Let \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\ridge>0\) and \(\macrostep>0\), and write \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) for the numbers of Theorem SF.1 and Theorem SF.2.
  1. (i)
    The deterministic mode-energy equations (SE.7) close on the risk: if \((\fQ_{ij})\) solves them on an interval on which \(\fRisk>0\), then \(\fRisk=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\fQ_{ij}\) solves
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.19} \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif\macrotime}=-2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\macrostep^2}{2B}\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}},\qquad \fRisk(0)=\cR(0), \end{equation}
    and (SF.19) has at most one solution with \(\fRisk>0\) on any interval \([0,T]\).
  2. (ii)
    Under Assumption S3 and for \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\), one step of size \(\lr\ge0\) from a frozen state, \(\Delta^+=\Dt-\lr\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\), satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.20} \begin{gathered} \EE\,\cR(\Delta^+)-\cR(t)=-2\lr\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\cR(t)}+\frac{\lr^2B}{2}\,\psi(\ridge)+r_t,\\ \abs{r_t}\le\lr\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\varepsilon_B+\frac{\lr^2B}{2}\,\gamma_\star\ridge_+e_B , \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    with \(\varepsilon_B\) of Theorem SC.2 and \(e_B\) of Theorem SD.1.
With isotropic data the deterministic mode-energy equations close on the risk. Part (i) is the continuous-time form of the main text’s recursion (8): with \(\lr=\macrostep/B\) and \(t=B\macrotime\) it reads \(\dif\fRisk/\dif t=-2\lr\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt\fRisk+\frac{\lr^2}2\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\), so the drift and volatility of the main text are \(\mathfrak d=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) and \(\mathfrak v=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\). Part (ii) is the balance for one step from a fixed state. Its quadratic term is the same-time value \(B\psi(\ridge)\), which equals \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\) at \(\bmom=0\) and differs from it by the lag terms for \(\bmom>0\); over many steps the lag terms are added (Corollary SF.1).
Proof sketch.
Every mode has the same drift kernel, and the expected update is a multiple of the error itself. At \(\mu_i=\lambda_j=1\) the denominator of the drift kernel (7) is \((z+\tilde s_1)(z+s_1)=z\gin\gout/q\) by the anchors of the scalar equation, so the kernel loses its dependence on the mode and becomes an integral along the root (Theorem SF.1(i),(iii)):
\[ \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty q(-z)\,\Lw\big(q(-z)\big)\,\dif u\quad(z=\ridge+u^2),\qquad \EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)=\frac{\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,\Dt+E_\ridge , \]
with \(\norm{E_\ridge}_\F\le\varepsilon_B\to0\). The volatility is shared equally, \(\mathfrak v_{ij}=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}/(\Nout\Nin)\) (Theorem SF.2(ii)). Since \(\mu_i\lambda_j=1\), the risk is \(\fRisk=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\fQ_{ij}\), and summing the mode-energy equations (SE.7) over the pairs gives
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\big(2B^2\fRisk\big)=-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt\fRisk}\,2B^2\fRisk+\macrostep^2B\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}} , \]
which is (SF.19) after division by \(2B^2\). The right side is continuously differentiable in \(\fRisk>0\), so the Picard–Lindelöf theorem gives uniqueness. This is (i).
For one step from a fixed state the change of the risk is exactly a linear term, the pairing of the error with the mean update, plus half the second moment of the update (Proposition SC.2). By the display above, the linear term is \(-\lr\langle\Dt,\EE\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\rangle=-2\lr\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\cR(t)}\) up to \(\lr\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\varepsilon_B\), by \(\norm{\Dt}_\F^2=2\cR(t)\) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. For the quadratic term, the regularized polar map of a matrix \(K\) satisfies, exactly ((SD.27)),
\[ \norm{\Qridge\ridge(K)}_\F^2=\Tr\,KK^\T\big(KK^\T+\ridge\Id\big)^{-1}=\Nout-\ridge\,\Tr\big(KK^\T+\ridge\Id\big)^{-1} . \]
For the normalized buffer, the deterministic equivalent of the resolvent trace divided by \(B\) is \(\sigma(-\ridge)\), so the second moment is \(B(\gout-\ridge\,\sigma(-\ridge))=B\psi(\ridge)\) up to \(B\gamma_\star\ridge_+e_B\) (Theorem SD.1, Theorem SF.2(i)). This is (ii).

Corollary SF.1 (Isotropic dynamics)
Let \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), fix \(\macrostep>0\), \(\ridge>0\), \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\gamma_\star<\infty\), \(E_\star<\infty\), \(c_M<\infty\) and \(0<r_0\le r_1<\infty\), and along a sequence of dimensions with \(B\to\infty\) assume:
  1. (a)
    Assumption S2 holds and \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\);
  2. (b)
    the algorithm of Definition SA.2 is run with momentum \(\bmom\), normalized ridge \(\ridge\) and step \(\lr_t=\macrostep/B\);
  3. (c)
    \(W_0\) is deterministic, \(\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2\le E_\star\), \(r_0\le\cR(0)\le r_1\) and \(\norm{\Delta_0}/\norm{\Delta_0}_\F\to0\);
  4. (d)
    the initial buffer \(M^\circ_{-1}\) is independent of all batches and \(\EE\norm{M^\circ_{-1}}_\F^2\le c_MB\).
Let \(T_\star\) be given by (SE.10) at \(C_\Sigma=1\). Then (SF.19) has a unique solution \(\fRisk\) on \([0,T_\star]\), and
  1. (i)
    with probability tending to one, \(t_{\rm stop}>BT_\star\) and \(\inf_{t\le BT_\star}\cR(t)\ge r_0/2\);
  2. (ii)
    \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\abs{\cR(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor)-\fRisk(\macrotime)}\to0\) in probability;
  3. (iii)
    on the event of (i), \(\sup_{t\le BT_\star}\norm{\Dt}/\norm{\Dt}_\F\to0\) in probability.
The corollary is the precise form of the main text’s claim that, with isotropic data, the risk of Muon follows the scalar recursion (8). At the step \(\lr=\macrostep/B\) and up to the macroscopic time \(T_\star\), the risk at step \(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor\) is uniformly close to the solution of (SF.19), whose coefficients are the integrated volatility \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) and the drift \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) at the fixed ridge \(\ridge>0\). Hypothesis (c) asks that the initial error be diffuse: its operator norm is a vanishing fraction of its Frobenius norm, so no single singular direction carries a fixed fraction of the risk. Part (iii) says that this persists. The time \(T_\star>0\) does not depend on the dimension. The corollary does not reach the zero-ridge coefficients \(C_\bmom\) and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\), which enter only as limits of the coefficients.
Proof sketch.
The corollary is Theorem SE.1 at identity covariances. The covariance bound of {{(H1)}} holds with \(C_\Sigma=1\), the weighted pairing is the Frobenius pairing, and the normalized error is \(\Dt/\norm{\Dt}_\F\), so the diffuseness of {{(H3)}} is the ratio \(\norm{\Delta_0}/\norm{\Delta_0}_\F\) of hypothesis (c) ((SF.2)). The mode-energy equations have a unique solution on \([0,T_\star]\) with risk at least \(r_0/2\) (Proposition SE.1), and by Proposition SF.4(i) its risk solves (SF.19), which has only one positive solution. The spectral energy measure is the single atom \(\norm{\Delta}_\F^2\,\delta_{(1,1)}=2\cR(\Delta)\,\delta_{(1,1)}\), so the measure statement of the theorem is the risk statement (ii).
In the isotropic case the block argument behind the theorem (Section G.4) is a computation for the risk alone. The algorithm is compared, over blocks of \(L=\lfloor B^{1/8}\rfloor\) steps, with the model in which the state is held at its value \(D\) at the start of the block and the momentum buffer is a stationary average of independent batches. Test the exact block expansion (SE.28) of Lemma SE.26 against the constant function. The test is then \(\norm\Delta_\F^2=2\cR(\Delta)\). Its drift term is the pairing of \(D\) with the mean update, which is \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}D/\sqrt{\cR(D)}\) up to a vanishing error (Theorem SF.1(iii)). Its volatility term is the total mass \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}/B\) of the forcing measure ((SF.16)). Halving gives
\[ \EE_t\big[\cR(\Delta_{t+L})\big]-\cR(D)=\frac LB\Big(-2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\cR(D)}+\frac{\macrostep^2}{2B}\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\Big)+\frac LB\,\epsilon_t, \]
where the mean of \(\abs{\epsilon_t}\) tends to zero, uniformly over the blocks that start at a diffuse state whose risk and energy stay within the bounds of the hypotheses (proof of Lemma SE.26). The main term is \(L/B\) times the right side of (SF.19). Its volatility is the integrated coefficient, not the same-time value \(B\psi(\ridge)\) of (SF.20). A block is long compared with the memory \(1/(1-\bmom)\) of the buffer, so the covariance of the sum of its \(L\) updates contains the correlations of all lags (Theorem SD.8(iv)). The first steps of a block, during which the buffer still remembers the previous block, cost a total of order \(\log^2(2B)/L\), and the fluctuations of the block increments form a martingale whose maximum vanishes.
The error stays diffuse, which is what keeps the mean update close to \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}D/\sqrt{\cR(D)}\) along the trajectory. The operator norm of the mean update is at most a constant times the operator norm of the normalized error, plus a vanishing term, at every state, diffuse or not (Lemma SE.7(iv)), so the argument is not circular. The centered increments of the error over a block have operator norm \(O(L/B)\), and their partial sums vanish in operator norm by a matrix martingale inequality (Lemma SE.23). A discrete Gronwall inequality then bounds \(\max_{t\le BT_\star}\norm{\Dt}\) by a constant times \(\norm{\Delta_0}\) plus a vanishing term, while the Frobenius norm stays of order one because the risk stays above \(r_0/2\); this is (iii). Gronwall’s inequality for (SF.19) and routine estimates give (i) and (ii).

Proposition SF.5 (The zero-ridge constant)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) and \(\gin,\gout>0\). Put \(K_0\defeq1\), and for \(\bmom>0\) let \(K_\bmom\) be the unique \(q>0\) with \(q\Fw(q)=\min(\gin,\gout)\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(t>0\),
    \[ 0<q(-t)\,\Lw\big(q(-t)\big)\le\min\Big(K_\bmom,\frac{\gin\gout}{t}\Big), \]
    so the integral (SF.21) converges and \(0<C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)<\infty\). Moreover \(C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)=C_\bmom(\gout,\gin)\), and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.23} C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\,q(-t)\,\Lw\big(q(-t)\big)\,\dif t . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) be the kernel (SF.10) at momentum \(\bmom\), for the measures (SF.1). Then, at this fixed \(\bmom\), \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\to C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\).
The proposition makes sense of the zero-ridge drift \(C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\) of (SF.21), which is the drift \(\mathfrak d_\bmom\) of the main text’s recursion (8) and the constant of (9): the integral converges, the constant is positive and symmetric in the two aspect ratios, and it is the limit of the drift kernel \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\) at a fixed momentum. Like every zero-ridge statement of the chapter, (ii) concerns the deterministic kernel and not the algorithm at zero ridge. The form (SF.23) in the spectral parameter \(t=u^2\) is the one used for the batch limits. At \(\bmom=0\) the integrand is the participation level, \(q\Jfun(q)=\psi\), so \(C_0\) is an average over the spectral scale of the effective rank of the update divided by \(B\).
Proof sketch.
The integrand is bounded near the origin and decays like \(1/t\), which gives convergence. Since \(\Jfun\le1\) and the weights sum to one, \(\Lw\le1\) and \(q\Lw(q)\le q\). Two bounds on the root follow from the scalar equation. The trace equations give \(\sigma(-t)\le\gout/t\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\le\gin/t\), so \(q(-t)=t\,\sigma(-t)\tilde\sigma(-t)\le\gin\gout/t\). And the participation level stays below \(\min(\gin,\gout)\), so for \(\bmom>0\) the root stays below the root \(K_\bmom\) at which \(r_\bmom\) reaches that level, because \(r_\bmom\) is increasing; at \(\bmom=0\) the integrand is \(\Phi(q)<1\) directly. Together,
\[ 0<q(-t)\,\Lw\big(q(-t)\big)\le\min\Big(K_\bmom,\frac{\gin\gout}t\Big), \]
so the integrand of (SF.21) at \(t=u^2\) is at most \(\min(K_\bmom,\gin\gout/u^2)\), which is integrable. The scalar equation is symmetric in \((\gin,\gout)\), so the root and the constant are too, and the substitution \(t=u^2\) gives (SF.23).
The ridge enters only as a shift of the spectral parameter, so the limit is a limit under the integral. By Theorem SF.1(i) the kernel at ridge \(\ridge\) is the same integral with the root evaluated at \(-(\ridge+u^2)\). The root is continuous in the spectral parameter (Theorem SB.1(iv)) and \(\Lw\) is continuous, so the integrand converges pointwise as \(\ridge\downarrow0\). The bound above at \(t=\ridge+u^2\) dominates it by the same function \(\min(K_\bmom,\gin\gout/u^2)\), and dominated convergence gives (ii). The limit is taken at a fixed \(\bmom\); at zero ridge the limits \(\bmom\downarrow0\) and \(\ridge\downarrow0\) do not commute for \(\gamma>1\) (Proposition SF.12).

Theorem SF.4 (Small aspect ratios)
Let \(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\to0\) with \(r_j\defeq\gout^{(j)}/\gin^{(j)}\to r\in(0,\infty)\), and let \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}^{(j)})\) be the isotropic measure pair at \((\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)})\) of Proposition SF.6. Let \(\mathsf P_j\) be the image of the probability measure \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)}/\gout^{(j)}\) under \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda/\gout^{(j)}\), and write \(\mathsf m_j(t)\defeq S_{\mathsf P_j}(-t)\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(t>0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.37} \Big|t\,\mathsf m_j(t)^2+\Big(t+\frac1{r_j}-1\Big)\mathsf m_j(t)-1\Big|\le\frac{3\gout^{(j)}}{r_j^2t^2}. \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    \(\mathsf P_j\to\mathsf M_r\) weakly, and \(\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x)\to J_r\defeq\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf M_r(\dif x)>0\).
  3. (iii)
    The constant satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.38} \begin{gathered} C\big(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\big)=\frac{r_j}{\sqrt{2\gout^{(j)}}}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x) \sim\frac{r}{\sqrt{2\gout^{(j)}}}\,J_r,\\ J_r=\int_{a_r}^{b_r}\frac{\sqrt{(b_r-x)(x-a_r)}}{2\pi\sqrt x}\,\dif x . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    In particular \(J_1=8/(3\pi)\), and \(C(\gamma)\sim8/(3\pi\sqrt{2\gamma})\) as \(\gamma\downarrow0\).
The theorem gives the zero-ridge drift without momentum at large batch, \(N\ll B\). In the square case \(C(\gamma)\sim\frac{8}{3\pi}(2\gamma)^{-1/2}\), that is \(\mathfrak d_0\approx\frac8{3\pi}\sqrt{B/(2N)}\), the large-batch limit of the approximate form of \(\mathfrak d_0\) in (9). The constant \(J_r\) is the mean square root of the Marchenko–Pastur law \(\mathsf M_r\) of ratio \(r=\gout/\gin\), with its atom at zero when \(r>1\), so the drift constant is a mean singular value. Part (i) is a quantitative form of the convergence that (ii) states.
Proof sketch.
Without momentum the drift constant is a half-moment of a spectral measure. The isotropic measure pair exists and is unique (Proposition SF.6): it is obtained as a limit of the expected spectral measures of the Gram matrices of one minibatch gradient, and the transforms identify the limit. The integrand of the drift is then \(\psi(t)=\int\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+t}\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\), and the elementary integral \(\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\frac{x}{x+t}\,\dif t=\pi\sqrt x\) turns the form (SF.23) into a mean of square roots, that is of singular values (Proposition SF.7):
\[ C(\gin,\gout)=\frac1{\sqrt2\,\gin\gout}\int\sqrt\lambda\;\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda) =\frac{r_j}{\sqrt{2\gout}}\int\sqrt x\;\mathsf P_j(\dif x), \]
the second form after the rescaling \(\lambda=\gout x\) that defines \(\mathsf P_j\).
At small aspect ratio the closure becomes the Marchenko–Pastur quadratic. The transform of \(\mathsf P_j\) is \(\mathsf m_j(t)=\sigma(-\gout t)\), and both transforms are bounded by their mass over the spectral parameter, so the argument \(v_j\) of \(\Phi\) in the closure is at most \(\gout/(r_jt)\), which is small. Without momentum the closure reads \(\gout(1-t\,\mathsf m_j)=\Phi(v_j)\), and \(\Phi\) is linear up to a quadratic error, \(0\le v-\Phi(v)\le3v^2\) (Lemma SF.1(iii)). Inserting \(v_j\) and dividing by \(\gout\) leaves the quadratic equation of (SF.37) with the residual \(3\gout/(r_j^2t^2)\). This is (i).
The rescaled laws converge to \(\mathsf M_r\), and so do their half-moments. For each \(t\), every limit point of \(\mathsf m_j(t)\) is a positive root of \(t\,m^2+(t+\frac1r-1)m-1=0\), and that root is the transform of \(\mathsf M_r\) (Lemma SF.9). The first moments are exactly \(1/r_j\), because the first moment of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}\) is \(\gin\gout\) (Lemma SF.3(ii)). Convergence of the transforms on the negative axis then gives weak convergence, and the bounded first moments give convergence of the half-moments (Lemma SF.2(i)). The theorem describes what a fixed positive ridge does at small batch with momentum. The main text uses the regularized polar map as a model of \(K\) Newton–Schulz steps, with an effective ridge of order \(a^{-2K}\), and cites this theorem for the effect of a small ridge. A past batch of age \(j\) enters the buffer with weight \(\emaw j=(1-\bmom)\bmom^j\), and the ridge whitens it only if its weight is large enough; the number of ages that pass is about \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}\). The theorem says that the participation level \(\psi(\ridge)\) is this count up to a bounded error, that the drift is about \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}/\sqrt{\pi\gin\gout}\), which decays like \(\log\gamma/\gamma\) in the square case instead of reaching the zero-ridge value \(1/(2\sqrt\pi)\), and that the volatility is at least of order \(B\psi(\ridge)^2\). The two hypotheses ask that the count be past the first age and well below its ceiling \(\gamma_\wedge\).
Proof sketch.
The participation level counts the ages whose weight passes the threshold set by the root. The terms \(\Phi(\emaw j^2q)\) of \(r_\bmom(q)\) are within \(\sqrt{\pi/2}\,(\emaw j^2q)^{-1/2}\) of one for the ages with \(\emaw j^2q\ge1\), and at most \(\emaw j^2q\) for the others; both corrections decay geometrically with the distance of the age to the threshold. Hence, for \((1-\bmom)^2q\ge1\) (Lemma SF.6(i)),
\[ \mathsf n_\bmom(q)-\mathsf c_\bmom<r_\bmom(q)\le\mathsf n_\bmom(q)+\mathsf c'_\bmom,\qquad \mathsf n_\bmom(q)=\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)^2q\big)}{2\log(1/\bmom)} , \]
where \(\mathsf n_\bmom(q)\) is, up to one, the number of ages with \(\emaw j^2q\ge1\).
At a fixed ridge the root is about \(\gin\gout/\ridge\), and the count at that root is \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}\). Write \(H=\psi(\ridge)\) and \(\mathsf W(m)=\sqrt{(\gin-m)(\gout-m)}\). The scalar equation at \(s=\ridge\) says that the root is \(\mathsf W(H)^2/\ridge\), so
\[ \mathsf n_\bmom\big(q(-\ridge)\big)=\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\frac{\log\big(\mathsf W(H)/\sqrt{\gin\gout}\big)}{\log(1/\bmom)}\le\widehat{\mathfrak p} . \]
The upper count then gives \(H\le\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom\le\gamma_\wedge/2\). Below half the ceiling, \(\mathsf W(H)\ge\sqrt{\gin\gout}\,(1-H/\gamma_\wedge)\), so the logarithm costs at most \(2H/(\gamma_\wedge\log(1/\bmom))\), and the lower count gives \(H\ge\widehat{\mathfrak p}-e_\bmom\). The case \((1-\bmom)^2q(-\ridge)<1\), in which not even the newest batch passes the threshold, would force a ridge that the first hypothesis excludes.
The drift is a fixed function of the level, so each whitened age contributes the same amount. By the master estimate (Theorem SF.3, explained in the note on Theorem SF.11),
\[ \Big|\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)-\frac1{\sqrt\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^{H}\mathsf W(m)\,\dif m\Big|<\frac{2.32}{(1-\bmom)\sqrt{\gin\gout}} . \]
For \(m\le H\) the integrand lies between \(\sqrt{\gin\gout}(1-m/\gamma_\wedge)\) and \(\sqrt{\gin\gout}\), so the main term times \(\sqrt{\gin\gout}\) is \(H/\sqrt\pi\) up to \(H^2/(\sqrt\pi\gamma_\wedge)\). With the bound on \(H\) this gives the drift bound.
The volatility exceeds its same-time value by the correlations of updates that whiten common ages. By Proposition SF.3(ii), \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\psi(\ridge)+2N\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)\). The lag lower bound (Lemma SF.12) bounds the determinant of the lag kernel by one, writes the double integral in the two roots, and keeps only pairs of roots past the threshold and within a factor \(e^2\) of each other, where the count is accurate. A shift of \(\ell\) ages raises the count by about \(\ell\), so the lag \(\ell\) is seen only at levels that stay below \(\psi(\ridge)\) after the shift, a range of length about \(\psi(\ridge)-\ell\):
\[ \overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)\ge\frac{\mathsf k_\bmom}{\gamma^3}\,\big(\gamma-\psi(\ridge)\big)^2\big(\psi(\ridge)-\ell-\mathsf c''_\bmom-\mathsf c'_\bmom\big)_+ . \]
Summing over \(\ell\), with \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}(X-\ell)_+\ge(X-1)_+^2/2\), gives the volatility bound. At leading order the integrated volatility is \(B\psi(\ridge)^2\) (Corollary SF.2(iii)).

\[ \mathcal P(x)\defeq\tfrac12-\tfrac12(1-x)^2+(1-x)^2\log(1-x)\quad(0\le x<1),\qquad\mathcal P(1)\defeq\tfrac12 . \]
Theorem SF.14 (Crossover)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) be fixed, let \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\to\infty\), and let \(\ridge_\gamma\in(0,1)\) satisfy \(\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge_\gamma)/\gamma\to c\in(0,\infty)\). Then \(\psi(\ridge_\gamma)/\gamma\to c\wedge1\), the same-time total satisfies \(B\psi(\ridge_\gamma)/N\to c\wedge1\),
\[ \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge_\gamma)\longrightarrow\Xi(c)\defeq\frac{(c\wedge1)\big(1-\tfrac12(c\wedge1)\big)}{\sqrt\pi}, \]
and, with \(\mathcal P\) of (SF.63),
\[ \frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge_\gamma)}{N\gamma}\longrightarrow\mathcal P(c\wedge1). \]
The theorem connects the fixed-ridge regime with the zero-ridge regime at small batch, in the square case. The ridge lets about \(\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge)\) past batches be whitened, while there are only \(\gamma=N/B\) directions to fill. When the ridge shrinks with \(\gamma\) so that \(\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge_\gamma)\approx c\gamma\), the update whitens the fraction \(c\wedge1\) of the directions, and the drift and the volatility have explicit limits in this fraction. As \(c\to0\) they reduce to the fixed-ridge laws, \(\Xi(c)\sim c/\sqrt\pi\) and \(\mathcal P(c)\sim c^2\), and for \(c\ge1\), that is \(\ridge\lesssim\bmom^{2\gamma}\), they equal the zero-ridge limits \(1/(2\sqrt\pi)\) and \(\frac12\) of Theorem SF.11. The main text uses these laws at the ridge calibrated to Newton–Schulz, where they predict the NS-5 speedups of Figure 6.
Proof sketch.
The whitened fraction \(\psi(\ridge_\gamma)/\gamma\) tends to \(c\wedge1\), by the counting argument of Theorem SF.13. Write \(H=\psi(\ridge_\gamma)\) and \(\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge_\gamma)\). The root at the ridge is \((\gamma-H)^2/\ridge_\gamma\), and the count of ages that pass its threshold, \(\mathsf n_\bmom\) of that theorem’s note, brackets \(H\) (Lemma SF.6(i)):
\[ \mathfrak p+\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)(\gamma-H)\big)}{\log(1/\bmom)}-\mathsf c_\bmom<H\le\mathfrak p+\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)\gamma\big)}{\log(1/\bmom)}+\mathsf c'_\bmom . \]
The corrections are logarithmic in \(\gamma\), hence negligible against \(\mathfrak p\approx c\gamma\), unless \(\gamma-H\) is small. If \(c<1\), the upper bound keeps \(H\) below \((1-\delta)\gamma\), and then the lower bound gives \(H\ge c\gamma-o(\gamma)\). If \(c\ge1\) and \(H\le(1-\delta)\gamma\) along a subsequence, the lower bound gives \(H>(1-\delta/2)\gamma\), a contradiction; so \(H/\gamma\to1\). When the ridge would admit more ages than there are directions, all directions are whitened, as at zero ridge.
In the square case the drift is a quadratic function of the whitened fraction, up to \(O(1/\gamma)\). The master estimate (Theorem SF.3(ii)) with \(\mathsf W(m)=\gamma-m\) reads
\[ \Big|\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)-\Xi_0\Big(\frac{\psi(\ridge)}\gamma\Big)\Big|<\frac{2.32}{(1-\bmom)\gamma},\qquad \Xi_0(x)=\frac{x-\frac12x^2}{\sqrt\pi}, \]
because \(\gamma^{-2}\int_0^\psi(\gamma-m)\,\dif m=\psi/\gamma-\frac12(\psi/\gamma)^2\). Continuity of \(\Xi_0\) gives the drift limit \(\Xi(c)=\Xi_0(c\wedge1)\). The order-one quantity is the drift itself, so \(\gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge_\gamma)\to\infty\).
The lag sum at a positive ridge is the zero-ridge computation with the levels cut at \(\psi\) instead of \(\gamma\) (Theorem SF.12). The lag coefficients have the same exact representation in the participation levels as at zero ridge (Proposition SF.10; see the note on Theorem SF.11), with the levels restricted to \((0,\psi)\) and an extra factor \(\mathsf u_\ridge(y)=(1-\ridge y/\mathsf A(y)^2)^{-1/2}\ge1\) at each root, where \(\mathsf A(y)=\gamma-r_\bmom(y)\) is the residual count. At level \(\eta\) there are \(X=\psi-\eta\) shifts left before the cut and \(x=\gamma-\eta\) residual directions. The lag \(\ell<X\) contributes about \(x-\ell\). Dividing the sum of these contributions by the residual count leaves \(\mathsf H(\eta)=X-X^2/(2x)\). Its average is exactly
\[ \frac1\gamma\int_0^{\psi}\Big(\psi-\eta-\frac{(\psi-\eta)^2}{2(\gamma-\eta)}\Big)\dif\eta=\frac\gamma2\,\mathcal P\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big), \]
and at \(\psi=\gamma\) it is the value \(\gamma/4\) of the zero-ridge case (Theorem SF.9). The errors of the counting formula cost \(O_\bmom((1+\psi)/\gamma)\) in the lag sum. So does the ridge factor, which is close to one except for roots within a bounded logarithmic distance of the top root \(q(-\ridge)\); the shifted roots \(\bmom^{-2\ell}z\), equally spaced in logarithmic scale with step \(2\log(1/\bmom)\), meet that window about once per step, about \(\psi\) times in all (Lemma SF.18). Hence \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)/N=\psi/\gamma+\gamma\,\mathcal P(\psi/\gamma)+O_\bmom((1+\psi)/\gamma)\), and dividing by \(\gamma\) and using the continuity of \(\mathcal P\) gives the volatility limit \(\mathcal P(c\wedge1)\).

Remark SF.1 (Momentum as a change of batch and of step)
In the oversampled square case \(N<B\), with \(\gamma=N/B\) small, the small-aspect theorems describe momentum at the level of the scalar equation (SF.19), read with its formal zero-ridge coefficients. For every step \(\macrostep\), with \(\macrostep'\defeq\macrostep/\sqrt{\sw2}=\macrostep\sqrt{(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)}\),
\[ -2\macrostep\,C_\bmom\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\macrostep^2}{2B}\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom} =-2\macrostep'\big(\sqrt{\sw2}\,C_\bmom\big)\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\macrostep'^2}{2B}\big(\sw2\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\big). \]
On the drift, \(\sqrt{\sw2}\,C_\bmom(\gamma)=C_0(\gamma)(1+O(\gamma))\) by Theorem SF.7(iii): momentum acts as the effective batch \(B/\sw2=B(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)\). On the volatility the factor is \(V_\bmom\), not \(1/\sw2\): \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}=N\) and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=NV_\bmom(1+O(\gamma))\) (Theorem SF.8), so that \(\sw2\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=\sw2V_\bmom\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}(1+O(\gamma))\) with \(\sw2V_\bmom\in(\sw2,1)\) (Lemma SF.10). Momentum at step \(\macrostep\) is therefore no momentum at step \(\macrostep'\) with the volatility multiplied by \(\sw2V_\bmom<1\). The step-rescaling reading is only approximate in the oversampled isotropic case: momentum carries less noise than the rescaled step, by the factor \(\sw2V_\bmom\), which is \(0.826\ldots\) at \(\bmom=\frac12\) and tends to \(0.7776\ldots\) as \(\bmom\uparrow1\). The main text writes \(B_{\rm eff}\defeq B/\sw2\) for the effective batch and \(c_\bmom\defeq\sw2V_\bmom\) for this factor, so that \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=c_\bmom NB_{\rm eff}/B\,\big(1+O(\gamma)\big)\). The factor \(1/\sw2\) for the volatility of Corollary SG.1(ii) belongs to a different regime, the resolved rows of a power-law spectrum with \(\gamma\to\infty\); that regime and the oversampled isotropic one are disjoint, and neither statement specializes to the other.
At large batch, momentum at step \(\macrostep\) is no momentum at the larger step \(\macrostep'=\macrostep\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/B}\), with the drift \(C_0\) of no momentum and the volatility of no momentum multiplied by \(c_\bmom=\sw2V_\bmom<1\), up to factors \(1+O(\gamma)\). The displayed identity is algebra; its content is the two large-batch laws \(\sqrt{\sw2}\,C_\bmom=C_0(1+O(\gamma))\) (Theorem SF.7(iii)) and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=NV_\bmom(1+O(\gamma))\) with \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}=N\) for \(N<B\) (Theorem SF.8 and Proposition SF.2). This is the reading behind \(B_{\rm eff}\) and \(c_\bmom\) in Figure 4 and in the large-batch discussion of the speedup in Section 3. It concerns the scalar equation with its formal zero-ridge coefficients, and the factor \(1/\sw2\) of the power-law regime (Corollary SG.1(ii)) belongs to a disjoint regime.
