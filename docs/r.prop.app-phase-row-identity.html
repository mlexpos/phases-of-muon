Proposition SI.1 (One step of a row)
Let \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be fixed, let \(u\) be a unit vector in \(\R^{\Nout}\), let \(\lr>0\), and let \({\mathsf S}\) be a random matrix with \(\EE\norm {\mathsf S}_\F^2<\infty\). Then
\[ \EE\norm{(\Delta-\lr {\mathsf S})^\T u}^2-\norm{\Delta^\T u}^2 =-2\lr\,\EE\langle\Delta^\T u,{\mathsf S}^\T u\rangle+\lr^2\,\EE\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u}^2 . \]
Proof.
Expanding the square gives
\[ \norm{(\Delta-\lr {\mathsf S})^\T u}^2=\norm{\Delta^\T u}^2-2\lr\langle\Delta^\T u,{\mathsf S}^\T u\rangle+\lr^2\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u}^2 . \]
Each term has finite expectation, because \(\abs{\langle\Delta^\T u,{\mathsf S}^\T u\rangle}\le\norm\Delta_\F\norm {\mathsf S}_\F\) and \(\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u}^2\le\norm {\mathsf S}_\F^2\). Taking expectations proves the identity.
□
