SJ SignSGD: drift and volatility

  1. SJ.1 The update and its risk identity
  2. SJ.2 Gaussian sign tools
  3. SJ.3 Isotropic drift
  4. SJ.4 Half-anisotropic data
This section computes the drift and the volatility of one step of SignSGD, the comparator of the main text, defined in (5). SignSGD is Adam at \(\beta_1=\beta_2=0\); it keeps no buffer, so this section has no momentum version, and the momentum \(\bmom\) of the other sections of the appendix does not enter it. Every statement concerns one step from a given state with one fresh batch; none is a statement about a trajectory. Section SJ.1 fixes the update and its exact risk identity. Section SJ.2 proves the Gaussian sign tools, among them the bounds on the batch constant \(\mathcal N_B\) and the exact isotropic volatility. Section SJ.3 proves the isotropic drift for the actual batch with an explicit error and deduces the isotropic recursion Proposition SJ.4. Section SJ.4 introduces an explicit law for the state in a Haar eigenbasis (Definition SJ.3) and proves under it the half-anisotropic drift (Theorem SJ.2), the covariance-weighted volatility (Theorem SJ.3) and the corrected row volatility (Theorem SJ.4), and from them the half-anisotropic recursion Proposition SJ.8.
Standing conventions. The sign of a real number is \(1\), \(-1\) or \(0\) according as the number is positive, negative or zero, and \(\sign\) of a matrix acts entrywise. The model, risk and moment identities are those of Definition SA.1, Assumption S1 and Proposition SA.1, and the conventions of Section SA.1 apply. Outer products are written \(x\tensor y\). For a matrix \(\Delta\) we write \(\Delta_{i\cdot}\) for its \(i\)th row and \(\Delta_{\cdot j}\) for its \(j\)th column, both as vectors. The step \(\lr\ge0\) is deterministic. Unless a subscript says otherwise, an expectation integrates the fresh batch and, in Section SJ.4, also the Haar matrix \(U\); such expectations are called annealed. A constant is called universal if it does not depend on any parameter of the model.

SJ.1 The update and its risk identity

For a state \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) and a batch \((\Xin^{(a)},\Xout^{(a)})_{a\le B}\) as in Definition SA.1, the physical gradient is \(G^\circ=\frac1B\sum_ad_a^\circ\,\Xout^{(a)}\tensor\Xin^{(a)}\) with \(d_a^\circ=(\Xout^{(a)})^\T\Delta\Xin^{(a)}\), as in Proposition SA.1(ii).
Definition SJ.1 (SignSGD step)
The SignSGD step from the state \(\Delta\) with step \(\lr\ge0\) is
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.1} \Delta^+\defeq\Delta-\lr S,\qquad S\defeq\sign(G^\circ). \end{equation}
The sign does not see positive scalars, so \(S=\sign(\Gt)\) for the normalized gradient of (SA.10) whenever the risk is positive. The update \(S\) is determined by the state and the batch, and \(\norm S_\F^2\le\Nout\Nin\). Hence Proposition SA.4 applies to it, with \(S\) as the update, and gives
\begin{align} \EE_t\big[\cR(t+1)\big]&=\cR(t)-\lr\big\langle\Dt,\EE_tS\big\rangle_\Sigma+\frac{\lr^2}2\,\EE_t\norm S_\Sigma^2,\tag{SJ.2}\\ \EE_t\big[\CMscr Q_i(\Delta_{t+1})\big]&=\CMscr Q_i(\Dt)-2\lr\,\EE_t\big\langle\Dt^\T u_i,S^\T u_i\big\rangle+\lr^2\,\EE_t\norm{S^\T u_i}^2.\tag{SJ.3} \end{align}
The second line is (SA.21) with \(\sum_j\cD_{ij}(t)=\EE_t\langle\Dt^\T u_i,S^\T u_i\rangle\) and \(\sum_j\cV_{ij}(t)=\EE_t\norm{S^\T u_i}^2\). The row energy is unhalved, so its quadratic term carries \(\lr^2\) and not \(\lr^2/2\).
The batch constant of the main text is
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.4} \mathcal N_B\defeq\EE\Big[\Big(\sum_{b=1}^Bx_b^2y_b^2\Big)^{1/2}\Big],\qquad x_b,y_b\ \text{independent standard Gaussian}. \end{equation}

SJ.2 Gaussian sign tools

Lemma SJ.1 (Arcsine law)
Let \((g_1,g_2)\) be a centered jointly Gaussian pair with variances \(v_1,v_2>0\) and correlation \(\rho=\EE[g_1g_2]/\sqrt{v_1v_2}\in[-1,1]\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.5} \EE\big[\sign(g_1)\sign(g_2)\big]=\frac2\pi\arcsin\rho . \end{equation}
If \(v_1=0\) or \(v_2=0\), the left side is \(0\).
Proof
Proof.
Sheppard’s formula [95], in the form of [97, Theorem 40], states that for \(\rho\)-correlated standard Gaussians \(g,h\) one has
\[ \PP\big(\sign(g)\ne\sign(h)\big)=\frac{\arccos\rho}\pi . \]
Its hypothesis holds for \(g=g_1/\sqrt{v_1}\) and \(h=g_2/\sqrt{v_2}\), and dividing by the positive standard deviations does not change the signs. Each \(g_i\) has a density, so the signs are \(\pm1\) almost surely and
\[ \EE\big[\sign(g_1)\sign(g_2)\big]=1-2\,\PP\big(\sign(g_1)\ne\sign(g_2)\big)=1-\frac2\pi\arccos\rho=\frac2\pi\arcsin\rho . \]
A centered Gaussian of variance zero vanishes almost surely, and its sign is then \(0\).
□
Lemma SJ.2 (Small shifts)
Let \(X\) have an even density \(f\), and put \(h(x)\defeq\EE\sign(X+x)\) for real \(x\).
  1. (i)
    For every real \(x\), with oriented integrals,
    \[ h(x)=2\int_0^xf(y)\,\dif y . \]
  2. (ii)
    If \(a>0\), \(\alpha>0\), \(L_f\ge0\) and \(\abs{f(y)-f(0)}\le L_f\abs y^\alpha\) for \(\abs y\le a\), then
    \[ \abs{h(x)-2xf(0)}\le\frac{2L_f}{\alpha+1}\abs x^{\alpha+1}\qquad(\abs x\le a). \]
  3. (iii)
    If \(f\) is continuously differentiable on \([-a,a]\) with a \(K_f\)-Lipschitz derivative, then \(\abs{h(x)-2xf(0)}\le\frac{K_f}3\abs x^3\) for \(\abs x\le a\).
  4. (iv)
    If \(X\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma>0\), then for every real \(x\)
    \begin{equation}\tag{SJ.6} \Big|h(x)-\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac x\sigma\Big|\le\frac{\abs x^3}{3\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma^3}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The identity (i) splits the law at the shifted origin. Symmetry puts mass \(\frac12\) on each half-line, so
\[ h(x)=\PP(X>-x)-\PP(X<-x)=1-2\,\PP(X<-x)=2\int_{-x}^0f(y)\,\dif y=2\int_0^xf(y)\,\dif y . \]
The bound (ii) integrates the modulus of \(f\) in (i):
\[ \abs{h(x)-2xf(0)}\le2\Big|\int_0^x\abs{f(y)-f(0)}\,\dif y\Big|\le2L_f\int_0^{\abs x}y^\alpha\,\dif y=\frac{2L_f}{\alpha+1}\abs x^{\alpha+1}. \]
For (iii), evenness forces \(f'(0)=0\). The Lipschitz bound then gives \(\abs{f'(y)}\le K_f\abs y\), and integrating once,
\[ \abs{f(y)-f(0)}\le\frac{K_f}2\,y^2\qquad(\abs y\le a). \]
This is (ii) with \(\alpha=2\) and \(L_f=K_f/2\).
For (iv), the Gaussian density \(f_\sigma(y)=f_\sigma(0)e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2)}\) satisfies, by \(0\le1-e^{-u}\le u\) for \(u\ge0\),
\[ \abs{f_\sigma(y)-f_\sigma(0)}\le\frac{f_\sigma(0)}{2\sigma^2}\,y^2\qquad(y\in\R). \]
This is (ii) with \(\alpha=2\), \(L_f=f_\sigma(0)/(2\sigma^2)\) and every \(a\), and \(2f_\sigma(0)=\sqrt{2/\pi}/\sigma\).
□
Remark SJ.1 (Why a regularity hypothesis)
Symmetry alone gives a quadratic error at best. For the Laplace density \(f(y)=\frac12e^{-\abs y}\), (i) gives \(h(x)=\sign(x)(1-e^{-\abs x})\), and
\[ \frac{h(x)-2xf(0)}{x^2}=\frac{1-e^{-x}-x}{x^2}\longrightarrow-\frac12\qquad(x\downarrow0). \]
Proposition SJ.1 (The batch constant)
For every integer \(B\ge1\), the constant (SJ.4) satisfies \(\mathcal N_1=2/\pi\) and
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.7} \max\Big\{\frac2\pi\sqrt B,\ \sqrt B-\frac4{\sqrt B}\Big\}\le\mathcal N_B\le\sqrt B,\qquad 0\le1-\frac{\mathcal N_B}{\sqrt B}\le\frac4B . \end{equation}
In particular \(\mathcal N_B/\sqrt B\to1\) as \(B\to\infty\).
Proof
Proof.
Put \(T_B\defeq\sum_bx_b^2y_b^2\), so that \(\mathcal N_B=\EE\sqrt{T_B}\). A standard Gaussian \(g\) has \(\EE\abs g=\sqrt{2/\pi}\), and independence gives
\[ \mathcal N_1=\EE\abs{x_1}\,\EE\abs{y_1}=\frac2\pi . \]
The normalized sum \(T_B/B\) has mean \(1\) and variance \(8/B\). Indeed the summands \(x_b^2y_b^2\) are independent with mean \(1\) and second moment \((\EE x_b^4)(\EE y_b^4)=9\), so
\[ \EE\frac{T_B}B=1,\qquad \EE\Big(\frac{T_B}B-1\Big)^2=\frac{9-1}B=\frac8B . \]
The upper bound is Jensen’s inequality: \(\mathcal N_B\le(\EE T_B)^{1/2}=\sqrt B\).
The Jensen deficit is at most half the variance. For every \(s\ge0\), rationalizing \(\sqrt s-1\) gives the identity
\[ \sqrt s=1+\frac{s-1}2-\frac{(s-1)^2}{2(\sqrt s+1)^2}. \]
The last term lies between \(0\) and \((s-1)^2/2\). Take \(s=T_B/B\) and expectations; the linear term has mean zero, so
\[ 0\le1-\EE\sqrt{T_B/B}\le\frac12\cdot\frac8B=\frac4B , \]
which is the second part of (SJ.7) and gives \(\mathcal N_B\ge\sqrt B-4/\sqrt B\).
The remaining lower bound compares the Euclidean and the \(\ell^1\) norms. For real \(z_1,\dots,z_B\),
\[ \Big(\sum_bz_b^2\Big)^{1/2}\ge\frac1{\sqrt B}\sum_b\abs{z_b}. \]
With \(z_b=x_by_b\) and expectations, \(\mathcal N_B\ge B^{-1/2}\cdot B\cdot\frac2\pi=\frac2\pi\sqrt B\).
□
The isotropic volatility is exact. It needs every gradient entry to be nonzero, which follows from the fact that the zero set of a real polynomial on \(\R^d\) that is not identically zero has Lebesgue measure zero; this is the polynomial case of [91], since polynomials are real analytic.
Proposition SJ.2 (Isotropic volatility)
Let \(\Sigmain=\Idin\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Idout\) in Assumption S1, let \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic, and let \(S=\sign(G^\circ)\) for a batch of size \(B\ge1\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.8} \EE\norm S_\Sigma^2=\EE\norm S_\F^2=\begin{cases}\Nin\Nout,&\Delta\ne0,\\ 0,&\Delta=0.\end{cases} \end{equation}
In particular the step (SJ.1) satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.9} \EE\,\cR(\Delta^+)=\cR(\Delta)-\lr\,\langle\Delta,\EE S\rangle_\F+\frac{\lr^2}2\,\Nin\Nout\,\mathbf 1_{\{\Delta\ne0\}} . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
With identity covariances, \(\norm S_\Sigma^2=\Tr(SS^\T)=\norm S_\F^2\) by (SA.2), so the first equality holds pointwise.
If \(\Delta=0\) then \(G^\circ=0\) and \(S=0\).
If \(\Delta\ne0\), every entry of \(G^\circ\) is nonzero almost surely. Indeed \(BG^\circ_{ij}\) is a polynomial in the batch coordinates. Setting all samples but the first to zero leaves the polynomial
\[ (z_1)_i\,(w_1)_j\,\big(z_1^\T\Delta w_1\big), \]
a product of three polynomials that are not identically zero, the last one because some \(\Delta_{kl}\ne0\). A product of polynomials that are not identically zero is not identically zero. The batch has a Gaussian density, so the zero set of this polynomial has probability zero [91]. A finite union over the entries shows that \(\norm S_\F^2=\Nin\Nout\) almost surely.
The risk identity is (SJ.2) at a deterministic state, with \(\langle\Delta,\EE S\rangle_\Sigma=\langle\Delta,\EE S\rangle_\F\) for identity covariances.
□

SJ.3 Isotropic drift

Throughout this subsection \(\Sigmain=\Idin\), \(\Sigmaout=\Idout\), the state \(\Delta\ne0\) is deterministic, and
\[ F\defeq\norm\Delta_\F=\sqrt{2\cR(\Delta)},\qquad G^\circ=\frac1B\sum_{a=1}^B\big(z_a^\T\Delta w_a\big)\,z_a\tensor w_a . \]
Fix an entry \((i,j)\). Write \(\bar\Delta\) for \(\Delta\) with row \(i\) and column \(j\) deleted, \(r\defeq(\Delta_{il})_{l\ne j}\) for the rest of row \(i\) and \(q\defeq(\Delta_{kj})_{k\ne i}\) for the rest of column \(j\). For one sample \((z,w)\) write \(\xi\defeq z_i\), \(\zeta\defeq w_j\), and \(z'\), \(w'\) for the remaining coordinates; the four blocks \(\xi,\zeta,z',w'\) are independent standard Gaussian. The single-sample coordinate is
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.10} X\defeq\big(z^\T\Delta w\big)\,\xi\zeta=\Delta_{ij}\,\xi^2\zeta^2+\xi^2\zeta\,(r^\T w')+\xi\zeta^2\,(q^\T z')+\xi\zeta\,\big(z'^\T\bar\Delta w'\big), \end{equation}
and \(BG^\circ_{ij}\) is the sum of \(B\) independent copies of \(X\).
Lemma SJ.3 (Moments of one coordinate)
The coordinate (SJ.10) has mean \(\EE X=\Delta_{ij}\), variance
\[ s^2\defeq\Var X=F^2+2\norm{\Delta_{i\cdot}}^2+2\norm{\Delta_{\cdot j}}^2+3\Delta_{ij}^2,\qquad F^2\le s^2\le8F^2, \]
and third centered moment
\[ m_3\defeq\EE(X-\Delta_{ij})^3=24\Delta_{ij}\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F^2+126\Delta_{ij}\big(\norm r^2+\norm q^2\big)+200\Delta_{ij}^3+54\,q^\T\bar\Delta r, \]
so that \(\abs{m_3}\le200\abs{\Delta_{ij}}F^2+54F\norm r\norm q\). For every integer \(p\ge1\), \(\EE\abs X^p\le((2p-1)!!)^2F^p\), and the standardized coordinate \(Y\defeq(X-\Delta_{ij})/s\) satisfies \(\EE Y^4\le88208\).
Proof
Proof.
Call the four terms of (SJ.10) \(T_1,\dots,T_4\). The moments are Gaussian pairings [89]; a product with an odd total degree in one of the independent blocks has mean zero. Only \(T_1\) has even degree in every block, so \(\EE X=\Delta_{ij}\EE\xi^2\EE\zeta^2=\Delta_{ij}\).
The second moment is the sum of the four squares, because every cross product has odd degree in \(z'\) or in \(w'\):
\[ \EE X^2=9\Delta_{ij}^2+3\norm r^2+3\norm q^2+\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F^2 . \]
With \(F^2=\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F^2+\norm r^2+\norm q^2+\Delta_{ij}^2\), \(\norm{\Delta_{i\cdot}}^2=\norm r^2+\Delta_{ij}^2\) and \(\norm{\Delta_{\cdot j}}^2=\norm q^2+\Delta_{ij}^2\), subtracting \(\Delta_{ij}^2\) gives the formula for \(s^2\). Each added term is at most \(F^2\) times its coefficient, so \(F^2\le s^2\le8F^2\).
The third moment keeps \(T_1^3\), the terms \(3T_1T_k^2\) for \(k=2,3,4\), and \(6T_2T_3T_4\). With \(\EE\xi^4=3\) and \(\EE\xi^6=15\),
\[ \EE X^3=225\Delta_{ij}^3+135\Delta_{ij}\big(\norm r^2+\norm q^2\big)+27\Delta_{ij}\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F^2+54\,q^\T\bar\Delta r . \]
Here \(54=6\,\EE\xi^4\EE\zeta^4\) and \(\EE[(r^\T w')(q^\T z')(z'^\T\bar\Delta w')]=q^\T\bar\Delta r\). The centered moment is \(m_3=\EE X^3-3\Delta_{ij}\EE X^2+2\Delta_{ij}^3\), which gives the stated formula. Its coefficients \(24,126,200\) are at most \(200\), and \(\abs{q^\T\bar\Delta r}\le F\norm q\norm r\), which gives the bound.
For the absolute moments, put \(d=z^\T\Delta w\). Conditionally on \(w\), \(d\) is centered Gaussian with variance \(\norm{\Delta w}^2\), so
\[ \EE d^{2p}=(2p-1)!!\,\EE\norm{\Delta w}^{2p}\le\big((2p-1)!!\big)^2F^{2p}. \]
The inequality holds because \(\norm{\Delta w}^2=F^2\sum_l\pi_lg_l^2\) for weights \(\pi_l\ge0\) summing to \(1\) (the normalized eigenvalues of \(\Delta^\T\Delta\)) and independent standard Gaussian \(g_l\), and Jensen’s inequality gives \((\sum_l\pi_lg_l^2)^p\le\sum_l\pi_lg_l^{2p}\). The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, which needs no independence of \(d\) and \(\xi\zeta\), gives
\[ \EE\abs X^p\le\big(\EE d^{2p}\,\EE\abs{\xi\zeta}^{2p}\big)^{1/2}\le\big((2p-1)!!\big)^2F^p . \]
Finally \((a+b)^4\le8(a^4+b^4)\), \(\EE X^4\le105^2F^4=11025F^4\) and \(s\ge F\) give
\[ \EE Y^4\le\frac{8\,(\EE X^4+\Delta_{ij}^4)}{s^4}\le8\,(11025+1)=88208 .\qedhere \]
□
The characteristic function of \(Y\) decays at a rate that depends only on the size of the deleted block \(\bar\Delta\). Let \(\xi,\zeta,g\) be independent standard Gaussian scalars and put
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.11} \psi(x)\defeq\EE\big(1+x^2\xi^2\zeta^2\big)^{-1/2}\quad(x\ge0),\qquad C_{\rm ch}\defeq\sqrt8\,\big(\EE\abs g^{-1/2}\big)^2 . \end{equation}
The function \(\psi\) is nonincreasing, \(\psi(x)<1\) for \(x>0\), and \(\EE\abs g^{-1/2}=2^{-1/4}\Gamma(1/4)/\sqrt\pi\) is finite.
Lemma SJ.4 (Decay of the characteristic function)
If \(\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F\ge F/2\), then \(\phi_Y(t)\defeq\EE e^{\mathrm i tY}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.12} \abs{\phi_Y(t)}\le\psi\big(\abs t/8\big)\le\min\big\{1,\ C_{\rm ch}\abs t^{-1/2}\big\}\qquad(t\ne0). \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The coordinate is conditionally Gaussian in \(z'\). Given \(\xi,\zeta,w'\), the terms of (SJ.10) containing \(z'\) are \(\xi\zeta\,z'^\T(\bar\Delta w'+\zeta q)\), a centered Gaussian of variance \(\xi^2\zeta^2\norm{\bar\Delta w'+\zeta q}^2\). Integrating \(z'\) first and taking absolute values gives
\[ \abs{\EE e^{\mathrm itX}}\le\EE\exp\Big(-\frac{t^2\xi^2\zeta^2}2\,\norm{\bar\Delta w'+\zeta q}^2\Big). \]
The integral over \(w'\) is bounded by the Frobenius norm of \(\bar\Delta\). For \(\lambda\ge0\) and a fixed vector \(h\), write \(\bar\Delta=\sum_l\sigma_l\,a_l\tensor b_l\) in singular value form. The coordinates \(b_l^\T w'\) are independent standard Gaussian, and completing the square gives, for one of them,
\[ \EE\,e^{-\lambda(\sigma_lg+c)^2/2}=(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2)^{-1/2}\,e^{-\lambda c^2/(2(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2))}\le(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2)^{-1/2}. \]
Directions orthogonal to the \(a_l\) contribute a factor at most \(1\), so
\[ \EE_{w'}e^{-\lambda\norm{\bar\Delta w'+h}^2/2}\le\prod_l(1+\lambda\sigma_l^2)^{-1/2}\le\big(1+\lambda\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F^2\big)^{-1/2}. \]
With \(\lambda=t^2\xi^2\zeta^2\) this gives
\[ \abs{\EE e^{\mathrm itX}}\le\psi\big(\abs t\,\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F\big). \]
Centering multiplies the characteristic function by a phase, so \(\abs{\phi_Y(t)}=\abs{\EE e^{\mathrm itX/s}}\le\psi(\abs t\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F/s)\). By Lemma SJ.3, \(\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F/s\ge(F/2)/(\sqrt8F)>1/8\), and \(\psi\) is nonincreasing, which gives the first inequality. The second follows from \((1+x^2)^{-1/2}\le x^{-1/2}\):
\[ \psi(x)\le x^{-1/2}\,\EE\abs\xi^{-1/2}\,\EE\abs\zeta^{-1/2}, \]
evaluated at \(x=\abs t/8\).
□
The following lemma compares a normalized batch sum with a Gaussian. It compares expected signs directly, uniformly in the shift, and uses only the characteristic function.
Lemma SJ.5 (Sign comparison)
There is a universal constant \(C_2\) such that the following holds with \(C_1\defeq4\sqrt{2\pi}/(3\pi)\). Let \(Y\) be centered with variance \(1\), \(\EE Y^4\le88208\), and characteristic function satisfying (SJ.12). Let \(Y_1,\dots,Y_B\) be independent copies of \(Y\), \(B\ge4\), and \(g\) standard Gaussian. Then for every real \(x\)
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.13} \Big|\EE\sign\Big(\frac1{\sqrt B}\sum_{a=1}^BY_a+x\Big)-\EE\sign(g+x)\Big|\le\frac{C_1\abs{\EE Y^3}}{\sqrt B}+\frac{C_2}B . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Write \(M_4\defeq88208\), \(\varkappa_3\defeq\EE Y^3\), \(\phi\defeq\phi_Y\), \(V\defeq B^{-1/2}\sum_aY_a\), and put
\[ \delta\defeq\min\big\{1,\sqrt{3/(2M_4)}\big\},\qquad \rho_0\defeq\psi(\delta/8)\in(0,1),\qquad C_4\defeq\frac{M_4}{24}+\frac18 . \]
Expected signs through the characteristic function. For \(\varepsilon>0\) and real \(v\),
\[ \frac2\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-\varepsilon u}\,\frac{\sin(uv)}u\,\dif u=\frac2\pi\arctan\frac v\varepsilon , \]
since both sides vanish at \(v=0\) and have the same derivative \(\frac2\pi\varepsilon/(\varepsilon^2+v^2)\) in \(v\). Apply this at \(v=V+x\) and at \(v=g+x\), take expectations and subtract. The shift contributes the phase \(e^{\mathrm iux}\), so
\[ \Big|\EE\frac2\pi\arctan\frac{V+x}\varepsilon-\EE\frac2\pi\arctan\frac{g+x}\varepsilon\Big|\le\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\big|\phi(u/\sqrt B)^B-e^{-u^2/2}\big|\,\frac{\dif u}u . \]
As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) the arctangents tend to the signs, including the value \(0\) at \(0\), and dominated convergence turns the left side into the left side of (SJ.13). It remains to bound the integral.
Small frequencies. Symmetrization bounds \(\abs\phi\) near the origin. For an independent copy \(Y'\), \(\abs{\phi(t)}^2=\EE\cos(t(Y-Y'))\), \(\EE(Y-Y')^2=2\) and \(\EE(Y-Y')^4=2\EE Y^4+6\le8M_4\). The inequality \(\cos y\le1-y^2/2+y^4/24\) gives, for \(\abs t\le\delta\),
\[ \abs{\phi(t)}^2\le1-t^2+\frac{M_4}3t^4\le1-\frac{t^2}2,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\abs{\phi(t)}\le e^{-t^2/4}. \]
Taylor’s formula with remainder for \(e^{\mathrm itY}\) to third order and for \(e^{-t^2/2}\) to second order gives
\[ \abs{\phi(t)-e^{-t^2/2}}\le\frac{\abs{\varkappa_3}}6\abs t^3+C_4t^4 . \]
Telescoping the \(B\)th powers, with both factors bounded by \(e^{-t^2/4}\) and \((B-1)/(4B)\ge1/8\), gives for \(0\le u\le\delta\sqrt B\)
\[ \big|\phi(u/\sqrt B)^B-e^{-u^2/2}\big|\le e^{-u^2/8}\Big(\frac{\abs{\varkappa_3}u^3}{6\sqrt B}+\frac{C_4u^4}B\Big). \]
With \(\int_0^\infty u^2e^{-u^2/8}\dif u=4\sqrt{2\pi}\) and \(\int_0^\infty u^3e^{-u^2/8}\dif u=32\), the integral over \([0,\delta\sqrt B]\) against \(\dif u/u\) is at most
\[ \frac{4\sqrt{2\pi}\,\abs{\varkappa_3}}{6\sqrt B}+\frac{32C_4}B . \]
Large frequencies. For \(\abs t\ge\delta\), (SJ.12) and the monotonicity of \(\psi\) give \(\abs{\phi(t)}\le\rho_0\) and \(\abs{\phi(t)}^4\le\min\{1,C_{\rm ch}^4t^{-2}\}\). Hence
\[ \int_{\delta\sqrt B}^\infty\abs{\phi(u/\sqrt B)}^B\frac{\dif u}u=\int_\delta^\infty\abs{\phi(t)}^B\frac{\dif t}t\le\rho_0^{B-4}\int_\delta^\infty\min\{1,C_{\rm ch}^4t^{-2}\}\frac{\dif t}t\le J\rho_0^{B-4}, \]
where, with \(S_0\defeq\max\{\delta,C_{\rm ch}^2\}\), \(J\defeq\log(S_0/\delta)+C_{\rm ch}^4/(2S_0^2)\). Maximizing \(y\mapsto y\rho_0^{y}\) gives \(B\rho_0^{B-4}\le\rho_0^{-4}/(e\abs{\log\rho_0})\). The Gaussian tail satisfies
\[ \int_{\delta\sqrt B}^\infty e^{-u^2/2}\frac{\dif u}u\le\frac{e^{-\delta^2B/2}}{\delta^2B}\le\frac1{\delta^2B}. \]
Conclusion. Multiplying the three bounds by \(2/\pi\) gives (SJ.13) with \(C_1=\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{4\sqrt{2\pi}}6\) and
\[ C_2\defeq\frac2\pi\Big(32C_4+\frac{J\rho_0^{-4}}{e\abs{\log\rho_0}}+\delta^{-2}\Big).\qedhere \]
□
The coordinate drift follows by combining the comparison with the Gaussian shift (SJ.6) and the batch constant.
Proposition SJ.3 (Drift of one coordinate)
Let \(B\ge4\) and suppose \(\norm{\bar\Delta}_\F\ge F/2\) for the entry \((i,j)\). With \(s,m_3\) from Lemma SJ.3 and \(C_1,C_2\) from Lemma SJ.5,
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.14} \begin{aligned} \Big|\EE\sign(G^\circ_{ij})-\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\mathcal N_B\frac{\Delta_{ij}}F\Big|\le{}&\frac{C_1\abs{m_3}}{s^3\sqrt B}+\frac{C_2}B+\frac{B^{3/2}\abs{\Delta_{ij}}^3}{3\sqrt{2\pi}\,s^3}\\ &+\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\sqrt B\,\abs{\Delta_{ij}}\,(s^2-F^2)}{sF(s+F)}+\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{4\abs{\Delta_{ij}}}{F\sqrt B}. \end{aligned} \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The sign of \(G^\circ_{ij}\) is the sign of a shifted normalized sum. With \(Y_a\) the standardized copies of \(X\),
\[ \sign\big(G^\circ_{ij}\big)=\sign\Big(\frac1{\sqrt B}\sum_aY_a+\frac{\sqrt B\,\Delta_{ij}}s\Big). \]
Lemma SJ.3 and Lemma SJ.4 verify the hypotheses of Lemma SJ.5, with \(\EE Y^3=m_3/s^3\). The comparison at \(x=\sqrt B\Delta_{ij}/s\) produces the first two terms. The Gaussian shift (SJ.6) with \(\sigma=1\) replaces \(\EE\sign(g+x)\) by \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\,x\) at the cost of the third term.
The last two terms replace \(\sqrt B/s\) by \(\mathcal N_B/F\). By the triangle inequality and Proposition SJ.1,
\[ \Big|\frac{\sqrt B}s-\frac{\mathcal N_B}F\Big|\le\sqrt B\Big(\frac1F-\frac1s\Big)+\frac{\sqrt B-\mathcal N_B}F\le\frac{\sqrt B\,(s^2-F^2)}{sF(s+F)}+\frac4{F\sqrt B}. \]
Multiplying by \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\abs{\Delta_{ij}}\) gives the last two terms.
□
The isotropic regime is the regime in which every entry of the state is small, with explicit constants.
Definition SJ.2 (Isotropic regime)
Fix \(K\ge1\), \(0<f_-\le f_+\) and \(C_\Delta>0\). A deterministic state \(\Delta\) with batch size \(B\) is in the isotropic regime if, with \(k\defeq\min\{\Nout,\Nin,B\}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.15} \max\{\Nout,\Nin,B\}\le Kk,\qquad f_-\le\norm\Delta_\F\le f_+,\qquad \max_{i,j}\abs{\Delta_{ij}}\le\frac{C_\Delta}k . \end{equation}
Theorem SJ.1 (Isotropic drift)
There is a constant \(C_r\) depending only on \(K,f_-,C_\Delta\) such that, for every state in the isotropic regime with \(k\ge k_0\defeq\max\{4,8KC_\Delta^2/f_-^2\}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.16} \Big|\EE\sign(G^\circ_{ij})-\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\mathcal N_B\frac{\Delta_{ij}}F\Big|\le e_k\defeq\frac{C_2}B+\frac{C_r}{k^{3/2}}\qquad\text{for all }(i,j). \end{equation}
With \(\mathsf D\defeq\langle\Delta,\EE S\rangle_\F\) and \(\mathsf D_0\defeq\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\mathcal N_BF=\mathcal N_B\sqrt{4\cR(\Delta)/\pi}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.17} \abs{\mathsf D-\mathsf D_0}\le KF\Big(C_2+\frac{C_r}{\sqrt k}\Big),\qquad \Big|\frac{\mathsf D}{\mathsf D_0}-1\Big|\le\frac{K(C_2+C_r/\sqrt k)}{\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\mathcal N_B}\le\frac{\pi K(C_2+C_r)}{2\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sqrt k}. \end{equation}
No hypothesis is made on the operator norm or the rank of \(\Delta\).
Proof
Proof.
The entry bound makes the deleted block large. Every row and column has squared norm at most \(KkC_\Delta^2/k^2=KC_\Delta^2/k\), so for \(k\ge k_0\)
\[ \norm{\bar\Delta}_\F^2\ge F^2-\frac{2KC_\Delta^2}k\ge\frac34F^2 , \]
and Proposition SJ.3 applies to every entry.
The four non-batch terms of (SJ.14) are of order \(k^{-3/2}\). With \(V\defeq(4K+3)C_\Delta^2\) and \(K_3\defeq200C_\Delta/f_-+54KC_\Delta^2/f_-^2\), Lemma SJ.3 gives
\[ 0\le s^2-F^2\le\frac Vk,\qquad \frac{\abs{m_3}}{s^3}\le\frac{K_3}k . \]
Insert these, \(k\le B\le Kk\), \(\abs{\Delta_{ij}}\le C_\Delta/k\) and \(s\ge F\ge f_-\) into (SJ.14). The result is (SJ.16) with
\[ C_r\defeq C_1K_3+\frac{K^{3/2}C_\Delta^3}{3\sqrt{2\pi}\,f_-^3}+\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\sqrt K\,C_\Delta V}{2f_-^3}+\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{4C_\Delta}{f_-}. \]
The sum over entries costs the \(\ell^1\) norm of \(\Delta\). Since \(\sum_{ij}\Delta_{ij}^2/F=F\),
\[ \abs{\mathsf D-\mathsf D_0}\le\sum_{i,j}\abs{\Delta_{ij}}\,e_k\le\sqrt{\Nout\Nin}\,F\,e_k\le KkF\Big(\frac{C_2}k+\frac{C_r}{k^{3/2}}\Big). \]
Dividing by \(\mathsf D_0\) and using \(\mathcal N_B\ge\frac2\pi\sqrt B\ge\frac2\pi\sqrt k\) from Proposition SJ.1 gives the relative bound.
□
The summed error is of order one against a leading term of order \(\sqrt k\). The isotropic recursion of the main text follows.
Proposition SJ.4 (Isotropic SignSGD recursion)
Let \(\Sigmain=\Idin\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Idout\), and let \(\Dt\) be the state at step \(t\). Suppose that, almost surely, \(\Dt\) with the batch size \(B\) is in the isotropic regime of Definition SJ.2 and \(k\ge k_0\). Then the SignSGD step satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.18} \EE_t\big[\cR(t+1)\big]=\cR(t)-\lr\,\mathcal N_B\sqrt{\frac{4\cR(t)}\pi}+\frac{\lr^2}2\,\Nin\Nout+E_t,\qquad \abs{E_t}\le\lr\,K\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\Big(C_2+\frac{C_r}{\sqrt k}\Big), \end{equation}
with \(C_2\) universal and \(C_r\) from Theorem SJ.1. The error is at most \(\pi K(C_2+C_r)/(2\sqrt{2/\pi}\sqrt k)\) times the drift term.
Proof
Proof.
Conditionally on the past, \(\Dt\) is fixed and the batch has its unconditional law, because the batch is independent of the past. The identity (SJ.9) of Proposition SJ.2 at \(\Delta=\Dt\ne0\) gives
\[ \EE_t\big[\cR(t+1)\big]=\cR(t)-\lr\,\mathsf D+\frac{\lr^2}2\,\Nin\Nout . \]
Theorem SJ.1 replaces \(\mathsf D\) by \(\mathsf D_0=\mathcal N_B\sqrt{4\cR(t)/\pi}\) with the stated error, since \(F=\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\).
□
In the main text’s notation, (SJ.18) is (8) with \(\mathfrak d=\mathcal N_B/\sqrt\pi\) and \(\mathfrak v=\Nin\Nout\); the volatility is exact and the drift carries a relative error of order \(k^{-1/2}\).

SJ.4 Half-anisotropic data

SignSGD depends on the basis in which the sign is taken. The main text draws the eigenbasis of \(\Sigmaout\) from the Haar measure ((13)). The following definition adds the law of the state that the proofs need.
Definition SJ.3 (Haar state law)
Fix \(K\ge1\), \(0<c\le L\), \(0<f_-\le f_+\) and \(C_{\rm col}>0\). Let \(\Nout,\Nin,B\) be positive integers with \(k\defeq\min\{\Nout,\Nin,B\}\ge2\) and \(\max\{\Nout,\Nin,B\}\le Kk\). Let \(\Lambda=\diag(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_{\Nout})\) and \(D\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic with
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.19} c\,\Idout\preceq\Lambda\preceq L\,\Idout,\qquad f_-\le\norm D_\F\le f_+,\qquad \max_j\norm{D_{\cdot j}}^2\le\frac{C_{\rm col}}k . \end{equation}
Let \(U\) be Haar distributed on the orthogonal group \(O(\Nout)\) and independent of the whitened batch \((z_b,w_b)_{b\le B}\). The half-anisotropic Haar model is
\[ \Sigmaout=U\Lambda U^\T,\qquad \Sigmain=\Idin,\qquad \Delta=UD,\qquad \Xout^{(b)}=U\Lambda^{1/2}z_b,\qquad \Xin^{(b)}=w_b . \]
In this model \(u_i=Ue_i\) is the eigenvector of \(\mu_i\), the row energies are \(\CMscr Q_i(\Delta)=\norm{u_i^\T\Delta}^2=\norm{D_{i\cdot}}^2\), and the risk is deterministic:
\[ 2\cR\defeq\norm\Delta_\Sigma^2=\Tr\big(\Lambda DD^\T\big). \]
This is the whitened form of Assumption S1, and the residuals are \(d_b^\circ=z_b^\T\Lambda^{1/2}Dw_b\), which do not involve \(U\). We use
\[ \bar\mu\defeq\frac{\Tr\Lambda}{\Nout},\qquad T_D\defeq\Lambda DD^\T\Lambda,\qquad L_B\defeq\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\mathcal N_B}{\sqrt{2\cR\,\bar\mu}}=\frac{\mathcal N_B}{\sqrt{\pi\cR\,\bar\mu}}, \]
the fixed constants
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.20} P_-\defeq cf_-^2,\quad P_+\defeq Lf_+^2,\quad H_0\defeq L^2f_+^2,\quad A_0\defeq\frac{H_0}c,\quad A_1\defeq\frac{L^2C_{\rm col}}c,\quad A_2\defeq LC_{\rm col}, \end{equation}
which satisfy \(P_-\le2\cR\le P_+\), \(\Tr T_D\le H_0\) and \(c\le\bar\mu\le L\), and the batch mean of the update given the eigenbasis,
\[ M(U)\defeq\EE\big[S\,\big|\,U\big],\qquad S=\sign(G^\circ), \]
whose entries lie in \([-1,1]\). A constant is called admissible if it depends only on \(K,c,L,f_-,f_+,C_{\rm col}\).
An entrywise bound of order \(1/\min\{\Nout,\Nin,B\}\) on the state does not make the residual of a deleted coordinate asymptotically Gaussian, which is why Definition SJ.3 prescribes the law of the state. For \(\Delta=d^{-1}\mathbf 1\tensor\mathbf 1\in\R^{d\times d}\) every entry is \(1/d\) and \(\norm\Delta_\F=1\), but
\[ z^\T\Delta w=\Big(d^{-1/2}\sum_iz_i\Big)\Big(d^{-1/2}\sum_jw_j\Big) \]
is a product of two independent standard Gaussians for every \(d\).
The independence of \(D\) and \(U\) is used in every proof below. After one step the new state need not be of the form \(UD'\) with \(D'\) independent of \(U\), so the results below do not iterate.

SJ.4.1 Haar tools

For \(q\ge1\) put \(d_q(n)\defeq n(n+2)\cdots(n+2q-2)\).
Lemma SJ.6 (Spherical moments)
Let \(v\) be uniform on the unit sphere of \(\R^n\), \(n\ge2\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.21} \EE\norm v_1^2=1+\frac{2(n-1)}\pi . \end{equation}
For a vector \(a\), a positive semidefinite \(T\) and an integer \(1\le q\le4\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.22} \EE\abs{v^\T a}^{2q}=\frac{(2q-1)!!\,\norm a^{2q}}{d_q(n)},\qquad \EE\big(v^\T Tv\big)^q\le\frac{(2q-1)!!\,(\Tr T)^q}{d_q(n)}. \end{equation}
For symmetric \(H\) with \(\Tr H=0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.23} \EE\big(v^\T Hv\big)^4=\frac{12(\Tr H^2)^2+48\Tr H^4}{d_4(n)} . \end{equation}
In particular, if \(c\Id\preceq\Lambda\preceq L\Id\) in dimension \(n\) and \(\bar\mu=\Tr\Lambda/n\), then \(\EE(v^\T\Lambda v-\bar\mu)^4\le60L^4/n^2\).
Proof
Proof.
A standard Gaussian vector \(g\) in \(\R^n\) is \(g=Rv\) with radius \(R\) and direction \(v\) independent, and \(\EE R^{2q}=d_q(n)\) by the Gaussian moments of \(\norm g^2\). Every identity follows by computing a Gaussian moment and dividing by \(\EE R^{2q}\).
For (SJ.21), \(\EE R^2=n\), and \(\EE\abs{g_ig_j}=2/\pi\) for \(i\ne j\) gives \(\EE\abs{v_iv_j}=2/(\pi n)\). Expanding the square of the \(\ell^1\) norm,
\[ \EE\norm v_1^2=\sum_i\EE v_i^2+\sum_{i\ne j}\EE\abs{v_iv_j}=1+n(n-1)\frac2{\pi n}. \]
For (SJ.22), rotation invariance gives \(\EE\abs{g^\T a}^{2q}=(2q-1)!!\norm a^{2q}\). For \(T\), write \(g^\T Tg=\Tr T\sum_l\pi_lg_l^2\) with weights \(\pi_l\ge0\) summing to \(1\); Jensen’s inequality gives \(\EE(\sum_l\pi_lg_l^2)^q\le(2q-1)!!\).
For (SJ.23), diagonalize \(H\) with eigenvalues \(h_l\) of sum zero. Then \(g^\T Hg=\sum_lh_l(g_l^2-1)\) is a sum of independent centered terms with \(\EE(g_l^2-1)^2=2\) and \(\EE(g_l^2-1)^4=60\). Only multiplicities four and two-two survive in the fourth power:
\[ \EE(g^\T Hg)^4=60\sum_lh_l^4+24\sum_{l<l'}h_l^2h_{l'}^2=12(\Tr H^2)^2+48\Tr H^4 . \]
For \(H=\Lambda-\bar\mu\Id\), \(\norm H\le L\), so \(\Tr H^2\le nL^2\) and \(\Tr H^4\le nL^4\), and \(d_4(n)\ge n^4\) gives the last bound.
□
Lemma SJ.7 (Haar sign matrix)
Let \(U\) be Haar on \(O(n)\), \(n\ge2\), let \(a\in\R^n\) be nonzero, and put \(Y\defeq U^\T\sign(Ua)\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.24} \EE_U\big[Y\tensor Y\big]=\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)\Id+\frac{2n}\pi\,\frac{a\tensor a}{\norm a^2}. \end{equation}
The same holds conditionally on a random nonzero \(a\) independent of \(U\).
Proof
Proof.
Put \(v=a/\norm a\), so that \(Y=U^\T\sign(Uv)\). The vector \(Uv\) is uniform on the sphere, so it has no zero coordinate almost surely, and \(\norm Y^2=n\).
Invariance reduces the second moment to two numbers. If \(Q\) is orthogonal with \(Qv=v\), then \(UQ\) is Haar and replacing \(U\) by \(UQ\) replaces \(Y\) by \(Q^\T Y\). Hence \(\EE[Y\tensor Y]\) commutes with every such \(Q\). In an orthonormal basis starting with \(v\), reflections of single other basis vectors make the off-diagonal entries vanish, and permutations of the other basis vectors make their diagonal entries equal. Therefore
\[ \EE[Y\tensor Y]=c_n\,v\tensor v+d_n(\Id-v\tensor v). \]
The two numbers follow from two scalar identities. First, \(v^\T Y=(Uv)^\T\sign(Uv)=\norm{Uv}_1\), so \(c_n=1+2(n-1)/\pi\) by (SJ.21). Second, the trace is \(\EE\norm Y^2=n\), so \(c_n+(n-1)d_n=n\). Solving gives \(d_n=1-2/\pi\) and \(c_n-d_n=2n/\pi\). Conditioning on an independent \(a\) gives the last claim.
□
The arcsine law and the Haar sign matrix give the row volatility exactly for one sample. At this batch size the per-row volatility does not depend on the state, which may even be a function of the eigenbasis.
Proposition SJ.5 (One sample)
Let \(B=1\), \(\Nout\ge2\), \(\Sigmaout=U\Lambda U^\T\) with \(U\) Haar, \(\Lambda=\diag(\mu_i)\succ0\), \(\Sigmain=\Idin\) and \(u_i\defeq Ue_i\), and let the sample be \(\Xout=U\Lambda^{1/2}z\), \(\Xin=w\) with \((z,w)\) standard Gaussian and independent of \(U\). Let \(\Delta\) be any function of \(U\) with \(\Delta\ne0\) almost surely, and \(S=\sign(G^\circ)\). Then
\[ \EE\norm{S^\T u_i}^2=\Nin\,\EE\big[u_i^\T\Sigmaoutsign u_i\big]=\Nin\Big(1-\frac2\pi+\frac{2\Nout}\pi\,\EE\frac{\mu_ig_i^2}{\sum_l\mu_lg_l^2}\Big), \]
where \((\Sigmaoutsign)_{kl}\defeq\frac2\pi\arcsin\big((\Sigmaout)_{kl}/\sqrt{(\Sigmaout)_{kk}(\Sigmaout)_{ll}}\big)\) and \(g\) is a standard Gaussian vector in \(\R^{\Nout}\).
Proof
Proof.
For one sample the update factorizes. Given \(U\), the vector \(y=\Xout\) has the nondegenerate law \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigmaout)\), so its coordinates are nonzero almost surely, and so are those of \(x=\Xin\). The residual \(y^\T\Delta x\) is, given \(U\) and \(x\), Gaussian with variance \(\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Delta x}^2\), which is positive almost surely because \(\Delta\ne0\). Hence, entrywise,
\[ S=\sign\big(y^\T\Delta x\big)\,\sign(y)\tensor\sign(x), \]
and therefore
\[ \norm{S^\T u_i}^2=\big(u_i^\T\sign(y)\big)^2\norm{\sign(x)}^2=\Nin\big(u_i^\T\sign(y)\big)^2 . \]
The residual sign has disappeared, so the state no longer enters.
Given \(U\), Lemma SJ.1 applies to every pair of coordinates of \(y\), whose variances \((\Sigmaout)_{kk}\) are positive, and gives \(\EE[\sign(y)\tensor\sign(y)\mid U]=\Sigmaoutsign\). This is the first equality.
For the second, write \(y=Ua\) with \(a=\Lambda^{1/2}g\), nonzero almost surely and independent of \(U\). Then \(u_i^\T\sign(y)=e_i^\T U^\T\sign(Ua)\), and Lemma SJ.7 gives
\[ \EE\big[(u_i^\T\sign(y))^2\,\big|\,g\big]=1-\frac2\pi+\frac{2\Nout}\pi\,\frac{\mu_ig_i^2}{\sum_l\mu_lg_l^2}.\qedhere \]
□

SJ.4.2 Half-anisotropic drift

The coordinate drift of an anisotropic gradient is the isotropic one for a whitened state. Let \(\Sigmaout=\Sigma\) be positive semidefinite in dimension \(\Nout\) and \(\Sigmain=\Idin\), let \(\Delta\) be deterministic with \(2\cR=\Tr(\Sigma\Delta\Delta^\T)>0\), and fix an entry \((p,j)\) with \(v_p\defeq\Sigma_{pp}>0\). Put
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.25} \mu_{pj}\defeq\frac{(\Sigma\Delta)_{pj}}{\sqrt{v_p}},\qquad R_p\defeq\frac{\norm{(\Sigma\Delta)_{p\cdot}}^2}{v_p},\qquad C_j\defeq\Delta_{\cdot j}^\T\Sigma\Delta_{\cdot j}. \end{equation}
Lemma SJ.8 (Whitening a coordinate)
Let \(Q\) be orthogonal with first column \(\Sigma^{1/2}e_p/\sqrt{v_p}\) and put \(A\defeq Q^\T\Sigma^{1/2}\Delta\). Then \(\norm A_\F^2=2\cR\), the entry \(A_{1j}\) equals \(\mu_{pj}\), row \(1\) of \(A\) has squared norm \(R_p\), column \(j\) of \(A\) has squared norm \(C_j\), and \(\sign(G^\circ_{pj})\) has the law of \(\sign(G^A_{1j})\), where \(G^A\) is the isotropic gradient of the state \(A\). In particular Proposition SJ.3 applies to \((p,j)\) with \(F^2=2\cR\), \(\Delta_{ij}\) replaced by \(\mu_{pj}\), and \(\norm{\bar A}_\F^2=2\cR-R_p-C_j+\mu_{pj}^2\), whenever this is at least \(\cR/2\) and \(B\ge4\); its comparison term is
\[ \sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\mathcal N_B\frac{\mu_{pj}}{\sqrt{2\cR}}=\frac{\mathcal N_B\,(\Sigma\Delta)_{pj}}{\sqrt{\pi\cR\,v_p}} . \]
Proof
Proof.
Represent the output feature as \(y=\Sigma^{1/2}Qz\) with \(z\) standard Gaussian. Since \(Q^\T\Sigma^{1/2}e_p=\sqrt{v_p}\,e_1\), the coordinate is \(y_p=\sqrt{v_p}\,z_1\), and the residual is \(y^\T\Delta x=z^\T Ax\). Hence
\[ \big(y^\T\Delta x\big)\,y_px_j=\sqrt{v_p}\,\big(z^\T Ax\big)\,z_1x_j , \]
and the positive factor \(\sqrt{v_p}\) does not change the sign of the batch sum. Row \(1\) of \(A\) is \(e_p^\T\Sigma\Delta/\sqrt{v_p}\) and column \(j\) has squared norm \(\Delta_{\cdot j}^\T\Sigma\Delta_{\cdot j}\), and \(\norm A_\F^2=\Tr(\Sigma\Delta\Delta^\T)\). The deleted block has the squared norm left after removing row \(1\) and column \(j\).
□
In the Haar model, \(\Sigma\Delta=U\Lambda D\). Write \(\omega_p\defeq U^\T e_p\) for the \(p\)th row of \(U\), a uniform unit vector, so that
\[ v_p=\omega_p^\T\Lambda\omega_p\in[c,L],\qquad \mu_{pj}=\frac{\omega_p^\T\Lambda D_{\cdot j}}{\sqrt{v_p}},\qquad R_p=\frac{\omega_p^\T T_D\omega_p}{v_p},\qquad C_j=D_{\cdot j}^\T\Lambda D_{\cdot j}\le\frac{A_2}k . \]
The last quantity is deterministic.
Lemma SJ.9 (Deletion estimates)
In the Haar model put
\[ Q_0\defeq105\Big(\frac{4A_0}{P_-}\Big)^4,\qquad k_*\defeq\max\{4,\lceil4A_2/P_-\rceil\}, \]
and let \(k\ge k_*\). On the event \(\mathcal A_p\defeq\{R_p\le P_-/4\}\), the deleted block of Lemma SJ.8 has squared norm at least \(\cR\). For \(1\le q\le4\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.26} \EE R_p^q\le\frac{(2q-1)!!\,A_0^q}{\Nout^q},\qquad \EE\abs{\mu_{pj}}^{2q}\le\frac{(2q-1)!!\,A_1^q}{\Nout^qk^q},\qquad \PP(\mathcal A_p^c)\le\frac{Q_0}{\Nout^4}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Ellipticity removes the random denominator: \(v_p\ge c\). Then (SJ.22) with \(T=T_D\), \(\Tr T_D\le H_0\), and with \(a=\Lambda D_{\cdot j}\), \(\norm a^2\le L\,C_j\le L^2C_{\rm col}/k\), together with \(d_q(\Nout)\ge\Nout^q\), gives the two moment bounds. On \(\mathcal A_p\), \(R_p+C_j\le P_-/4+A_2/k\le P_-/2\le\cR\), so the deleted block has squared norm \(2\cR-R_p-C_j+\mu_{pj}^2\ge\cR\). Markov’s inequality for \(R_p^4\) gives the probability bound.
□
Proposition SJ.6 (Coordinate errors in Frobenius norm)
In the Haar model with \(k\ge k_*\), let \(D_v\defeq\diag(v_1,\dots,v_{\Nout})\) and
\[ E\defeq M(U)-\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\mathcal N_B}{\sqrt{2\cR}}\,D_v^{-1/2}U\Lambda D . \]
There is an admissible constant \(C_E\) such that \(\EE_U\norm E_\F^2\le C_E\).
Proof
Proof.
The entry \(E_{pj}\) is the error of Proposition SJ.3 for the whitened state of Lemma SJ.8. We square and sum the five terms of (SJ.14) before averaging over \(U\); this keeps the batch remainder at the scale of the whole matrix.
Good events. On \(\mathcal A_p\) the bound (SJ.14) applies, with \(F^2=2\cR\ge P_-\). Call its five terms \(t_1,\dots,t_5\), so that \(E_{pj}^2\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A_p}\le5\sum_lt_l^2\). Here \(s^3\ge(2\cR)^{3/2}\),
\[ m_3^2\le80000\,\mu_{pj}^2(2\cR)^2+5832\,(2\cR)R_pC_j,\qquad s^2-2\cR\le5(R_p+C_j), \]
the last because \(\mu_{pj}^2\le R_p\). Summing over the \(\Nout\Nin\) entries with (SJ.26) and \(\Nout,\Nin,B\in[k,Kk]\) gives

Theorem SJ.2 (Half-anisotropic drift)
In the Haar model of Definition SJ.3 with \(k\ge k_*\), there is an admissible constant \(C_*\) such that
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.27} \EE_U\big\|U^\T M(U)-L_B\,\Lambda D\big\|_\F^2\le C_* , \end{equation}
while the leading term satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.28} b_-B\le\norm{L_B\Lambda D}_\F^2\le b_+B,\qquad b_-\defeq\frac{8c^2f_-^2}{\pi^3L^2f_+^2},\quad b_+\defeq\frac{2L^2f_+^2}{\pi c^2f_-^2}. \end{equation}

Proof
Proof.
The diagonal normalization of Proposition SJ.6 is replaced by the constant \(\bar\mu\). Since \(U^\T U\Lambda D=\Lambda D\) and \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\mathcal N_B/\sqrt{2\cR\bar\mu}=L_B\),
\[ U^\T M-L_B\Lambda D=U^\T E+U^\T N,\qquad N\defeq\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\mathcal N_B}{\sqrt{2\cR}}\big(D_v^{-1/2}-\bar\mu^{-1/2}\Id\big)U\Lambda D . \]
The correction \(N\) is controlled by the fluctuation of the diagonal of \(\Sigmaout\). On \([c,L]\) the derivative of \(x^{-1/2}\) is at most \(\frac12c^{-3/2}\) in absolute value, and the \(p\)th row of \(U\Lambda D\) has squared norm \(e_p\defeq\omega_p^\T T_D\omega_p\). Hence
\[ \EE_U\norm N_\F^2\le\frac{\frac2\pi B}{4P_-c^3}\sum_p\EE_U\big[(v_p-\bar\mu)^2e_p\big]. \]

Summing over \(\Nout\) rows and using \(B\le K\Nout\) gives
\[ \EE_U\norm N_\F^2\le C_N\defeq\frac2\pi K\sqrt{180}L^2H_0/(4P_-c^3). \]
Orthogonal invariance of the Frobenius norm, \(\norm{X+Y}_\F^2\le2\norm X_\F^2+2\norm Y_\F^2\) and Proposition SJ.6 give (SJ.27) with \(C_*\defeq2C_E+2C_N\).
For (SJ.28), write
\[ \norm{L_B\Lambda D}_\F^2=\frac2\pi\,\frac{\mathcal N_B^2\,\norm{\Lambda D}_\F^2}{2\cR\,\bar\mu}, \]
and use \(c^2f_-^2\le\norm{\Lambda D}_\F^2\le L^2f_+^2\), \(c^2f_-^2\le2\cR\bar\mu\le L^2f_+^2\), and \(4B/\pi^2\le\mathcal N_B^2\le B\) from Proposition SJ.1.

SJ.4.3 Half-anisotropic volatility

The update factors through the eigenbasis. With \(A\defeq\Lambda^{1/2}D\), which is \(U^\T\At\) for the rotated error of (SA.9),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.31} G^\circ=UH,\qquad H\defeq\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B\big(z_b^\T Aw_b\big)\,\Lambda^{1/2}z_b\tensor w_b , \end{equation}
and \(H\) is independent of \(U\). Its \(j\)th column is the mean \(h_j=\frac1B\sum_bX_b\) of independent copies of
\[ X\defeq\big(z^\T Aw\big)\,w_j\,\Lambda^{1/2}z\in\R^{\Nout},\qquad m_j\defeq\Lambda D_{\cdot j},\qquad Z\defeq X-m_j . \]
Lemma SJ.10 (Column moments)
There are admissible constants \(V_0\), \(A_4\), \(A_8\) such that, for every column \(j\),
\begin{align} \EE X&=m_j,\qquad V_j\defeq\EE[Z\tensor Z]=(2\cR+2C_j)\Lambda+2T_D+3\,m_j\tensor m_j,\tag{SJ.32}\\ \EE[h_j\tensor h_j]&=\frac{2\cR+2C_j}B\,\Lambda+\frac2B\,T_D+\Big(1+\frac3B\Big)m_j\tensor m_j,\tag{SJ.33}\\ \EE[HH^\T]&=\frac{2\cR\,(\Nin+2)}B\,\Lambda+\Big(1+\frac{2\Nin+3}B\Big)T_D,\tag{SJ.34} \end{align}
with \(C_j=D_{\cdot j}^\T\Lambda D_{\cdot j}\), and
\begin{gather} V_j\succeq2\cR\,\Lambda,\qquad \norm{V_j}\le V_0,\qquad \norm{m_j}^2\le\frac{L^2C_{\rm col}}k,\tag{SJ.35}\\ \EE\abs{a^\T Z}^4\le A_4\norm a^4,\quad \EE\abs{a^\T Z}^8\le A_8\norm a^8\quad(a\in\R^{\Nout}),\tag{SJ.36}\\ \EE\norm Z^4\le A_4\Nout^2,\quad \EE\norm Z^8\le A_8\Nout^4 .\tag{SJ.37} \end{gather}
Every column \(h_j\) is nonzero almost surely.
Proof
Proof.
Mean and covariance. Put \(y=\Lambda^{1/2}z\). Integrating \(w\) first with the Gaussian identities \(\EE[(y^\T Dw)w_j]=y^\T D_{\cdot j}\) and \(\EE[(y^\T Dw)^2w_j^2]=y^\T DD^\T y+2(y^\T D_{\cdot j})^2\), and then \(z\) with the pairing identity \(\EE[y\tensor y\,(y^\T My)]=\Tr(\Lambda M)\Lambda+2\Lambda M\Lambda\) for symmetric \(M\) [89], gives
\[ \EE X=\Lambda D_{\cdot j},\qquad \EE[X\tensor X]=(2\cR+2C_j)\Lambda+2T_D+4\,m_j\tensor m_j . \]
Subtracting \(m_j\tensor m_j\) gives (SJ.32). Averaging \(B\) independent copies gives (SJ.33), and summing over \(j\) with \(\sum_jC_j=2\cR\) and \(\sum_jm_j\tensor m_j=T_D\) gives (SJ.34).
Bounds. All terms of \(V_j\) are positive semidefinite, so \(V_j\succeq2\cR\Lambda\). With \(C_j\le LC_{\rm col}/k\), \(\norm{m_j}^2\le L^2C_{\rm col}/k\) and \(\norm{T_D}\le\Tr T_D\le H_0\),
\[ \norm{V_j}\le V_0\defeq LP_++2H_0+5L^2C_{\rm col}. \]
Higher moments. Put \(r=z^\T Aw\). Conditionally on \(w\), \(r\) is Gaussian with variance \(\norm{Aw}^2\), and the convexity argument of Lemma SJ.3 gives \(\EE r^{2q}\le((2q-1)!!)^2(2\cR)^q\). For \(p=4,8\), Hölder’s inequality on the three dependent factors of \(a^\T X\) gives
\[ \EE\abs{a^\T X}^p\le\big(\EE\abs r^{3p}\,\EE\abs{w_j}^{3p}\,\EE\abs{a^\T\Lambda^{1/2}z}^{3p}\big)^{1/3}\le\big((3p-1)!!\big)^2P_+^{p/2}L^{p/2}\norm a^p . \]
Centering costs a factor \(2^p\), which gives the directional bounds with \(A_4\defeq16(11!!)^2P_+^2L^2\) and \(A_8\defeq256(23!!)^2P_+^4L^4\). The inequalities \((\sum_lZ_l^2)^2\le\Nout\sum_lZ_l^4\) and \((\sum_lZ_l^2)^4\le\Nout^3\sum_lZ_l^8\) give the vector bounds.
Nonvanishing. Some coordinate of \(h_j\) is a polynomial in the batch that is not identically zero. Indeed choose \(w=e_j\) if \(D_{\cdot j}\ne0\) and \(w=e_j+e_l\) with \(D_{\cdot l}\ne0\) otherwise, so that \(w_j=1\) and \(Aw\ne0\). Taking \(z=Aw\) in the first sample and all other samples zero makes \(h_j\) a nonzero multiple of \(\Lambda^{1/2}Aw\ne0\). The batch has a Gaussian density, so this coordinate is nonzero almost surely [91].

Proposition SJ.7 (Quadratic forms of a column)
There are admissible constants \(V_*\) and \(D_*\) such that, for every symmetric \(\Pi\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nout}\) and every column \(j\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.38} \EE\Big|h_j^\T\Pi h_j-\frac{2\cR}B\Tr(\Pi\Lambda)\Big|\le\norm\Pi\Big(\sqrt{\frac{V_*}k}+\frac{D_*}k\Big). \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Put \(W\defeq\frac1B\sum_bZ_b\), \(V\defeq V_j\) and \(a\defeq\norm\Pi\), so that \(h_j^\T\Pi h_j=m_j^\T\Pi m_j+2m_j^\T\Pi W+W^\T\Pi W\).
The fluctuation. The variance of the Gram term is
\[ \Var\big(W^\T\Pi W\big)=\frac{\Var(Z_1^\T\Pi Z_1)}{B^3}+\frac{2(B-1)}{B^3}\Tr(\Pi V\Pi V). \]
To see this, expand \(B^2W^\T\Pi W=\sum_bZ_b^\T\Pi Z_b+2\sum_{b<b'}Z_b^\T\Pi Z_{b'}\). A covariance between two terms vanishes unless every sample index occurs in both, because an index that occurs once has a centered factor. The diagonal terms are independent, and each of the \(B(B-1)/2\) cross pairs has second moment \(\Tr(\Pi V\Pi V)\). The two pieces are bounded by
\[ \Var(Z_1^\T\Pi Z_1)\le a^2\EE\norm{Z}^4\le a^2A_4\Nout^2,\qquad \Tr(\Pi V\Pi V)=\norm{V^{1/2}\Pi V^{1/2}}_\F^2\le\Nout a^2V_0^2 . \]
The linear term has \(\Var(2m_j^\T\Pi W)=4m_j^\T\Pi V\Pi m_j/B\le4a^2V_0\norm{m_j}^2/B\). With \(\Var(X_1+X_2)\le2\Var X_1+2\Var X_2\) and \(\Nout\le Kk\le KB\),
\[ \Var\big(h_j^\T\Pi h_j\big)\le a^2\Big[\frac{2A_4\Nout^2}{B^3}+\frac{4\Nout V_0^2}{B^2}+\frac{8V_0L^2C_{\rm col}}{kB}\Big]\le\frac{a^2V_*}k, \]
with \(V_*\defeq2A_4K^2+4KV_0^2+8V_0L^2C_{\rm col}\).
The bias. The mean is \(\Tr(\Pi V)/B+m_j^\T\Pi m_j\). By (SJ.32), its difference from \((2\cR/B)\Tr(\Pi\Lambda)\) is at most
\[ a\Big[\frac{2C_j\Tr\Lambda+2\Tr T_D+3\norm{m_j}^2}B+\norm{m_j}^2\Big]\le\frac ak\big(2L^2C_{\rm col}K+2H_0+4L^2C_{\rm col}\big)\defeq\frac{aD_*}k . \]
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the mean absolute fluctuation by the square root of the variance, and adding the bias gives (SJ.38).
□
The Haar sign matrix reduces the weighted volatility to the columns of \(H\). Define the covariance-weighted volatility of the actual update and the corresponding sum for the main text’s array:
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.39} \begin{aligned} \mathcal Q&\defeq\EE\norm S_\Sigma^2=\EE\Tr\big(\Sigmaout SS^\T\big),\\ \mathcal Q_{\rm print}&\defeq\Nin\Big[\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)\Tr\Lambda+\frac2\pi\,\Nout\,\frac{\Tr\Lambda^2}{\Tr\Lambda}\Big]=\sum_i\mu_i\,\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}, \end{aligned} \end{equation}
where \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}\defeq\Nin\big[1+\frac2\pi\big(\frac{\mu_i}{\bar\mu}-1\big)\big]\) is the main text’s SignSGD row volatility, written for general \(\Nout,\Nin\).
Lemma SJ.11 (Haar reduction)
In the Haar model, put \(R_j\defeq h_j^\T\Lambda h_j/\norm{h_j}^2\). Then \(c\le R_j\le L\) almost surely, every entry of \(G^\circ\) is nonzero almost surely, \(\norm S_\F^2=\Nout\Nin\), and

Proof
Proof.
Condition on \(H\), which is independent of \(U\) and has nonzero columns almost surely by Lemma SJ.10. The \(j\)th column of \(U^\T S\) is \(U^\T\sign(Uh_j)\), and Lemma SJ.7 applies to it. Taking the trace against \(\Lambda\), and the \((i,i)\) entry, gives
\begin{gather*} \EE_U\big[\sign(Uh_j)^\T U\Lambda U^\T\sign(Uh_j)\big]=\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)\Tr\Lambda+\frac2\pi\Nout R_j,\\ \EE_U\big[(e_i^\T U^\T\sign(Uh_j))^2\big]=1-\frac2\pi+\frac2\pi\Nout\frac{(h_j)_i^2}{\norm{h_j}^2}. \end{gather*}
Summing over \(j\) and averaging over \(H\) gives (SJ.40); no independence between columns is needed. A Haar rotation of a fixed nonzero vector has no zero coordinate almost surely, so every entry of \(UH\) is nonzero and \(\norm S_\F^2=\Nout\Nin\).

Theorem SJ.3 (Half-anisotropic weighted volatility)
In the Haar model of Definition SJ.3 there is an admissible constant \(C_{\rm vol}\) such that

Proof.

SJ.4.4 The corrected row volatility

The weighted total of the main text’s row array is correct to leading order, but the individual rows are not: the actual row volatility depends on the state. The proof controls the random column norms in (SJ.40) to fourth order.
Lemma SJ.12 (Fourth moments of sums)
Let \(Z\in\R^{\Nout}\) be centered with covariance \(V\) and satisfy (SJ.36) and (SJ.37), let \(Z_1,\dots,Z_B\) be independent copies, and put \(S_s\defeq\sum_{b\le s}Z_b\),
\[ \mathcal W\defeq\sum_{b\ne b'}\langle Z_b,Z_{b'}\rangle,\qquad \mathcal D_B\defeq\sum_{b=1}^B\big(\norm{Z_b}^2-\Tr V\big). \]
Then, for \(0\le s\le B\) and \(a\in\R^{\Nout}\),
\begin{gather*} \EE\norm{S_s}^4\le3A_4s^2\Nout^2,\qquad \EE(a^\T S_s)^4\le3A_4s^2\norm a^4,\\ \EE\mathcal W^4\le144A_4^2B^4\Nout^2,\qquad \EE\mathcal D_B^4\le(16A_8+3A_4^2)B^2\Nout^4 . \end{gather*}
Proof
Proof.
Sums. In the expansion of \(\norm{S_s}^4\), a term in which some sample index occurs once has mean zero. The remaining terms give
\[ \EE\norm{S_s}^4=s\,\EE\norm Z^4+s(s-1)\big[(\Tr V)^2+2\Tr V^2\big]. \]
With \(\Tr V\le\Nout\sqrt{A_4}\) and \(\Tr V^2\le\Nout A_4\), which follow from \(a^\T Va\le(\EE\abs{a^\T Z}^4)^{1/2}\), this is at most \(3A_4s^2\Nout^2\). The scalar case is \(s\,\EE(a^\T Z)^4+3s(s-1)(a^\T Va)^2\le3A_4s^2\norm a^4\).
The off-diagonal sum. A random partition splits \(\mathcal W\) into independent sums. Let \(\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_B\) be independent fair Bernoulli selectors, independent of the \(Z_b\). For fixed vectors,
\[ \mathcal W=4\,\EE_\epsilon\Big\langle\sum_b\epsilon_bZ_b,\ \sum_{b'}(1-\epsilon_{b'})Z_{b'}\Big\rangle , \]
because diagonal coefficients vanish and each ordered off-diagonal pair has coefficient \(\frac14\) in mean. Jensen’s inequality gives a factor \(4^4\). For a fixed partition into \(s\) and \(s'\) samples the two sums are independent; conditioning on the second and applying the two bounds above gives
\[ \EE\abs{\langle S_s,S'_{s'}\rangle}^4\le3A_4s^2\,\EE\norm{S'_{s'}}^4\le9A_4^2s^2s'^2\Nout^2 . \]
With \(ss'\le B^2/4\), the constant is \(4^4\cdot9/16=144\).
The diagonal sum. For \(Y\defeq\norm Z^2-\Tr V\), Jensen’s inequality gives \(\EE Y^4\le16\,\EE\norm Z^8\le16A_8\Nout^4\) and \(\EE Y^2\le\EE\norm Z^4\le A_4\Nout^2\), and for independent centered summands \(\EE\mathcal D_B^4=B\,\EE Y^4+3B(B-1)(\EE Y^2)^2\).

Lemma SJ.13 (Column norms)
In the Haar model put \(x_0\defeq2\cR\Tr\Lambda/B\). There are admissible constants \(C_{\rm good}\), \(C_{\rm bad}\) such that, for every column \(j\) and row \(i\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.42} \EE\Big|\frac{(h_j)_i^2}{\norm{h_j}^2}-\frac{(h_j)_i^2}{x_0}\Big|\le\frac{C_{\rm good}}{k^{3/2}}+\frac{C_{\rm bad}}{k^2}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The centered norm. Write \(h_j=m_j+S_B/B\) with \(S_B\) as in Lemma SJ.12 for \(Z=X-m_j\). Then exactly
\[ \norm{h_j}^2-\EE\norm{h_j}^2=\frac{2m_j^\T S_B}B+\frac{\mathcal D_B}{B^2}+\frac{\mathcal W}{B^2}. \]
The first term has fourth moment at most \(48A_4\norm{m_j}^4/B^2\le48A_4H_0^2/k^2\). Lemma SJ.12 and \(\Nout\le Kk\le KB\) bound the other two by \((16A_8+3A_4^2)K^4/k^2\) and \(144A_4^2K^2/k^2\). With \(\abs{x+y+z}^4\le27(x^4+y^4+z^4)\),
\[ \EE\big(\norm{h_j}^2-\EE\norm{h_j}^2\big)^4\le\frac{F_4}{k^2},\qquad F_4\defeq27\big[48A_4H_0^2+(16A_8+3A_4^2)K^4+144A_4^2K^2\big]. \]
The bias. Taking traces in (SJ.33),
\[ 0\le\EE\norm{h_j}^2-x_0=\frac{2C_j\Tr\Lambda+2\Tr T_D}B+\Big(1+\frac3B\Big)\norm{m_j}^2\le\frac{b_0}k, \]
with \(b_0\defeq2L^2KC_{\rm col}+2LP_++4L^2C_{\rm col}\). Hence, with \(Q_4\defeq8(F_4+b_0^4)\),
\[ \EE\big(\norm{h_j}^2-x_0\big)^4\le\frac{Q_4}{k^2},\qquad \EE\big(\norm{h_j}^2-x_0\big)^2\le\frac{\sqrt{Q_4}}k . \]
The same decomposition and Lemma SJ.12 give \(\EE(h_j)_i^4\le H_4/k^2\) with \(H_4\defeq8(L^4C_{\rm col}^2+3A_4)\). Also \(x_0\ge a_0\defeq P_-c/K\).
The ratio. Put \(\delta\defeq\norm{h_j}^2-x_0\). On \(\{\norm{h_j}^2\ge x_0/2\}\),
\[ (h_j)_i^2\,\Big|\frac1{\norm{h_j}^2}-\frac1{x_0}\Big|\le\frac{2}{a_0^2}\,(h_j)_i^2\abs\delta , \]
whose mean is at most \(2a_0^{-2}(H_4/k^2)^{1/2}(\sqrt{Q_4}/k)^{1/2}=C_{\rm good}k^{-3/2}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz. Markov’s inequality gives \(\PP(\norm{h_j}^2<x_0/2)\le16Q_4/(a_0^4k^2)\defeq b_1/k^2\). On that event \((h_j)_i^2/\norm{h_j}^2\le1\), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[ \frac1{x_0}\,\EE\big[(h_j)_i^2\,\mathbf 1_{\{\norm{h_j}^2<x_0/2\}}\big]\le\frac{\sqrt{H_4b_1}}{a_0k^2}. \]
Adding the three contributions gives (SJ.42) with \(C_{\rm bad}\defeq b_1+\sqrt{H_4b_1}/a_0\).

Theorem SJ.4 (Corrected row volatility)
In the Haar model of Definition SJ.3, define the raw annealed row volatility of the update and the corrected array
\begin{align} \volraw_i&\defeq\EE\norm{S^\T u_i}^2,\tag{SJ.43}\\ \mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}&\defeq\Nin\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)+\frac2\pi\,\Nout\Big[\frac{(\Nin+2)\,\mu_i}{\Tr\Lambda}+\frac{(B+2\Nin+3)\,(T_D)_{ii}}{2\cR\,\Tr\Lambda}\Big].\tag{SJ.44} \end{align}
There is an admissible constant \(C_{\rm row}\) such that, for every \(i\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.45} \abs{\volraw_i-\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}}\le C_{\rm row}\,\frac{\Nin}{\sqrt k},\qquad \Big|\frac{\volraw_i}{\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}}-1\Big|\le\frac{C_{\rm row}}{(1-2/\pi)\sqrt k}. \end{equation}
The actual total is exact, \(\sum_i\volraw_i=\Nout\Nin\), while
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.46} \sum_i\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}-\Nout\Nin=\frac2\pi\,\Nout\Big[2+\frac{(B+2\Nin+3)\Tr T_D}{2\cR\,\Tr\Lambda}\Big]\in\Big(0,\ \frac2\pi\Nout\big[2+(3K+3)L/c\big]\Big]. \end{equation}
The corrected array exceeds the main text’s array row by row:
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.47} \mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}-\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac2\pi\,\Nout\Big[\frac{2\mu_i}{\Tr\Sigmaout}+\frac{(B+2\Nin+3)\,(\Lambda DD^\T\Lambda)_{ii}}{2\cR\,\Tr\Sigmaout}\Big]\ge0 . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The row volatility. By Lemma SJ.11,
\[ \volraw_i=\Nin\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)+\frac2\pi\,\Nout\sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\EE\frac{(h_j)_i^2}{\norm{h_j}^2}. \]
Replacing each \(\norm{h_j}^2\) by \(x_0\) costs, by Lemma SJ.13 and \(\Nout\le Kk\), at most
\[ \frac2\pi\,\Nout\Nin\Big(\frac{C_{\rm good}}{k^{3/2}}+\frac{C_{\rm bad}}{k^2}\Big)\le C_{\rm row}\frac{\Nin}{\sqrt k},\qquad C_{\rm row}\defeq\frac2\pi K(C_{\rm good}+C_{\rm bad}). \]
The replaced sum is \(\sum_j\EE(h_j)_i^2/x_0=(\EE HH^\T)_{ii}/x_0\), and (SJ.34) with \(x_0=2\cR\Tr\Lambda/B\) identifies it:
\[ \frac{(\EE HH^\T)_{ii}}{x_0}=\frac{(\Nin+2)\mu_i}{\Tr\Lambda}+\frac{(B+2\Nin+3)(T_D)_{ii}}{2\cR\Tr\Lambda}. \]
This proves the first bound in (SJ.45). The relative bound follows from \(\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}\ge\Nin(1-2/\pi)\).
Totals. By Lemma SJ.11, \(\norm S_\F^2=\Nout\Nin\), and \(\sum_i\norm{S^\T u_i}^2=\norm S_\F^2\) by orthogonality. Summing (SJ.44) over \(i\) gives the identity in (SJ.46). Its bracket is positive, and \(\Tr T_D\le2\cR L\), \(\Tr\Lambda\ge c\Nout\) and \(B+2\Nin+3\le(3K+3)\Nout\) give the upper bound.
The difference. The main text’s array is \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=\Nin(1-\frac2\pi)+\frac2\pi\Nout\Nin\mu_i/\Tr\Lambda\). Subtracting it from (SJ.44) gives (SJ.47), with \(\Tr\Sigmaout=\Tr\Lambda\) and \(T_D=\Lambda DD^\T\Lambda\). Both terms are nonnegative.
□
For \(\Nout=\Nin=N\) the total excess is of order \(N\) against a total \(N^2\), and the weighted totals agree to leading order by Theorem SJ.3. For an individual row the state-dependent term can be of the same order as \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}\): for \(\Lambda=\Id\), \(B=N\) and \(D=e_1\tensor\mathbf 1/\sqrt N\), the corrected coefficient of the first row is \((1+\frac6\pi)N+\frac{10}\pi\), whereas \(\mathfrak v_1^{\text{s-SGD}}=N\).
Proposition SJ.8 (Half-anisotropic SignSGD recursion)
In the Haar model of Definition SJ.3 with \(k\ge k_*\), let \(\lr\ge0\) be deterministic and \(\Delta^+=\Delta-\lr S\). Then, with \(\mathfrak d_i\defeq\mu_i\mathcal N_B/\sqrt{\pi\bar\mu}\), there are real numbers \(E_1,\dots,E_{\Nout}\) such that
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.48} \EE\,\CMscr Q_i(\Delta^+)=\CMscr Q_i(\Delta)-\frac{2\lr\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt\cR}\,\CMscr Q_i(\Delta)+\lr^2\,\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}+E_i,\qquad \sum_i\abs{E_i}\le2\lr f_+\sqrt{C_*}+\lr^2C_{\rm row}\,\frac{\Nout\Nin}{\sqrt k}. \end{equation}
The risk satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.49} \EE\,\cR(\Delta^+)=\cR-\lr\sum_i\frac{\mu_i\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt\cR}\,\CMscr Q_i(\Delta)+\frac{\lr^2}2\sum_i\mu_i\,\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}+\mathcal E_\lr, \end{equation}
with \(\mathcal E_\lr\) bounded as in Corollary SJ.2.
Proof
Proof.
Given \(U\), the state is fixed and the whitened batch is independent of \(U\), so (SJ.3) applies given \(U\). Averaging over \(U\) gives
\[ \EE\,\CMscr Q_i(\Delta^+)=\CMscr Q_i(\Delta)-2\lr\,\EE\langle\Delta^\T u_i,S^\T u_i\rangle+\lr^2\volraw_i . \]
The identity \(L_B\mu_i=\mathfrak d_i/\sqrt\cR\) holds by the definition of \(L_B\). Define \(E_i\) as \(-2\lr\) times the error in (SJ.29) plus \(\lr^2(\volraw_i-\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr})\). Corollary SJ.1 and Theorem SJ.4 bound the sum of their absolute values. The risk identity is Corollary SJ.2, since \(L_B\norm{\Lambda D}_\F^2=\sum_iL_B\mu_i^2\norm{D_{i\cdot}}^2\) and \(\mathcal Q_{\rm print}=\sum_i\mu_i\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}\).
□
The proposition is the main text’s recursion (14) for SignSGD at one step, with three differences that are part of its statement: the drift uses \(\mathcal N_B\), which differs from \(\sqrt B\) by a factor in \([1-4/B,1]\); the per-row volatility is the state-dependent \(\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}\), and the main text’s array \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}\) is correct only in the covariance-weighted total (SJ.49); and the hypotheses of Definition SJ.3, among them a covariance bounded below by a fixed \(c>0\) and a state independent of the eigenbasis, which hold at one step and are not shown to persist.
