Lemma SF.6 (Participation count)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) and put
\[ \mathsf n_\bmom(q)\defeq\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)^2q\big)}{2\log(1/\bmom)},\qquad \mathsf c_\bmom\defeq\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{1-\bmom},\qquad \mathsf c_\bmom'\defeq1+\frac1{1-\bmom^2}, \]
\[ \mathsf e_\bmom^-\defeq\bmom^2\,\Phi'(1),\qquad \mathsf e_\bmom^+\defeq\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{1-\bmom}+\frac1{1-\bmom^2}. \]
  1. (i)
    If \((1-\bmom)^2q\ge1\), then \(\mathsf n_\bmom(q)-\mathsf c_\bmom<r_\bmom(q)\le\mathsf n_\bmom(q)+\mathsf c_\bmom'\). Moreover \(r_\bmom\big((1-\bmom)^{-2}\big)\le\mathsf c'_\bmom\).
  2. (ii)
    For every \(q>0\), \(0<q\,r_\bmom'(q)\le\mathsf e_\bmom^+\). If \((1-\bmom)^2q\ge1\), then \(q\,r_\bmom'(q)\ge\mathsf e_\bmom^-\).
Proof.
Put \(x_j\defeq\emaw j^2q=(1-\bmom)^2q\,\bmom^{2j}\). Then \(r_\bmom(q)=\sum_j\Phi(x_j)\) and \(q\,r_\bmom'(q)=\sum_jx_j\Phi'(x_j)\) by Lemma SF.4(i).
For part (i), let \((1-\bmom)^2q\ge1\) and \(n\defeq\lfloor\mathsf n_\bmom(q)\rfloor\). Since \((1-\bmom)^2q=\bmom^{-2\mathsf n_\bmom(q)}\), we have \(x_j=\bmom^{2(j-\mathsf n_\bmom(q))}\), so \(x_j\ge1\) exactly for \(j\le n\), and
\[ x_j^{-1/2}=\bmom^{\mathsf n_\bmom(q)-j}\le\bmom^{n-j}\qquad(j\le n). \]
The lower bound uses \(\Phi(x)\ge1-\sqrt{\pi/2}\,x^{-1/2}\) (Lemma SF.4(ii)) on the terms \(j\le n\):
\[ r_\bmom(q)\ge\sum_{j\le n}\Big(1-\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x_j^{-1/2}\Big)\ge n+1-\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\sum_{k\ge0}\bmom^k =n+1-\mathsf c_\bmom>\mathsf n_\bmom(q)-\mathsf c_\bmom . \]
The upper bound uses \(\Phi\le1\) on the terms \(j\le n\) and \(\Phi(x)\le x\) on the others. Since \(x_{n+1}<1\) and \(x_j=\bmom^{2(j-n-1)}x_{n+1}\),
\[ r_\bmom(q)\le n+1+\sum_{j>n}x_j<n+1+\frac1{1-\bmom^2}\le\mathsf n_\bmom(q)+\mathsf c_\bmom' . \]
At \(q=(1-\bmom)^{-2}\) one has \(\mathsf n_\bmom(q)=0\), which gives the second claim.
For part (ii), split the sum at \(x_j=1\) and use \(x\Phi'(x)\le\min(x,\sqrt{\pi/2}\,x^{-1/2})\). The numbers \(x_j^{-1/2}\) over the terms with \(x_j\ge1\), taken from the largest index down, are at most \(1,\bmom,\bmom^2,\dots\), and the numbers \(x_j\) over the terms with \(x_j<1\), taken from the smallest index up, are below \(1,\bmom^2,\bmom^4,\dots\). The two geometric sums give \(\mathsf e_\bmom^+\), and positivity is Lemma SF.4(i). If \(x_0\ge1\), then \(x_{n+1}\in[\bmom^2,1)\), and since \(\Phi'\) is decreasing,
\[ q\,r_\bmom'(q)\ge x_{n+1}\Phi'(x_{n+1})\ge\bmom^2\Phi'(1)=\mathsf e_\bmom^- . \]
□
