Theorem SI.5 (Extrema of the isotropic ratio)
The ratio \(\rho\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\), strictly decreasing on \((0,1]\) and strictly increasing on \([1,\infty)\), and
\[ \min_{\gamma>0}\rho(\gamma)=\rho(1)=\pi C(1)^2,\qquad \sup_{\gamma>0}\rho(\gamma)=\lim_{\gamma\downarrow0}\rho(\gamma)=\frac{32}{9\pi},\qquad \lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\rho(\gamma)=1 . \]
The minimum is attained only at \(\gamma=1\) and the supremum is not attained. For \(\gamma\ge1\), \(\rho(\gamma)=(1-\mathsf d/\gamma)^2\) with \(\mathsf d\defeq\mathcal D/\mathcal A=1-\sqrt\pi\,C(1)\in(0,1)\).
Proof.
For \(0<\gamma<1\), (SI.18) gives
\[ \rho(\gamma)=\frac2\pi\,\gamma^{-3}I(\gamma)^2,\qquad I'(\gamma)=\int_0^\gamma w(y)\,\dif y , \]
where the derivative has no boundary term because the integrand of \(I\) vanishes at \(y=\gamma\). The derivative of \(\gamma^{-3}I^2\) is \(\gamma^{-4}I\,(2\gamma I'-3I)\), and
\[ 2\gamma I'(\gamma)-3I(\gamma)=\int_0^\gamma(3y-\gamma)\,y^{-1/2}w_\circ(y)\,\dif y . \]
The same integral with \(w_\circ(y)\) replaced by the constant \(w_\circ(\gamma/3)\) vanishes, since \(\int_0^\gamma(3y-\gamma)y^{-1/2}\dif y=2\gamma^{3/2}-2\gamma^{3/2}=0\). Subtracting it gives
\[ 2\gamma I'(\gamma)-3I(\gamma)=\int_0^\gamma(3y-\gamma)\,y^{-1/2}\big(w_\circ(y)-w_\circ(\gamma/3)\big)\,\dif y<0 , \]
because \(w_\circ\) is strictly decreasing, so the integrand is negative except at \(y=\gamma/3\). Hence \(\rho\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,1)\).
For \(\gamma\ge1\), (SI.19) and \(\mathcal A^2=\pi/2\) give
\[ \rho(\gamma)=\frac2\pi\big(\mathcal A-\mathcal D/\gamma\big)^2=\Big(1-\frac{\mathsf d}\gamma\Big)^2 , \]
which increases strictly to \(1\). The formula (SI.18) is continuous in \(\gamma\), which gives continuity at \(1\), and \(\rho(1)=(1-\mathsf d)^2=\pi C(1)^2\) identifies \(\mathsf d\).
As \(\gamma\downarrow0\), the substitution \(y=\gamma y'\) gives
\[ \gamma^{-3/2}I(\gamma)=\int_0^1(1-y')\,y'^{-1/2}\,w_\circ(\gamma y')\,\dif y'\longrightarrow\int_0^1(1-y')\,y'^{-1/2}\,\dif y'=\frac43 , \]
since \(0<w_\circ\le1\) and \(w_\circ(\gamma y')\to1\) for each \(y'\). Hence \(\rho(\gamma)\to\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{16}9=\frac{32}{9\pi}\), and \(\frac{32}{9\pi}>1\) because \(\pi<\frac{32}9\). Strict monotonicity on each side of \(1\) gives the remaining claims.
□
