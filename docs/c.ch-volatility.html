Volatility

The volatility is the noise that the updates inject into each mode. It is computed in the frozen-state model of Section G.3, where the updates \({\mathsf S}_{t'}(\ridge)=\Qridge\ridge(M_{t'})\) are stationary and \({\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_{t'}(\ridge)\) is the update minus its mean. With momentum, consecutive buffers share most of their batches, so over the \(B\) steps of a unit of macroscopic time the quadratic terms accumulate as a long-run variance. For symmetric \(F,G\) the lag covariances and their integrated sum are
\[ \begin{gathered} C_\ell[F,G](\ridge,\ridge')=\frac1B\EE\Tr\big[F\,{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_t(\ridge)\,G\,{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_{t+\ell}(\ridge')^\T\big],\\ \Gamma[F,G](\ridge,\ridge')=C_0[F,G](\ridge,\ridge')+\sum_{\ell\ge1}\big(C_\ell[F,G](\ridge,\ridge')+C_\ell[F,G](\ridge',\ridge)\big), \end{gathered} \]
and \(C_\ell=C_\ell[\Sigmaout,\Sigmain]\), \(\Gamma=\Gamma[\Sigmaout,\Sigmain]\). Their deterministic equivalents \(\overline C_\ell\), \(\overline\Gamma\) are given by the lag kernels \(\mathfrak k_\ell\) of Section G.3. With \(P_\mu\), \(P'_\lambda\) the spectral projections of \(\Sigmaout,\Sigmain\) and \(r_\mu,t_\lambda\) their ranks, the forcing measure and the mode and row coefficients are
\[ \begin{gathered} \zeta_\ridge=\sum_{\mu,\lambda}\overline\Gamma[P_\mu,P'_\lambda](\ridge,\ridge)\,\delta_{(\mu,\lambda)},\qquad \mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\frac{B\,\zeta_\ridge(\{(\mu_i,\lambda_j)\})}{r_{\mu_i}t_{\lambda_j}},\\ \mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=B\,\overline\Gamma[u_i\tensor u_i,\Id](\ridge,\ridge)\quad\text{when }\Sigmain=\Id, \end{gathered} \]
and \(\mathfrak v_{ij}\) is the volatility kernel of (6). The two results of this chapter read \(\mathfrak v_{ij}\) in the two cases the main text uses: the half-anisotropic rows, and no momentum.

Proposition SD.4 (Recovery of the rows)
If \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\), then \(\zeta_\ridge=B^{-1}\sum_i\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\,\delta_{(\mu_i,1)}\), and \(\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)/\Nin\), so that \(\sum_j\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) for every \(i\).
The proposition is the relation \(\mathfrak v_i=\sum_j\mathfrak v_{ij}\) that the main text uses in the row recursion (14) of the half-anisotropic setting \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\). There the volatility of row \(i\) is the long-run variance of the whole row \({\mathsf S}^\T u_i\) of the update, and its deterministic counterpart \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) is defined directly from the row test. The proposition says that this row coefficient is the sum of the mode coefficients of the row, and that all \(\Nin\) modes of a row carry the same share \(\mathfrak v_i/\Nin\). It is an exact identity between deterministic quantities at every finite dimension; no limit is taken. With the closed form of Section G.3 it gives the explicit row coefficient \(\mathfrak v_i=(\Nin/B)\big(\mathfrak c_0(\mu_i,1;\ridge)+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathfrak c_\ell(\mu_i,1;\ridge)\big)\) ((SD.91)), whose same-step term is \(\volraw_i(\ridge)\) (Corollary SD.4), the deterministic equivalent of the expected row energy of the update (Theorem SD.1).
Proof sketch.
The deterministic covariances are linear in each of the two tests, so the mass of the forcing measure at a point is the sum of the row coefficients of the rows sitting there. The deterministic lag covariance \(\overline C_\ell[F,G]\) is a limit of expected traces \(B^{-1}\EE\Tr[F{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_tG\,{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c\,\T}_{t+\ell}]\), and in the pairing expansion of Section G.3 each test enters every tree through exactly one factor. Hence \(\overline C_\ell\) and its lag sum \(\overline\Gamma\) are linear in \(F\) and in \(G\). When \(\Sigmain=\Id\) the input side has the single eigenvalue \(1\), whose spectral projection is \(\Id\), with multiplicity \(\Nin\). The atom of \(\zeta_\ridge\) at \((\mu,1)\) is therefore, splitting the projection \(P_\mu\) onto the eigenvalue \(\mu\) of \(\Sigmaout\) into its rows,
\[ \zeta_\ridge\big(\{(\mu,1)\}\big)=\overline\Gamma[P_\mu,\Id](\ridge,\ridge)=\sum_{i:\,\mu_i=\mu}\overline\Gamma[u_i\tensor u_i,\Id](\ridge,\ridge)=\frac1B\sum_{i:\,\mu_i=\mu}\mathfrak v_i(\ridge), \]
where the last step is the definition of \(\mathfrak v_i\). This is the first claim, \(\zeta_\ridge=B^{-1}\sum_i\mathfrak v_i\,\delta_{(\mu_i,1)}\).
The row coefficient depends on the row only through its eigenvalue, which turns the atom into equal mode coefficients. With the test \(u_i\tensor u_i\), the row index enters every tree value only through one normalized trace \(B^{-1}\mu_i^p\) with \(p\ge1\), so \(\mathfrak v_i\) is a function of \(\mu_i\) alone (Theorem SD.7(iv)). The atom at \((\mu,1)\) is thus \(r_\mu\mathfrak v_i/B\) for any row \(i\) with \(\mu_i=\mu\), where \(r_\mu\) is the multiplicity of \(\mu\). The mode coefficient divides the atom equally among the \(r_\mu\Nin\) modes \((i,j)\) that sit at \((\mu,1)\) (Proposition SD.3), so
\[ \mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\frac{B}{r_\mu\Nin}\cdot\frac{r_\mu\,\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)}B=\frac{\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)}{\Nin},\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\mathfrak v_i(\ridge). \]
Full proof
Here \(r_\bmom(q)=q\Fw(q)\), and \(b_\ell,e_\ell\), \(\mathfrak k_\ell=\mathfrak k_\ell[\Sigmaout,\Sigmain]\) and the mode integrals \(\mathfrak c_\ell(\mu,\lambda;\ridge)\) are the closed-form objects of Section G.3; \(\overline{\mathfrak C}_\ell(\ridge,\ridge)\) is the measure with atoms \(\overline C_\ell[P_\mu,P'_\lambda](\ridge,\ridge)\). The same-step row and column energies are
\[ \volraw_i(\ridge)=\frac{\tilde s_1(-\ridge)\,\mu_i}{\ridge+\tilde s_1(-\ridge)\,\mu_i},\qquad \widetilde{\mathfrak v}^{(0)}_j(\ridge)=\frac{s_1(-\ridge)\,\lambda_j}{\ridge+s_1(-\ridge)\,\lambda_j}, \]
and \(\overline Z_{\mathfrak k}(\ridge)\) is \(\frac4{\pi^2}\) times the double integral of the lag-zero kernel \(\mathfrak k_0(\ridge+x^2,\ridge+y^2)\) over \(x,y>0\) ((SD.87), where the kernel is written in the variables of one resolvent pair and identified with \(\mathfrak k_0\) by Corollary SD.5(i)).
Remark SD.3 (No momentum)
At \(\bmom=0\) the only weight is \(\emaw0=1\), the buffer is the current gradient, \(F_0=L_0=\Jfun\), \(r_0'(q)=\EE\,g^2(1+qg^2)^{-2}\) and \(F_0'(q)=-\EE\,g^4(1+qg^2)^{-2}\), and the branch is the no-momentum system of Remark SB.1. In the closed form, \(b_\ell\) carries the factor \(\bmom^\ell\), so \(b_\ell=0\) and \(\mathfrak k_\ell=0\) for every \(\ell\ge1\); the same-step scalars are the divided differences \(b_0=\big(r_0(q)-r_0(q')\big)/(q-q')\) and \(e_0=-\big(F_0(q)-F_0(q')\big)/(q-q')\). All lag covariances and their deterministic equivalents vanish for \(\ell\ge1\) (Theorem SD.2(ii), Theorem SD.4(ii)), so \(\Gamma=C_0\), \(\overline\Gamma=\overline C_0\), the forcing measure is \(\zeta_\ridge=\overline{\mathfrak C}_0(\ridge,\ridge)\), and
\[ \mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\frac1B\,\mathfrak c_0(\mu_i,\lambda_j;\ridge),\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\volraw_i(\ridge),\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{\Nout}\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\widetilde{\mathfrak v}^{(0)}_j(\ridge), \]
by Corollary SD.3 and Corollary SD.4, for every pair of covariances. For \(\Sigmain=\Id\) this is \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=\volraw_i(\ridge)\) of Theorem SD.7(iv), with the spectral form of Remark SD.1. The covariance-weighted same-step total is \(\overline C_0(\ridge,\ridge)=\overline Z_{\mathfrak k}(\ridge)\) of Corollary SD.5, with the kernel (SD.86) formed from \(F_0'\) and \(r_0'\).
Without momentum the buffer is the current gradient, so updates at different steps are built from disjoint batches and are independent; every lag covariance vanishes and the volatility is the same-step variance of the update. In the closed form of Section G.3 this is the factor \(\bmom^\ell\) carried by \(b_\ell\), and the same-step scalars become divided differences of the no-momentum functions \(r_0\) and \(F_0\). Summed over a row, the same-step term is \(\volraw_i(\ridge)\) (Corollary SD.4), the deterministic equivalent of the expected row energy \(\EE\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u_i}^2\): the exact identity \({\mathsf S}{\mathsf S}^\T=\Id-\ridge(\Mt\Mt^\T+\ridge\Id)^{-1}\) reduces it to the deterministic equivalent of the Gram resolvent (Theorem SD.1). This is the coefficient behind the main text’s statement that without momentum, at \(B\ge N\), the update is orthogonal and \(\mathfrak v_i=1\). The remark itself is at a positive ridge. As \(\ridge\downarrow0\) the integrand of the row energy tends to the indicator of \((0,\infty)\), whose integral against the spectral measure \(\rho_i\) of \(u_i\) for \(\Mt\Mt^\T\) is \(\rho_i((0,\infty))=1-\norm{P_0u_i}^2\), with \(P_0\) the projection on the left null space of \(\Mt\) (Remark SD.1); this is \(1\) when the gradient has full row rank. The appendix makes no statement at \(\ridge=0\).
