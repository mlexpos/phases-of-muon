Lemma SI.9 (Square supersolution)
Let \(\epsilon>0\), \(T\ge4\) and \(H>0\). Let \(g\ge0\) be continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with \(\int_0^\infty g\le1\), let \(f\ge0\) be continuous with \(f(0)>T\epsilon\), and let \(Y>0\) be continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with \(Y=f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt Y\). Let \(h\) be continuous on \([0,H]\) with \(h\ge\sqrt\epsilon\), \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\) and \(f\le h^2\). Assume that on \([0,H]\) either
\[ \text{(a)}\ \ g*h\le2h,\qquad\text{or}\qquad\text{(b)}\ \ g*h\le\sqrt\epsilon . \]
  1. (i)
    On \([0,H]\), \(f\le Y\le(h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) and \(Y-f\le2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon\); under (b), moreover \(Y-f\le2\epsilon\).
  2. (ii)
    The first hit \(\hat u\defeq\inf\{u\ge0:\ Y(u)\le T\epsilon\}\) satisfies \(0<\hat u\le H\) and \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\), and
    \[ 0\le\mathcal E\defeq\int_0^{\hat u}\big(\sqrt Y-\sqrt f\big)\,\dif u\le\int_0^H\sqrt{2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon}\,\dif u ; \]
    under (b), moreover \(\mathcal E\le\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,H\).
  3. (iii)
    Let \(C>0\), \(D\ge1\), \(0<r_0<2\) and \(0<\epsilon<C^2\), and take \(h(u)=C(1+u/D)^{-r_0}\) and \(H=D[(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}-1]\). Then \(h\ge\sqrt\epsilon\) on \([0,H]\), \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\), \(1+H\le D(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}\), and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.51} \int_0^H\sqrt{2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon}\,\dif u\le D\,C^{1/r_0}\Big[\frac{\sqrt2}{1-r_0/2}+1\Big]\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)} . \end{equation}
Proof.
Put \(U\defeq(h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\), so that \(U\ge\epsilon\). Since \(\int g\le1\), \(g*(h+\sqrt\epsilon)\le g*h+\sqrt\epsilon\). Under (a),
\[ f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt U\le h^2+\sqrt\epsilon(2h+\sqrt\epsilon)=U . \]
Under (b), using \(h\ge\sqrt\epsilon\),
\[ f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt U\le h^2+2\epsilon\le h^2+2\sqrt\epsilon h+\epsilon=U . \]
Lemma SI.3, with kernel \(\sqrt\epsilon g\), \(G=f\) and \(m=\epsilon\), gives \(Y\le U\) on \([0,H]\). The equation gives \(Y\ge f\) and
\[ Y-f=\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt Y\le\sqrt\epsilon\,g*(h+\sqrt\epsilon), \]
which is at most \(2\sqrt\epsilon h+\epsilon\) under (a) and at most \(2\epsilon\le2\sqrt\epsilon h+\epsilon\) under (b). This proves (i).
At \(H\) the bound gives \(Y(H)\le(2\sqrt\epsilon)^2=4\epsilon\le T\epsilon\), while \(Y(0)=f(0)>T\epsilon\). The set \(\{u\le H:\ Y(u)\le T\epsilon\}\) is closed, nonempty and does not contain \(0\); its least element \(\hat u\) is the first hit, and continuity gives \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\). No monotonicity of \(Y\) is used. The inequality \(\sqrt x-\sqrt y\le\sqrt{x-y}\) for \(x\ge y\ge0\) and (i) give the bounds on \(\mathcal E\). This proves (ii).
For (iii), \(h\) is decreasing with \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\), and \(1+H/D=(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}\) gives the bound on \(1+H\) because \(D\ge1\). The inequality \(\sqrt{x+y}\le\sqrt x+\sqrt y\) gives
\[ \int_0^H\sqrt{2\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon}\,\dif u\le\sqrt{2C}\,\epsilon^{1/4}D\,I_{r_0/2}(H/D)+\sqrt\epsilon\,H . \]
Since \(r_0<2\), \(I_{r_0/2}(x)\le(1+x)^{1-r_0/2}/(1-r_0/2)\). Inserting \(1+H/D=(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_0}\),
\[ \sqrt{2C}\,\epsilon^{1/4}D\,I_{r_0/2}(H/D)\le\frac{\sqrt2\,D\,C^{1/r_0}}{1-r_0/2}\,\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)},\qquad \sqrt\epsilon\,H\le D\,C^{1/r_0}\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)} , \]
which proves (SI.51).
□
