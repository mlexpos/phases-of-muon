Lemma SF.13 (Participation slope)
For every \(y>0\),
\[ \mathsf e_\bmom(y)\le\frac14+\frac1{2\log(1/\bmom)}\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom . \]
Proof.
The slope is a lattice sum of the logistic density \(\mathsf l(v)\defeq e^v/(1+e^v)^2\), which is unimodal, with maximum \(\frac14\) and integral \(1\) over \(\R\). Put \(x_j\defeq\emaw j^2y\). By Lemma SF.4(i) and the identity \(t/(1+t)^2=\mathsf l(\log t)\) for \(t>0\),
\[ \mathsf e_\bmom(y)=\sum_{j\ge0}x_j\Phi'(x_j)=\sum_{j\ge0}\EE\frac{x_jg^2}{(1+x_jg^2)^2} =\EE\sum_{j\ge0}\mathsf l\Big(\log\big((1-\bmom)^2yg^2\big)-2j\log(1/\bmom)\Big). \]
For fixed \(g\ne0\) the arguments of \(\mathsf l\) lie on a lattice of spacing \(2\log(1/\bmom)\).
A lattice sum of a unimodal function is at most its maximum plus its integral divided by the spacing. Let \(F\ge0\) be unimodal with mode \(v_*\), and let the points \(p\) form a lattice of spacing \(h\). Each point \(p<v_*\) other than the largest such point satisfies
\[ F(p)\le\frac1h\int_p^{p+h}F , \]
because \(F\) is nondecreasing on \([p,p+h]\). Each point \(p>v_*\) other than the smallest such point satisfies \(F(p)\le h^{-1}\int_{p-h}^pF\) for the same reason. These cells are disjoint. The two remaining points \(p_-\le v_*\le p_+=p_-+h\) satisfy \(F\ge\min(F(p_-),F(p_+))\) on \([p_-,p_+]\), so that
\[ F(p_-)+F(p_+)\le\max F+\frac1h\int_{p_-}^{p_+}F . \]
Summing gives \(\sum_pF(p)\le\max F+h^{-1}\int_\R F\). With \(F=\mathsf l\) and \(h=2\log(1/\bmom)\) the inner sum is at most \(\frac14+1/(2\log(1/\bmom))\) for every \(g\ne0\), and taking the expectation proves the lemma.
□
