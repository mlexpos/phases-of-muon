E3: Volatility and the lag kernel

SN.9 Volatility and the lag kernel

The volatility is computed in the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3: the error is held at a state whose normalized error \(\Ahat\) has vanishing operator norm, and the momentum buffer \(M_{t'}=\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw jG_{t'+1-j}\) is a stationary average of independent batches at that state. The updates \({\mathsf S}_{t'}=\Qridge\ridge(M_{t'})\) are then stationary, and \({\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_{t'}\) is the update minus its mean. For matrices \(F,G\) diagonal in the eigenbases, \(C_\ell[F,G]=B^{-1}\EE\Tr[F{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_tG\,{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c\,\T}_{t+\ell}]\) is the covariance of two updates \(\ell\) steps apart; with \(F=u_i\tensor u_i\) and \(G=v_j\tensor v_j\) it is that of the entries of mode \((i,j)\). The engine produces the forcing measure \(\zeta_\ridge\). Its atom at a point \((\mu,\lambda)\) is the deterministic long-run covariance of the update tested on the eigenspaces of \(\mu\) and \(\lambda\), and each mode \((i,j)\) contributes the mass \(\mathfrak v_{ij}/B\) at \((\mu_i,\lambda_j)\). The engine shows that the covariance measure of \(T\) consecutive updates, divided by \(BT\), is within an explicit error of its long-run value and within \(o(1)\) of \(\zeta_\ridge\), and that \(\mathfrak v_{ij}\) is a series of nonnegative double integrals of a closed-form kernel.
Proof sketch.
Over a block of \(T\) steps the updates accumulate their lag covariances, which decay geometrically in the lag. Expanding the square of \(S_T=\sum_{t'\le T}{\mathsf S}^{\mathrm c}_{t'}\) over pairs of times, stationarity makes all pairs at lag \(\ell\) equal. This gives an exact identity, and the geometric decay bounds its distance to the long-run variance \(\Gamma[F,G]=C_0[F,G]+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}C_\ell[F,G]\) (Theorem SD.2(i),(iii)):
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac1{BT}\,\EE\Tr\big[F\,S_T\,G\,S_T^\T\big]&=C_0[F,G]+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}\Big(1-\frac\ell T\Big)C_\ell[F,G]\\ &=\Gamma[F,G]+O\Big(\frac{\bmom^T}{1-\bmom}+\frac{\bmom}{T(1-\bmom)^2}\Big). \end{aligned} \]
The decay comes from the recursion \(M_{t+\ell}=\bmom^\ell\Mt+\sum_{r<\ell}\emaw rG_{t+\ell+1-r}\). Replacing \(\Mt\) in it by a copy built from independent batches gives a buffer with the law of \(M_{t+\ell}\), independent of \(\Mt\), at distance \(\bmom^\ell\) times the difference of two independent buffers. The regularized map is \(\ridge^{-1/2}\)-Lipschitz in Frobenius norm, so \(\abs{C_\ell}\le\mathsf K_\star\bmom^\ell\). The error term of the display holds also in total variation for the covariance measure of \(T\) consecutive updates (Theorem SD.8(iv)), the form the dynamics use.
At each fixed lag, the covariance is unchanged in the limit when every residual \(d=z^\T\Ahat w\), which depends on its own sample, is replaced by an independent Gaussian weight. Call a filter an odd function applied to the singular values of a buffer: \(\Qridge\ridge\) is the filter \(x/\sqrt{x^2+\ridge}\), and it is an average of the resolvent filters \(x/(x^2+s)\), which give \((KK^\T+s\Id)^{-1}K\) (Lemma SA.3). Adding one sample is a rank-one change of each buffer. By the Woodbury formula, with a \(2\times2\) matrix to invert, the change of a tested product of two resolvents is a function of \(d\) and of quadratic forms that concentrate at normalized traces, so it depends on the sample only through the law of \(d\), which is asymptotically Gaussian because \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\). Summed over the samples, one at a time, the errors vanish (Lemma SD.6).
In the resulting model, which has no state, the lag covariances have deterministic limits in which only tree-shaped Gaussian pairings survive. For polynomial filters the tested product of the two buffers is a polynomial in Gaussian variables, and its expectation is a sum over pairings, each gluing the cyclic product into a connected graph. Pairings whose graph is a tree contribute order one and the others \(O(1/B)\) (Lemma SD.8). The tree values are products of normalized traces \(B^{-1}\sum_i\mu_i^p\), \(B^{-1}\sum_j\lambda_j^p\) and of moments of the weights, hence deterministic, and polynomial approximation extends the limit to all odd bounded continuous filters (Lemma SD.10). The resolvent filters give the lag kernel \(\mathfrak k_\ell[F,G](s,u)\), and the filter of \(\Qridge\ridge\) gives the deterministic covariance \(\overline C_\ell[F,G](\ridge,\ridge)\), the double integral of \(\frac4{\pi^2}\mathfrak k_\ell[F,G](\ridge+x^2,\ridge+y^2)\) over \(x,y>0\) (Theorem SD.3 and Theorem SD.6). With the geometric decay, \(\Gamma\) is within \(o(1)\) of \(\overline\Gamma=\overline C_0+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_\ell\), without a rate (Theorem SD.4(iv)).
The trees can be summed, and the lag kernel is an explicit expression in the solution of the fixed-point system of Figure 2 at two spectral parameters and in two scalar functions of the momentum. In every tree, the subtrees hanging off the path from the vertex carrying the test \(F\) to the vertex carrying the test \(G\) involve one buffer only, and they sum to its one-time deterministic equivalent (Section C.1): the branch \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)\) at \(-s\) for the earlier buffer and, primed, at \(-u\) for the later one (Lemma SD.18). Only batches on the path are shared; a batch of age \(j\) enters the two buffers with weights \(\emaw j\) and \(\bmom^\ell\emaw j\). The sum over paths is a geometric series of \(2\times2\) matrices, and, first for large \(s,u\) and then by analyticity for all \(s,u>0\) (Theorem SD.5),
\[ \mathfrak k_\ell[F,G](s,u)=\frac{su\,b_\ell\,\Tr\big(F\Sigmaout\Rbar(-s)\Rbar(-u)\big)\Tr\big(G\Sigmain\Rtbar(-s)\Rtbar(-u)\big)}{B^2\,\Delta_\ell(s,u)},\quad b_\ell=\sum_{j\ge0}\EE\frac{\bmom^\ell\emaw j^2g^2}{(1+q\emaw j^2g^2)(1+\bmom^{2\ell}q'\emaw j^2g^2)}, \]
where \(\Rbar(-s)=(s\Id+\tilde s_1\Sigmaout)^{-1}\), \(\Rtbar(-s)=(s\Id+s_1\Sigmain)^{-1}\), and \(\Delta_\ell>0\) is the determinant of the \(2\times2\) series, built from the branch, the traces of the same form with \(F=\Sigmaout\) and \(G=\Sigmain\), \(b_\ell\) and a companion scalar \(e_\ell\). The lag enters only through \(b_\ell\) and \(e_\ell\), and \(b_\ell\) carries the factor \(\bmom^\ell\). With spectral projections as tests the kernel factors into multiplicities and a nonnegative mode kernel, \(\mathfrak k_\ell[P_\mu,P'_\lambda](s,u)=r_\mu t_\lambda B^{-2}\,\mathfrak h_\ell(\mu,\lambda;s,u)\) for the spectral projections \(P_\mu,P'_\lambda\) of \(\Sigmaout,\Sigmain\) with ranks \(r_\mu,t_\lambda\), and the atom of \(\zeta_\ridge\) at \((\mu_i,\lambda_j)\) splits into equal mode coefficients (Corollary SD.3, Proposition SD.3)
\[ \mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\frac1B\Big(\mathfrak c_0(\mu_i,\lambda_j;\ridge)+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathfrak c_\ell(\mu_i,\lambda_j;\ridge)\Big), \]
where \(\mathfrak c_\ell(\mu,\lambda;\ridge)\ge0\) is the double integral of \(\frac4{\pi^2}\mathfrak h_\ell(\mu,\lambda;\ridge+x^2,\ridge+y^2)\) over \(x,y>0\).
