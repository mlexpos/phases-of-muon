Lemma SF.4 (Scalar facts)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\).
  1. (i)
    For \(q\ge0\),
    \[ r_\bmom(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\Phi(\emaw j^2q),\qquad r_\bmom'(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\emaw j^2\,\Phi'(\emaw j^2q)>0 . \]
    For \(\bmom>0\), \(r_\bmom\) is an increasing bijection of \([0,\infty)\) onto itself; at \(\bmom=0\), \(r_0=\Phi\) is an increasing bijection of \([0,\infty)\) onto \([0,1)\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(x>0\), \(\Phi(x)\le\min(1,x)\), the derivative \(\Phi'\) is decreasing, and
    \[ 1-\Phi(x)\le\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x^{-1/2},\qquad x\,\Phi'(x)\le\min\Big(x,\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x^{-1/2}\Big). \]
  3. (iii)
    For \(x>0\), \(\Jfun(x)\le\min(1,1/x)\) and \(-3\le\Jfun'(x)\le0\).
  4. (iv)
    For \(q,z\ge0\),
    \[ \Lambda_\bmom(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\Lambda_0(\emaw j^2q),\qquad \Lambda_0(z)\le2\sqrt z, \]
    for \(z>0\),
    \[ 0\le\sqrt{2\pi}-\Lambda_0(z)\le2z^{-1/2}, \]
    and \(\Lambda_0(z)\uparrow\sqrt{2\pi}\) as \(z\to\infty\).
  5. (v)
    \(\Fw\le\sw2\) and \(\Lw\le1\), and \(q\mapsto q\Lw(q)\) is strictly increasing.
Proof.
Part (i) is termwise. Each term of \(r_\bmom(q)=\sum_j\emaw j^2q\,\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\) is \(\Phi(\emaw j^2q)\) by (SF.4). The derivative and its positivity are Lemma SB.1(iii), with \(\EE g^2(1+vg^2)^{-2}=\Phi'(v)\) from Lemma SF.1(ii). By the same lemma, \(r_\bmom\) increases to the number of positive weights, which is infinite for \(\bmom>0\) and equal to \(1\) at \(\bmom=0\).
Part (ii) uses the Gaussian density. The bound \(\Phi\le1\) is Lemma SF.1(ii), and \(\Phi(x)=x\Jfun(x)\le x\) because \(\Jfun\le1\). The derivative \(\Phi'(x)=\EE g^2(1+xg^2)^{-2}\) is decreasing in \(x\). Since the standard Gaussian density is at most \((2\pi)^{-1/2}\),
\[ 1-\Phi(x)=\EE\frac1{1+xg^2}\le\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_\R\frac{\dif y}{1+xy^2}=\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x^{-1/2}. \]
Finally \(x\Phi'(x)=\EE[xg^2(1+xg^2)^{-2}]\) is at most \(\EE[xg^2]=x\), and at most \(\EE[(1+xg^2)^{-1}]=1-\Phi(x)\) because \(xg^2/(1+xg^2)\le1\).
Part (iii) reads off the integrand: \(g^2/(1+xg^2)\) is at most \(g^2\) and at most \(1/x\), and \(\Jfun'(x)=-\EE g^4(1+xg^2)^{-2}\in[-\EE g^4,0]=[-3,0]\).
Part (iv) is a substitution. In the \(j\)-th term of \(\Lw\) the substitution \(y=\emaw j^2x\) gives
\[ \int_0^qx^{-1/2}\emaw j\Jfun(\emaw j^2x)\,\dif x=\int_0^{\emaw j^2q}y^{-1/2}\Jfun(y)\,\dif y=\Lambda_0(\emaw j^2q). \]
The bound \(\Lambda_0(z)\le\int_0^zy^{-1/2}\dif y=2\sqrt z\) uses \(\Jfun\le1\). The full integral \(\int_0^\infty y^{-1/2}\Jfun(y)\,\dif y=\sqrt{2\pi}\) is computed in the proof of Lemma SG.2, and by (iii)
\[ \sqrt{2\pi}-\Lambda_0(z)=\int_z^\infty y^{-1/2}\Jfun(y)\,\dif y\le\int_z^\infty y^{-3/2}\,\dif y=2z^{-1/2}. \]
Part (v) uses \(\Jfun\le1\) again: \(\Fw\le\sum_j\emaw j^2=\sw2\) and \(\Lw\le\sum_j\emaw j=1\). Finally \(q\Lw(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\Phi(\emaw j^2q)/\emaw j\), a sum of strictly increasing functions.
□
