Proposition SG.8 (No-momentum endpoints)
Let \(\bmom=0\), let \(\alpha>0\) be fixed, and let \(N,B\to\infty\) with \(\gamma=N/B\to\infty\), \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\), \(\Nout=\Nin=N\) and \(\mu_i=i^{-\alpha}\). For \(\gamma>1\) let \(\resscale\) and \(i_\#=\resscale^{1/\alpha}\) be as in Proposition SG.6. At each such dimension the limits \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak d_i(\ridge)\) and \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) exist, and:
  1. (i)
    for every \(i\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)=\frac{\resscale\mu_i}{1+\resscale\mu_i}=\frac1{1+(i/i_\#)^\alpha},\qquad \sum_{i=1}^N\mathfrak v_i(0)=B ; \]
  2. (ii)
    for every row sequence \(1\le i=i_N\le N\),
    \[ \frac{i}{i_\#}\to0\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}{2\gamma}},\qquad \frac{i}{i_\#}\to\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathfrak d_i(0)\sim A_\alpha\,\mu_i\sqrt{\frac{\resscale}{2\gamma}} . \]
This proposition gives the no-momentum row of Table 1 and the statement of the main text that unresolved modes at \(\bmom=0\) have the drift of SGD. The volatility is exact at every dimension: row \(i\) carries the share \(\resscale\mu_i/(1+\resscale\mu_i)\) of the \(B\) directions that one gradient spans. The drift has two endpoint laws on either side of the threshold \(i_\#\) of Proposition SG.6. For \(i\ll i_\#\), \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(2\gamma)}\) is the square-root law with \(B_{\rm eff}=B\). For \(i\gg i_\#\), \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim A_\alpha\mu_i\sqrt{\resscale/(2\gamma)}\) is linear in \(\mu_i\), with the constant \(A_\alpha=c_0\) for \(\alpha\le1\) and the integral (SG.39) for \(\alpha>1\).
Proof sketch.
The participation coordinate of Section C.5 applies at \(\bmom=0\), with the participation confined to \((0,1)\). The computation of the branch uses only its equations, so with \(m(s)\in(0,1)\) defined by \(s_N(m(s))=s\) and \(\Lw/\Fw=1\),
\[ \volraw_i(\ridge)=f_i\big(m(\ridge)\big),\qquad \mathfrak d_i(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\infty f_i\big(m(\ridge+u^2)\big)\,\dif u . \]
Now \(r_0(q)=q\Jfun(q)\) increases only to one, so \(Q_m\to\infty\) as \(m\uparrow1\) while \(\Lambda_N(m)\) stays bounded, and \(s_N(m)\to0\); hence \(m(s)\to1\) and \(\Lambda_N(m(s))\uparrow\Lambda_N(1)=\resscale\) as \(s\downarrow0\). Without momentum successive buffers are independent, so all lag coefficients vanish (Theorem SD.7(iv)) and \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=\volraw_i(\ridge)\to\resscale\mu_i/(1+\resscale\mu_i)\), whose sum over \(i\) is \(Bm_N(\resscale)=B\); this is (i). The drift integrand is nonincreasing in \(u\) with total variation at most one, and an integration by parts gives \(\abs{\mathfrak d_i(\ridge)-\mathfrak d_i(0)}\le\sqrt2\sqrt\ridge/(\pi\gamma)\).
On resolved rows the drift is the head Lorentzian. If \(i/i_\#\to0\) then \(\resscale\mu_i\to\infty\), and for a fixed small \(\delta\) the range \(m\le\delta\) covers all \(u\ge\sqrt{s_N(\delta)}=o(\sqrt{\gamma\mu_i})\). There \(\tilde s_1=\gamma(1+O(\delta)+o(1))\) and
\[ \int_{\sqrt{s_N(\delta)}}^\infty\frac{\tilde s_1\mu_i}{u^2+\tilde s_1\mu_i}\,\dif u=\frac\pi2\sqrt{\gamma\mu_i}\,\big(1+O(\delta)+o(1)\big), \]
which after the factor \(\sqrt2/(\pi\gamma)\) and \(\delta\downarrow0\) gives \(\sqrt{\mu_i/(2\gamma)}\).
On unresolved rows the share is linear in \(\mu_i\), and the drift is a fixed integral over the participation. If \(i/i_\#\to\infty\) then \(\resscale\mu_i\to0\), and since \(\Lambda_N\le\resscale\) one has \(f_i=\Lambda_N\mu_i(1+O(\resscale\mu_i))\) uniformly. Changing variables to \(m\) gives
\[ \frac{\mathfrak d_i(0)}{\mu_i\sqrt{\resscale/(2\gamma)}}=\big(1+o(1)\big)\frac1\pi\int_0^1\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_N(m)}{\resscale}}\sqrt{1-\frac m\gamma}\sqrt{\frac m{Q_m}}\,\vartheta_N(m)\,\dif m . \]
For fixed \(m\), \(\Lambda_N(m)/\resscale\to m^\nu\) and \(m\Lambda_N'/\Lambda_N\to\nu\), with \(\nu=1\) for \(\alpha\le1\) and \(\nu=\alpha\) for \(\alpha>1\), by the continuum laws of Section C.5. Dominated convergence then gives an integral in \(q=Q_m\) that equals \(\pi^{-1}\int_0^\infty q^{-1/2}\Jfun(q)\,\dif q=\sqrt{2\pi}/\pi=c_0\) when \(\nu=1\); for \(\nu=\alpha\) an integration by parts turns it into \(\frac{2\alpha}{\pi(\alpha+1)}\int_0^\infty q^{-3/2}(q\Jfun(q))^{(\alpha+1)/2}\,\dif q=A_\alpha\). This is (ii).
