The phase map and time to accuracy

In this chapter the kernels are fixed numbers. The deterministic row model (Definition SI.1) runs the row recursion (14) in continuous time at a constant step \(\lr\), with weights \(\mu_i>0\), drifts \(\mathfrak d_i>0\), volatilities \(\mathfrak v_i\ge0\) and initial row energies \(\fQ_i(0)\), \(i\le N\); its risk is \(\fRisk=\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i\), \(F_0=\fRisk(0)\), and \(\mathcal S\) is as in (16). The coefficients are modelled on the zero-ridge kernels of the main text and are not proved to be those of the algorithm along the trajectory (Remark SI.1), so every statement is about the model. The matched step \(2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S\) makes the floor \(\fRisk(\infty)\) equal to \(\epsilon\), and \(t_a=\inf\{t:\fRisk(t)\le a\}\). The power-law arrays (Definition SI.3) have \(\mu_i=i^{-\alpha}\), \(\fQ_i(0)=i^{-\texp}\), \(p=\alpha+\texp>1\), \(B\ge N\), \(\gamma=N/B\), \(H_N(a)=\sum_{i\le N}i^{-a}\), \(\bar\mu=H_N(\alpha)/N\), and
\[ \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}=\sqrt{\mu_iB/N},\quad \mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SVD}}=1;\qquad \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=\mu_i\sqrt{B/(\pi\bar\mu)},\quad \mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=N\big[1+\tfrac2\pi\big(\tfrac{\mu_i}{\bar\mu}-1\big)\big], \]
the large-batch Muon kernels without momentum (SignSVD; the adopted drift is \(\sqrt2\) times the main text’s \(\sqrt{\mu_iB/(2N)}\), and with the main text’s value every ratio of hitting times below is halved and no exponent changes, Remark SI.1) and the kernels of Table 1. For \(j\in\{\text{s-SVD},\text{s-SGD}\}\), \(\mathcal S_j\) is the noise constant, \(T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}=\sqrt\gamma\), \(T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}=\sqrt{\pi\bar\mu/B}\), \(q_{\text{s-SVD}}=\alpha/2\), \(q_{\text{s-SGD}}=\alpha\), \(r_j=(p-1)/(2q_j)\), and \(D_N=\pi H_N(\alpha)/H_N(\alpha/2)\). The chapter solves the model when all drifts are equal, then gives the power-law hitting times and their ratio in windows of dimension and accuracy, and finally at every accuracy.

Theorem SI.4 (Matched floors)
In the setting of Proposition SI.2, let \(\epsilon>0\), \(T>1\) and \(F_0>T\epsilon\), and take the matched step \(\lr=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S=4\mathfrak d\sqrt\epsilon/\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}\). Put \(X\defeq\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}>\sqrt T\). Then the floor is \(\epsilon\) and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.13} t_{T\epsilon}=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}\Big[X-\sqrt T+\log\frac{X-1}{\sqrt T-1}\Big]. \end{equation}
If \(x\defeq1/X\le\frac12\), then
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.14} \Big|\frac{t_{T\epsilon}}{\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}}-1\Big|\le x\Big[\sqrt T+\abs{\log(\sqrt T-1)}+\log\frac1x+2x\Big]. \end{equation}
For two such models with the same \(F_0\), \(\epsilon\) and \(T\),
\[ \frac{t^{(1)}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{(2)}_{T\epsilon}}=\frac{\mathfrak v^{(1)}_{\rm tot}/(\mathfrak d^{(1)})^2}{\mathfrak v^{(2)}_{\rm tot}/(\mathfrak d^{(2)})^2} . \]
This theorem is the constant-learning-rate part of the main text’s statement, after (12), that the Muon speedup is also the ratio of the times to a common floor. With a common drift the model closes on the risk, as the recursion (8) in continuous time, and at the matched step the time to \(T\epsilon\) is explicit. Its leading term is \(\sqrt{F_0/\epsilon}\) times \(\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}/(4\mathfrak d^2)\), half the inverse of the rate \(2\mathfrak d^2/\mathfrak v\), so the ratio of the times of two models is the inverse ratio of their rates, exactly and at every accuracy.
Proof sketch.
With a common drift the risk solves a scalar equation, which integrates in closed form: summing the row equations with weights \(\mu_i/2\) turns the drift term into \(-2\lr\mathfrak d\sqrt{\fRisk}\), so exactly (Proposition SI.2)
\[ \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif t}=-2\lr\mathfrak d\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\lr^2\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}2 . \]
In \(\mathsf u=\sqrt{\fRisk}\) this reads \(\mathsf u'=-a_\circ(\mathsf u-b_\circ)/\mathsf u\), with \(a_\circ=\lr\mathfrak d\) and \(b_\circ=\lr\mathcal S/2\). The difference \(\mathsf u-b_\circ\) keeps its sign, so for \(\sqrt{F_0}>b_\circ\) the risk decreases to \(b_\circ^2\) without reaching it, and separating variables gives ((SI.12))
\[ a_\circ t=\sqrt{F_0}-\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}+b_\circ\log\frac{\sqrt{F_0}-b_\circ}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}-b_\circ} . \]
At the matched step the closed form evaluated at the level \(T\epsilon\) is the time. There \(b_\circ=\sqrt\epsilon\) and \(a_\circ=4\mathfrak d^2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}\), and since \(F_0>T\epsilon>b_\circ^2\), setting \(\sqrt{\fRisk}=\sqrt{T\epsilon}\) gives (SI.13). With \(X-1=X(1-x)\) the exact relative error is
\[ \frac{t_{T\epsilon}}{\frac{\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}}{4\mathfrak d^2}X}-1 =x\Big[-\sqrt T+\log\frac1x+\log(1-x)-\log(\sqrt T-1)\Big], \]
and \(\abs{\log(1-x)}\le2x\) for \(x\le\frac12\) gives (SI.14). The bracket in (SI.13) depends only on \(F_0\), \(\epsilon\) and \(T\), so it cancels in a ratio.

Theorem SI.6 (Isotropic comparison)
Let \(N,B\ge1\), \(\gamma=N/B\), \(m=\min(B,N)\), and take the isotropic arrays (SI.15) with a common initial risk \(F_0=\fRisk^{\text{s-SVD}}(0)=\fRisk^{\text{s-SGD}}(0)\). Choose \(\lr^\star_{\text{s-SVD}}\) and \(\lr^\star_{\text{s-SGD}}\) by (16), so that both models have floor \(\epsilon\), and fix \(T>1\) with \(F_0>T\epsilon\).
  1. (i)
    For every such \(\epsilon\), \(t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}/t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}=\rho(\gamma)\,B/\mathcal N_B^2\). As \(\epsilon/F_0\to0\), uniformly in \(N\), \(B\) and \(C(\gamma)\),
    \[ t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}\sim\frac m{4C(\gamma)^2}\sqrt{\frac{F_0}\epsilon},\qquad t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}\sim\frac{\pi N^2}{4\mathcal N_B^2}\sqrt{\frac{F_0}\epsilon} . \]
  2. (ii)
    If in addition \(B\to\infty\), with \(N\) and \(\gamma\) allowed to vary, then
    \[ t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}\sim\frac{\pi N^2}{4B}\sqrt{\frac{F_0}\epsilon},\qquad \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\Big/\rho(\gamma)\longrightarrow1 , \]
    and the ratio tends to \(\rho(\gamma_0)\) if \(\gamma\to\gamma_0\in(0,\infty)\).
  3. (iii)
    For every \(\gamma>0\),
    \[ \rho(1)\le\rho(\gamma)<\frac{32}{9\pi},\qquad \rho(1)\le\frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\le\frac{\pi^2}4\cdot\frac{32}{9\pi}, \]
    with \(\rho(\gamma)=\rho(1)\) only at \(\gamma=1\); and \(\rho(\gamma)\to\frac{32}{9\pi}\) as \(\gamma\downarrow0\) and \(\rho(\gamma)\to1\) as \(\gamma\to\infty\).
At \(\bmom=0\) this is the precise form of the statement, after (12), that the Muon speedup is the ratio of the times to a common floor. The identity in (i) is exact: the ratio is \(\rho(\gamma)\) times \(B/\mathcal N_B^2\in[1,\pi^2/4]\), which tends to one at large batch. By (iii), \(\rho\) has its minimum \(\rho(1)=\frac14(1+\log(1+\sqrt2)/\sqrt2)^2\approx0.6587\) at the square aspect ratio, where SignSGD is faster, tends to \(1\) at small batch (\(\gamma\to\infty\)), and has supremum \(32/(9\pi)\approx1.13\) at large batch (\(\gamma\downarrow0\)), the values of (12) at \(\bmom=0\). With momentum the same algebra applies to the pair \((C_\bmom(\gamma),\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom})\).
Proof sketch.
Each isotropic array has a common drift, so by Theorem SI.4 and \(N^2/(mB)=\gamma\max(1,\gamma)\) (Proposition SI.3),
\[ \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}} =\frac{N^2/(\mathcal N_B^2/\pi)}{m/C(\gamma)^2}=\rho(\gamma)\,\frac B{\mathcal N_B^2}. \]
The Gaussian bounds \(\max\{\frac2\pi\sqrt B,\sqrt B-4/\sqrt B\}\le\mathcal N_B\le\sqrt B\) (Proposition SJ.1) give \(1\le B/\mathcal N_B^2\le\pi^2/4\), and \(B/\mathcal N_B^2\le(1-4/B)^{-2}\) for \(B>4\). The relative error in Theorem SI.4 depends only on \(\epsilon/F_0\) and \(T\), which gives (i) and (ii).
The shape of \(\rho\) comes from an integral for \(C(\gamma)\) over the participation level. Let \(\Phi(v)=\EE[vZ^2/(1+vZ^2)]\) with \(Z\) standard Gaussian, and \(V\) its inverse. The square-case scalar equation of the isotropic analysis gives the spectral parameter as a function of the level \(y\), \(t=(\gamma-y)^2/V(y)\). Changing variables from \(t\) to \(y\) in the half moment that defines \(C(\gamma)\) and integrating by parts gives (Lemma SI.4)
\[ C(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2\,I(\gamma)}{\pi\gamma^2},\qquad I(\gamma)=\int_0^{\min(\gamma,1)}(\gamma-y)\,w(y)\,\dif y,\qquad w=V^{-1/2}, \]
where \(w_\circ(y)=\sqrt y\,w(y)\) decreases strictly from \(1\). For \(\gamma\ge1\), \(I(\gamma)=\gamma\mathcal A-\mathcal D\) with \(\mathcal A=\int_0^1w=\sqrt{\pi/2}\) and \(\mathcal D=\int_0^1yw\), so \(\rho(\gamma)=(1-\mathcal D/(\mathcal A\gamma))^2\) increases to \(1\). For \(\gamma<1\), \(\rho=\frac2\pi\gamma^{-3}I^2\), whose derivative has the sign of \(2\gamma I'-3I\); this is negative, because the constant \(w_\circ(\gamma/3)\) integrates to zero against \((3y-\gamma)y^{-1/2}\) and \(w_\circ\) is decreasing:
\[ 2\gamma I'(\gamma)-3I(\gamma)=\int_0^\gamma(3y-\gamma)\,y^{-1/2}\big(w_\circ(y)-w_\circ(\gamma/3)\big)\,\dif y<0 . \]
As \(\gamma\downarrow0\), \(\gamma^{-3/2}I(\gamma)\to\int_0^1(1-y)y^{-1/2}\dif y=\frac43\), so \(\rho\to\frac{32}{9\pi}\) (Theorem SI.5); the closed form of \(C(1)\) (Theorem SF.6) gives \(\rho(1)\).

Theorem SI.7 (Time to \(T\epsilon\) in the power-law phases)
Fix \(\alpha>0\) and \(\texp\) with \(\alpha+\texp>1\) and \(\texp\notin\{1,\alpha+1\}\), and fix \(T\ge4\). Consider the arrays of Definition SI.3 at their matched steps along a sequence of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and integers \(B\ge N\), such that
  • •
    \(\epsilon N^{\alpha+\texp-1}\to\infty\) if \(\texp<1\);
  • •
    \(\epsilon N\to\infty\) if \(\texp>1\) and \(\alpha>1\);
  • •
    \((N,B,\epsilon)\) lies in the low-exponent window (SI.33) for all large indices if \(\texp>1\) and \(\alpha\le1\).
Then there are constants \(0<c_-\le c_+\), depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\), such that for all large indices of the sequence, with \(x\asymp y\) meaning \(c_-\le x/y\le c_+\),
\begin{align} t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\gamma\,H_N(\alpha/2)\,\epsilon^{-\alpha/[2(\alpha+\texp-1)]}\ \ (\texp<1),& t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\sqrt\gamma\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\ \ (\texp>1),\tag{SI.35}\\ t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\frac{N^2\pi\bar\mu}B\,\epsilon^{-\alpha/(\alpha+\texp-1)}\ \ (\texp<\alpha+1),& t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\sqrt{\frac{\pi\bar\mu}B}\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\ \ (\texp>\alpha+1).\tag{SI.36} \end{align}
Moreover \(H_N(\alpha/2)\asymp N^{1-\alpha/2}\) for \(\alpha<2\), \(H_N(\alpha/2)\asymp\log N\) for \(\alpha=2\), and \(H_N(\alpha/2)\asymp1\) for \(\alpha>2\), with constants depending only on \(\alpha\).
This theorem supplies the exponents of Theorem 5.1: the exponent of \(1/\epsilon\) in each display is \(e_{\text{Muon}}\) or \(e_{\text{s-SGD}}\) of (18). Like every statement of this chapter, it concerns the model with the adopted arrays at the matched step, not the algorithm. Because the Muon drift scales like \(\sqrt{\mu_i}\) and the SignSGD drift like \(\mu_i\), the time saturates at \(\epsilon^{-1/2}\) beyond \(\texp=1\) for SignSVD but only beyond \(\texp=\alpha+1\) for SignSGD. The prefactors carry all the dependence on \(N\) and \(B\), and their ratio is \(D_N\) of (17). The limit is joint in \(N\) and \(\epsilon\). For \(\texp<1\) the window keeps \(\epsilon\) above the accuracy scale \(\epsilon_N\). At fixed \(N\) the exponent is \(\frac12\) in every phase (Corollary SI.2), and Theorem SI.13 treats \(\epsilon/\epsilon_N\to0\).
Proof sketch.
The risk of the model solves a scalar equation once time is measured in the right units. In the row equations the drift of every row is divided by \(\sqrt{\fRisk}\), so the natural time is the accumulated effective step \(s=\clock(t)=\int_0^t\lr\,\fRisk(t')^{-1/2}\,\dif t'\) ((SI.8)). In this time each row solves a linear equation driven by \(\sqrt Q\), and summing the rows with weights \(\mu_i/2\) gives exactly
\[ \begin{gathered} Q(s)=F(s)+\int_0^sk(s-v)\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v,\\ F(s)=\tfrac12\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i(0)e^{-2\mathfrak d_is},\qquad k(s)=\tfrac\lr2\sum_i\mu_i\mathfrak v_ie^{-2\mathfrak d_is}, \end{gathered} \]
where \(Q(s)\) is the risk at time \(s\) (Theorem SI.1 and Theorem SI.2). The forcing \(F\) is the risk the model would have without noise, and the kernel \(k\) feeds the volatility back in. The equation has a unique solution, and its limit is \(\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4\), which equals \(\epsilon\) at the matched step.
After a change of time scale, the time to \(T\epsilon\) factors into a prefactor that carries \(N\) and \(B\) and a dimensionless integral. The time scale \(T_0^j\) of Definition SI.3 makes the rescaled decay rates \(2\mathfrak d_iT_0^j\) equal to \(2i^{-q_j}\). The rescaled risk \(Y_j(u)=Q_j(T_0^ju)\) then solves (Proposition SI.4)
\[ Y_j=f_{N,q_j}+\sqrt\epsilon\,g_j*\sqrt{Y_j},\qquad f_{N,q}(u)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^Ni^{-(\alpha+\texp)}e^{-2ui^{-q}}, \]
where \(f_{N,q}\) is the rescaled noiseless risk and \(g_j\) is a kernel of total mass one. Converting back to step counts, the first time \(\hat u_j\) at which \(Y_j\) reaches \(T\epsilon\) gives exactly ((SI.30) and (SI.26))
\[ t^j_{T\epsilon}=\frac{\mathcal S_jT_0^j}{2\sqrt\epsilon}\int_0^{\hat u_j}\sqrt{Y_j(u)}\,\dif u,\qquad \mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}=\frac\gamma2H_N(\alpha/2),\qquad \mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac{\pi N^2\bar\mu}{2B}. \]
The phase is decided by whether the square root of the noiseless risk is integrable. The forcing is squeezed between two power laws (Lemma SI.7), the lower one valid for \(N\ge4\) and \(u\le(N/4)^q\):
\[ A_p\,(1+u)^{-2r_q}\le f_{N,q}(u)\le C_{p,q}^2\,(1+u)^{-2r_q},\qquad r_q=\frac{p-1}{2q}. \]
Thus \(r_j=r_{q_j}\). If \(r_j>1\), then \(\sqrt{f_{N,q_j}}\) is integrable uniformly in \(N\) ((SI.43)), and the integral in the time formula is of order one. If \(r_j<1\), the integral keeps growing until the first passage. At that time \(f_{N,q}(\hat u)\le T\epsilon\). The lower power law then bounds the first passage from below,
\[ 1+\hat u\ge\Big(\frac{A_p}{T\epsilon}\Big)^{1/(2r)}, \]
and the integral is of order \(\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)}\) (Lemma SI.10). With the prefactor this gives
\[ \begin{gathered} t^j_{T\epsilon}\asymp\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,\epsilon^{-1/(2r_j)}\ \ (r_j<1),\qquad t^j_{T\epsilon}\asymp\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\ \ (r_j>1),\\ \frac1{2r_{\text{s-SVD}}}=\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha+\texp-1)},\quad \frac1{2r_{\text{s-SGD}}}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\texp-1}. \end{gathered} \]
The boundary \(r_j=1\) is the line \(\texp=1\) for SignSVD and \(\texp=\alpha+1\) for SignSGD, because \(q_{\text{s-SVD}}\) is half of \(q_{\text{s-SGD}}\).
The noise term is controlled by comparison with a function of the form \((h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\), and this comparison is where the windows come from. Any function that dominates the right side of the rescaled equation when inserted into it lies above \(Y_j\) (Lemma SI.3). Take for \(h\) a power-law envelope of \(\sqrt{f_{N,q_j}}\) that decreases to \(\sqrt\epsilon\) at a horizon \(H\). Then \((h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) dominates as soon as the kernel does not amplify the envelope, that is, \(g_j*h\le2h\) or \(g_j*h\le\sqrt\epsilon\) on \([0,H]\) (Lemma SI.9). This places the first passage before \(H\), which supplies the upper bound in the previous paragraph. It also bounds the noise part of the integral by a fixed multiple of \(\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)}\), where \(r_0\le r_j\) is the exponent of the envelope and \(r_0>1\) exactly when \(r_j>1\) ((SI.51)). For \(r_j<1\) this bound is of the order of the main term, and the theorems give orders and not equivalents for these models; for \(r_j>1\) it tends to zero.
The kernel condition holds in each range of exponents, and checking it is what fixes the windows. For \(\texp<1\) (Theorem SI.8) the SignSGD kernel is decreasing with mass one, so \(g_j*h\le h/(1-r_j)\) (Lemma SI.13), which is at most \(2h\) because here \(r_{\text{s-SGD}}=r_{\text{s-SVD}}/2<\frac12\). The SignSVD kernel, normalized by \(H_N(\alpha/2)\), has small mass up to times of order \(N^{\alpha/2}\) when \(\alpha<2\) (Lemma SI.15), a logarithmic substitute at \(\alpha=2\) (Lemma SI.17) and an integrable tail when \(\alpha>2\) (Lemma SI.14). The horizon is then \(H_j\asymp\epsilon^{-1/(2r_j)}\), and since \(2r_jq_j=\alpha+\texp-1\) the window \(\epsilon N^{\alpha+\texp-1}\ge E_*\), with \(E_*\) a fixed threshold, keeps \(H_j\) below \((N/4)^{q_j}\), where the lower power law holds. For \(\alpha>1\) and \(\texp\ge1\) (Theorem SI.9) the kernels have integrable power tails (Lemma SI.14), except the SignSVD kernel for \(1<\alpha\le2\). For \(1<\alpha<2\) the normalization \(H_N(\alpha/2)\ge N^{1-\alpha/2}\) makes the late part of the convolution small up to the horizon \(\sqrt N\), which the window \(\epsilon N\ge E_*\) guarantees (Lemma SI.16). At \(\alpha=2\), Lemma SI.18 adds a correction term to the comparison function, needs no relation between \(N\) and \(\epsilon\), and bounds the noise part by a fixed constant that tends to zero as \(N\to\infty\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). For \(\alpha\le1\) and \(\texp\ge1\) (Theorem SI.10) no tail is usable, but the kernels are decreasing with maxima \(k_{N,j}=g_j(0)\to0\) (Proposition SI.5), so
\[ (g_j*h_j)(u)\le k_{N,j}\,C_j\,I_{r_j}(H_j)\le\sqrt\epsilon\qquad(0\le u\le H_j), \]
where \(C_j=C_{p,q_j}\) and the second inequality is the low-exponent window (SI.33). Elementary harmonic-sum bounds give the orders of \(H_N(\alpha/2)\) in the last assertion (Lemma SI.8).

The windows of Theorem SI.8, Theorem SI.9 and Theorem SI.10 are the sets of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(B\ge N\) and \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\) in which, respectively, \(N\ge N_*\) and \(\epsilon N^{p-1}\ge E_*\) (\(\texp<1\)); \(N\ge N_*\) and \(\epsilon N\ge E_*\) (\(\alpha>1\), \(\texp\ge1\)); and \((N,B,\epsilon)\) lies in the low-exponent window (\(\alpha\le1\), \(\texp\ge1\)). Corollary SI.3 (Ratio of the hitting times)
In the windows of Theorem SI.8 (\(\texp<1\)), Theorem SI.9 (\(\alpha>1\), \(\texp\ge1\)) and Theorem SI.10 (\(\alpha\le1\), \(\texp\ge1\)), with constants depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.38} \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\asymp D_N\cdot\begin{cases} \epsilon^{-\alpha/[2(p-1)]},&\texp<1,\\ \epsilon^{-1/2}/\log(1/\epsilon),&\texp=1,\\ \epsilon^{-(\alpha+1-\texp)/[2(p-1)]},&1<\texp<\alpha+1,\\ \log(1/\epsilon),&\texp=\alpha+1,\\ 1,&\texp>\alpha+1. \end{cases} \end{equation}

Remark SI.4 (The adopted SignSGD array and the corrected modes)
The SignSGD section computes the raw annealed row volatility of the actual sign update, \(\mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}\), for a Haar eigenbasis independent of the batch, comparable dimensions and a covariance bounded below (Theorem SJ.4). At \(\Nout=\Nin=N\), with \(\Sigmaout=U\Lambda U^\T\), the state \(\Delta=UD\) and \(\cR=\frac12\Tr(\Lambda DD^\T)\), its difference from the adopted array is
\[ \mathfrak v_i^{\rm corr}-\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac2\pi\,N\Big[\frac{2\mu_i}{\Tr\Sigmaout}+\frac{(B+2N+3)\,(\Lambda DD^\T\Lambda)_{ii}}{2\cR\,\Tr\Sigmaout}\Big]\ \ge0 , \]
where \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}=N(1-\frac2\pi)+\frac2\pi N^2\mu_i/\Tr\Sigmaout\). The corrected array depends on the state, its total exceeds \(N^2\), and the power-law family has no covariance lower bound fixed in \(N\). The phase theorems of this subsection concern the adopted array (SI.25). Replacing it by another nonnegative array with total \(N^2\) and the same drift leaves \(\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}\) unchanged, but not the kernel \(g_{\text{s-SGD}}\) on which the joint estimates rest.
For power-law spectra the main text uses SignSGD kernels without the nonnegative, state-dependent term that the SignSGD analysis finds in the per-row volatility (the footnote to (14)); this remark gives that term and the reason it is dropped. The corrected array is proved only for a covariance bounded below, which the power-law family is not, and it depends on the state, so it does not fit a model with fixed coefficients. It is at least the adopted array, and its total exceeds \(N^2\). Any array with total \(N^2\) and the same drift has the same noise constant \(\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}\), hence the same matched step, but the joint estimates use the shape of the rescaled kernel \(g_{\text{s-SGD}}\) (Section G.6), so the phase theorems are statements about the adopted array only.
Remark SI.5 (Momentum arrays)

\[ -\frac{2\lr\,\mathfrak d_i^{\bmom}}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,\fQ_i+\lr^2\mathfrak v_i^{\bmom} =-\frac{2\lr'\,\mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,\fQ_i+\lr'^2\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SVD}}, \qquad \lr'\defeq\lr\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}B} . \]
By (SI.1) the noise constant of the momentum array is \(\mathcal S^{\bmom}=\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/B}\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}\), so its matched step \(2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S^{\bmom}\) is mapped to the matched step \(\lr^\star_{\text{s-SVD}}\). At matched steps the two models therefore solve the same row equations from the same initial values, and they have the same solution by the uniqueness in Theorem SI.2. In particular \(t^{\bmom}_{T\epsilon}=t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}\), and Theorem SI.7, Corollary SI.3 and Proposition SI.6 hold for the momentum array without change. Written with the noise constant of the momentum array, the saturated prefactor \(\sqrt\gamma\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}\) of (SI.35) is \(\sqrt{N/B_{\rm eff}}\,\mathcal S^{\bmom}\).
The oversampled isotropic case refines the volatility factor. There the volatility with momentum is \(c_\bmom B_{\rm eff}/B\) times the volatility without momentum, with \(c_\bmom\defeq\sw2V_\bmom\in(\sw2,1)\), up to a factor \(1+O(\gamma)\) (Remark SF.1 and Lemma SF.10). A factor \(c>0\) on every volatility of an array multiplies every matched-step hitting time by \(c\). Indeed, the noise constant becomes \(c\,\mathcal S\) and the matched step becomes \(\lr^\star/c\), so the right side of (SI.2) becomes \(1/c\) times its value for the original array at its matched step. The risk of the modified model at time \(t\) is therefore the original risk at time \(t/c\).
This remark is behind the main-text sentence that a constant factor in the volatility only rescales the time to reach a target, and behind the fact that the power-law hitting times of Muon in Figure 8 do not depend on \(\bmom\). The adopted array with momentum multiplies the drift by \(\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/B}\) and the volatility by \(B_{\rm eff}/B\), which is the SignSVD array at the step \(\lr\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/B}\); this maps the matched step to the matched step, and uniqueness of the row equations makes the two trajectories, and hence the hitting times, identical. More generally, a factor \(c>0\) on every volatility multiplies the noise constant by \(c\) and divides the matched step by \(c\), so the right side of the row equations at the matched step is divided by \(c\), and every hitting time is multiplied by \(c\). In the oversampled isotropic case the volatility with momentum is \(c_\bmom B_{\rm eff}/B\) times the one without, up to a factor \(1+O(\gamma)\), with \(c_\bmom=\sw2V_\bmom\in(\sw2,1)\) of Lemma SF.10, the \(c_\bmom\) of the main text.

Theorem SI.13 (Frozen regime)
Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(\texp\) with \(p=\alpha+\texp>1\), and \(T\ge4\). Consider a sequence of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(N\to\infty\), integers \(B\ge N\) and \(x=\epsilon/\epsilon_N\to0\). For all large indices both first hits are attained, and for both \(j\)
\[ \frac{\mathsf I_j}{J_N(p,q_j)}\longrightarrow1,\qquad t^j_{T\epsilon}\sim\frac{\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,J_N(p,q_j)}{2\sqrt\epsilon},\qquad \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\sim\rho_N . \]
Moreover, if \(r_j<1\), there are \(N_2\) and \(c\) such that
\[ \Big|\frac{\mathsf I_j}{J_N(p,q_j)}-1\Big|\le c\,\Big(x^{1/4}+x^{1/2}\log\big(e+x^{-1/2}\big)\Big)^{1/2} \]
for all \(N\ge N_2\), \(B\ge N\), \(\epsilon<1/(2T)\) and \(x\le1\).
Below the accuracy scale \(\epsilon_N\), which bounds the risk carried by the modes beyond \(N\), more accuracy no longer changes the comparison. The clock integral of each model freezes at \(J_N(p,q_j)\), the integral of the square root of its noiseless risk, so each time is \(\epsilon^{-1/2}\) times a constant that depends on \(N\), and the ratio is asymptotic to its accuracy-first value \(\rho_N\). This is the levelling off of (17) below \(\epsilon_N\), and the tie of Phase D in Figure 7 once \(\epsilon\lesssim\epsilon_N\). The theorem needs no window, and for \(r_j<1\) it comes with a rate in \(x=\epsilon/\epsilon_N\).
Proof sketch.
At finite \(N\) every rate is bounded below, which pins the clock integral to \(J_N\) at small accuracy. The forcing \(f_{N,q_j}\) and the kernel \(g_j=\sum_iw_ic_ie^{-c_iu}\), whose weights sum to one, are finite sums of exponentials with rates at least \(b_{\min}=2N^{-q_j}\). The envelope \(h=\sqrt{F_0}\,e^{-b_{\min}u/4}\) satisfies \(g_j*h\le\frac43h\), so \((h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) dominates the equation and the first passage occurs before \((2/b_{\min})\log(F_0/\epsilon)\). After it, the forcing decays at rate at least \(b_{\min}\) from a value at most \(T\epsilon\), which bounds the part of \(J_N\) that is missed; and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with \(\int g_j=1\) gives \(\mathsf I_j\le J_N+\sqrt{\mathsf d\,\mathsf I_j}\). With \(\mathsf y_j=\epsilon N^{2q_j}\) this gives (Lemma SI.11 and (SI.59))
\[ J_N-\sqrt{T\mathsf y_j}\le\mathsf I_j\le\big(\sqrt{J_N}+\sqrt{\mathsf d}\big)^2,\qquad \mathsf d=\sqrt{\mathsf y_j}\,\log\frac{F_0}\epsilon . \]
The errors are small relative to \(J_N\) when \(r_j\ge1\), and a restart removes a logarithm when \(r_j<1\). Since \(2q_jr_j=p-1\), \(\sqrt{\mathsf y_j}=\sqrt x\,N^{q_j(1-r_j)}\). For \(r_j>1\), \(J_N\to J(p,q_j)>0\) and \(\mathsf y_j\to0\), so both errors vanish. For \(r_j=1\), \(\mathsf y_j=x\), \(J_N\sim\sqrt{q_j/8}\,\log N\) and \(\mathsf d=o(\log N)\). For \(r_j<1\), \(J_N\asymp N^{q_j(1-r_j)}\) (Proposition SI.8), so the lower error has relative order \(\sqrt x\), but \(\mathsf d/J_N\) is of order \(\sqrt x\log N\), because \(\log(F_0/\epsilon)\) contains \((p-1)\log N\). The proof therefore first runs the power-law comparison of Section G.6 up to a fixed multiple \(u_0\) of \(N^{q_j}\), where the kernel condition \(g_j*h\le2h\) still holds, at a cost \(O(x^{1/4}+x^{1/2})N^{q_j(1-r_j)}\). This gives the stated rate. The times follow from \(t^j_{T\epsilon}=\mathcal S_jT_0^j\mathsf I_j/(2\sqrt\epsilon)\), and dividing \(D_N\mathsf I_{\text{s-SGD}}/\mathsf I_{\text{s-SVD}}\) by \(\rho_N\) gives the ratio.

with constants depending also on \(\omega\).
This is the precise form of Theorem 5.1. For all large \(N\), all \(B\ge N\) and all small \(\epsilon\), with no window, the ratio is, when both models lie in \(\mathcal U\), \(D_N\) times the quotient of the clock orders \(\Upsilon_j\), and off the lines \(\texp=1\) and \(\texp=\alpha+1\) this is \(D_N\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-\kappa}\). Above the accuracy scale the ratio grows like \(\epsilon^{-\kappa}\) as \(\epsilon\) decreases, the main text’s reading of Phase C, and below \(\epsilon_N\) it levels off. The set \(\mathcal U\) excludes SignSVD at \(\alpha=1\), \(\texp\ge1\) and SignSGD at \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\), \(\texp\ge\alpha+1\), hence the main text’s exclusion of \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\); with one model excluded, one half of the order survives. At \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\) the ratio is a power of \(N\) times a power of \(\log N\), the input of Theorem SI.15.
Proof sketch.
The clock integral of each model is of order \(\Upsilon_j\) at every accuracy: it grows as in Section G.6 until \(\epsilon\) reaches the accuracy scale, then freezes at the order \(N^{q_j(1-r_j)}\) of \(J_N\) (Theorem SI.14). The lower bound needs no kernel estimate. Since \(Y_j\ge f_{N,q_j}\), the first passage is no earlier than that of the forcing, and the lower envelope of the forcing, valid up to \((N/4)^{q_j}\), keeps it above \(T\epsilon\) until \(\min\{\sigma_*,(N/4)^{q_j}\}\), where \(1+\sigma_*=(A_p/T\epsilon)^{1/(2r_j)}\). Integrating the envelope up to that time gives the two branches of \(\Upsilon_j\), which for \(r_j<1\) cross at \(\epsilon_N\) because \(q_j(1-r_j)=(p-1)(1-r_j)/(2r_j)\):
\[ \Upsilon_j(N,\epsilon)=\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)}\qquad(r_j<1). \]
The upper bound needs the kernel controlled up to times of order \(N^{q_j}\), and this is where \(\mathcal U\) comes from. For \(r_j<1\) the comparison of Section G.6 covers \(\epsilon\ge E_*\epsilon_N\), and the comparison followed by a restart, as in Theorem SI.13, covers the band below. For \(r_j\ge1\) the forcing envelope decays faster than a slow part of the kernel: each kernel in \(\mathcal U\) is bounded by an integrable fast part plus a slow part of height of order \(N^{-q}\) (Lemma SI.25),
\[ g_j(v)\le K_f(1+v)^{-\upsilon}+K_sN^{-q}\Big(\frac{1+v}{N^q}\Big)^{-\mathsf b},\qquad \upsilon>1,\ 0<\mathsf b<1,\ 0\le v\le N^q-1 . \]
The slow part acting on the envelope \(h\) leaves a remainder \(w^2/\sqrt\epsilon\), where \(w\) is a power-law envelope of exponent \(\mathsf b/2<1\) that the kernel does not amplify; then \((h+w+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) dominates the equation (Lemma SI.20), and a restart as in Theorem SI.13 closes the bound. This bound is not available for the SignSVD kernel at \(\alpha=1\) and the SignSGD kernel at \(\alpha\in\{\frac12,1\}\), the triples excluded from \(\mathcal U\); SignSVD at \(\alpha\ge2\) is treated directly.
The ratio law is then a division. Exactly, \(t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}/t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}=D_N\mathsf I_{\text{s-SGD}}/\mathsf I_{\text{s-SVD}}\) ((SI.41)), and the lower bound holds for every model, which gives the one-sided statements. For \(r_j\ne1\), \(e_j-\frac12\) is \((1-r_j)/(2r_j)\) or \(0\) as \(r_j<1\) or \(r_j>1\), so \(\Upsilon_j=\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-(e_j-1/2)}\) and the quotient is \(\max\{\epsilon,\epsilon_N\}^{-\chi}\). At \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\), \(\Upsilon_j\asymp N^{\min\{\omega,p-1\}(e_j-1/2)}(\log N)^{[r_j=1]}\) and \(D_N\asymp N^{e_D}(\log N)^{[\alpha=1]-[\alpha=2]}\), which gives the last display.
Full proof
At the accuracy \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\), with arbitrary integers \(B_N\ge N\), write \(\mathsf R=t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}/t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}\). With \(e_D\), \(\chi\) and \(L\) as before Corollary SI.6, put \(S(\omega)=e_D+\chi\min\{\omega,p-1\}\) and \(\mathsf E=\{\alpha=1,\ \texp\ge1\}\cup\{\alpha=\frac12,\ \texp\ge\frac32\}\).
Theorem SI.15 (Accuracy-exponent map)
Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(\texp\) with \(p=\alpha+\texp>1\), \(\omega>0\) and \(T\ge4\). As \(N\to\infty\):
  1. (i)
    if \(\omega>p-1\), then \(\mathsf R\sim\rho_N\), so every conclusion of Theorem SI.12 and Corollary SI.4 holds for \(\mathsf R\);
  2. (ii)
    if \((\alpha,\texp)\notin\mathsf E\), then \(\mathsf R\asymp N^{S(\omega)}(\log N)^L\);
  3. (iii)
    if \(\alpha=1\) and \(1\le\texp<2\), then \(\mathsf R\lesssim N^{S(\omega)}(\log N)^L\).
Setting \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\) turns the ratio law into one power of \(N\), and its exponent \(S(\omega)\) is the competition of the main text’s paragraph on which algorithm wins: the dimension exponent \(e_D\), negative for \(\alpha<2\), against the accuracy exponent \(\chi\min\{\omega,p-1\}\), which is nondecreasing in \(\omega\) until the accuracy reaches \(\epsilon_N\) at \(\omega=p-1\) and is constant beyond. Part (ii) holds whenever both models lie in \(\mathcal U\), and (iii) is the one-sided bound for \(\alpha=1\), \(1\le\texp<2\), where only SignSGD does. Part (i) is sharper in the frozen range \(\omega>p-1\): the ratio is asymptotic to the accuracy-first value \(\rho_N\), whose limits and orders Theorem SI.12 computes; where the order only says that the two are balanced, this gives the limit, the tie constants of Phases D and E and of the chord. It is the theorem the main text cites for Phase B in Figure 7.
Proof sketch.
Parts (ii) and (iii) are Corollary SI.6 at \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\), which lies below its threshold \(\epsilon_0\) for large \(N\). Both models lie in \(\mathcal U\) exactly when \((\alpha,\texp)\notin\mathsf E\), and for \(\alpha=1\), \(1\le\texp<2\) only SignSGD does, which leaves the upper half. The exponent is explicit ((SI.48)):
\[ \begin{aligned} \chi(p-1)&=\max\Big\{\frac{p-1}2,\alpha\Big\}-\max\Big\{\frac{p-1}2,\frac\alpha2\Big\}\\ &=\frac\alpha2,\ \ \frac{\alpha+1-\texp}2,\ \ 0\quad(\texp<1,\ 1\le\texp\le\alpha+1,\ \texp\ge\alpha+1), \end{aligned} \]
so \(S(\omega)=e_D+\min\{\omega/(p-1),1\}\,\chi(p-1)\).
Part (i) is Theorem SI.13, since \(\omega>p-1\) means \(x=N^{p-1-\omega}\to0\); what it needs in addition is the behaviour of \(\rho_N=D_NJ_N(p,\alpha)/J_N(p,\alpha/2)\) as \(N\to\infty\). For \(r_q<1\) the finite forcing integral grows like a power of \(N\) with an explicit constant. The substitution \(u=N^qz\) turns the rescaled forcing into a Riemann sum,
\[ 2N^{p-1}f_{N,q}(N^qz)=\frac1N\sum_{i\le N}\Big(\frac iN\Big)^{-p}e^{-2z(i/N)^{-q}} \longrightarrow\int_0^1t^{-p}e^{-2zt^{-q}}\,\dif t=\frac{(2z)^{-2r_q}}q\,\Gamma(2r_q,2z), \]
with \(\Gamma(a,y)=\int_y^\infty t^{a-1}e^{-t}\dif t\). Halving every rate gives a majorant \(4^{r}C_{p,q}^2z^{-2r}e^{-z}\) that does not depend on \(N\) (Lemma SI.19), so dominated convergence gives (Proposition SI.8)
\[ \frac{J_N(p,q)}{N^{q(1-r_q)}}\longrightarrow(8q)^{-1/2}\mathcal K(r_q),\qquad \mathcal K(r)=\int_0^\infty y^{-r}\,\Gamma(2r,y)^{1/2}\,\dif y,\qquad \mathcal K(\tfrac12)=\sqrt{2\pi}. \]
For \(r_q=1\), \(J_N(p,q)\sim\sqrt{q/8}\,\log N\), and for \(r_q>1\), \(J_N(p,q)\to J(p,q)\). Multiplying the quotient of these by the order of \(D_N\) gives each limit and order of Theorem SI.12. For example, at \(\alpha=\texp=1\) the factors \(D_N\sim\frac\pi2N^{-1/2}\log N\) and \(J_N(p,1)/J_N(p,\frac12)\sim2\sqrt\pi\,N^{1/2}/\log N\) multiply to \(\pi^{3/2}\).
Full proof
SignSVD wins, SignSGD wins, or the two are balanced according as \(\mathsf R\to\infty\), \(\mathsf R\to0\) or \(\mathsf R\asymp1\). For \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(\texp<2\alpha-1\), \(\omega_c\) is the critical exponent of the main text: \(\omega_c=(2-\alpha)(p-1)/\alpha\) if \(\texp<1\) and \(\omega_c=(2-\alpha)(p-1)/(\alpha+1-\texp)\) if \(1\le\texp<2\alpha-1\), so that \(0<\omega_c<p-1\). The constants \(K_A\) and \(K_c\) are the explicit positive limits of \(\rho_N\) given by Theorem SI.12 for \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\texp<1\) and on the chord \(\texp=2\alpha-1\), \(1<\alpha<2\).
Corollary SI.7 (Phases of the accuracy-exponent map)
Under the hypotheses of Theorem SI.15:
  1. (a)
    Phase A, \(\alpha>2\), \(\texp\le\alpha+1\): SignSVD wins for every \(\omega\).
  2. (b)
    Phase B, \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(\texp\ge1\) with \((\alpha,\texp)\notin\mathsf E\), or \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(\texp>2\alpha-1\): SignSGD wins for every \(\omega\).
  3. (c)
    Phase C, \(1<\alpha<2\), \(\texp<2\alpha-1\): SignSGD wins if \(\omega<\omega_c\) and SignSVD wins if \(\omega>\omega_c\); at \(\omega=\omega_c\) the two are balanced if \(\texp\ne1\), and SignSGD wins, with order \(1/\log N\), if \(\texp=1\).
  4. (d)
    Phase D, \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\texp<1\): SignSGD wins if \(\omega<p-1\), and the two are balanced if \(\omega\ge p-1\), with limit \(K_A\) if \(\omega>p-1\).
  5. (e)
    Tie, \(\alpha>2\), \(\texp>\alpha+1\): balanced for every \(\omega\).
  6. (f)
    Chord, \(1<\alpha<2\), \(\texp=2\alpha-1\): SignSGD wins if \(\omega<p-1\), and the two are balanced if \(\omega\ge p-1\), with limit \(K_c\) if \(\omega>p-1\).
  7. (g)
    \(\alpha=1\): for \(\texp<1\), SignSGD wins if \(\omega<p-1\) and SignSVD wins, with order \(\log N\), if \(\omega\ge p-1\); for \(\texp=1\), SignSGD wins if \(\omega<1\), and \(\mathsf R\to\pi^{3/2}\) if \(\omega>1\); for \(1<\texp<2\), SignSGD wins for every \(\omega\).
  8. (h)
    \(\alpha=2\): SignSVD wins for \(\texp<3\), the two are balanced for \(\texp=3\), and SignSGD wins for \(\texp>3\), for every \(\omega\).
This is the phase diagram of Figure 7 in exact form, with its boundary lines; the Tie is the main text’s Phase E. In Phase A Muon wins at every accuracy, in Phase B SignSGD does, in Phase C SignSGD wins at low accuracy and Muon once \(\epsilon\ll N^{-\omega_c}\), and in Phase D SignSGD wins above the accuracy scale and the two tie below it. The chord \(\texp=2\alpha-1\) between B and C is where \(\omega_c\) reaches \(p-1\), that is, where the critical accuracy meets the accuracy scale, so as \(\omega\to\infty\) the boundary between B and C becomes the chord. The level \(\omega_c=1\) is the curve \(\texp_c(\alpha)\) of Proposition SI.6, the cut of the map at \(\epsilon=N^{-1}\), where that proposition’s window ends. Not covered are the winner for \(\omega\le p-1\) on \(\{\alpha=1,\texp\ge2\}\cup\{\alpha=\frac12,\texp\ge\frac32\}\), and the limit constants for \(\omega\le p-1\) in the balanced cases; at \(\alpha=\texp=\omega=1\) only \(\mathsf R\lesssim1\) is proved.
Proof sketch.
Off \(\mathsf E\) the winner is the sign of \(S(\omega)\), and of \(L\) where \(S(\omega)=0\) (Theorem SI.15(ii)). Write \(\omega'=\min\{\omega,p-1\}\). For \(0<\alpha<1\), \(e_D=-\frac\alpha2\). If \(\texp<1\), then \(S(\omega)=\frac\alpha2(\omega'/(p-1)-1)\) and \(L=0\); this is negative below the accuracy scale and zero at and beyond it, which is Phase D, with the limit \(K_A\) of Theorem SI.15(i). If \(\texp\ge1\), then \(\chi(p-1)=\max\{\frac{\alpha+1-\texp}2,0\}\le\frac\alpha2\), so \(S(\omega)\le0\), with equality only at \(\texp=1\), \(\omega\ge p-1\), where \(L=-1\); this is the first part of Phase B. For \(1<\alpha<2\), \(e_D=\frac\alpha2-1<0\), and for \(1\le\texp\le\alpha+1\)
\[ S(\omega)=\frac\alpha2-1+\frac{\omega'}{p-1}\cdot\frac{\alpha+1-\texp}2 , \]
which vanishes at \(\omega'=\omega_c\). At \(\omega'=p-1\) it equals \((2\alpha-1-\texp)/2\), so \(\omega_c<p-1\) exactly when \(\texp<2\alpha-1\). Below the chord SignSGD wins for \(\omega<\omega_c\) and SignSVD for \(\omega>\omega_c\), which is Phase C; at \(\omega=\omega_c\), \(L=-[\texp=1]\) gives the balance or the win of order \(1/\log N\). On the chord \(S(\omega)<0\) for \(\omega<p-1\) and \(S=L=0\) beyond, with the limit \(K_c\); above it, and for \(\texp>\alpha+1\), \(S(\omega)<0\), which is the second part of Phase B. The case \(\texp<1\) is the same with \(\alpha\) in place of \(\alpha+1-\texp\).
For \(\alpha\ge2\) the dimension factor does not decay, \(e_D=0\), and only the accuracy factor and the logarithms count. For \(\alpha>2\), \(S(\omega)>0\) when \(\texp<\alpha+1\), which is Phase A; for \(\texp\ge\alpha+1\), \(S(\omega)=0\) and \(L=[\texp=\alpha+1]\), which gives SignSVD on the line and the Tie beyond it. At \(\alpha=2\), \(S(\omega)>0\) for \(\texp<3\), and \(S(\omega)=0\) with \(L=-1+[\texp=3]\) for \(\texp\ge3\). At \(\alpha=1\) and \(1\le\texp<2\) only the upper bound of Theorem SI.15(iii) is available. There \(S(\omega)=-\frac12+\frac{\omega'}{p-1}\cdot\frac{2-\texp}2<0\) unless \(\texp=1\) and \(\omega\ge p-1\), which forces \(\mathsf R\to0\); at \(\texp=1\), \(\omega>1\) the frozen limit \(\pi^{3/2}\) of \(\rho_N\) applies.
