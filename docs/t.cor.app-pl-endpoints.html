Corollary SG.1 (Crossover windows and joint endpoints)
Assume Assumption S4, let \(i=i_N\to\infty\) be a row sequence, and let \(i_\star\defeq i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) for \(\alpha\le1\) and \(i_\star\defeq i_{\mathrm c}\) for \(\alpha>1\).
  1. (i)
    If \(\alpha\le1\) and \(i/i_\star\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then
    \[ \frac{\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)}{a_{\mathfrak d}}\to1+x^{-\alpha/2},\qquad \sw2\,\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1+2x^{\alpha/2}+c_0^{-2}x^\alpha,\qquad \frac{\mu_i}{c_Ni_\star^{-\alpha}}\to x^{-\alpha}. \]
  2. (ii)
    The joint endpoint equivalents are
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.34} \begin{array}{c|c|c} & i/i_\star\to0 & i/i_\star\to\infty\\\hline \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0) & \sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)} & a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\\[3pt] \mathfrak v_i(0) & 1/\sw2 & \gamma\,\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\\[3pt] \mu_i & c_Ni^{-\alpha}\ \text{(exact)} & c_Ni^{-\alpha}\ \text{(exact)} \end{array} \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    One has \(i_\star/N\to0\). There are row sequences \(i\to\infty\) in both windows \(i/i_\star\to0\) and \(i/i_\star\to\infty\) if and only if
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.35} \frac{BN^{\alpha-1}}{\log^2\gamma}\to\infty\ \ (\alpha<1),\qquad \frac{B}{\log^3\gamma}\to\infty\ \ (\alpha=1),\qquad B\to\infty\ \ (\alpha>1), \end{equation}
    and then \(1\ll i_\star\ll N\).
  4. (iv)
    For \(\alpha>1\), equating the two mesoscopic endpoint equivalents gives the indices \(i_{\mathrm c}/(\sw2d_\alpha^2)\) for the drift and \(B(\alpha-1)/(4\sw2K_\alpha^2)\) for the volatility, whose ratio is \((\alpha-1)d_\alpha^2/(4K_\alpha)\); all indices of the corollary are independent of \(c_N\).

Definitions and assumptions used

Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
The exponent \(\alpha>0\) and the momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) are fixed. Along a sequence of positive integers \(N\) and \(B=B_N\),
\[ N\to\infty,\qquad B\to\infty,\qquad \gamma=\frac NB\to\infty, \]
and the spectrum is \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\) for \(1\le i\le N\), with amplitudes \(c_N>0\), with \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\) and \(\Nout=\Nin=N\).
This corollary is the Muon part of Table 1 for \(\bmom>0\): it reads the drift and volatility crossovers in one coordinate \(i/i_\star\). Below \(i_\star\) the row keeps the resolved kernels of the large-batch section, \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\) and \(\mathfrak v_i\to1/\sw2=B_{\rm eff}/B\); above it both kernels follow the bulk profiles of \(i/N\), while the covariance \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\) is exact throughout. Part (iii) says when both windows contain rows: always for \(\alpha>1\), and for \(\alpha<1\) only if the batch exceeds \(N^{1-\alpha}\) up to logarithms; the crossover always lies far below \(N\). Part (iv) records that for \(\alpha>1\) the points where the endpoint laws are equal differ from \(i_{\mathrm c}\) only by constants, and that no index depends on the amplitude \(c_N\).
Proof sketch.
Both theorems are equivalences along row sequences, and they come from one approximation that is uniform over all rows (Theorem SG.1). For \(\alpha\le1\) the approximants are ((SG.29))
\[ \sqrt2\,\widehat{\mathfrak d}_i=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}{\gamma\sw2}}+a_N\Psi_\alpha\Big(\frac iN\Big),\qquad \widehat{\mathfrak v}_i=\frac1{\sw2}+\frac{2c_0}{\sqrt{\sw2}}\sqrt{\gamma\Gamma_\alpha\Big(\frac iN\Big)}+\gamma\Gamma_\alpha\Big(\frac iN\Big), \]
and for \(\alpha>1\) they are \(b_N\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}((i_{\mathrm c}/i)^\alpha)\) and \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}(i/i_{\mathrm c})\), each multiplied by a factor that tends to one when \(i/N\to0\) and that turns the mesoscopic law into the bulk profile when \(i/N\) stays positive ((SG.30)). For every sequence \(n_N\to\infty\),
\[ \sup_{n_N\le i\le N}\max\Big\{\Big|\frac{\mathfrak d_i(0)}{\widehat{\mathfrak d}_i}-1\Big|,\ \Big|\frac{\mathfrak v_i(0)}{\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i}-1\Big|\Big\}\longrightarrow0 . \]
The drift half is the head-plus-tail argument of Theorem SG.2. The volatility half is the argument of Theorem SG.3: the quadratic \(\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i\) is the flat square of that sketch, with \(b\) replaced by \(\sqrt{\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)}\), and the two agree on mesoscopic rows because \(\Gamma_\alpha(x)\sim x^\alpha\Psi_\alpha(x)^2/c_0^2\) (Lemma SG.21). A failure of uniformity would produce a row sequence along which a ratio stays away from one, so the uniform statement follows from the sequential one.
Parts (i) and (ii) are the two theorems in the coordinate \(i/i_\star\). For \(\alpha\le1\) the volatility index is \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}=c_0^{2/\alpha}i_\star\), so \(i/i_\star\to x\) means \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to y=c_0^{-2/\alpha}x\), and then \(2c_0y^{\alpha/2}=2x^{\alpha/2}\) and \(y^\alpha=c_0^{-2}x^\alpha\), which is (i). In the table the left column is the resolved case of both theorems, the two indices differing by a fixed factor. In the right column the drift is the unresolved case of Theorem SG.2, and the volatility follows from the uniform approximation, since \(\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\) beyond \(i_\star\): for \(\alpha\le1\) because \(\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\to\infty\) there, and for \(\alpha>1\) by the end \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\sim4K_\alpha X/(\alpha-1)\) of the crossover profile.
Parts (iii) and (iv) are computations with the indices. The definitions give
\[ \frac{i_\star}N=\big(\gamma\sw2d^2\log^2\gamma\big)^{-1/\alpha}\ (\alpha<1),\quad \frac{i_\star}N=\big(\gamma\sw2d^2\log^3\gamma\big)^{-1}\ (\alpha=1),\quad \frac{i_\star}N=\frac1{K_\alpha\gamma}\ (\alpha>1), \]
which tend to zero. A row sequence \(i\to\infty\) with \(i/i_\star\to0\) exists exactly when \(i_\star\to\infty\), and \(i=\lfloor\sqrt{i_\star}\rfloor\) realizes it; \(i=N\) realizes the other window. The condition \(i_\star\to\infty\) is (SG.35). For (iv), equating \(\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\) with \(d_\alpha\sqrt{c_NK_\alpha}N^{-1/2}i^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) gives \(i=i_{\mathrm c}/(\sw2d_\alpha^2)\), and equating \(1/\sw2\) with \(4K_\alpha^2i/((\alpha-1)B)\) gives \(i=B(\alpha-1)/(4\sw2K_\alpha^2)\); \(c_N\) cancels in both.
