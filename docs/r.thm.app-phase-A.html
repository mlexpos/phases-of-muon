Theorem SI.8 (The range \(\texp<1\))
Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(1-\alpha<\texp<1\) and \(T\ge4\). There are \(N_*\) and \(E_*\) and \(0<c_-\le c_+\), all depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\), such that for all \(N\ge N_*\), \(B\ge N\) and \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\) with \(\epsilon N^{p-1}\ge E_*\), and for both \(j\), the first hit \(\hat u_j\) is attained and
\[ c_-\le\frac{t^j_{T\epsilon}}{\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,\epsilon^{-1/(2r_j)}}\le c_+ . \]
In particular \(t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}\asymp\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}\epsilon^{-\alpha/[2(p-1)]}\) and \(t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}\asymp\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}\epsilon^{-\alpha/(p-1)}\). Every sequence with \(N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and \(\epsilon N^{p-1}\to\infty\) lies in this window for all large indices, uniformly over \(B\ge N\); for example \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<p-1\).
Proof of Theorem SI.8.
Here \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}=(p-1)/\alpha\in(0,1)\) and \(r_{\text{s-SGD}}=r_{\text{s-SVD}}/2<\frac12\). Take \(r_{0,j}=r_j\) for both models. For SignSGD put \(D_{\text{s-SGD}}\defeq1\) and \(\tau_{\text{s-SGD}}\defeq4^{-\alpha}\). For SignSVD put:
  • •
    if \(0<\alpha<2\): \(D_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq1\), \(\tau_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq\min\{4^{-q_{\text{s-SVD}}},\mathsf c_1\}\) and \(N_{\rm V}\defeq N_1\), with \(\mathsf c_1,N_1\) of Lemma SI.15 at \(r=r_{\text{s-SVD}}\);
  • •
    if \(\alpha=2\): \(D_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq1\), \(\tau_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq\frac14\) and \(N_{\rm V}\defeq\lceil\exp(A^{\log}_{r_{\text{s-SVD}}})\rceil\), with \(A^{\log}_r\) of Lemma SI.17;
  • •
    if \(\alpha>2\): \(\upsilon_{\rm V}\defeq2-2/\alpha>1\), \(K_{\rm V}\defeq2E_{\alpha,\alpha/2}\), \(D_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq D(K_{\rm V},r_{\text{s-SVD}},\upsilon_{\rm V})\) of (SI.55), \(\tau_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq4^{-\alpha/2}\) and \(N_{\rm V}\defeq4\).
Put \(N_*\defeq\max\{4,N_{\rm V}\}\) and
\[ E_*\defeq\max\Big\{1,\ C_{\text{s-SVD}}^2\Big(\frac{D_{\text{s-SVD}}}{\tau_{\text{s-SVD}}}\Big)^{2r_{\text{s-SVD}}},\ C_{\text{s-SGD}}^2\Big(\frac{D_{\text{s-SGD}}}{\tau_{\text{s-SGD}}}\Big)^{2r_{\text{s-SGD}}}\Big\} , \]
and let \(H_j\defeq D_j[(C_j/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_j}-1]\), which is positive since \(\epsilon<C_j^2\).
The horizons lie below the cutoff. Since \((p-1)/(2r_j)=q_j\) and \(C_j^2(D_j/\tau_j)^{2r_j}\le E_*\le\epsilon N^{p-1}\),
\[ H_j\le D_jC_j^{1/r_j}\epsilon^{-1/(2r_j)}\le\tau_jN^{q_j}\le(N/4)^{q_j} . \]
The convolution bound (a) of Lemma SI.9 holds on \([0,H_j]\). For SignSGD the kernel is decreasing with integral \(1\), so Lemma SI.13 gives \(g*h\le h/(1-r_{\text{s-SGD}})\le2h\) on all of \([0,\infty)\). For SignSVD with \(0<\alpha<2\), \(H_{\text{s-SVD}}\le\mathsf c_1N^{q_{\text{s-SVD}}}\) and Lemma SI.15 gives \(g*h\le\frac34h\). For \(\alpha=2\), Lemma SI.17(ii) gives, for \(u\le H_{\text{s-SVD}}\le N/4\),
\[ \frac{(g*h)(u)}{h(u)}\le\frac{\log(1+u)+A^{\log}_{r_{\text{s-SVD}}}}{H_N(1)}\le2 , \]
since \(H_N(1)\ge\log(N+1)\ge\log(1+u)\) and \(H_N(1)\ge\log N\ge A^{\log}_{r_{\text{s-SVD}}}\). For \(\alpha>2\), Lemma SI.7(i) and \(H_N(\alpha/2)\ge1\) give \(g(v)\le K_{\rm V}(1+v)^{-\upsilon_{\rm V}}\) for all \(v\), and Lemma SI.14 applies on every interval.
By Lemma SI.9, the first hit is attained with \(0<\hat u_j\le H_j\), and by (SI.51)
\[ \mathcal E_j\le D_jC_j^{1/r_j}\Big[\frac{\sqrt2}{1-r_j/2}+1\Big]\epsilon^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)} . \]
Lemma SI.10(i) applies with \(H=H_j\le(N/4)^{q_j}\). Its lower bound is a fixed multiple of \(\epsilon^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)}\). For its upper bound, \(1+H_j\le D_j(C_j/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_j}\) gives
\[ C_jI_{r_j}(H_j)\le\frac{C_j(1+H_j)^{1-r_j}}{1-r_j}\le\frac{D_j^{1-r_j}C_j^{1/r_j}}{1-r_j}\,\epsilon^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)} . \]
Hence \(\int_0^{\hat u_j}\sqrt{Y_j}\) is between two fixed multiples of \(\epsilon^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)}\). Multiplying by \(\mathcal S_jT_0^j/(2\sqrt\epsilon)\), by (SI.30), gives the order \(\mathcal S_jT_0^j\epsilon^{-1/(2r_j)}\), and \(1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})=\alpha/[2(p-1)]\), \(1/(2r_{\text{s-SGD}})=\alpha/(p-1)\).
All thresholds are fixed, so a sequence with \(N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and \(\epsilon N^{p-1}\to\infty\) meets them for large indices, whatever \(B\ge N\). For \(\epsilon=N^{-\omega}\), \(\epsilon N^{p-1}=N^{p-1-\omega}\to\infty\).
