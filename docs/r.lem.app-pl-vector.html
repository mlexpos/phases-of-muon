Lemma SG.18 (The flat response)
Along every such row sequence,
\[ \Big\|\mathcal V_i(g)-\frac{g}{\sqrt{\sw2}}-b\sign(g)\Big\|_{L^2(g)}=o(1+b), \]
and for fixed \(0<\delta<M\) the \(L^2(g)\) norm of the part of \(\mathcal V_i\) over \(\delta<m<M\) is \(o(1+b)\).
Proof.
The tail is an averaged integral. Put \(B_i^{[M]}\defeq\int_M^\gamma\mathcal J_i\,\dif m\). Since \(W_i\mathcal Z_g=\mathcal J_i\,(E\vartheta_N)\,\mathcal A_g\), the proof of Lemma SG.10, with \(\mathcal B_i\) replaced by \(\mathcal J_i\) and Lemma SG.2 replaced by Lemma SG.15(ii) with \(m_0=1\), whose constant has a finite second moment in \(g\), gives, through Minkowski’s integral inequality,
\[ \Big\|\frac1\pi\int_M^\gamma W_i\mathcal Z_g\,\dif m-B_i^{[M]}\sign(g)\Big\|_{L^2(g)}\le\frac CMB_i^{[M]}+o\big(B_i^{[M]}\big), \]
uniformly over the rows; here \(\mathcal A_g\) is bounded for \(m\ge M\) by Lemma SG.15(i). By Lemma SG.10 and (SG.45),
\[ B_i^{[M]}=\frac{\pi\gamma}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\gamma\mu_i}}\,a_N\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\,(1+o(1))=(1+o(1))\,\frac{\sqrt\gamma\,x^{\alpha/2}}{c_0}\,\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x), \]
using \(a_N/\sqrt{\mu_i}=x^{\alpha/2}\). This is \(b+o(1+b)\). Indeed, if \(b\to0\) positivity bounds it by \(b+o(1)\); if \(b\) is bounded below, then (SG.47) gives \(e^{T-\log\gamma}\le CT^2\), respectively \(CT^3\), with \(T\) of Lemma SG.12, so \(T\le\log\gamma+C\log\log\gamma\) and Lemma SG.12(iii) gives \(\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}\sim\Psi_\alpha\).
The head has a bounded norm. On \(m\le M\), \(\norm{\mathcal Z_g(Q_m)}_{L^2}\le\sqrt{Q_m}\le C_M\sqrt m\) and \(\vartheta_N\le C_M\Lambda_N'/\Lambda_N\), because \(1/E(m)\le C_M/m\le C_M\Lambda_N'/\Lambda_N\). Hence, with \(u=\mu_i\Lambda_N(m)\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.48} \Big\|\frac1\pi\int_0^MW_i\mathcal Z_g\,\dif m\Big\|_{L^2}\le C_M\int_0^M\frac{\sqrt{\mu_i\Lambda_N}}{1+\mu_i\Lambda_N}\frac{\Lambda_N'}{\Lambda_N}\,\dif m\le C_M\int_0^\infty\frac{\sqrt u}{1+u}\frac{\dif u}{u}=C_M\pi . \end{equation}
The head has a limit when \(a_i\defeq m_N(1/\mu_i)\to0\): then it tends to \(g/\sqrt{\sw2}\) in \(L^2\). The Riemann error of \(m_N(r/\mu_i)\) relative to \(a_i\) is at most \(1/(Ba_i)\le2/i\), because the first \(i\) terms of \(Ba_i\) are at least \(1/2\). With the small-\(V\) laws of Lemma SG.5, this gives, locally uniformly in \(r>0\),
\[ \mu_i\Lambda_N(a_ir)\to r,\qquad a_i\frac{\Lambda_N'}{\Lambda_N}(a_ir)\to\frac1r,\qquad Q_{a_ir}\sim\frac{a_ir}{\sw2},\qquad E(a_ir)\sim a_ir, \]
and \(\mathcal Z_g(Q_m)=g\sqrt{Q_m}\,(1+O(Q_mg^2))\), so \(\mathcal Z_g(Q_{a_ir})=g\sqrt{a_ir/\sw2}+o_{L^2}(\sqrt{a_i})\). With \(m=a_ir\) the head becomes
\[ \frac{g}{\pi\sqrt{\sw2}}\int_0^\infty\frac{\dif r}{\sqrt r\,(1+r)}=\frac{g}{\sqrt{\sw2}} . \]
The ranges of small and large \(r\) are controlled by (SG.48) in the variable \(u\), and on \(\delta<m<M\) one has \(\mu_i\Lambda_N(\delta)\to\infty\), as in the proof of Lemma SG.11, so the bound (SG.48) restricted to that range tends to zero.
The cases are combined by the size of \(b\). By (SG.47) and Lemma SG.5, \(a_i\sim\gamma x^\alpha/(1-\alpha)\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(a_i\sim\gamma x\log(1/x)\) for \(\alpha=1\), so \(a_i\asymp b^2/T^2\to0\) when \(b\) is bounded. Then head, middle and tail give the claim after \(M\to\infty\). If \(b\to\infty\), the head and \(g/\sqrt{\sw2}\) are \(O(1)=o(b)\). Passing to subsequences along which \(b\) converges in \([0,\infty]\) proves the first claim. For the middle range, Lemma SG.6(ii) and Lemma SG.5(iii) give \(\Lambda_N(m)\asymp\Lambda_N(1)\) on \([\delta,M]\), so with \(y\defeq\mu_i\Lambda_N(1)\) its norm is at most \(C_{\delta,M}\min(\sqrt y,y^{-1/2})\); this tends to zero unless \(y\) stays in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), and in that case \(a_i\asymp1\) and \(b^2\asymp a_iT^2\to\infty\).
□
