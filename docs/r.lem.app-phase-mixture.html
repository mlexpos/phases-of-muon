Lemma SI.11 (Finite exponential mixtures)
Let \(b_{\min}>0\), let
\[ f(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_ie^{-b_iu},\qquad g(u)=\sum_{l=1}^{n'}w_l\,c_l\,e^{-c_lu}, \]
with \(a_i\ge0\), \(F_0\defeq\sum_ia_i>0\), \(b_i,c_l\ge b_{\min}\), \(w_l\ge0\) and \(\sum_lw_l=1\). Let \(T\ge4\), \(0<\epsilon<F_0/T\), and let \(Y>0\) be the continuous solution of \(Y=f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt Y\). Put
\[ \mathsf J\defeq\int_0^\infty\sqrt{f(u)}\,\dif u,\qquad H\defeq\frac2{b_{\min}}\log\frac{F_0}\epsilon,\qquad\mathsf d\defeq\sqrt\epsilon\,H . \]
Then \(0<\mathsf J\le2\sqrt{F_0}/b_{\min}\), the first \(T\epsilon\) hit \(\hat u\) satisfies \(0<\hat u\le H\) and \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\), and
\[ \mathsf J-\frac{2\sqrt{T\epsilon}}{b_{\min}}\le\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\le\mathsf J+2\sqrt{\mathsf d\,\mathsf J}+\mathsf d . \]
Proof.
The equation has a unique positive continuous solution by Theorem SI.1, applied with the rates \(b_i\) and \(c_l\) and zero coefficients where needed. Since \(f\le F_0e^{-b_{\min}u}\), the integral \(\mathsf J\) is positive and at most \(2\sqrt{F_0}/b_{\min}\).
Let \(h(u)\defeq\sqrt{F_0}\,e^{-b_{\min}u/4}\), so that \(f\le h^2\) and \(h(H)=\sqrt\epsilon\). Integrating each exponential gives
\[ \frac{(g*h)(u)}{h(u)}=\sum_lw_l\,\frac{c_l}{c_l-b_{\min}/4}\big(1-e^{-(c_l-b_{\min}/4)u}\big)\le\frac43 . \]
Hence \(U\defeq(h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) satisfies
\[ f+\sqrt\epsilon\,g*\sqrt U\le h^2+\tfrac43\sqrt\epsilon\,h+\epsilon\le U , \]
and Lemma SI.3 with \(m=\epsilon\) gives \(Y\le U\) on every compact interval. At \(H\), \(Y(H)\le4\epsilon\le T\epsilon\), while \(Y(0)=F_0>T\epsilon\), so the first hit is attained in \((0,H]\) with \(Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\).
For the lower bound, all rates are at least \(b_{\min}\), so \(f(v)\le f(\hat u)e^{-b_{\min}(v-\hat u)}\) for \(v\ge\hat u\), and \(f(\hat u)\le Y(\hat u)=T\epsilon\). Hence
\[ \int_{\hat u}^\infty\sqrt f\,\dif v\le\frac{2\sqrt{T\epsilon}}{b_{\min}},\qquad \int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\,\dif u\ge\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt f\,\dif u\ge\mathsf J-\frac{2\sqrt{T\epsilon}}{b_{\min}} . \]
For the upper bound, write \(\mathsf I\defeq\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt Y\). The inequality \(\sqrt{x+y}\le\sqrt x+\sqrt y\) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give
\[ \mathsf I\le\mathsf J+\epsilon^{1/4}\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt{(g*\sqrt Y)(u)}\,\dif u\le\mathsf J+\epsilon^{1/4}\Big(\hat u\int_0^{\hat u}(g*\sqrt Y)(u)\,\dif u\Big)^{1/2} . \]
Exchanging the order of integration and using \(\int g=1\),
\[ \int_0^{\hat u}(g*\sqrt Y)(u)\,\dif u=\int_0^{\hat u}\sqrt{Y(v)}\int_0^{\hat u-v}g(w)\,\dif w\,\dif v\le\mathsf I . \]
Since \(\sqrt\epsilon\,\hat u\le\mathsf d\), this gives \(\mathsf I\le\mathsf J+\sqrt{\mathsf d\,\mathsf I}\). The quadratic \(x^2-\sqrt{\mathsf d}\,x-\mathsf J\) is increasing beyond its positive root and nonnegative at \(x=\sqrt{\mathsf J}+\sqrt{\mathsf d}\), so \(\sqrt{\mathsf I}\le\sqrt{\mathsf J}+\sqrt{\mathsf d}\). Squaring proves the upper bound.
□
