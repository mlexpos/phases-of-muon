Proposition SF.5 (The zero-ridge constant)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) and \(\gin,\gout>0\). Put \(K_0\defeq1\), and for \(\bmom>0\) let \(K_\bmom\) be the unique \(q>0\) with \(q\Fw(q)=\min(\gin,\gout)\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(t>0\),
    \[ 0<q(-t)\,\Lw\big(q(-t)\big)\le\min\Big(K_\bmom,\frac{\gin\gout}{t}\Big), \]
    so the integral (SF.21) converges and \(0<C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)<\infty\). Moreover \(C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)=C_\bmom(\gout,\gin)\), and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.23} C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\,q(-t)\,\Lw\big(q(-t)\big)\,\dif t . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) be the kernel (SF.10) at momentum \(\bmom\), for the measures (SF.1). Then, at this fixed \(\bmom\), \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\to C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\).
Proof.
For part (i), \(K_\bmom\) is well defined: for \(\bmom>0\) every EMA weight is positive, so by Lemma SB.1(iii) the map \(q\mapsto q\Fw(q)\) is a continuous strictly increasing bijection of \([0,\infty)\). The integrand is positive because \(q(-t)>0\) by Proposition SF.1(ii) and \(\Lw>0\). By Lemma SB.1(i), \(\Jfun\le1\), so
\[ \Lw(q)=\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j\,\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\le\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j=1,\qquad q\,\Lw(q)\le q . \]
The first identity of (SF.5) gives \(\sigma(-t)\le\gout/t\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\le\gin/t\), so that
\[ q(-t)=t\,\sigma(-t)\,\tilde\sigma(-t)\le\frac{\gin\gout}{t}. \]
At \(\bmom=0\), \(q\Jfun(q)=\Phi(q)<1=K_0\) by Lemma SF.1(ii). For \(\bmom>0\), Proposition SF.1(ii) gives \(q(-t)\Fw(q(-t))<\min(\gin,\gout)=K_\bmom\Fw(K_\bmom)\), so \(q(-t)<K_\bmom\) by monotonicity, and \(q\Lw(q)\le q<K_\bmom\). The integrand of (SF.21) is therefore at most \(\min(K_\bmom,\gin\gout/u^2)\). The equation (SF.7) is symmetric in \((\gin,\gout)\) for every \(\bmom\), so \(q(-t)\) is unchanged when the two ratios are exchanged, which gives the symmetry. The substitution \(t=u^2\) gives (SF.23).
Part (ii) is a limit under the integral. By Theorem SF.1(i),
\[ \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty q(-\ridge-u^2)\,\Lw\big(q(-\ridge-u^2)\big)\,du . \]
The function \(t\mapsto q(-t)\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\) by Theorem SB.1(iv), and \(\Lw\) is continuous by Lemma SB.1(ii), so the integrand converges to that of (SF.21) for every \(u>0\). By part (i) it is at most \(\min(K_\bmom,\gin\gout/u^2)\). Dominated convergence gives the limit.
□
