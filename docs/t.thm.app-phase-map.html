Proved in LeanTheorem numbering in the paper: Theorem M.3
The low-exponent window (SI.33) is the set of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(N\ge4\), \(B\ge N\), \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\) and, for both \(j\),
\[ k_{N,j}\,C_j\,I_{r_j}\big(H_j(\epsilon)\big)\le\sqrt\epsilon,\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_j(\epsilon)\le(N/4)^{q_j}\ \text{ if }r_j\le1, \]
where \(\epsilon_0\) is a fixed constant depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\), \(C_j\) is the constant of the upper power-law bound on the noiseless risk of model \(j\) (Lemma SI.7), depending only on \(\alpha\) and \(\texp\), \(H_j(\epsilon)=(C_j/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r_j}-1\), \(I_r(x)=\int_0^x(1+w)^{-r}\,\dif w\), and \(k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}=2H_N(\alpha)/H_N(\alpha/2)\) and \(k_{N,\text{s-SGD}}=2(1-\frac2\pi)H_N(\alpha)/N+4H_N(2\alpha)/(\pi H_N(\alpha))\) are the heights of the rescaled noise kernels ((SI.29)).
Theorem SI.7 (Time to \(T\epsilon\) in the power-law phases)
Fix \(\alpha>0\) and \(\texp\) with \(\alpha+\texp>1\) and \(\texp\notin\{1,\alpha+1\}\), and fix \(T\ge4\). Consider the arrays of Definition SI.3 at their matched steps along a sequence of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and integers \(B\ge N\), such that
  • •
    \(\epsilon N^{\alpha+\texp-1}\to\infty\) if \(\texp<1\);
  • •
    \(\epsilon N\to\infty\) if \(\texp>1\) and \(\alpha>1\);
  • •
    \((N,B,\epsilon)\) lies in the low-exponent window (SI.33) for all large indices if \(\texp>1\) and \(\alpha\le1\).
Then there are constants \(0<c_-\le c_+\), depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\), such that for all large indices of the sequence, with \(x\asymp y\) meaning \(c_-\le x/y\le c_+\),
\begin{align} t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\gamma\,H_N(\alpha/2)\,\epsilon^{-\alpha/[2(\alpha+\texp-1)]}\ \ (\texp<1),& t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\sqrt\gamma\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\ \ (\texp>1),\tag{SI.35}\\ t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\frac{N^2\pi\bar\mu}B\,\epsilon^{-\alpha/(\alpha+\texp-1)}\ \ (\texp<\alpha+1),& t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}&\asymp\sqrt{\frac{\pi\bar\mu}B}\,\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\ \ (\texp>\alpha+1).\tag{SI.36} \end{align}
Moreover \(H_N(\alpha/2)\asymp N^{1-\alpha/2}\) for \(\alpha<2\), \(H_N(\alpha/2)\asymp\log N\) for \(\alpha=2\), and \(H_N(\alpha/2)\asymp1\) for \(\alpha>2\), with constants depending only on \(\alpha\).

Definitions and assumptions used

Definition SI.3 (Adopted power-law arrays)
Definition SI.3 (Adopted power-law arrays)
Fix \(\alpha>0\) and \(\texp\in\R\) with \(p\defeq\alpha+\texp>1\), and integers \(B\ge N\ge1\). Put \(\gamma\defeq N/B\in(0,1]\) and \(\bar\mu\defeq H_N(\alpha)/N\), and for \(1\le i\le N\) let \(\mu_i\defeq i^{-\alpha}\) and \(\fQ_i(0)\defeq i^{-\texp}\). The SignSVD and SignSGD arrays are
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.25} \begin{aligned} \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}&\defeq\gamma^{-1/2}i^{-\alpha/2}=\sqrt{\mu_i}\sqrt{B/N},&\qquad \mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SVD}}&\defeq1,\\ \mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SGD}}&\defeq\mu_i\sqrt{\frac B{\pi\bar\mu}},&\qquad \mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}&\defeq N\Big[1+\frac2\pi\Big(\frac{\mu_i}{\bar\mu}-1\Big)\Big]. \end{aligned} \end{equation}
For \(j\in\{\text{s-SVD},\text{s-SGD}\}\), the time scales, rate exponents and forcing exponents are
\[ T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq\sqrt\gamma,\quad T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}\defeq\sqrt{\frac{\pi\bar\mu}B},\qquad q_{\text{s-SVD}}\defeq\frac\alpha2,\quad q_{\text{s-SGD}}\defeq\alpha,\qquad r_j\defeq r_{q_j}=\frac{p-1}{2q_j} . \]
For \(\epsilon>0\) each model is run at its matched step \(\lr^\star_j\defeq2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S_j\). Its risk \(Q_j\) solves (SI.4) with forcing \(F_j\) and kernel \(k_j\), and its dimensionless risk, kernel and first hit at a fixed multiplier \(T\) are
\[ Y_j(u)\defeq Q_j(T_0^ju),\qquad g_j(u)\defeq\frac{T_0^j\,k_j(T_0^ju)}{\sqrt\epsilon},\qquad \hat u_j\defeq\inf\{u\ge0:\ Y_j(u)\le T\epsilon\} . \]
This theorem supplies the exponents of Theorem 5.1: the exponent of \(1/\epsilon\) in each display is \(e_{\text{Muon}}\) or \(e_{\text{s-SGD}}\) of (18). Like every statement of this chapter, it concerns the model with the adopted arrays at the matched step, not the algorithm. Because the Muon drift scales like \(\sqrt{\mu_i}\) and the SignSGD drift like \(\mu_i\), the time saturates at \(\epsilon^{-1/2}\) beyond \(\texp=1\) for SignSVD but only beyond \(\texp=\alpha+1\) for SignSGD. The prefactors carry all the dependence on \(N\) and \(B\), and their ratio is \(D_N\) of (17). The limit is joint in \(N\) and \(\epsilon\). For \(\texp<1\) the window keeps \(\epsilon\) above the accuracy scale \(\epsilon_N\). At fixed \(N\) the exponent is \(\frac12\) in every phase (Corollary SI.2), and Theorem SI.13 treats \(\epsilon/\epsilon_N\to0\).
Proof sketch.
The risk of the model solves a scalar equation once time is measured in the right units. In the row equations the drift of every row is divided by \(\sqrt{\fRisk}\), so the natural time is the accumulated effective step \(s=\clock(t)=\int_0^t\lr\,\fRisk(t')^{-1/2}\,\dif t'\) ((SI.8)). In this time each row solves a linear equation driven by \(\sqrt Q\), and summing the rows with weights \(\mu_i/2\) gives exactly
\[ \begin{gathered} Q(s)=F(s)+\int_0^sk(s-v)\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v,\\ F(s)=\tfrac12\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i(0)e^{-2\mathfrak d_is},\qquad k(s)=\tfrac\lr2\sum_i\mu_i\mathfrak v_ie^{-2\mathfrak d_is}, \end{gathered} \]
where \(Q(s)\) is the risk at time \(s\) (Theorem SI.1 and Theorem SI.2). The forcing \(F\) is the risk the model would have without noise, and the kernel \(k\) feeds the volatility back in. The equation has a unique solution, and its limit is \(\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4\), which equals \(\epsilon\) at the matched step.
After a change of time scale, the time to \(T\epsilon\) factors into a prefactor that carries \(N\) and \(B\) and a dimensionless integral. The time scale \(T_0^j\) of Definition SI.3 makes the rescaled decay rates \(2\mathfrak d_iT_0^j\) equal to \(2i^{-q_j}\). The rescaled risk \(Y_j(u)=Q_j(T_0^ju)\) then solves (Proposition SI.4)
\[ Y_j=f_{N,q_j}+\sqrt\epsilon\,g_j*\sqrt{Y_j},\qquad f_{N,q}(u)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^Ni^{-(\alpha+\texp)}e^{-2ui^{-q}}, \]
where \(f_{N,q}\) is the rescaled noiseless risk and \(g_j\) is a kernel of total mass one. Converting back to step counts, the first time \(\hat u_j\) at which \(Y_j\) reaches \(T\epsilon\) gives exactly ((SI.30) and (SI.26))
\[ t^j_{T\epsilon}=\frac{\mathcal S_jT_0^j}{2\sqrt\epsilon}\int_0^{\hat u_j}\sqrt{Y_j(u)}\,\dif u,\qquad \mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}=\frac\gamma2H_N(\alpha/2),\qquad \mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}=\frac{\pi N^2\bar\mu}{2B}. \]
The phase is decided by whether the square root of the noiseless risk is integrable. The forcing is squeezed between two power laws (Lemma SI.7), the lower one valid for \(N\ge4\) and \(u\le(N/4)^q\):
\[ A_p\,(1+u)^{-2r_q}\le f_{N,q}(u)\le C_{p,q}^2\,(1+u)^{-2r_q},\qquad r_q=\frac{p-1}{2q}. \]
Thus \(r_j=r_{q_j}\). If \(r_j>1\), then \(\sqrt{f_{N,q_j}}\) is integrable uniformly in \(N\) ((SI.43)), and the integral in the time formula is of order one. If \(r_j<1\), the integral keeps growing until the first passage. At that time \(f_{N,q}(\hat u)\le T\epsilon\). The lower power law then bounds the first passage from below,
\[ 1+\hat u\ge\Big(\frac{A_p}{T\epsilon}\Big)^{1/(2r)}, \]
and the integral is of order \(\epsilon^{-(1-r)/(2r)}\) (Lemma SI.10). With the prefactor this gives
\[ \begin{gathered} t^j_{T\epsilon}\asymp\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,\epsilon^{-1/(2r_j)}\ \ (r_j<1),\qquad t^j_{T\epsilon}\asymp\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\ \ (r_j>1),\\ \frac1{2r_{\text{s-SVD}}}=\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha+\texp-1)},\quad \frac1{2r_{\text{s-SGD}}}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\texp-1}. \end{gathered} \]
The boundary \(r_j=1\) is the line \(\texp=1\) for SignSVD and \(\texp=\alpha+1\) for SignSGD, because \(q_{\text{s-SVD}}\) is half of \(q_{\text{s-SGD}}\).
The noise term is controlled by comparison with a function of the form \((h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\), and this comparison is where the windows come from. Any function that dominates the right side of the rescaled equation when inserted into it lies above \(Y_j\) (Lemma SI.3). Take for \(h\) a power-law envelope of \(\sqrt{f_{N,q_j}}\) that decreases to \(\sqrt\epsilon\) at a horizon \(H\). Then \((h+\sqrt\epsilon)^2\) dominates as soon as the kernel does not amplify the envelope, that is, \(g_j*h\le2h\) or \(g_j*h\le\sqrt\epsilon\) on \([0,H]\) (Lemma SI.9). This places the first passage before \(H\), which supplies the upper bound in the previous paragraph. It also bounds the noise part of the integral by a fixed multiple of \(\epsilon^{(r_0-1)/(2r_0)}\), where \(r_0\le r_j\) is the exponent of the envelope and \(r_0>1\) exactly when \(r_j>1\) ((SI.51)). For \(r_j<1\) this bound is of the order of the main term, and the theorems give orders and not equivalents for these models; for \(r_j>1\) it tends to zero.
The kernel condition holds in each range of exponents, and checking it is what fixes the windows. For \(\texp<1\) (Theorem SI.8) the SignSGD kernel is decreasing with mass one, so \(g_j*h\le h/(1-r_j)\) (Lemma SI.13), which is at most \(2h\) because here \(r_{\text{s-SGD}}=r_{\text{s-SVD}}/2<\frac12\). The SignSVD kernel, normalized by \(H_N(\alpha/2)\), has small mass up to times of order \(N^{\alpha/2}\) when \(\alpha<2\) (Lemma SI.15), a logarithmic substitute at \(\alpha=2\) (Lemma SI.17) and an integrable tail when \(\alpha>2\) (Lemma SI.14). The horizon is then \(H_j\asymp\epsilon^{-1/(2r_j)}\), and since \(2r_jq_j=\alpha+\texp-1\) the window \(\epsilon N^{\alpha+\texp-1}\ge E_*\), with \(E_*\) a fixed threshold, keeps \(H_j\) below \((N/4)^{q_j}\), where the lower power law holds. For \(\alpha>1\) and \(\texp\ge1\) (Theorem SI.9) the kernels have integrable power tails (Lemma SI.14), except the SignSVD kernel for \(1<\alpha\le2\). For \(1<\alpha<2\) the normalization \(H_N(\alpha/2)\ge N^{1-\alpha/2}\) makes the late part of the convolution small up to the horizon \(\sqrt N\), which the window \(\epsilon N\ge E_*\) guarantees (Lemma SI.16). At \(\alpha=2\), Lemma SI.18 adds a correction term to the comparison function, needs no relation between \(N\) and \(\epsilon\), and bounds the noise part by a fixed constant that tends to zero as \(N\to\infty\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). For \(\alpha\le1\) and \(\texp\ge1\) (Theorem SI.10) no tail is usable, but the kernels are decreasing with maxima \(k_{N,j}=g_j(0)\to0\) (Proposition SI.5), so
\[ (g_j*h_j)(u)\le k_{N,j}\,C_j\,I_{r_j}(H_j)\le\sqrt\epsilon\qquad(0\le u\le H_j), \]
where \(C_j=C_{p,q_j}\) and the second inequality is the low-exponent window (SI.33). Elementary harmonic-sum bounds give the orders of \(H_N(\alpha/2)\) in the last assertion (Lemma SI.8).
