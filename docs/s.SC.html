This section identifies the drift kernel \(\mathfrak d_{ij}\) of the main text. In the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3, the expected regularized update \(\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\) has a deterministic equivalent in unnormalized Frobenius norm, uniformly in the normalized ridge \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\), and its projection on the eigenpair \((u_i,v_j)\) is \(\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)\,(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)/\sqrt{\cR(t)}\) with an explicit integral for the kernel function \(\mathfrak d\). The section has two random-matrix theorems. Theorem SC.1 proves that the expected mixed resolvent \(\EE[R(-s)\Mt]\) converges in Frobenius norm to the candidate \(T(s)\) built from the branch of Theorem SB.1, uniformly on compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\), and that it satisfies a dimension-independent bound of order \(1/s\) for \(s\ge1\). Theorem SC.2 integrates the first statement along the negative axis, with the tail bound controlling large \(s\), and identifies the expected update. Corollary SC.1 reads the result in the eigenbases and defines \(\mathfrak d\); Proposition SC.2 records the one-step risk identity in which the drift enters.
The section works in the setting of Section SA: the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3 under Assumption S3, the normalized state \(\Ahat\) of (SA.10), the EMA weights (SA.18), the Gram resolvents (SA.36), the integral representation Lemma SA.3, the residual law Lemma SA.7, and the conventions of Section SA.1. From Section SB it takes the branch \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)(-s)\) and the deterministic equivalents \(\Rbar(-s)=(\tilde s_1(-s)\Sigmaout+s\Id)^{-1}\) and \(\Rtbar(-s)=(s_1(-s)\Sigmain+s\Id)^{-1}\) of Definition SB.1, the source factor \(\Lw(q)=\sum_j\emaw j\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\) of (SB.1), the Gram closure Theorem SB.2 with its trace form Corollary SB.1, and the finite identities of Section SB.2: the notation \(R_s(M)\), \(\widetilde R_s(M)\), \(\mathsf X_s(M)=R_s(M)M\) of (SB.10), the sample decomposition (SB.26) of the buffer, the rank-two deletion Lemma SB.4 with its coefficients \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) of (SB.13), the coefficient and moment bounds Lemma SB.5 and Lemma SB.6, the Bessel bounds Lemma SB.7, the Gaussian response Lemma SB.8, the replacement of the deletion coefficients Lemma SB.12, and the uniform Gaussian limit Lemma SB.13. As there, for a sample \((j,a)\) we write \(M_0^{(ja)}=\Mt-\mathsf h_{ja}\,\mathsf u_{ja}\tensor\mathsf v_{ja}\) for the buffer with that sample deleted, \(R_0^{(ja)}=R_s(M_0^{(ja)})\), \(\widetilde R_0^{(ja)}\) and \(\mathsf X_0^{(ja)}\) accordingly, \(\mathcal G_{ja}\) for the \(\sigma\)-field generated by all samples other than \((j,a)\), and \(\alpha_{1,ja},\alpha_{2,ja},\alpha_{3,ja}\) for the coefficients of Lemma SB.4 with \((M_0,\mathsf h,\mathsf u,\mathsf v)=(M_0^{(ja)},\mathsf h_{ja},\mathsf u_{ja},\mathsf v_{ja})\); samples of one age are exchangeable, and the index \(a\) is dropped when only the law matters. The expected resolvents \(\mathcal P(s)=\EE R(-s)\), \(\mathcal P_j(s)=\EE R_0^{(j1)}\), the expected traces \(\sigma_B(s)\), \(\tilde\sigma_B(s)\) and the product \(q_B(s)=s\sigma_B\tilde\sigma_B\) are those of (SB.30). Constants: for a compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) with \(s_-=\min I\), \(C_I\) denotes a constant depending only on \((I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\); a constant \(C\) without subscript depends only on \((C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\); both may change from line to line.
The statements of this section hold for the frozen-state model with any fixed weight sequence \(w=(\emaw j)_{j\ge0}\) in the class
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.1} 0\le\emaw j\le1\quad(j\ge0),\qquad \sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j\le1,\qquad \emaw j>0\ \text{for some }j , \end{equation}
in place of the EMA weights, with \(\Fw\), \(\Lw\), \(\sw p\), the branch of Theorem SB.1 and the candidates below computed for \(w\); every proof uses the weights only through (SC.1). This is the weight class (SB.27) for which Theorem SB.2, Corollary SB.1 and the lemmas of Section SB.3 are stated. The EMA weights belong to it by (SA.18) and are the case of interest; statements at \(\bmom=0\) concern the EMA weights at \(\bmom=0\), that is, \(w=(1,0,0,\dots)\).

SC.1 The candidates and the main statements

For \(s>0\) the expected mixed resolvent of the frozen-state model and its candidate are
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.2} \mathcal H(s)\defeq\EE\big[R(-s)\Mt\big]=\EE\,\mathsf X_s(\Mt),\qquad T(s)\defeq s\,\Lw\big(q(-s)\big)\,\Rbar(-s)\,\frac{\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}}\,\Rtbar(-s), \end{equation}
where \(\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain/\sqrt{2\cR(t)}=\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Ahat\Sigmain^{1/2}=\EE\Gt\) by (SA.11). For \(z\) in the sector \(\mathbb S\) of (SB.11) we write likewise \(\mathcal H(z)\defeq\EE\,\mathsf X_z(\Mt)\). The candidate for the expected update is the integral of \(T\) along the negative axis,
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.3} \Gtildebar_\ridge\defeq\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty T(\ridge+u^2)\,du\qquad(\ridge>0), \end{equation}
which Theorem SC.2 shows to exist. In the eigenbases of the covariances the candidate is diagonal, which motivates the kernel function
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.4} \mathfrak d(\mu,\lambda)\defeq\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{z\,\Lw(q(-z))\,\mu\lambda}{\big(z+\tilde s_1(-z)\mu\big)\big(z+s_1(-z)\lambda\big)}\,du, \qquad z=\ridge+u^2,\quad \mu,\lambda\ge0 . \end{equation}
The branch in (SC.4) is the one of the full spectral measures (SA.41), not a branch recomputed at \((\mu,\lambda)\); the function \(\mathfrak d\) therefore depends on the dimension, on \(\ridge\) and on \(\bmom\), and this dependence is suppressed in the notation. The main-text kernel is \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)\) (Corollary SC.1).
Theorem SC.1 (Mixed resolvent closure and tail bound)
  1. (i)
    Under Assumption S3, for every compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.5} \sup_{s\in I}\norm{\mathcal H(s)-T(s)}_\F\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    In the frozen-state model under Assumption S1, with \(\max(\Nout,\allowbreak\Nin)\le\gamma_\star B\), \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\allowbreak\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\), \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\) and weights satisfying (SC.1), but without any condition on \(\norm{\Ahat}\) and for every finite dimension, there is \(C\) depending only on \((C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\) such that
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.6} \norm{\mathcal H(s)}_\F\le\frac{C}{s}\quad(s\ge1),\qquad \norm{T(s)}_\F\le\frac{C_\Sigma}{s}\quad(s>0). \end{equation}
    Moreover, for every \(s_\circ>0\) there is \(C_\circ\) depending only on \((s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\) such that
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.7} \norm{\mathcal H(z)}_\F\le\frac{C_\circ}{\Ree z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb S,\ \Ree z\ge s_\circ). \end{equation}
Theorem SC.2 (Regularized mean)
Under Assumption S3, the integral (SC.3) converges absolutely in Frobenius norm, with \(\norm{\Gtildebar_\ridge}_\F\le C_\Sigma/\sqrt\ridge\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.8} \varepsilon_B\defeq\sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\norm{\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)-\Gtildebar_\ridge}_\F\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
Consequently, for every deterministic \(V\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.9} \sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\big|\big\langle V,\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)-\Gtildebar_\ridge\big\rangle\big|\le\norm V_\F\,\varepsilon_B, \qquad \langle V,W\rangle\defeq\Tr(VW^\T). \end{equation}
Proposition SC.1 (History truncation)
Under Assumption S3, for \(J\ge J_w\defeq\min\{j:\emaw j>0\}\) let \(\emaw j^{(J)}\defeq\emaw j\mathbf 1_{j\le J}\), let \(M^{(J)}=\sum_{j\le J}\emaw jG_{t+1-j}\) be the truncated buffer of Lemma SA.6, and let \(T^{(J)}\) and \(\Gtildebar^{(J)}_\ridge\) be the candidates (SC.2) and (SC.3) built from the solution of the system (SB.2) for the weights \(\emaw{}^{(J)}\).
  1. (i)
    For every fixed \(J\), \(\sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\norm{\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(M^{(J)})-\Gtildebar^{(J)}_\ridge}_\F\to0\) as the dimension grows.
  2. (ii)
    \(\lim_{J\to\infty}\sup_B\sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\norm{\Gtildebar_\ridge-\Gtildebar^{(J)}_\ridge}_\F=0\), where \(\sup_B\) runs over the whole sequence of dimensions.
  3. (iii)
    Consequently
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.10} \lim_{J\to\infty}\limsup_{B\to\infty}\sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]} \norm{\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)-\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(M^{(J)})}_\F=0 . \end{equation}
The order of the limits in (SC.10) is part of the statement: the dimension limit is taken first at fixed history length \(J\), and \(J\to\infty\) afterwards. No rate in \(J\) at fixed dimension is asserted, and the finite-dimensional bound of Lemma SA.6(ii), whose right side grows with the dimension, is not used.
Corollary SC.1 (The drift kernel)
Under Assumption S3, let \(\Gtilde=\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\) be the frozen-state update and put \((E_\ridge)_{ij}\defeq u_i^\T\big(\EE\Gtilde-\Gtildebar_\ridge\big)v_j\).
  1. (i)
    For every pair \((i,j)\) and every \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.11} \EE\big[u_i^\T\Gtilde v_j\big]=\frac{\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}}\,(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)+(E_\ridge)_{ij} =\frac{\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)+(E_\ridge)_{ij}, \end{equation}
    and \(\sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\big(\sum_{i,j}(E_\ridge)_{ij}^2\big)^{1/2}=\varepsilon_B\to0\). Consequently the frozen-state drift (SA.19) of the update \(\Gtilde\) is
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.12} \cD_{ij}(t)=\frac{\mathfrak d_{ij}}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,\CMscr Q_{ij}(t)+(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)(E_\ridge)_{ij}, \qquad \mathfrak d_{ij}\defeq\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j), \end{equation}
    and for every bounded function \(\phi\) on \([0,\infty)^2\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.13} \Big|\sum_{i,j}\phi(\mu_i,\lambda_j)\,(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)\,(E_\ridge)_{ij}\Big|\le\norm\phi_\infty\,\norm{\Dt}_\F\,\varepsilon_B . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    In the frozen-state model with \(\max(\Nout,\Nin)\le\gamma_\star B\) and \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\), at every finite dimension and for every \(\ridge>0\), the function \(\mathfrak d\) is continuous on \([0,\infty)^2\), vanishes when \(\mu=0\) or \(\lambda=0\), and satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.14} 0\le\mathfrak d(\mu,\lambda)\le\frac{\mu\lambda}{\sqrt{2\ridge}},\qquad \abs{\partial_\mu\mathfrak d(\mu,\lambda)}\le\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2\ridge}},\qquad \abs{\partial_\lambda\mathfrak d(\mu,\lambda)}\le\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{2\ridge}}, \end{equation}
    with one-sided derivatives at \(0\); in particular, for \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\), \(\mathfrak d\) is Lipschitz on \([0,C_\Sigma]^2\) with constant \(C_\Sigma/\sqrt{2\ridge_-}\) in each variable.
In the letters of (6), the first term of (SC.12) is \(\fDrift_{ij}=\fQ_{ij}\,\mathfrak d_{ij}/\sqrt{\fRisk}\): the drift of the mode \((i,j)\) is the projected risk times the kernel, divided by the square root of the risk. The factor \(\sqrt2\) in the first form of (SC.11) is the normalization of (SC.4), chosen so that the second form carries no constant.
Proposition SC.2 (One-step risk drift in the frozen state)
Under Assumption S3, let \(\lr\ge0\) be deterministic and put \(\Delta^+\defeq\Dt-\lr\,\Gtilde\) with \(\Gtilde=\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.15} \EE\big[\cR(\Delta^+)\big]-\cR(t)=-\lr\,\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\big\langle\EE\Gt,\EE\Gtilde\big\rangle+\frac{\lr^2}{2}\,\EE\norm{\Gtilde}_\Sigma^2 , \end{equation}
and the linear term satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.16} \Big|\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\big\langle\EE\Gt,\EE\Gtilde\big\rangle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\,\mathfrak d_{ij}\,\CMscr Q_{ij}(t)\Big| \le\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,C_\Sigma\,\varepsilon_B . \end{equation}
Proposition SC.2 is a statement about one step from a frozen state: the history of the buffer is computed at \(\Dt\), and the risk \(\cR(t)\) is a fixed number. The replacement error \(\lr\sqrt{2\cR(t)}C_\Sigma\varepsilon_B\) in the linear term is small relative to the drift scale \(\lr\sqrt{\cR(t)}\) only; it need not be small in absolute terms along a sequence of physical states whose risk grows. The quadratic term is an exact second moment, bounded by \(C_\Sigma^2\min(\Nout,\Nin)\lr^2/2\) through Lemma SA.2(i), and its deterministic equivalent is the subject of the volatility section.

SC.2 Finite identities and bounds

The closure is proved by deleting one sample from the buffer, as the Gram closure was. Two further consequences of Lemma SB.4 are needed: the action of the full resolvent on the deleted feature, and the change of the mixed resolvent under the deletion.
The lemmas of this subsection are finite-dimensional. They hold at every dimension in the frozen-state model with weights in (SC.1), \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\), \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\max(\Nout,\Nin)\le\gamma_\star B\), with no condition on \(\norm{\Ahat}\), and their constants depend only on the quantities named in each statement.
Lemma SC.1 (Deletion identities for the mixed resolvent)
In the setting of Lemma SB.4, with \(\mathsf X\defeq\mathsf X_s(M)\),
\begin{align} \mathsf h\,R\mathsf u&=\alpha_1\,R_0\mathsf u-\alpha_2\,\mathsf X_0\mathsf v ,\tag{SC.17}\\ \mathsf X-\mathsf X_0&=\alpha_3\,R_0(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf u)\mathsf X_0+s\,\alpha_1\,R_0(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v)\widetilde R_0 -\alpha_1\,\mathsf X_0(\mathsf v\tensor\mathsf u)\mathsf X_0-s\,\alpha_2\,\mathsf X_0(\mathsf v\tensor\mathsf v)\widetilde R_0 . \tag{SC.18} \end{align}
Both identities hold at \(z\in\mathbb S\) in place of \(s\), with the quantities of Lemma SB.4 at \(z\) and \(\mathsf X\defeq\mathsf X_z(M)\).
Proof
Proof.
The first identity is (SB.14) applied to \(\mathsf u\). Multiplying (SB.14) on the right by \(\mathsf u\) and using the definitions (SB.12) of \(\mathsf U\) and \(\mathsf C\),
\[ R\mathsf u=R_0\mathsf u\,\big(1+\alpha_3\mathsf U-\alpha_1\mathsf C\big)+\mathsf X_0\mathsf v\,\big(-\alpha_1\mathsf U+\alpha_2\mathsf C\big). \]
The two scalar coefficients simplify by the definitions (SB.13):
\[ 1+\alpha_3\mathsf U-\alpha_1\mathsf C=\frac{\Lambda-s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V-\mathsf h\mathsf C(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)}{\Lambda} =\frac{(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2-\mathsf h\mathsf C(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)}{\Lambda}=\frac{1+\mathsf h\mathsf C}{\Lambda}, \]
\[ -\alpha_1\mathsf U+\alpha_2\mathsf C=\frac{-\mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)\mathsf U+\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf C}{\Lambda}=-\frac{\mathsf h\mathsf U}{\Lambda}. \]
Multiplying by \(\mathsf h\) gives \(\mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)/\Lambda=\alpha_1\) and \(-\mathsf h^2\mathsf U/\Lambda=-\alpha_2\), which is (SC.17).
The second identity follows from the first and from (SB.14). Since \(M=M_0+\mathsf h\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v\),
\[ \mathsf X=RM=RM_0+(\mathsf hR\mathsf u)\tensor\mathsf v=\mathsf X_0+(R-R_0)M_0+(\mathsf hR\mathsf u)\tensor\mathsf v . \]
In the middle term we use \(\mathsf u^\T R_0M_0=\mathsf u^\T\mathsf X_0\) and the identity \(\mathsf X_0^\T M_0=M_0^\T R_0M_0=\Id-s\widetilde R_0\) of Lemma SB.3, so that \(\mathsf v^\T\mathsf X_0^\T M_0=\mathsf v^\T-s\,\mathsf v^\T\widetilde R_0\). Inserting (SB.14),
\[ \begin{aligned} (R-R_0)M_0={}&\alpha_3\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u) -\alpha_1\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor\big(\mathsf v-s\widetilde R_0\mathsf v\big)\\ &-\alpha_1\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(\mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u) +\alpha_2\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor\big(\mathsf v-s\widetilde R_0\mathsf v\big). \end{aligned} \]
By (SC.17) the last term of \(\mathsf X\) is \((\alpha_1R_0\mathsf u-\alpha_2\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor\mathsf v\), which cancels the two terms of \((R-R_0)M_0\) that carry \(\tensor\mathsf v\). What remains is (SC.18), since \((R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u)=R_0(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf u)\mathsf X_0\) and similarly for the other three terms.
At \(z\in\mathbb S\) the proof is verbatim with \(s\) replaced by \(z\): it is algebraic and uses only Lemma SB.4 and the identities of Lemma SB.3, which hold at \(z\).
□
Lemma SC.2 (Conditional moments of the coefficients)
Let \(s_\circ>0\) and \(p\ge1\) be an integer. In the setting of Lemma SB.4, let \(M_0\) be a function of the samples other than \((j,a)\), let \((\mathsf h,\mathsf u,\mathsf v)=(\varpi d,\mathsf u_{ja},\mathsf v_{ja})\) with \(0\le\varpi\le1\) and \(d=d_{ja}\), and let \(\mathcal G=\mathcal G_{ja}\). Then for every \(s\ge s_\circ\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.19} \norm{\alpha_1}_{p,\mathcal G}\le C\varpi,\qquad \norm{\alpha_3}_{p,\mathcal G}\le C\varpi^2,\qquad \norm{\alpha_2}_{p,\mathcal G}\le\frac{C\varpi^2}{s}, \end{equation}
with \(C\) depending only on \((p,s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\), and \(\alpha_2\ge0\). The same bounds, with \(s\) replaced by \(x\defeq\Ree z\), hold for the coefficients of Lemma SB.4 at every \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\ge s_\circ\), and there \(\Ree\alpha_2\ge0\).
Proof
Proof.
The pair \((d,\mathsf N)\) with \(\mathsf N=\norm{\mathsf u}^2+\norm{\mathsf v}^2\) is a function of the sample \((j,a)\) alone, which is independent of \(\mathcal G\); the conditional norms are therefore the unconditional norms of the bounds below.
The bounds on \(\alpha_1\) and \(\alpha_3\) are those of Lemma SB.5. For \(s\ge s_\circ\) apply that lemma with the compact interval \(I=[s_\circ,s]\), whose constant \(C_I=\max(1,s_\circ^{-1})(1+\tfrac{3}{4\sqrt{s_\circ}})^3\) depends only on \(s_\circ\):
\[ \abs{\alpha_1}\le C_I\varpi(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3,\qquad \abs{\alpha_3}\le C_I\varpi^2(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3 . \]
The bound on \(\alpha_2\) carries the factor \(1/s\) because \(\mathsf U\) does. By Lemma SB.3, \(0\le\mathsf U\le\norm{\mathsf u}^2/s\le\mathsf N/s\), and by (SB.16) and \(\abs{\mathsf h}\le\abs d\),
\[ \alpha_2=\frac{\mathsf h^2\mathsf U}{\Lambda}\le\varpi^2d^2\,\frac{\mathsf N}{s}\Big(1+\frac{3\abs d\mathsf N}{4\sqrt{s_\circ}}\Big)^2 \le\frac{\varpi^2}{s}\Big(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt{s_\circ}}\Big)^2(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3 , \]
the last step by the elementary inequality (SB.20). Positivity of \(\alpha_2\) holds because \(\mathsf U\ge0\) and \(\Lambda>0\).
At \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\ge s_\circ\) the coefficients are complex, and the pointwise bounds are obtained from the sector bounds. Put \(\kappa\defeq s_\circ^{-1/2}\) and \(\mathsf Z\defeq(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3\). By Lemma SB.3 at \(z\), \(\abs{\mathsf U}\le\mathsf N/x\), \(\abs{z\mathsf V}\le\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\mathsf N\) and \(\abs{\mathsf C}\le\mathsf N/(4\sqrt x)\); by (SB.17) and \(\abs{\mathsf h}\le\abs d\), \(\abs\Lambda^{-1}\le(1+\kappa\abs d\mathsf N)^2\). With (SB.20) for \(c=\kappa\) these give
\[ \abs{\alpha_1}\le(1+\kappa)^3\varpi\,\mathsf Z,\qquad \abs{\alpha_2}\le\frac{(1+\kappa)^2\varpi^2}{x}\,\mathsf Z,\qquad \abs{\alpha_3}\le2(1+\kappa)^2\varpi^2\,\mathsf Z . \]
The sign of \(\alpha_2\) is replaced by that of its real part. Multiplying (SC.17) at \(z\) by \(\mathsf u^\T\) gives \(\mathsf h\,\mathsf u^\T R_z(M)\mathsf u=\alpha_1\mathsf U-\alpha_2\mathsf C=\mathsf h\mathsf U/\Lambda\), so \(\alpha_2=\mathsf h^2\,\mathsf u^\T R_z(M)\mathsf u\). The matrix \(R_z(M)\) has real orthonormal eigenvectors and eigenvalues \((s_k^2+z)^{-1}\) and \(z^{-1}\), all with positive real part, so \(\Ree\alpha_2\ge0\).
The moments of the common factor are bounded by Hölder’s inequality and Lemma SB.6:
\[ \big\|(1+\abs d)^4(1+\mathsf N)^3\big\|_p\le\norm{1+\abs d}_{8p}^4\,\norm{1+\mathsf N}_{6p}^3 \le\big(1+\norm d_{8p}\big)^4\big(1+\norm{\mathsf N}_{6p}\big)^3 , \]
and the right side depends only on \((p,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\).

Lemma SC.3 (Deletion with a bounded multiplier)
Let \(s_\circ>0\). In the setting of Lemma SC.2, let \(h\) be a scalar random variable depending on the sample \((j,a)\) and on \(\mathcal G=\mathcal G_{ja}\), with \(\abs h\le D_h\) for a constant \(D_h\). Then for every \(s\ge s_\circ\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.20} \big\|\EE_{\mathcal G}\big[h\,(\mathsf X_s(M)-\mathsf X_s(M_0))\big]\big\|_\F\le\frac{C\,D_h\,\varpi}{s\sqrt B}, \end{equation}
with \(C\) depending only on \((s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\). In particular, for a sample of age \(j\) and \(s\ge s_\circ\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.21} \norm{\mathcal H(s)-\mathcal H_j(s)}_\F\le\frac{C\,\emaw j}{s\sqrt B},\qquad \mathcal H_j(s)\defeq\EE\,\mathsf X_0^{(j1)} . \end{equation}
Both bounds, with \(s\) replaced by \(x\defeq\Ree z\), hold at every \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\ge s_\circ\), for \(\mathsf X_z\) in place of \(\mathsf X_s\), for a complex multiplier \(h\) with \(\abs h\le D_h\), and with \(\mathcal H(z)\) and \(\mathcal H_j(z)\defeq\EE\,\mathsf X_z(M_0^{(j1)})\).
Proof
Proof.
The conditional moments of the rank-one factors are bounded through Lemma SB.7. For a scalar \(e\) depending on the sample and on \(\mathcal G\), the identities \(B\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf u=\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z\tensor z)\Sigmaout^{1/2}\), \(B\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v=\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z\tensor w)\Sigmain^{1/2}\) and \(\EE_{\mathcal G}[(z\tensor z)e]=\EE_{\mathcal G}[(z\tensor z-\Id)e]+\Id\,\EE_{\mathcal G}e\), together with \(\norm{\Id_{\Nout}}_\F=\sqrt{\Nout}\le\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\) and \(\abs{\EE_{\mathcal G}e}\le\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}\), give
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.22} \begin{gathered} \big\|\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf u)e]\big\|_\F\le\frac{C_\Sigma(\sqrt2+\sqrt{\gamma_\star B})}{B}\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}\le\frac{C\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}}{\sqrt B},\\ \big\|\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf v\tensor\mathsf v)e]\big\|_\F\le\frac{C\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}}{\sqrt B},\qquad \big\|\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v)e]\big\|_\F=\big\|\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf v\tensor\mathsf u)e]\big\|_\F\le\frac{C_\Sigma\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}}{B}. \end{gathered} \end{equation}
The four terms of (SC.18) are bounded one by one. The outer factors \(R_0\), \(\widetilde R_0\) and \(\mathsf X_0\) are functions of \(\mathcal G\) with norms at most \(1/s\), \(1/s\) and \((2\sqrt s)^{-1}\) by Lemma SB.3, and the multiplied coefficients satisfy \(\norm{h\alpha_i}_{2,\mathcal G}\le D_h\norm{\alpha_i}_{2,\mathcal G}\). With (SC.22) and Lemma SC.2 at \(p=2\),

The next lemma measures how the conditional mean of the Gram coefficient \(\alpha_2\) reacts to a low-rank change of the minor. For a deterministic \(K\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), a weight \(\varpi\in[0,1]\) and \(s>0\), or \(z\in\mathbb S\) in place of \(s\), let

Lemma SC.4 (Rank sensitivity of the conditional Gram coefficient)
Let \(s_\circ>0\), \(s\ge s_\circ\), \(\varpi\in[0,1]\), and let \(K,K'\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) with \(\operatorname{rank}(K-K')\le k\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.24} \abs{\phi_\varpi(K)-\phi_\varpi(K')}\le\frac{C\,\varpi^2\,k}{\sqrt s\,B}, \end{equation}
with \(C\) depending only on \((s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\). The same bound, with \(s\) replaced by \(x\defeq\Ree z\), holds at every \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\ge s_\circ\).
Proof
Proof.
Write \(\mathsf U,\mathsf V,\mathsf C,\Lambda\) for the scalars (SB.12) computed from \(K\) and \(\mathsf U',\mathsf V',\mathsf C',\Lambda'\) for those computed from \(K'\), with the same sample, and put \(\delta\mathsf U=\mathsf U-\mathsf U'\), \(\delta\mathsf V=\mathsf V-\mathsf V'\), \(\delta\mathsf C=\mathsf C-\mathsf C'\), \(\mathsf h=\varpi d\).
The resolvent differences have low rank and nuclear norm of order \(k\). The difference \(KK^\T-K'K'^\T=(K-K')K^\T+K'(K-K')^\T\) has rank at most \(2k\), so
\[ R_s(K)-R_s(K')=R_s(K)\big(K'K'^\T-KK^\T\big)R_s(K') \]
has rank at most \(2k\) and operator norm at most \(2/s\) by Lemma SB.3; its nuclear norm is at most \(4k/s\). The same holds for \(\widetilde R_s\). For the mixed resolvent we use the block matrix \(\mathcal L(K)\) of the proof of Lemma SB.4, whose inverse (SB.18) has \(\mathsf X_s(K)\) as its upper-right block: \(\mathcal L(K)-\mathcal L(K')\) has rank at most \(2k\), hence so does \(\mathcal L(K)^{-1}-\mathcal L(K')^{-1}=\mathcal L(K)^{-1}(\mathcal L(K')-\mathcal L(K))\mathcal L(K')^{-1}\), and so does its block \(\mathsf X_s(K)-\mathsf X_s(K')\), whose operator norm is at most \(2\cdot(2\sqrt s)^{-1}\); its nuclear norm is at most \(2k/\sqrt s\).
The differences of the scalars are Gaussian forms of these matrices. With \(F\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}(R_s(K)-R_s(K'))\Sigmaout^{1/2}/B\), a symmetric matrix with \(\norm F_*\le4C_\Sigma k/(sB)\), we have \(\delta\mathsf U=z^\T Fz\). Diagonalizing \(F=O\diag(f)O^\T\) and using that \(O^\T z\) is standard Gaussian,
\[ \norm{z^\T Fz}_2\le\sum_i\abs{f_i}\,\norm{g^2}_2=\sqrt3\,\norm F_*\le\frac{4\sqrt3\,C_\Sigma k}{sB}. \]
The same bound holds for \(\delta\mathsf V\). For \(\delta\mathsf C=z^\T F'w\) with \(F'\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}(\mathsf X_s(K)-\mathsf X_s(K'))\Sigmain^{1/2}/B\), a singular value decomposition \(F'=\sum_kf'_k\,p_k\tensor r_k\) writes \(z^\T F'w=\sum_kf'_k(p_k^\T z)(r_k^\T w)\) as a sum of products of independent standard normals, so
\[ \norm{z^\T F'w}_2\le\sum_kf'_k\,\norm g_2^2=\norm{F'}_*\le\frac{2C_\Sigma k}{\sqrt s\,B}. \]
The difference of the coefficients is a rational expression in these differences. From \(\alpha_2=\mathsf h^2\mathsf U/\Lambda\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.25} \alpha_2-\alpha_2'=\mathsf h^2\Big[\frac{\delta\mathsf U}{\Lambda}-\frac{\mathsf U'\,(\Lambda-\Lambda')}{\Lambda\Lambda'}\Big],\qquad \Lambda-\Lambda'=\mathsf h\,\delta\mathsf C\,\big(2+\mathsf h(\mathsf C+\mathsf C')\big)+s\mathsf h^2\big(\mathsf V\,\delta\mathsf U+\mathsf U'\,\delta\mathsf V\big). \end{equation}
By Lemma SB.3, \(0\le\mathsf U,\mathsf U'\le\mathsf N/s\), \(0\le s\mathsf V\le\mathsf N\) and \(\abs{\mathsf C},\abs{\mathsf C'}\le\mathsf N/(4\sqrt s)\); by (SB.16) and \(s\ge s_\circ\), \(\Lambda^{-1},\Lambda'^{-1}\le c_\circ^2(1+\abs d)^2(1+\mathsf N)^2\) with \(c_\circ=1+\tfrac{3}{4\sqrt{s_\circ}}\). Inserting these bounds in (SC.25), with \(\abs{\mathsf h}\le\abs d\) and \(Z\defeq(1+\abs d)(1+\mathsf N)\),
\[ \begin{aligned} \abs{\alpha_2-\alpha_2'}&\le\varpi^2d^2\Big[c_\circ^2Z^2\abs{\delta\mathsf U} +\frac{\mathsf N}{s}c_\circ^4Z^4\Big(\abs d\,\abs{\delta\mathsf C}\Big(2+\frac{\abs d\mathsf N}{2\sqrt s}\Big)+d^2\mathsf N\big(\abs{\delta\mathsf U}+\abs{\delta\mathsf V}\big)\Big)\Big]\\ &\le C\varpi^2Z^{8}\big(\abs{\delta\mathsf U}+\abs{\delta\mathsf V}+\abs{\delta\mathsf C}\big), \end{aligned} \]
with \(C\) depending only on \(s_\circ\).
The expectation is bounded by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Since \(\phi_\varpi(K)-\phi_\varpi(K')=\EE[\alpha_2-\alpha_2']\),
\[ \abs{\phi_\varpi(K)-\phi_\varpi(K')}\le C\varpi^2\,\norm{Z^8}_2\big(\norm{\delta\mathsf U}_2+\norm{\delta\mathsf V}_2+\norm{\delta\mathsf C}_2\big) \le C\varpi^2\Big(\frac{8\sqrt3C_\Sigma k}{sB}+\frac{2C_\Sigma k}{\sqrt sB}\Big), \]
where \(\norm{Z^8}_2\le\norm{1+\abs d}_{32}^8\norm{1+\mathsf N}_{32}^8\le C\) by Hölder’s inequality and Lemma SB.6. Since \(s^{-1}\le s_\circ^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}\), this is (SC.24).
At \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\ge s_\circ\) the proof is the same, with \(s\) replaced by \(z\) in the identities and by \(x\) in the bounds, except at three steps. First, by Lemma SB.3 at \(z\) the resolvent differences have rank at most \(2k\) and operator norms at most \(2/x\) and \(2(2\sqrt x)^{-1}\), hence nuclear norms at most \(4k/x\) and \(2k/\sqrt x\). Second, \(F\) and \(F'\) are complex; their real and imaginary parts are real matrices with nuclear norms at most \(\norm F_*\) and \(\norm{F'}_*\), the part of \(F\) being symmetric, and the two Gaussian bounds applied to each part give \(\norm{\delta\mathsf U}_2\le2\sqrt3\,\norm F_*\), \(\norm{\delta\mathsf V}_2\le2\sqrt3\,\norm{\widetilde F}_*\) for the analogous \(\widetilde F\), and \(\norm{\delta\mathsf C}_2\le2\norm{F'}_*\). Third, in (SC.25) the factor \(s\) becomes \(z\), the bounds \(\abs{z\mathsf V},\abs{z\mathsf U'}\le\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\mathsf N\) and \(\abs{\mathsf U'}\le\mathsf N/x\) replace \(s\mathsf V,s\mathsf U'\le\mathsf N\) and \(\mathsf U'\le\mathsf N/s\), and (SB.17) gives \(\abs\Lambda^{-1},\abs{\Lambda'}^{-1}\le(1+s_\circ^{-1/2})^2Z^2\). The constants change by absolute factors.

Lemma SC.5 (Covariance of the Gram coefficient and the mixed resolvent)
Let \(s_\circ>0\). For every age \(j\) and every \(s\ge s_\circ\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.27} \norm{\mathcal C_j}_\F\le\frac{C\,\emaw j^2}{s^{3/2}\sqrt B},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \sum_{j\ge0}\norm{\mathcal C_j}_\F\le\frac{C\,\sw2}{s^{3/2}\sqrt B}, \end{equation}
with \(C\) depending only on \((s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\). The same bounds, with \(s\) replaced by \(x\defeq\Ree z\), hold at every \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\ge s_\circ\).
Proof
Proof.
The covariance is expanded along a martingale over the remaining samples. Enumerate the samples other than \((j,1)\) as \(l=1,2,\dots\), age by age, and let \(\mathcal A_l\) be the \(\sigma\)-field generated by the first \(l\) of them, \(\mathcal A_0\) the trivial one. Put
\[ D_l\defeq\EE[\phi_j\mid\mathcal A_l]-\EE[\phi_j\mid\mathcal A_{l-1}]\qquad(l\ge1). \]
Since \(\phi_j\) is bounded by Lemma SC.2 and is a function of the enumerated samples, Lévy’s upward theorem [101, Theorem 14.2] gives \(\EE[\phi_j\mid\mathcal A_l]\to\phi_j\) almost surely, hence in \(L^2\) by boundedness; that is, \(\phi_j-b_j=\sum_{l\ge1}D_l\) in \(L^2\). At fixed dimension \(\norm{\mathsf X_0^{(j1)}}_\F\le\sqrt{\min(\Nout,\Nin)}/(2\sqrt s)\), so the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality passes the expansion to the covariance:
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.28} \mathcal C_j=\sum_{l\ge1}\EE\big[D_l\,\mathsf X_0^{(j1)}\big]. \end{equation}
Each martingale difference is bounded by the rank sensitivity. Let \(\phi_j^{[l]}\) denote \(\phi_j\) with the sample \(l\) replaced by an independent copy. Conditioning on \(\mathcal A_l\) after this replacement is the same as conditioning on \(\mathcal A_{l-1}\), so \(D_l=\EE[\phi_j-\phi_j^{[l]}\mid\mathcal A_l]\). The replacement changes the minor \(M_0^{(j1)}\) by a matrix of rank at most two, and Lemma SC.4 with \(k=2\) gives, for every value of the samples,
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.29} \abs{D_l}\le\sup\big\{\abs{\phi_{\emaw j}(K)-\phi_{\emaw j}(K')}:\ \operatorname{rank}(K-K')\le2\big\}\le\frac{2C\,\emaw j^2}{\sqrt s\,B}. \end{equation}
Each term of (SC.28) is a deletion term. Let \(\mathsf X_0^{(j1),[-l]}\) be the mixed resolvent of the buffer with both samples \((j,1)\) and \(l\) deleted. Conditionally on all samples other than \(l\), the matrix \(\mathsf X_0^{(j1),[-l]}\) is fixed, while \(D_l\) is a function of the samples up to \(l\) with \(\EE[D_l\mid\mathcal A_{l-1}]=0\); since the sample \(l\) is independent of the others, the conditional mean of \(D_l\) given all samples other than \(l\) is \(\EE[D_l\mid\mathcal A_{l-1}]=0\). Hence \(\EE[D_l\mathsf X_0^{(j1),[-l]}]=0\) and
\[ \EE\big[D_l\,\mathsf X_0^{(j1)}\big]=\EE\big[D_l\big(\mathsf X_0^{(j1)}-\mathsf X_0^{(j1),[-l]}\big)\big]. \]
If the sample \(l\) has age \(k\), this is the situation of Lemma SC.3 with \(M=M_0^{(j1)}\), the minor \(M_0^{(j1),[-l]}\), \(\varpi=\emaw k\) and the multiplier \(h=D_l\), bounded by (SC.29); conditioning on all samples other than \(l\) and then taking expectations gives
\[ \big\|\EE\big[D_l\,\mathsf X_0^{(j1)}\big]\big\|_\F\le\frac{2C\emaw j^2}{\sqrt sB}\cdot\frac{C\emaw k}{s\sqrt B}=\frac{C\,\emaw j^2\,\emaw k}{s^{3/2}B\sqrt B}. \]
The terms are summed over the enumeration. There are at most \(B\) samples of each age \(k\), so
\[ \norm{\mathcal C_j}_\F\le\sum_{k\ge0}B\,\frac{C\emaw j^2\emaw k}{s^{3/2}B\sqrt B}=\frac{C\emaw j^2}{s^{3/2}\sqrt B}\sum_{k\ge0}\emaw k\le\frac{C\emaw j^2}{s^{3/2}\sqrt B}, \]
by (SC.1). Summing over \(j\) gives the second bound of (SC.27).
At \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\ge s_\circ\) the proof is the same with \(s\) replaced by \(x\) in the bounds. The variables \(\phi_j\) and \(D_l\) are complex: Lévy’s theorem is applied to the real and imaginary parts of \(\phi_j\), (SC.29) follows from Lemma SC.4 at \(z\), \(\norm{\mathsf X_0^{(j1)}}_\F\le\sqrt{\min(\Nout,\Nin)}/(2\sqrt x)\), and the deletion terms are bounded by Lemma SC.3 at \(z\) with the complex multiplier \(h=D_l\).

Lemma SC.6 (Exact mixed identity)

Proof
Proof.
By the sample decomposition (SB.26),

Lemma SC.7 (Derivative bounds)
For \(x\ge0\) let \(f_x(d)=d/(1+xd^2)\) and \(\psi_x(d)=d^2/(1+xd^2)\) as in Lemma SB.12. Then for all \(d\in\R\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.31} \begin{gathered} \norm{f_x'}_\infty\le1,\qquad\norm{f_x'''}_\infty\le36x,\qquad\norm{\psi_x''}_\infty\le6,\\ \abs{\partial_xf_x(d)}\le\abs d^3,\qquad\abs{\partial_x\psi_x(d)}\le d^4,\qquad\abs{\partial_xf_x'(d)}\le3d^2 . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The first two bounds are (SB.41).

\[ \psi_x(d)=\frac1x\,\chi(\sqrt x\,d),\qquad\chi(y)\defeq\frac{y^2}{1+y^2},\qquad \psi_x''(d)=\chi''(\sqrt x\,d),\qquad\chi''(y)=\frac{2-6y^2}{(1+y^2)^3}, \]

\[ \begin{gathered} \partial_xf_x(d)=-\frac{d^3}{(1+xd^2)^2},\qquad \partial_x\psi_x(d)=-\frac{d^4}{(1+xd^2)^2},\\ \partial_xf_x'(d)=\partial_x\frac{1-xd^2}{(1+xd^2)^2}=\frac{-3d^2+xd^4}{(1+xd^2)^3}, \end{gathered} \]

Proof.

Proposition SC.3 (Response equation)
Under Assumption S3, for \(s\in I\) let \(b_B(s)\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}b_j\ge0\) with \(b_j\) as in (SC.26), and let \(\mathsf Y_B(s)\defeq\Id_{\Nin}+b_B(s)\Sigmain\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.35} \mathcal H(s)\,\mathsf Y_B(s)=\Lw\big(q(-s)\big)\,\Rbar(-s)\,\EE\Gt+E_B(s), \end{equation}
where, with \(r_B\) from Lemma SB.13, \(r_B'\) from Lemma SC.8, \(e_B^\star\) from Lemma SC.9 and \(\delta_B(I)\defeq\sup_{s\in I}\norm{\mathcal P(s)-\Rbar(-s)}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.36} \sup_{s\in I}\norm{E_B(s)}_\F\le C_I\Big[e_B^\star+r_B'+\norm{\Ahat}+\norm{\Ahat}^2+\delta_B(I)+\frac{1}{\sqrt B}\Big]\longrightarrow0 , \end{equation}
and the coefficient \(b_B\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SC.37} \sup_{s\in I}\abs{b_B(s)-\sigma(-s)\Fw(q(-s))}\le\sw2\Big(e_B^\star+\frac{C_\Sigma\gamma_\star}{s_-}r_B\Big)\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Fix \(s\in I\); every bound below is uniform in \(s\in I\). We start from the exact identity (SC.30) and treat its two terms.
