The windows of Theorem SI.8, Theorem SI.9 and Theorem SI.10 are the sets of \((N,B,\epsilon)\) with \(B\ge N\) and \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\) in which, respectively, \(N\ge N_*\) and \(\epsilon N^{p-1}\ge E_*\) (\(\texp<1\)); \(N\ge N_*\) and \(\epsilon N\ge E_*\) (\(\alpha>1\), \(\texp\ge1\)); and \((N,B,\epsilon)\) lies in the low-exponent window (\(\alpha\le1\), \(\texp\ge1\)). Here \(N_*\), \(E_*\) and \(\epsilon_0\) are fixed constants depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\). With \(f_{\infty,q}(u)=\frac12\sum_{i\ge1}i^{-p}e^{-2ui^{-q}}\), put \(J(p,q)=\int_0^\infty\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}(u)}\,\dif u\), which is finite when \(r_q=(p-1)/(2q)>1\).
Corollary SI.3 (Ratio of the hitting times)
In the windows of Theorem SI.8 (\(\texp<1\)), Theorem SI.9 (\(\alpha>1\), \(\texp\ge1\)) and Theorem SI.10 (\(\alpha\le1\), \(\texp\ge1\)), with constants depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.38} \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\asymp D_N\cdot\begin{cases} \epsilon^{-\alpha/[2(p-1)]},&\texp<1,\\ \epsilon^{-1/2}/\log(1/\epsilon),&\texp=1,\\ \epsilon^{-(\alpha+1-\texp)/[2(p-1)]},&1<\texp<\alpha+1,\\ \log(1/\epsilon),&\texp=\alpha+1,\\ 1,&\texp>\alpha+1. \end{cases} \end{equation}
Along every sequence with \(N\to\infty\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) in the window of Theorem SI.9 or Theorem SI.10,
\[ \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\sim\frac{D_N\log(1/\epsilon)}{2\sqrt{8\alpha}\,J(p,\alpha/2)}\quad(\texp=\alpha+1),\qquad \frac{t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}}{t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}}\sim D_N\,\frac{J(p,\alpha)}{J(p,\alpha/2)}\quad(\texp>\alpha+1). \]
Dividing the two hitting-time orders gives the ratio inside the windows, which is the windowed form of (17). Off the lines \(\texp=1\) and \(\texp=\alpha+1\) the exponent of \(1/\epsilon\) is \(\kappa\) of (18), and on those lines a logarithm appears. The prefactor is \(D_N\) exactly. In the saturated range \(\texp>\alpha+1\) the second display gives the constant \(D_NJ(p,\alpha)/J(p,\alpha/2)\), a quotient of the clock integrals of the two noiseless risks. For \(\alpha>2\), \(D_N\to\pi\zeta(\alpha)/\zeta(\alpha/2)\), so the ratio has a finite positive limit, which is Phase E of Figure 7.
Proof sketch.
In each window both models have the order of Theorem SI.8, Theorem SI.9 and Theorem SI.10 at once. By Section G.6, each time is \(\mathcal S_jT_0^j/(2\sqrt\epsilon)\) times a clock integral of order \(\epsilon^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)}\), \(\log(1/\epsilon)\) or \(1\) as \(r_j<1\), \(r_j=1\), \(r_j>1\), so
\[ t^j_{T\epsilon}\asymp\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,\Pi_{r_j}(\epsilon),\qquad \Pi_r(\epsilon)=\epsilon^{-1/(2r)},\ \ \epsilon^{-1/2}\log\tfrac1\epsilon,\ \ \epsilon^{-1/2}\quad(r<1,\ r=1,\ r>1). \]
The prefactors divide to \(D_N\) ((SI.27)); the rest is arithmetic with \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}=(p-1)/\alpha\), \(r_{\text{s-SGD}}=(p-1)/(2\alpha)\). For example, for \(1<\texp<\alpha+1\) only SignSGD has \(r_j<1\), and the quotient is \(\epsilon^{-\alpha/(p-1)+1/2}=\epsilon^{-(\alpha+1-\texp)/(2(p-1))}\); at \(\texp=1\), where \(p-1=\alpha\), it is \(\epsilon^{-1}/(\epsilon^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsilon))\).
In the windows of Theorem SI.9 and Theorem SI.10 the critical and saturated times have equivalents, and their quotient gives the constants. For \(r_j>1\) the noise part of the clock integral, the cost \(O(N^{-q_j(r_j-1)})\) of truncating the forcing at \(N\), and the part of \(J(p,q_j)\) beyond the first passage all vanish, so the clock integral tends to \(J(p,q_j)\). For \(r_j=1\), \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}(u)}=a_q/u+O(u^{-1-1/q})\) with \(a_q=(8q)^{-1/2}\), and the first passage satisfies \(\log(1+\hat u)\ge\frac12\log(1/\epsilon)-O(1)\) while the comparison horizon bounds it from above, so the clock integral is \(\frac{a_q}2\log(1/\epsilon)\) up to \(o(\log(1/\epsilon))\) (Lemma SI.10). This gives (SI.37). At \(\texp=\alpha+1\), SignSVD is saturated (\(r_{\text{s-SVD}}=2\)) and SignSGD is critical, and
\[ \frac{\mathcal S_{\text{s-SGD}}T_0^{\text{s-SGD}}\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsilon)/(4\sqrt{8\alpha})}{\mathcal S_{\text{s-SVD}}T_0^{\text{s-SVD}}\,J(p,\alpha/2)/(2\sqrt\epsilon)} =\frac{D_N\log(1/\epsilon)}{2\sqrt{8\alpha}\,J(p,\alpha/2)} . \]
For \(\texp>\alpha+1\) both are saturated, and the quotient is \(D_NJ(p,\alpha)/J(p,\alpha/2)\).
