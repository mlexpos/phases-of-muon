Proposition SI.5 (The low-exponent window)
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1\). The kernel heights (SI.29) satisfy, as \(N\to\infty\),
\begin{align*} k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}&\sim\begin{cases}2(1-\alpha/2)N^{-\alpha/2}/(1-\alpha),&0<\alpha<1,\\ N^{-1/2}\log N,&\alpha=1,\end{cases}\\ k_{N,\text{s-SGD}}&\sim\begin{cases} 2\big[(1-\frac2\pi)/(1-\alpha)+\frac2\pi(1-\alpha)/(1-2\alpha)\big]N^{-\alpha},&0<\alpha<\frac12,\\ \frac2\pi N^{-1/2}\log N,&\alpha=\frac12,\\ \frac4\pi(1-\alpha)\zeta(2\alpha)N^{\alpha-1},&\frac12<\alpha<1,\\ \frac4\pi\zeta(2)/\log N,&\alpha=1. \end{cases} \end{align*}
For fixed \(\texp\ge1\) and \(T\ge4\), the following sequences lie in the low-exponent window for all large \(N\), uniformly over integers \(B_N\ge N\): \(\epsilon_N=N^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)\) if \(0<\alpha<1\), and \(\epsilon_N=(\log N)^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<1\) if \(\alpha=1\).
Proof of Proposition SI.5.
The equivalents for the heights follow from (SI.29) and Lemma SI.8. For \(\alpha<\frac12\) both terms of \(k_{N,\text{s-SGD}}\) are of order \(N^{-\alpha}\) and their constants add. At \(\alpha=\frac12\) the second term is \(\frac2\pi N^{-1/2}\log N\) and dominates. For \(\frac12<\alpha<1\), \(H_N(2\alpha)\to\zeta(2\alpha)\) and the second term dominates because \(\alpha-1>-\alpha\). At \(\alpha=1\) the second term \(\frac4\pi\zeta(2)/\log N\) dominates the first, which is of order \((\log N)/N\).
For \(0<\epsilon<C^2\) and \(H=(C/\sqrt\epsilon)^{1/r}-1\), direct integration gives, for \(\epsilon\) below a fixed threshold,
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.56} \frac{k\,C\,I_r(H)}{\sqrt\epsilon}\le K_{C,r}\,k\cdot\begin{cases}\epsilon^{-1/(2r)},&0<r<1,\\ \epsilon^{-1/2}\log(1/\epsilon),&r=1,\\ \epsilon^{-1/2},&r>1,\end{cases} \end{equation}
with \(K_{C,r}\) depending only on \(C\) and \(r\). Here \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}\ge1\) and \(r_{\text{s-SGD}}\ge\frac12\), and each right side is at most \(K_{C,r}\,k\,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1/\epsilon)\).
Let \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\varpi_\alpha\defeq\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)\) and \(\epsilon_N=N^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<\varpi_\alpha\). The height equivalents give \(k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}=O(N^{-\alpha/2})\) and \(k_{N,\text{s-SGD}}=O(N^{-\varpi_\alpha}\log N)\). Since \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}\ge1\), the SignSVD side of (SI.56) is \(O(N^{-(\alpha-\omega)/2}\log N)\), and the SignSGD side is \(O(N^{\omega-\varpi_\alpha}(\log N)^2)\); both tend to zero. Moreover \(H_j(\epsilon_N)\le C_j^{1/r_j}N^{\omega/(2r_j)}\) and \(\omega/(2r_j)<q_j\) because \(2r_jq_j=p-1\ge\alpha>\omega\), so \(H_j(\epsilon_N)\le(N/4)^{q_j}\) for large \(N\).
Let \(\alpha=1\) and \(\epsilon_N=(\log N)^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<1\). The SignSVD side of (SI.56) is at most a fixed power of \(\log N\) times \(N^{-1/2}\log\log N\), and the SignSGD side is \(O((\log N)^{\omega-1}\log\log N)\); both tend to zero. Each \(H_j(\epsilon_N)\) is at most a fixed power of \(\log N\), hence below \((N/4)^{q_j}\) for large \(N\).
The comparisons of powers and logarithms used here follow from \(e^{cx}\ge(cx)^n/n!\) for \(c>0\) and an integer \(n\) larger than the power in question. Finally \(\epsilon_N<\epsilon_0\) and \(N\ge4\) for large \(N\), and no condition involves \(B\).
□
