Lemma SF.2 (Negative-axis transforms)
  1. (i)
    Let \(\nu_j\) \((j\ge1)\) and \(\nu\) be probability measures on \([0,\infty)\) with \(S_{\nu_j}(-t)\to S_\nu(-t)\) for every \(t>0\). Then \(\nu_j\to\nu\) weakly. If moreover \(\sup_j\int x\,\nu_j(\dif x)<\infty\) and \(\int x\,\nu(\dif x)<\infty\), then
    \[ \int\sqrt x\,\nu_j(\dif x)\longrightarrow\int\sqrt x\,\nu(\dif x). \]
  2. (ii)
    Two finite nonnegative measures on \([0,\infty)\) with equal masses and equal transforms at every \(-t\), \(t>0\), are equal.
Proof.
Part (i) first extends the convergence off the negative axis. For \(k\ge1\) let \(D_k\defeq\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{dist}(z,[0,\infty))>1/k\}\), a connected open set. For a probability measure \(\nu\) on \([0,\infty)\) the function \(S_\nu(z)\defeq\int(x-z)^{-1}\nu(\dif x)\) is analytic on \(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\), because for \(\abs{z-z_0}<\operatorname{dist}(z_0,[0,\infty))\) the expansion
\[ \frac{1}{x-z}=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(z-z_0)^n}{(x-z_0)^{n+1}} \]
converges uniformly in \(x\ge0\); and \(\abs{S_\nu(z)}\le k\) on \(D_k\). The points \(-t\) with \(t>1/k\) lie in \(D_k\) and accumulate at \(-2/k\in D_k\). Vitali’s convergence theorem [66, §5.21], whose hypotheses are the uniform bound on \(D_k\) and the convergence on this set, gives that \(S_{\nu_j}\) converges at every point of \(D_k\) to an analytic function on \(D_k\). That function equals \(S_\nu\) on the negative axis in \(D_k\), so it equals \(S_\nu\) on \(D_k\) by the identity theorem [66, §2.6]. Since \(\bigcup_kD_k=\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\),
\[ S_{\nu_j}(z)\longrightarrow S_\nu(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\R). \]
Theorem 2.4.4(c) of [1], applied to the deterministic measures \(\nu_j\) with the limit \(S_\nu\), the Stieltjes transform of the probability measure \(\nu\), gives \(\nu_j\to\nu\) weakly.
The half-moments converge by truncation. For \(L>0\) and \(x>L\) one has \(\sqrt x\le x/\sqrt L\), so
\[ 0\le\int\sqrt x\,\nu_j(\dif x)-\int\min(\sqrt x,\sqrt L)\,\nu_j(\dif x)\le\int_{(L,\infty)}\sqrt x\,\nu_j(\dif x)\le\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\sup_k\int x\,\nu_k(\dif x), \]
and the same bound holds for \(\nu\). The truncated integrand is bounded and continuous, so its integrals converge by weak convergence, and letting \(L\to\infty\) proves the claim.
Part (ii) is part (i) for a constant sequence. If the common mass is zero there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, after dividing both measures by their mass, the constant sequence \(\nu_j\defeq\nu'\) has the transforms of \(\nu\), so \(\nu'\to\nu\) weakly by part (i), that is, \(\nu'=\nu\).
□
