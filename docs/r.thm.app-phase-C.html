Theorem SI.10 (Low covariance exponents)
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1\), \(\texp\ge1\) and \(T\ge4\). There are \(0<c_-\le c_+\), depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\), such that for every \((N,B,\epsilon)\) in the low-exponent window (SI.33), and for both \(j\), the first hit \(\hat u_j\) is attained and
\[ c_-\le\frac{t^j_{T\epsilon}}{\mathcal S_jT_0^j\,\Pi_{r_j}(\epsilon)}\le c_+ . \]
Along every sequence with \(N\to\infty\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) in this window, the equivalents (SI.37) hold.
Proof of Theorem SI.10.
Take \(h_j\defeq C_j(1+u)^{-r_j}\) and \(H_j\defeq H_j(\epsilon)\). Each kernel is decreasing with maximum \(k_{N,j}\), so for \(u\le H_j\)
\[ (g_j*h_j)(u)\le k_{N,j}\int_0^uh_j(v)\,\dif v\le k_{N,j}C_jI_{r_j}(H_j)\le\sqrt\epsilon \]
by (SI.33). This is hypothesis (b) of Lemma SI.9, which gives the hit \(0<\hat u_j\le H_j\) and
\[ \mathcal E_j\le\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,H_j\le\sqrt2\,C_j^{1/r_j}\epsilon^{(r_j-1)/(2r_j)} . \]
If \(r_j<1\), (SI.33) gives \(H_j\le(N/4)^{q_j}\) and Lemma SI.10(i) applies; its upper bound is at most \([C_j^{1/r_j}/(1-r_j)+\sqrt2C_j^{1/r_j}]\epsilon^{-(1-r_j)/(2r_j)}\). If \(r_j=1\), then \(H_jN^{-q_j}\le4^{-q_j}\), \((1+H_j)\sqrt\epsilon=C_j\) and \(\mathcal E_j\le\sqrt2C_j\), and Lemma SI.10(ii) applies. If \(r_j>1\), \(\mathcal E_j\le\sqrt2C_j^{1/r_j}\) for \(\epsilon<1\) and \(\mathcal E_j\to0\) as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), and Lemma SI.10(iii) applies. The conclusions follow as in the proof of Theorem SI.9.
□
