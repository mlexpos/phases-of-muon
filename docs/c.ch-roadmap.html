Chapter of the paper's appendix

Roadmap of the results and their proofs

The preceding sections give additional related work, numerical methods, finite-ridge experiments and momentum ablations. The sections that follow present the results and their proofs. This proof appendix is written for a reader who has finished the main text. It restates, verbatim, every appendix result that the main text cites, and follows each with a paragraph on what the result says and which claim of the main text it supports, and, for theorems, propositions, lemmas and corollaries, a sketch of the proof. The line printed after each restated result gives its number in the supplement, which contains the complete appendix with full proofs, in sections SA to SJ; references such as Lemma SE.4 point into the supplement. The statements of the supplement are formalized in Lean, except the remarks and the numerical statements. The standard Gaussian results they use are proved in the accompanying foundations library, listed in Section SK of the supplement; the line after each restated result names the corresponding Lean declarations and their status.
The proof appendix has three parts. Section F fixes the model, the algorithm and the proportional regime, which are standing assumptions for everything that follows. Section G gives an informal overview of six proof strategies and how they fit together: the deterministic equivalent for the Gram resolvent of the momentum buffer (Section G.1), the expected regularized update (Section G.2), the volatility and the lag kernel (Section G.3), the passage from the algorithm to the deterministic dynamics (Section G.4), the power-law and large-batch asymptotics (Section G.5), and the Volterra analysis behind the phase map (Section G.6). The seven remaining chapters state the precise results and their formalization status, each opening with the setting its results share.
The first four strategies form a chain, and together they carry the central claim of the main text, Theorem 2.1. Section G.1 computes the resolvent of the Gram matrix of the momentum buffer at a fixed state through the fixed-point system of Figure 2. Section G.2 integrates that resolvent into the mean of the regularized update, which gives the drift kernel (7), and Section G.3 computes the covariance of consecutive updates, which gives the volatility kernel. Section G.4 compares the algorithm with the deterministic recursion (6) over blocks of steps, using the two kernels and the fact that a diffuse error stays diffuse. Section H restates this comparison and its half-anisotropic form, which is the row recursion (14); it rests on all four strategies. It comes first because the later chapters evaluate its coefficients: Section I restates the fixed-point system and the drift kernel (Section G.1 and Section G.2), and Section J reads the volatility kernel for the half-anisotropic rows and without momentum (Section G.1, Section G.2 and Section G.3).
The next three chapters turn the kernels into the quantitative claims of the main text. Section K treats identity covariances, where the fixed-point system becomes one scalar equation and the dynamics closes on the risk; it supplies the drift and volatility of Section 3, the learning-rate ratio and the speedup, at large and at small batch. It uses the first four strategies and, for its limits in the aspect ratio, Section G.5. Section L computes the half-anisotropic coefficients at zero ridge, for power-law spectra at thin batch and for every spectrum at oversampled batch, which gives Table 1 and the square-root law of Theorem 4.1; it is Section G.5 applied to the formulas of Section G.1, Section G.2 and Section G.3. Section M takes the kernels as fixed coefficients of the row recursion and derives the times to accuracy, their ratio and the phase diagram of Section 5; it rests on Section G.6, and its isotropic comparison also on Section G.1.
The last chapter, Section N, concerns the comparator. Its results are one-step Gaussian computations for the entrywise sign and use none of these six strategies. They give the SignSGD kernels of Section 3 and Section 4, on which the SignSGD arrays of Section M are modelled.
