The isotropic case · cited in the main text
Proved in LeanTheorem numbering in the paper: Theorem K.1
For a finite measure \(\nu\) on \([0,\infty)\) write \(S_\nu(-t)\defeq\int\nu(\dif x)/(x+t)\) for \(t>0\). At \(\bmom=0\) the root solutions \(\sigma(-t)\), \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\) of the fixed-point system of Figure 2 are the transforms of a unique pair of measures \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}\) on \([0,\infty)\) of masses \(\gout,\gin\), the isotropic measure pair. For \(r>0\), \(\mathsf M_r\) is the Marchenko–Pastur law with an atom \((1-1/r)_+\) at zero and density \(\sqrt{(b_r-x)(x-a_r)}/(2\pi x)\) on \((a_r,b_r)\), where \(a_r=(1-\sqrt r)^2/r\) and \(b_r=(1+\sqrt r)^2/r\).
Theorem SF.4 (Small aspect ratios)
Let \(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\to0\) with \(r_j\defeq\gout^{(j)}/\gin^{(j)}\to r\in(0,\infty)\), and let \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}^{(j)})\) be the isotropic measure pair at \((\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)})\) of Proposition SF.6. Let \(\mathsf P_j\) be the image of the probability measure \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)}/\gout^{(j)}\) under \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda/\gout^{(j)}\), and write \(\mathsf m_j(t)\defeq S_{\mathsf P_j}(-t)\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(t>0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.37} \Big|t\,\mathsf m_j(t)^2+\Big(t+\frac1{r_j}-1\Big)\mathsf m_j(t)-1\Big|\le\frac{3\gout^{(j)}}{r_j^2t^2}. \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    \(\mathsf P_j\to\mathsf M_r\) weakly, and \(\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x)\to J_r\defeq\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf M_r(\dif x)>0\).
  3. (iii)
    The constant satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.38} \begin{gathered} C\big(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\big)=\frac{r_j}{\sqrt{2\gout^{(j)}}}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x) \sim\frac{r}{\sqrt{2\gout^{(j)}}}\,J_r,\\ J_r=\int_{a_r}^{b_r}\frac{\sqrt{(b_r-x)(x-a_r)}}{2\pi\sqrt x}\,\dif x . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    In particular \(J_1=8/(3\pi)\), and \(C(\gamma)\sim8/(3\pi\sqrt{2\gamma})\) as \(\gamma\downarrow0\).
The theorem gives the zero-ridge drift without momentum at large batch, \(N\ll B\). In the square case \(C(\gamma)\sim\frac{8}{3\pi}(2\gamma)^{-1/2}\), that is \(\mathfrak d_0\approx\frac8{3\pi}\sqrt{B/(2N)}\), the large-batch limit of the approximate form of \(\mathfrak d_0\) in (9). The constant \(J_r\) is the mean square root of the Marchenko–Pastur law \(\mathsf M_r\) of ratio \(r=\gout/\gin\), with its atom at zero when \(r>1\), so the drift constant is a mean singular value. Part (i) is a quantitative form of the convergence that (ii) states.
Proof sketch.
Without momentum the drift constant is a half-moment of a spectral measure. The isotropic measure pair exists and is unique (Proposition SF.6): it is obtained as a limit of the expected spectral measures of the Gram matrices of one minibatch gradient, and the transforms identify the limit. The integrand of the drift is then \(\psi(t)=\int\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+t}\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\), and the elementary integral \(\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\frac{x}{x+t}\,\dif t=\pi\sqrt x\) turns the form (SF.23) into a mean of square roots, that is of singular values (Proposition SF.7):
\[ C(\gin,\gout)=\frac1{\sqrt2\,\gin\gout}\int\sqrt\lambda\;\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda) =\frac{r_j}{\sqrt{2\gout}}\int\sqrt x\;\mathsf P_j(\dif x), \]
the second form after the rescaling \(\lambda=\gout x\) that defines \(\mathsf P_j\).
At small aspect ratio the closure becomes the Marchenko–Pastur quadratic. The transform of \(\mathsf P_j\) is \(\mathsf m_j(t)=\sigma(-\gout t)\), and both transforms are bounded by their mass over the spectral parameter, so the argument \(v_j\) of \(\Phi\) in the closure is at most \(\gout/(r_jt)\), which is small. Without momentum the closure reads \(\gout(1-t\,\mathsf m_j)=\Phi(v_j)\), and \(\Phi\) is linear up to a quadratic error, \(0\le v-\Phi(v)\le3v^2\) (Lemma SF.1(iii)). Inserting \(v_j\) and dividing by \(\gout\) leaves the quadratic equation of (SF.37) with the residual \(3\gout/(r_j^2t^2)\). This is (i).
The rescaled laws converge to \(\mathsf M_r\), and so do their half-moments. For each \(t\), every limit point of \(\mathsf m_j(t)\) is a positive root of \(t\,m^2+(t+\frac1r-1)m-1=0\), and that root is the transform of \(\mathsf M_r\) (Lemma SF.9). The first moments are exactly \(1/r_j\), because the first moment of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}\) is \(\gin\gout\) (Lemma SF.3(ii)). Convergence of the transforms on the negative axis then gives weak convergence, and the bounded first moments give convergence of the half-moments (Lemma SF.2(i)). This is (ii), and (iii) follows from the display. At \(r=1\), \(J_1=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^4\sqrt{4-x}\,\dif x=\frac8{3\pi}\).
