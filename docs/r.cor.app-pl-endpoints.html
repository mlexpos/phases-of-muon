Corollary SG.1 (Crossover windows and joint endpoints)
Assume Assumption S4, let \(i=i_N\to\infty\) be a row sequence, and let \(i_\star\defeq i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) for \(\alpha\le1\) and \(i_\star\defeq i_{\mathrm c}\) for \(\alpha>1\).
  1. (i)
    If \(\alpha\le1\) and \(i/i_\star\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then
    \[ \frac{\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)}{a_{\mathfrak d}}\to1+x^{-\alpha/2},\qquad \sw2\,\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1+2x^{\alpha/2}+c_0^{-2}x^\alpha,\qquad \frac{\mu_i}{c_Ni_\star^{-\alpha}}\to x^{-\alpha}. \]
  2. (ii)
    The joint endpoint equivalents are
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.34} \begin{array}{c|c|c} & i/i_\star\to0 & i/i_\star\to\infty\\\hline \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0) & \sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)} & a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\\[3pt] \mathfrak v_i(0) & 1/\sw2 & \gamma\,\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\\[3pt] \mu_i & c_Ni^{-\alpha}\ \text{(exact)} & c_Ni^{-\alpha}\ \text{(exact)} \end{array} \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    One has \(i_\star/N\to0\). There are row sequences \(i\to\infty\) in both windows \(i/i_\star\to0\) and \(i/i_\star\to\infty\) if and only if
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.35} \frac{BN^{\alpha-1}}{\log^2\gamma}\to\infty\ \ (\alpha<1),\qquad \frac{B}{\log^3\gamma}\to\infty\ \ (\alpha=1),\qquad B\to\infty\ \ (\alpha>1), \end{equation}
    and then \(1\ll i_\star\ll N\).
  4. (iv)
    For \(\alpha>1\), equating the two mesoscopic endpoint equivalents gives the indices \(i_{\mathrm c}/(\sw2d_\alpha^2)\) for the drift and \(B(\alpha-1)/(4\sw2K_\alpha^2)\) for the volatility, whose ratio is \((\alpha-1)d_\alpha^2/(4K_\alpha)\); all indices of the corollary are independent of \(c_N\).
Proof of Corollary SG.1.
Part (i) is Theorem SG.2(i) and Theorem SG.3(ii) at \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to y=c_0^{-2/\alpha}x\), for which \(2c_0y^{\alpha/2}=2x^{\alpha/2}\) and \(y^\alpha=c_0^{-2}x^\alpha\). For part (ii), the left column is the resolved case of Theorem SG.2 and Theorem SG.3, the two indices of the case \(\alpha\le1\) differing by a fixed factor. In the right column the drift is the unresolved case of Theorem SG.2. For the volatility, Theorem SG.1 gives \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i\), and \(\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\) when \(i/i_\star\to\infty\): for \(\alpha\le1\) because \(\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\to\infty\) there (mesoscopic rows by the proof of Theorem SG.3(ii), bulk rows because \(\gamma\to\infty\)), and for \(\alpha>1\) by the computation in the last proof.
For part (iii), (SG.32) and \(i_{\mathrm c}=B/K_\alpha\) give
\[ \frac{i_\star}N=\big(\gamma\sw2d^2\log^2\gamma\big)^{-1/\alpha}\ (\alpha<1),\quad \frac{i_\star}N=\big(\gamma\sw2d^2\log^3\gamma\big)^{-1}\ (\alpha=1),\quad \frac{i_\star}N=\frac1{K_\alpha\gamma}\ (\alpha>1), \]
all tending to zero. A row sequence \(i\to\infty\) with \(i/i_\star\to0\) exists if and only if \(i_\star\to\infty\), which is (SG.35), and \(i=\lfloor\sqrt{i_\star}\rfloor\) realizes it; \(i=N\) realizes the other window. Part (iv) equates \(\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\) with \(d_\alpha\sqrt{c_NK_\alpha}N^{-1/2}i^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\), which gives \(i=B/(K_\alpha\sw2d_\alpha^2)\), and \(1/\sw2\) with \(\frac{4K_\alpha^2}{\alpha-1}\frac iB\), which gives \(i=B(\alpha-1)/(4\sw2K_\alpha^2)\); neither involves \(c_N\), and neither do the indices of (SG.32) and (SG.33).
□
