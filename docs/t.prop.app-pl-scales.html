Proposition SG.6 (Reference scales)
Assume Assumption S4. For \(\gamma>1\) let \(\resscale\defeq\Lambda_N(1)\) be the unique solution of
\[ \sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\resscale\mu_i}{1+\resscale\mu_i}=B, \]
and put \(i_\#\defeq(c_N\resscale)^{1/\alpha}\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.36} \begin{gathered} c_N\resscale\sim\begin{cases}(1-\alpha)BN^{\alpha-1},&\alpha<1,\\ B/\log\gamma,&\alpha=1,\\ (B/K_\alpha)^\alpha,&\alpha>1,\end{cases} \\ \frac{i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}}{i_\#}\sim\begin{cases}\big(\pi/(2\sw2\log^2\gamma)\big)^{1/\alpha},&\alpha<1,\\ 9\pi/(8\sw2\log^2\gamma),&\alpha=1,\end{cases} \qquad \frac{i_{\mathrm c}}{i_\#}\to1\ \ (\alpha>1), \end{gathered} \end{equation}
and \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}/i_\#\sim(\sw2\log^2\gamma)^{-1/\alpha}\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(\sim9/(4\sw2\log^2\gamma)\) for \(\alpha=1\).

Definitions and assumptions used

Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
Assumption S4 (Thin-batch power law)
The exponent \(\alpha>0\) and the momentum \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) are fixed. Along a sequence of positive integers \(N\) and \(B=B_N\),
\[ N\to\infty,\qquad B\to\infty,\qquad \gamma=\frac NB\to\infty, \]
and the spectrum is \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\) for \(1\le i\le N\), with amplitudes \(c_N>0\), with \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\) and \(\Nout=\Nin=N\).
The scalar \(\resscale\) is the level at which the soft count of output directions equals the batch size: \(\sum_i\resscale\mu_i/(1+\resscale\mu_i)=B\), the rank of one gradient. It is the resolution scalar of the no-momentum row of Table 1, and \(i_\#=(c_N\resscale)^{1/\alpha}\) is the row at which \(\resscale\mu_i=1\). The proposition compares this threshold with the crossover indices of the momentum coefficients. For \(\alpha>1\) the momentum crossover sits at the threshold, \(i_{\mathrm c}\sim i_\#\). For \(\alpha\le1\) it sits below it by a factor of order \((\log\gamma)^{-2/\alpha}\). Two indices therefore carry the name \(i_\#\) in the main text. The threshold of this proposition governs the no-momentum row of the table. The display (15), which cites this proposition, has the form of the momentum crossover index, \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) for \(\alpha\le1\) and \(i_{\mathrm c}\) for \(\alpha>1\), up to constant factors; for \(\alpha\le1\) it is thus smaller than the threshold \((c_N\resscale)^{1/\alpha}\) by the logarithmic factor above, and for \(\alpha>1\) the two agree up to constants. In this chapter \(i_\#\) always denotes \((c_N\resscale)^{1/\alpha}\).
Proof sketch.
The scalar \(\resscale\) is the participation coordinate at \(m=1\), and the continuum law of Section G.5 evaluates it. By definition \(\resscale=\Lambda_N(1)\), so \(V\defeq c_N\resscale N^{-\alpha}\) solves \(S_N(V)=1/\gamma\). The Riemann sum \(S_N\) is within \(1/N\) of \(S_\alpha\), hence
\[ S_\alpha(V)\in\frac1\gamma\Big[1,1+\frac1B\Big], \]
so \(V\to0\) and the small-\(V\) inversion of \(S_\alpha\) applies (Lemma SG.6 and Lemma SG.5). It gives \(c_N\resscale=N^\alpha V\sim N^\alpha(1-\alpha)/\gamma\), \(N/(\gamma\log\gamma)\) and \(N^\alpha(K_\alpha\gamma)^{-\alpha}\) in the three cases, which is the first display.
The ratios are then read off from the definitions of the crossover indices. With \(d^2=c_0^2/(1-\alpha)=2/(\pi(1-\alpha))\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(d^2=\frac49c_0^2=8/(9\pi)\) for \(\alpha=1\),
\[ \frac{i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}^\alpha}{c_N\resscale}\sim\frac{BN^{\alpha-1}}{\sw2d^2\log^2\gamma\cdot(1-\alpha)BN^{\alpha-1}}=\frac{\pi}{2\sw2\log^2\gamma}\quad(\alpha<1),\qquad \frac{i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}}{i_\#}\sim\frac{9\pi}{8\sw2\log^2\gamma}\quad(\alpha=1), \]
while \(i_{\mathrm c}=B/K_\alpha\sim i_\#\) for \(\alpha>1\), and \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}=c_0^{2/\alpha}i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) gives the volatility ratios. The logarithm for \(\alpha\le1\) is not an effective-batch effect: on the rows \(i\asymp i_\#\) the momentum drift exceeds the no-momentum scale \((\gamma\resscale)^{-1/2}\) by a factor of order \(\log\gamma\), because the momentum participation grows like \(\log Q_m\sim2\abs{\log\bmom}\,m\), and a fixed change of the batch size cannot remove a logarithm (Remark SG.2).
