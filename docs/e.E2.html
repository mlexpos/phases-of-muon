E2: Drift: the expected regularized update

SJ.12 Drift: the expected regularized update

This engine computes the mean of the update in the frozen-state model (Section SC), which is the input of every drift kernel. It works in the setting of Section G.1 and uses its output. It produces a deterministic matrix \(\Gtildebar_\ridge\), whose entries in the eigenbases of the covariances are multiples of the corresponding entries of \(\Dt\), with, for normalized ridges in a fixed interval \([\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\subset(0,\infty)\) (Theorem SC.2),
\[ \sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\norm{\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)-\Gtildebar_\ridge}_\F\longrightarrow0 . \]
The norm is the unnormalized Frobenius norm, the norm in which (SC.13) controls the drift errors of all modes together.
The update is an integral of the mixed resolvent along the negative axis. For a singular value \(\sigma_k\) of \(\Mt\), the scalar integral \(\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\sigma_k(\sigma_k^2+\ridge+u^2)^{-1}du\) equals \(\sigma_k(\sigma_k^2+\ridge)^{-1/2}\), the filter of the regularized polar map, so (Lemma SA.3)
\[ \EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\mathcal H(\ridge+u^2)\,du,\qquad \mathcal H(s)\defeq\EE\big[(\Mt\Mt^\T+s\Id)^{-1}\Mt\big]. \]
It suffices to find a deterministic equivalent of \(\mathcal H(s)\) in Frobenius norm, uniformly for \(s\) in compact sets, and a bound that controls the integral at large \(s\).
The mixed resolvent solves a linear response equation. Deleting one sample as in Section G.1, now through the identity \(\mathsf hR\mathsf u=\alpha_1R_0\mathsf u-\alpha_2\mathsf X_0\mathsf v\) with \(\alpha_2=\mathsf h^2\mathsf U/\Lambda\), writes \(\mathcal H\) exactly as a sum over ages of a source term carrying \(\alpha_1\) and a feedback term carrying \(\alpha_2\) (Lemma SC.6). In the source term the Gaussian response of a sample with residual function \(f\) is
\[ \EE\big[f(d)\,z\tensor w\big]=\Ahat\,\EE f'(d)+O\big(\norm{\Ahat}^2\big), \]
and multiplying by \(\Sigmaout^{1/2}\) and \(\Sigmain^{1/2}\) turns \(\Ahat\) into the mean gradient \(\EE\Gt=\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain/\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\). By Stein’s identity \(\EE f'(d)\to\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\), so the weights now enter to the first power, through \(\Lw\). In the feedback term \(\alpha_2\) is replaced by its conditional mean, whose expected sum over ages, \(b_B(s)\), tends to \(\sigma\Fw(q)\). The result is the response equation (Proposition SC.3)
\[ \mathcal H(s)\big(\Id+b_B(s)\,\Sigmain\big)=\Lw(q)\,\Rbar(-s)\,\EE\Gt+o(1)\quad\text{in Frobenius norm.} \]
Since \(s(\Id+\sigma\Fw(q)\Sigmain)=s\Id+s_1\Sigmain=\Rtbar(-s)^{-1}\) by the fixed-point system, the candidate below solves the same equation exactly with \(b_B\) replaced by its limit, and comparing the two equations shows that \(\mathcal H(s)\) is close to it (Theorem SC.1(i)):
\[ T(s)=s\,\Lw\big(q(-s)\big)\,\Rbar(-s)\,\EE\Gt\,\Rtbar(-s). \]
Because the Frobenius norm is not normalized by the dimension, every replacement must cost \(o(1)\) there, which is more than the trace statements of Section G.1 give. The delicate replacement is the covariance between the conditional mean of \(\alpha_2\) and the deleted mixed resolvent. It is expanded along a martingale over the other samples; replacing one of them changes the buffer by rank two, which moves the conditional mean by \(O(\emaw j^2/B)\) (Lemma SC.4), and the covariance is \(O(\emaw j^2B^{-1/2})\) in Frobenius norm (Lemma SC.5).
The integral is split at a cutoff. The exact identity, without any asymptotic replacement, bounds \(\norm{\mathcal H(s)}_\F\le C/s\) for \(s\ge1\) at every dimension and for every state, and \(\norm{T(s)}_\F\le C_\Sigma/s\) (Theorem SC.1(ii)). With \(\Gtildebar_\ridge=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty T(\ridge+u^2)\,du\) this gives, for every \(\mathsf R\ge1\),
\[ \norm{\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)-\Gtildebar_\ridge}_\F\le\frac2\pi\Big[\mathsf R\sup_{\ridge_-\le s\le\ridge_++\mathsf R^2}\norm{\mathcal H(s)-T(s)}_\F+\frac{C}{\mathsf R}\Big], \]
and the dimension limit followed by \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) gives the output.
The candidate acts entrywise in the eigenbases. Since \(\Rbar(-s)\) and \(\Rtbar(-s)\) are functions of \(\Sigmaout\) and \(\Sigmain\),
\[ u_i^\T T(s)v_j=\frac{s\,\Lw(q(-s))\,\mu_i\lambda_j}{(s+\tilde s_1(-s)\mu_i)(s+s_1(-s)\lambda_j)}\cdot\frac{u_i^\T\Dt v_j}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}}, \]
and integrating at \(s=\ridge+u^2\) gives \(u_i^\T\Gtildebar_\ridge v_j=\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)\,u_i^\T\Dt v_j/\sqrt{\cR(t)}\) with the kernel (SC.4). The same argument applied to the buffer truncated to its first \(J\) ages shows that, after the dimension limit, ages beyond \(J\) contribute a vanishing amount as \(J\to\infty\) (Proposition SC.1); the dynamics uses this.
