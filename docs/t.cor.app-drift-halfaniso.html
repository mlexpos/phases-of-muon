Corollary SC.2 (Half-anisotropic input)
Under Assumption S3, assume \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\), let \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\), write \(z=\ridge+u^2\), and put \(\mathfrak d_i\defeq\mathfrak d(\mu_i,1)\).
  1. (i)
    For every pair \((i,j)\) the kernel is \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d_i\), and
    \[ \mathfrak d_i=\frac{\sqrt2\,B}{\pi\,\Nin}\int_0^\infty\frac{z\,\Lw(q(-z))\,\tilde\sigma(-z)\,\mu_i}{z+\tilde s_1(-z)\mu_i}\,du . \]
  2. (ii)
    The row drift of (SA.21) is, with \(E_\ridge\) as in Corollary SC.1,
    \[ \sum_j\cD_{ij}(t)=\frac{\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,\CMscr Q_i(t)+\sum_j(u_i^\T\Dt v_j)(E_\ridge)_{ij}. \]
  3. (iii)
    At \(\bmom=0\),
    \[ \mathfrak d_i=\frac{\sqrt2\,B}{\pi\,\Nin}\int_0^\infty\frac{\tilde s_1(-z)\mu_i}{z+\tilde s_1(-z)\mu_i}\,du . \]
  4. (iv)
    At \(\bmom=0\), if \(\rho_i\) is a probability measure on \([0,\infty)\) with
    \[ \big(z+\tilde s_1(-z)\mu_i\big)^{-1}=\int\frac{\rho_i(dx)}{x+z}\qquad(z>0), \]
    then
    \[ \mathfrak d_i=\frac{B}{\sqrt2\,\Nin}\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{x}{\sqrt{x+\ridge}}\,\rho_i(dx). \]

Definitions and assumptions used

Assumption S3 (Spectrally diffuse proportional regime)
Assumption S3 (Spectrally diffuse proportional regime)
Assumption S1 and Assumption S2 hold, and we fix \(\gamma_\star<\infty\) and \(C_\Sigma\ge1\) with \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), \(\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\norm{\Sigmaout}\le C_\Sigma\) along the whole sequence of dimensions; the covariances are deterministic. The momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) is fixed, and the normalized ridge satisfies \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\) for fixed \(0<\ridge_-\le\ridge_+<\infty\). The state is frozen as in Definition SA.3, with \(\cR(t)>0\), and the normalized state is spectrally diffuse: \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\).
This is the drift of the half-anisotropic setting of Section 4. When \(\Sigmain=\Id\) every \(\lambda_j\) equals \(1\), so the kernel \(\mathfrak d_{ij}\) does not depend on \(j\), and the projected row risks \(\CMscr Q_i\) have the drift of (14) with \(\mathfrak d_i\) the common value of the \(\mathfrak d_{ij}\), not their sum. Part (i) writes \(\mathfrak d_i\) through the input trace \(\tilde\sigma\), and (iii) simplifies it at \(\bmom=0\). Part (iv) writes it as a moment of a row spectral measure \(\rho_i\); setting \(\ridge=0\) there formally gives the \(\sqrt x\)-moment \(\mathfrak d_i=\frac{B}{\sqrt2\,\Nin}\int\sqrt x\,\rho_i(dx)\), in the normalization of the main-text kernel (7). Neither the existence of \(\rho_i\) nor the limit \(\ridge\downarrow0\) is proved; the phase-comparison chapter adopts the zero-ridge kernels as assumptions.
Proof sketch.
With identity input covariance the input trace equation is algebraic. The second equation of (SB.2) becomes \(\tilde\sigma(-s)=(\Nin/B)/(s+s_1(-s))\), so the input factor of the kernel (SC.4) at \(\lambda=1\) is
\[ \frac{1}{z+s_1(-z)}=\frac{B\,\tilde\sigma(-z)}{\Nin}, \]
and substituting it gives (i). Part (ii) is the sum over \(j\) of (SC.12), since \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d_i\) and \(\CMscr Q_i=\sum_j\CMscr Q_{ij}\); its error terms, summed over the rows against a bounded function \(\psi(\mu_i)\), are controlled by (SC.13) with \(\phi(\mu,\lambda)=\psi(\mu)\).
At \(\bmom=0\) the source factor equals the coupling. There is one weight, so \(\Lw=\Fw=\Jfun\), and with \(q(-z)=z\sigma\tilde\sigma\) and the coupling \(\tilde s_1\sigma=q\Jfun(q)\) of Figure 2,
\[ z\,\Jfun\big(q(-z)\big)\,\tilde\sigma(-z)=\frac{q\Jfun(q)}{\sigma}=\tilde s_1(-z), \]
which turns (i) into (iii).
Part (iv) integrates a Stieltjes representation in \(u\). If \((z+\tilde s_1(-z)\mu_i)^{-1}=\int\rho_i(dx)/(x+z)\) with \(\rho_i\) a probability measure, then the integrand of (iii) is \(\tilde s_1\mu_i/(z+\tilde s_1\mu_i)=\int x\,\rho_i(dx)/(x+z)\), and the scalar integral
\[ \frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{x+\ridge+u^2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+\ridge}} \]
gives (iv) after exchanging the two integrals.
