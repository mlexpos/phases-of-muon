When \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\), the projected row risk is \(\CMscr Q_i(t)=\norm{\Dt^\T u_i}^2=\sum_j\CMscr Q_{ij}(t)\), and the row coefficient \(\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) of (SD.75) is the deterministic counterpart, in aggregate over the rows, of the long-run variance of the row \({\mathsf S}^\T u_i\) of the update: \(B\) times the deterministic integrated covariance \(\overline\Gamma[u_i\tensor u_i,\Id](\ridge,\ridge)\) of Section G.3.
Corollary SE.1 (Half-anisotropic input)
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem SE.1 with \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\). Let \(\mathfrak d_i\defeq\mathfrak d(\mu_i,1)\) and let \(\mathfrak v_i\defeq\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) be the row coefficient of (SD.75). Then the row equations
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.13} \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\big(B\fQ_i(\macrotime)\big)=-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\macrotime)}}\,B\fQ_i(\macrotime)+\macrostep^2\mathfrak v_i,\qquad \fRisk(\macrotime)=\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i(\macrotime),\qquad \fQ_i(0)=\norm{\Delta_0^\T u_i}^2, \end{equation}
have a unique solution on \([0,T_\star]\), given by \(\fQ_i=\sum_j\fQ_{ij}\) with \((\fQ_{ij})\) the solution of (SE.7). The row measures \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)\defeq\sum_i\CMscr Q_i(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor)\,\delta_{\mu_i}\) and \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)\defeq\sum_i\fQ_i(\macrotime)\,\delta_{\mu_i}\) on \([0,C_\Sigma]\) satisfy \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)}_\BL\to0\) in probability, and the same holds for the measures weighted by \(\mu\).
The corollary is the dynamical statement behind the half-anisotropic section of the main text, where \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\) and only the output covariance is anisotropic. There the natural unknowns are the projected row risks \(\fQ_i=\sum_j\fQ_{ij}\), the deterministic counterparts of \(\CMscr Q_i\), and the risk \(\fRisk=\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i\) involves only them. The corollary says that the row risks obey a closed system, the row recursion (14) in the macroscopic time \(\macrotime=t/B\): dividing (SE.13) by \(B\) and taking \(B\) steps of size \(\lr=\macrostep/B\) per unit of time, the change over one step is \(-2\lr\,\mathfrak d_i\fQ_i/\sqrt{\fRisk}+\lr^2\mathfrak v_i\). The algorithm follows this system in aggregate over the rows: the row measure, which places \(\CMscr Q_i\) at \(\mu_i\), is close in the bounded-Lipschitz distance to its deterministic counterpart, and its \(\mu\)-weighted version gives the risk. As in Theorem SE.1, nothing is claimed about a single row.
Proof sketch.
With an isotropic input side, both kernels are constant along each row, so summing the mode equations over the columns closes them on the rows. The drift kernel \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)\) is evaluated at \(\lambda_j=1\) for every \(j\), so \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d_i\) (Corollary SC.2). The mode volatilities of a row are equal, \(\mathfrak v_{ij}=\mathfrak v_i/\Nin\), and sum to the row coefficient \(\mathfrak v_i\) (Proposition SD.4). Summing (SE.7) over \(j\) and dividing by \(B\) therefore gives, exactly,
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\Big(B\sum_j\fQ_{ij}\Big) =-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,B\sum_j\fQ_{ij}+\macrostep^2\sum_j\mathfrak v_{ij} =-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,B\fQ_i+\macrostep^2\mathfrak v_i , \]
with \(\fRisk=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\fQ_{ij}=\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i\) and \(\fQ_i(0)=\sum_j(u_i^\T\Delta_0v_j)^2=\norm{\Delta_0^\T u_i}^2\) by Parseval’s identity in the basis \((v_j)\) (Lemma SA.1).
The row system has a unique solution on \([0,T_\star]\). The mode system has a solution on \([0,T_\star]\) (Proposition SE.1), so its row sums solve the row system there. The row system is locally Lipschitz where its risk is positive, so this solution is its only one (Picard–Lindelöf).
The row measures are images of the mode measures, and the projection \((\mu,\lambda)\mapsto\mu\) preserves the bounded-Lipschitz norm on measures carried by the segment \(\{\lambda=1\}\). Both \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)\) and \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)\) live on that segment, and their images under the projection are the row measures, since \(\CMscr Q_i=\sum_j\CMscr Q_{ij}\). A test on the rectangle restricts to a test \(\phi(\cdot,1)\) of the same size and Lipschitz constant, and a test \(\psi\) of \(\mu\) extends to the test \(\psi(\mu)\) on the rectangle, so the two norms coincide. Theorem SE.1(ii) therefore gives the row statement, and Theorem SE.1(iii) gives the \(\mu\)-weighted one, because \(\mu\lambda=\mu\) on the segment. All the probabilistic work is that of Theorem SE.1, sketched in Section G.4: the block comparison of the algorithm with the frozen-state model, the identification of the drift and the volatility, and the persistence of diffuseness.
