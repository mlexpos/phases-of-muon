Lemma SI.12 (Two finite forcing integrals)
Let \(p>1\), \(q>0\), \(C=C_{p,q}\) and \(A=A_p\).
  1. (i)
    If \(p=2q+1\), then for every \(N\ge1\)
    \[ \big|J_N(p,q)-a_q\,q\log N\big|\le2C+\frac{qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q}+\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}} . \]
  2. (ii)
    If \(p=q+1\), then for every \(N\ge4\cdot4^{1/q}\)
    \[ \sqrt A\,4^{-q/2}N^{q/2}\le J_N(p,q)\le3C\,N^{q/2} . \]
Proof.
Put \(L\defeq N^q\). Every rate of \(f_{N,q}\) is at least \(2/L\), so \(f_{N,q}(u)\le f_{N,q}(L)e^{-2(u-L)/L}\) for \(u\ge L\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.54} \int_L^\infty\sqrt{f_{N,q}}\,\dif u\le L\sqrt{f_{N,q}(L)}\le L\,C(1+L)^{-r_q} . \end{equation}
For (i), \(r_q=1\), so (SI.54) is at most \(C\). As in the proof of Lemma SI.10(ii),
\begin{gather*} 0\le\int_0^L\big(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}-\sqrt{f_{N,q}}\big)\,\dif u\le\frac{LN^{-q}}{\sqrt{2(p-1)}}=\frac1{\sqrt{2(p-1)}},\\ \Big|\int_0^L\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\,\dif u-a_q\log L\Big|\le C+\frac{qM_{p,q}2^{-p/q}}{a_q} , \end{gather*}
where the second bound splits the integral at \(1\) and uses \(\sqrt{f_{\infty,q}}\le C\) on \([0,1]\). Since \(a_q\log L=a_qq\log N\), adding the three errors proves (i).
For (ii), \(r_q=\frac12\). The upper envelope gives
\[ \int_0^L\sqrt{f_{N,q}}\,\dif u\le C\int_0^L(1+u)^{-1/2}\,\dif u\le2C\sqrt L , \]
and (SI.54) is at most \(C\sqrt L\), which proves the upper bound. For the lower bound put \(V\defeq(N/4)^q\ge4\). The lower envelope on \([0,V]\) gives
\[ J_N(p,q)\ge2\sqrt A\big(\sqrt{1+V}-1\big)\ge\sqrt A\sqrt V=\sqrt A\,4^{-q/2}N^{q/2} , \]
using \(\sqrt V\ge2\).
□
