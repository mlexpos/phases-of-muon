Theorem SG.3 (Volatility crossover and bulk profile)
Assume Assumption S4 and let \(i=i_N\) be a row sequence.
  1. (i)
    (Bulk.) If \(i/N\to x\in(0,1]\) and \(\ell=\ell_N\) are integers with \(\ell/\gamma\to\tau\in(0,1)\), then
    \[ \overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\longrightarrow C_{\alpha,x}(\tau),\qquad \frac{\mathfrak v_i(0)}{\gamma}\longrightarrow\Gamma_\alpha(x), \]
    locally uniformly in \(x\in(0,1]\) and \(\tau\in(0,1)\).
  2. (ii)
    Let \(0<\alpha\le1\), let \(i\to\infty\), and put
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.33} i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\defeq c_0^{2/\alpha}\,i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}=\begin{cases}N\big[(1-\alpha)/(\sw2\gamma\log^2\gamma)\big]^{1/\alpha},&\alpha<1,\\ 9B/(4\sw2\log^3\gamma),&\alpha=1.\end{cases} \end{equation}
    If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to y\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sw2\,\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1+2c_0y^{\alpha/2}+y^\alpha\), uniformly for \(y\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to0\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to\infty\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac{\gamma(i/N)^\alpha\big[\log((N/i)^\alpha)\big]^2}{1-\alpha}\quad(\alpha<1),\qquad \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac49\,\frac iB\,\big[\log(N/i)\big]^3\quad(\alpha=1). \]
  3. (iii)
    Let \(\alpha>1\) and \(i\to\infty\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to X_0\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X_0)\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to0\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to\infty\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac{4K_\alpha^2}{\alpha-1}\,\frac iB . \]
Proof of Theorem SG.3(ii).
By Lemma SG.4 we take \(c_N=1\); the indices do not depend on \(c_N\). At \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to y\in(0,\infty)\), (SG.33) gives
\[ \gamma x^\alpha=\frac{(1-\alpha)\,y^\alpha}{\sw2\log^2\gamma}(1+o(1))\quad(\alpha<1),\qquad \gamma x=\frac{9y}{4\sw2\log^3\gamma}(1+o(1))\quad(\alpha=1), \]
and \(\log(N/i)=\alpha^{-1}\log\gamma+O(\log\log\gamma)\), respectively \(\log\gamma+O(\log\log\gamma)\). Hence \(b_{i,N}\to y^{\alpha/2}/\sqrt{\sw2}\) by (SG.47), and Lemma SG.20 gives \(\sw2\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1+2c_0y^{\alpha/2}+y^\alpha\). If the ratio tends to zero, then \(b\to0\), and if it tends to infinity with \(i/N\to0\), then \(b\to\infty\): the functions \(x^\alpha[\log(x^{-\alpha})]^2\) and \(x[\log(1/x)]^3\) are increasing for small \(x\), so comparison with a fixed multiple of \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\) gives the extended limits. In the latter case \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim b^2\), which is the displayed equivalent. For bulk rows the equivalent \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\) is part (i). Uniformity on compact sets of \(y\) follows by passing to convergent subsequences of the ratio.
□
Proof of Theorem SG.3(i).
By Lemma SG.4 we take \(c_N=1\). Write
\[ g_i(m)\defeq\sqrt{f_i(m)(1-f_i(m))}\sqrt{(\gamma-m)/m}, \]
so that \(W_i=\gamma^{-1/2}g_i\vartheta_N\). Let \(i/N\to x\in(0,1]\) and \(\ell/\gamma\to\tau\in(0,1)\), and use the coordinates \(m_1=\gamma p\), \(m_2=\gamma p'\).
On compact sets of \(p\) the integrand converges. By Lemma SG.6(i), (ii) and (SG.42), \(c_N\Lambda_N(\gamma p)N^{-\alpha}\to V_\alpha(p)\) together with its logarithmic derivative, so \(f_i(\gamma p)\to f_x(p)\), \(g_i(\gamma p)\to A_x(p)\) with \(A_x\) of Lemma SG.21, and
\[ \vartheta_N(\gamma p)=1/E(\gamma p)+O(1/\gamma). \]
On the shell \(m_2=m_1+\ell+O(1)\), the ratio \(\bmom^{2\ell}Q_{m_2}/Q_{m_1}\) stays in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), and the divided difference of \(r_\bmom\) gives \(m_1-\mathsf M_\ell=O(1)\). Consequently
\[ \frac{\mathsf T_N}{\gamma}\to t(p,p'),\qquad \Delta^N_\ell\to\Delta_{\mathrm c}(p,p'),\qquad p'=p+\tau, \]
in the notation of Lemma SG.21. With Lemma SG.22(iii) for \(n=\gamma\), the integral (SG.22) restricted to compact sets of \(p\) tends to
\[ \int_0^{1-\tau}\frac{A_x(p)A_x(p+\tau)}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}(p,p+\tau)}\,\dif p=C_{\alpha,x}(\tau), \]
by the identity of Lemma SG.21.
The compact restriction is removed by bounds uniform in \(x\ge x_0>0\) and \(\tau\ge\tau_0>0\). Since \(\mu_j\le\mu_i\) for \(j\ge i\) and \((i/j)^\alpha\ge x_0^\alpha\) for \(j\le N\), the terms \(N/2\le j\le N\) of \(m_N\) give \(m\ge c\,x_0^\alpha\gamma f_i(m)\), hence
\[ g_i(m)\le C_{x_0},\qquad g_i(m)\le C_{x_0}\big(1-m/\gamma\big)\ \ (m\ge\gamma/2),\qquad \vartheta_N(m)\le C\big(1+m^{-1}+(\gamma-m)^{-1}\big), \]
the second bound by \(1-f_i(m)\le C(\gamma-m)/\gamma\) as in Lemma SG.17. With Lemma SG.22(i),(ii), the integrand is dominated by \(C\gamma^{-1}g_i(m_1)g_i(m_2)\vartheta_N(m_1)\vartheta_N(m_2)\mathsf D_\ell\). The three bounds give \(g_i\vartheta_N\le C_{x_0}(1+m^{-1})\) on \((0,\gamma)\) and \(g_i\vartheta_N\le C_{x_0}\) on \(m\ge\gamma/2\). Along the shell, this majorant has total \(O(\delta+\gamma^{-1}\log\gamma)\) on \(1<m_1<\delta\gamma\) and \(O(\delta)\) on \(m_2>(1-\delta)\gamma\); on \(m_1\le1\) the factor \(\min(m_1,1)^{1/2}\) of \(\mathsf D_\ell\) compensates the factor \(m_1^{-1}\) of \(\vartheta_N\). Off the shell, on \(\abs{m_1-m_2+\ell}\ge\delta\gamma\), the exponential factor of Lemma SG.22(i) gives \(\mathsf D_\ell\le C\min(m_1,1)^{1/4}\min(m_2,1)^{1/4}e^{-\abs{\log\bmom}\delta\gamma/2}\), and with \(g_i\vartheta_N\le C_{x_0}(1+m^{-1})\) the majorant has total at most
\[ C\gamma\,e^{-\abs{\log\bmom}\delta\gamma/2}\longrightarrow0 . \]
The rest of the domain, \(m_1\ge\delta\gamma\), \(m_2\le(1-\delta)\gamma\), \(\abs{m_1-m_2+\ell}<\delta\gamma\), lies in a compact set of \(p\), on which the integrand converges. This proves the first limit, locally uniformly.
Large lags are negligible. For \(\ell\ge\gamma+C\), write \(\mathsf a\defeq\bmom^\ell\). Stein’s lemma [96, Lemma 1], \(\EE[g\phi(g)]=\EE[\phi'(g)]\), applied to \(\phi(g)=g^3(1+sg^2)^{-1}\) gives
\[ \EE\frac{g^4}{1+sg^2}=3\,\EE\frac{g^2}{1+sg^2}-2s\,\EE\frac{g^4}{(1+sg^2)^2}\le3\,\EE\frac{g^2}{1+sg^2}\qquad(s\ge0), \]
and with \(s=q_1\emaw a^2\), \(\emaw a\le\emaw0\) and \(q_1\Fw(q_1)=m_1\) this gives \(\mathsf M_\ell\le3\emaw0^2\mathsf a^2q_2m_1\); also \(\mathsf D_\ell\le\mathsf a\sqrt{q_1q_2}\,\Fw(q_1)\) gives \(\mathsf D_\ell^2\le\mathsf a^2q_2m_1\sw2\). Since \(q_2/m_2\le Q_\gamma/\gamma\), these give \(1-\Delta^N_\ell\le C\mathsf a^2Q_\gamma\le1/2\), and \(\mathsf D_\ell\le C\mathsf a\sqrt{Q_\gamma}\min(m_1,1)^{1/2}\min(m_2,1)^{1/2}\). The bounds on \(g_i\) and \(\vartheta_N\) give \(\int_0^\gamma\min(m,1)^{1/2}g_i\vartheta_N\,\dif m\le C\gamma\), so
\[ \overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\le C\gamma\,\bmom^\ell\sqrt{Q_\gamma}\le C\gamma\,e^{-\abs{\log\bmom}(\ell-\gamma)} . \]
For \(\eta>0\) the lags beyond \((1+\eta)\gamma\) therefore contribute \(o(\gamma)\), and the lags in \([(1-\eta)\gamma,(1+\eta)\gamma]\) and in \([0,\eta\gamma]\) contribute at most \(3\eta\gamma+O(1)\), since \(\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\le1\) (Lemma SG.3). The Riemann sum \(\gamma^{-1}\sum_\ell\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\) over the remaining lags converges to the integral of \(C_{\alpha,x}\), which gives \(\mathfrak v_i(0)/\gamma\to2\int_0^1C_{\alpha,x}=\Gamma_\alpha(x)\).
Positivity and continuity of \(\Gamma_\alpha\) follow: \(f_x\) is strictly increasing, so \(C_{\alpha,x}>0\), and the bounds above are locally uniform in \(x\).
□
Proof of the volatility part of Theorem SG.1 and of Theorem SG.3(iii).
Take \(c_N=1\) and a row sequence \(i\to\infty\) along which \(i/N\) converges; as for the drift, this suffices. Let \(\alpha\le1\). If \(i/N\to x>0\), part (i) gives \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(x)\) and \(\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(x)\), since \(\gamma\to\infty\). If \(i/N\to0\), Lemma SG.21 gives \(\sqrt{\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)}=b_{i,N}(1+o(1))\), so \(\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i\) is the quadratic of Lemma SG.20 up to a factor \(1+o(1)\), and Lemma SG.20 gives the claim.
Let \(\alpha>1\). If \(i/N\to x>0\), then \(X\to\infty\), part (i) gives \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(x)\), and by Lemma SG.23
\[ \widehat{\mathfrak v}_i=G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\frac{(\alpha-1)\Gamma_\alpha(x)}{4K_\alpha^2x}\sim\frac{4K_\alpha X}{\alpha-1}\cdot\frac{(\alpha-1)\Gamma_\alpha(x)}{4K_\alpha^2x}=\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(x), \]
since \(X/(K_\alpha x)=\gamma\). If \(i/N\to0\), the second factor of \(\widehat{\mathfrak v}_i\) tends to one, and after a further subsequence along which \(X\) tends to \(0\), to a positive number, or to infinity, Lemma SG.23 gives the three cases. The endpoint statements of Theorem SG.3(iii) are those of Lemma SG.23, the case \(X\to\infty\) without \(i/N\to0\) through the uniform statement.
□
