Theorem SB.2 (Gram closure)
Under Assumption S3, in the frozen-state model with a weight sequence \(w\) satisfying (SB.27), let \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1)(z)\) be the solution of the weighted system (SB.2) for the weights \(w\) and the spectral measures (SA.41), and let \(\Rbar,\Rtbar\) be as in (SB.3). Then for every compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.28} \sup_{s\in I}\Big(\norm{\EE R(-s)-\Rbar(-s)}+\norm{\EE\widetilde R(-s)-\Rtbar(-s)}\Big)\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
Proof of Theorem SB.2.
We prove the output-side statement. The input side is the same argument applied to \(\Mt^\T=\sum_{j,a}\mathsf h_{ja}\,\mathsf v_{ja}\tensor\mathsf u_{ja}\), with the roles of \((\Sigmaout,z_{ja},\Ahat)\) and \((\Sigmain,w_{ja},\Ahat^\T)\) exchanged; this exchange leaves Assumption S3 and every lemma above invariant, and it produces the same scalars \((\sigma_B,\tilde\sigma_B,q_B)\). Fix \(s\in I\); all bounds below are uniform in \(s\in I\).
Step 1: the deletion identity in expectation. The starting point is the third identity of Lemma SB.3,
\[ \Id-sR(-s)=R(-s)\Mt\Mt^\T . \]
By (SB.26), the right side is a sum over samples,
\[ R(-s)\Mt\Mt^\T=\sum_{j\ge0}\sum_{a=1}^B\big(\mathsf h_{ja}R(-s)\Mt\mathsf v_{ja}\big)\tensor\mathsf u_{ja} , \]
and (SB.15), applied to each sample with the deleted buffer \(M_0^{(ja)}\), rewrites each term through the deleted resolvents:
\[ R(-s)\Mt\Mt^\T=\sum_{j\ge0}\sum_{a=1}^B\big(-\alpha_{3,ja}R_0^{(ja)}\mathsf u_{ja}+\alpha_{1,ja}\mathsf X_0^{(ja)}\mathsf v_{ja}\big)\tensor\mathsf u_{ja} . \]
Taking expectations and using exchangeability within each age,
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.38} \Id-s\,\mathcal P(s)=B\sum_{j\ge0}\EE\big[-\alpha_{3,j}\,R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)+\alpha_{1,j}\,\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]. \end{equation}
Step 2: the Gram term. Fix \(j\) and write
\[ \varpi\defeq\emaw j,\qquad x\defeq\varpi^2q_B\in[0,q_I],\qquad \psi\defeq\psi_x . \]
Adding and subtracting the target coefficient \(\check\alpha_{3,j}\) of (SB.33) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.39} \begin{aligned} B\,\EE\big[-\alpha_{3,j}R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big] ={}&B\,\EE\big[(-\alpha_{3,j}+\check\alpha_{3,j})R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]\\ &+s\,\varpi^2\tilde\sigma_B\cdot B\,\EE\big[\psi(d_j)\,R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]. \end{aligned} \end{equation}
The first term of (SB.39) is a replacement error, bounded by the Bessel inequality. Put \(e\defeq-\alpha_{3,j}+\check\alpha_{3,j}\) and write \(\mathcal G=\mathcal G_{j1}\). We use the two identities
\[ B\,\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j=\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z_j\tensor z_j)\Sigmaout^{1/2},\qquad \EE_{\mathcal G}[(z_j\tensor z_j)\,e]=\EE_{\mathcal G}[(z_j\tensor z_j-\Id)\,e]+\Id\,\EE_{\mathcal G}e . \]
With Lemma SB.7 for the centered part and \(\abs{\EE_{\mathcal G}e}\le\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}\) for the second, they give
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.40} \big\|B\,\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\,e]\big\|\le C_\Sigma\big(\sqrt2+1\big)\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}. \end{equation}
The matrix \(R_0^{(j)}\) is built from the samples other than \((j,1)\) and satisfies \(\norm{R_0^{(j)}}\le1/s\), and \(\EE\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}\le\norm e_2\) by Jensen’s inequality. Hence, by the tower property, (SB.40) and Lemma SB.12,
\[ \Big\|B\,\EE\big[e\,R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]\Big\| =\Big\|\EE\big[R_0^{(j)}\,B\,\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\,e]\big]\Big\| \le\frac{(1+\sqrt2)C_\Sigma}{s_-}\norm e_2\le C_I\,\varpi^2e_B . \]
The second derivative of \(\psi_x\) is bounded by \(6\), by a scaling computation. For \(x>0\),
\[ \psi_x(d)=\frac1x\,\chi(\sqrt x\,d),\qquad \chi(y)\defeq\frac{y^2}{1+y^2}, \]
so that
\[ \psi_x''(d)=\chi''(\sqrt x\,d),\qquad \chi''(y)=\frac{2-6y^2}{(1+y^2)^3},\qquad \abs{\chi''(y)}\le\frac{2+6y^2}{(1+y^2)^3}\le6 , \]
and at \(x=0\) the second derivative is the constant \(\psi_0''=2\).
The second term of (SB.39) factorizes by independence and is a Gaussian response. Since \(R_0^{(j)}\) is independent of \((z_j,w_j)\), and with \(d=z^\T\Ahat w\),
\[ \begin{gathered} B\,\EE\big[\psi(d_j)R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]=\mathcal P_j(s)\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}\,\EE[(z\tensor z)\,\psi(d)]\,\Sigmaout^{1/2},\\ \EE[(z\tensor z)\,\psi(d)]=\Id\,\EE\psi(d)+\EE[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,\psi(d)]. \end{gathered} \]
The second summand is bounded by (SB.23) with \(A=\Ahat\) and \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\):
\[ \norm{\EE[(z\tensor z-\Id)\,\psi(d)]}_\F\le\norm{\psi_x''}_\infty\norm{\Ahat}\le6\norm{\Ahat}. \]
The first summand equals \(\Id\,\Jfun(x)\) up to \(r_B\) by Lemma SB.13. Combining the two summands with \(\norm{\mathcal P_j(s)}\le1/s_-\), \(\norm{\Sigmaout}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(s\tilde\sigma_B\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\) from (SB.31),
\[ \begin{aligned} &\Big\|s\,\varpi^2\tilde\sigma_B\cdot B\,\EE\big[\psi(d_j)R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]-s\,\varpi^2\tilde\sigma_B\,\Jfun(\varpi^2q_B)\,\mathcal P_j(s)\Sigmaout\Big\|\\ &\qquad\le s\tilde\sigma_B\,\varpi^2\,\frac{C_\Sigma}{s_-}\big(r_B+6\norm{\Ahat}\big)\le C_I\varpi^2\big(r_B+\norm{\Ahat}\big). \end{aligned} \]
Step 3: the mixed term. The mixed term splits in the same way as the Gram term, with \(f\defeq f_x\) and \(x=\varpi^2q_B\):
\[ \begin{aligned} B\,\EE\big[\alpha_{1,j}\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big] ={}&B\,\EE\big[(\alpha_{1,j}-\check\alpha_{1,j})\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]\\ &+\varpi\cdot B\,\EE\big[f(d_j)\,\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]. \end{aligned} \]
The replacement error is bounded by the first inequality of Lemma SB.7. Put \(e\defeq\alpha_{1,j}-\check\alpha_{1,j}\), and use
\[ B\,\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j=\Sigmain^{1/2}(w_j\tensor z_j)\Sigmaout^{1/2} \]
and \(\norm{\mathsf X_0^{(j)}}\le(2\sqrt{s_-})^{-1}\). The tower property, Jensen’s inequality and Lemma SB.12 give
\[ \begin{aligned} \Big\|B\,\EE\big[e\,\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]\Big\| &\le\frac{1}{2\sqrt{s_-}}\,\EE\Big\|\Sigmain^{1/2}\,\EE_{\mathcal G}[(w_j\tensor z_j)\,e]\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Big\|_\F\\ &\le\frac{C_\Sigma}{2\sqrt{s_-}}\norm e_2\le C_I\,\varpi\,e_B . \end{aligned} \]
The first and third derivatives of \(f=f_x\) are bounded, by a scaling computation:
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.41} \norm{f'}_\infty\le1,\qquad\norm{f'''}_\infty\le36x\le36q_I . \end{equation}
Indeed, for \(x>0\),
\[ f_x(d)=\frac{1}{\sqrt x}\,\phi(\sqrt x\,d),\qquad \phi(y)\defeq\frac{y}{1+y^2}, \]
so that, at \(y=\sqrt x\,d\),
\[ f_x'(d)=\phi'(y)=\frac{1-y^2}{(1+y^2)^2},\qquad f_x'''(d)=x\,\phi'''(y)=x\,\frac{-6y^4+36y^2-6}{(1+y^2)^4}. \]
The two numerators are bounded by the elementary inequalities
\[ \abs{1-y^2}\le(1+y^2)^2,\qquad 6y^4+36y^2+6\le36(1+y^2)^2 . \]
At \(x=0\) the function \(f_0\) is the identity, whose first derivative is \(1\) and whose third derivative vanishes.
The main part is a Gaussian response whose leading term carries \(\Ahat^\T\). Independence of \(\mathsf X_0^{(j)}\) from \((z_j,w_j)\) gives
\[ B\,\EE\big[f(d_j)\,\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]=\EE\big[\mathsf X_0^{(j)}\big]\,\Sigmain^{1/2}\,\EE[(w\tensor z)\,f(d)]\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}, \]
and (SB.22) applied to \((w,z,\Ahat^\T)\), with \(\norm{\Ahat^\T}_\F=1\) and the derivative bounds (SB.41), gives
\[ \norm{\EE[(w\tensor z)\,f(d)]}\le\norm{\Ahat^\T\,\EE f'(d)}+\norm{f'''}_\infty\norm{\Ahat}^2 \le\norm{\Ahat}+36q_I\norm{\Ahat}^2 . \]
Here the spectral diffuseness \(\norm{\Ahat}\to0\) is used in operator norm: the leading term \(\Ahat^\T\,\EE f'(d)\) of the mixed response has Frobenius norm \(\abs{\EE f'(d)}\) but operator norm at most \(\norm{\Ahat}\). Combining the two parts with \(\norm{\EE[\mathsf X_0^{(j)}]}\le(2\sqrt{s_-})^{-1}\) and \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\),
\[ \Big\|B\,\EE\big[\alpha_{1,j}\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf u_j)\big]\Big\|\le C_I\,\varpi\,\big(e_B+\norm{\Ahat}+\norm{\Ahat}^2\big). \]
Step 4: summation and the closure identity. Summing the bounds of Steps 2 and 3 over \(j\) in (SB.38), with the weight sums of (SA.18),
\[ \Big\|\Id-s\,\mathcal P(s)-\sum_{j\ge0}s\,\emaw j^2\tilde\sigma_B\,\Jfun(\emaw j^2q_B)\,\mathcal P_j(s)\Sigmaout\Big\| \le C_I\big[\sw2(e_B+r_B+\norm{\Ahat})+e_B+\norm{\Ahat}+\norm{\Ahat}^2\big]. \]
Next we replace \(\mathcal P_j\) by \(\mathcal P\), at the cost of one deletion per age:
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.42} \norm{\mathcal P(s)-\mathcal P_j(s)}\le\frac{C_I\,\emaw j}{B}. \end{equation}
Taking \(\EE_{\mathcal G_{j1}}\) in (SB.14) and then the full expectation writes \(\mathcal P(s)-\mathcal P_j(s)\) as a sum of four terms, each an outer factor \(R_0^{(j)}\) or \(\mathsf X_0^{(j)}\) on either side of a conditional moment of a rank-one matrix. The outer factors satisfy \(\norm{R_0^{(j)}}\le1/s_-\) and \(\norm{\mathsf X_0^{(j)}}\le(2\sqrt{s_-})^{-1}\). The conditional moments are bounded by (SB.40) and by its two analogues
\[ B\,\norm{\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf v\tensor\mathsf v)\,e]}\le(1+\sqrt2)C_\Sigma\norm e_{2,\mathcal G},\qquad B\,\norm{\EE_{\mathcal G}[(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v)\,e]}_\F\le C_\Sigma\norm e_{2,\mathcal G}, \]
which follow from Lemma SB.7 in the same way, applied with \(e=\alpha_{3,j},\alpha_{1,j},\alpha_{2,j}\). Since \((d_j,\mathsf N_j)\) are functions of the deleted sample only, Lemma SB.5 and Lemma SB.6 bound the conditional \(L^2\) norms of these three coefficients by \(C_I\emaw j^2\), \(C_I\emaw j\) and \(C_I\emaw j^2\). Multiplying the three kinds of bounds gives (SB.42).
The replacement of \(\mathcal P_j\) by \(\mathcal P\) costs at most \(C_I\sw3/B\). Since \(s\tilde\sigma_B\Jfun\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\) and \(\norm{\Sigmaout}\le C_\Sigma\), (SB.42) gives
\[ \Big\|\sum_{j\ge0}s\,\emaw j^2\tilde\sigma_B\,\Jfun(\emaw j^2q_B)\,\big(\mathcal P_j(s)-\mathcal P(s)\big)\Sigmaout\Big\| \le\sum_{j\ge0}C_\Sigma^2\gamma_\star\,\emaw j^2\,\frac{C_I\emaw j}{B}=\frac{C_I\sw3}{B}. \]
Writing
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.43} a_B(s)\defeq s\,\tilde\sigma_B(s)\,\Fw(q_B(s)),\qquad c_B(s)\defeq s\,\sigma_B(s)\,\Fw(q_B(s)), \end{equation}
and recalling \(\Fw(q)=\sum_j\emaw j^2\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\), we have shown
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.44} \begin{gathered} E_B(s)\defeq\Id-s\,\mathcal P(s)-a_B(s)\,\mathcal P(s)\Sigmaout,\\ \sup_{s\in I}\norm{E_B(s)}\le C_I\big[e_B+r_B+\norm{\Ahat}+\norm{\Ahat}^2+B^{-1}\big]\longrightarrow0 , \end{gathered} \end{equation}
by Lemma SB.12 and Lemma SB.13 and Assumption S3.
The closure error controls the distance to the candidate resolvent. Since \(\mathcal P(s)(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)=\Id-E_B(s)\) and \(a_B\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.45} \norm{\mathcal P(s)-(s\Id+a_B(s)\Sigmaout)^{-1}}=\norm{E_B(s)(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1}}\le\frac{\norm{E_B(s)}}{s_-}. \end{equation}
The input-side argument gives in the same way
\[ \norm{\EE\widetilde R(-s)-(s\Id+c_B(s)\Sigmain)^{-1}}\le\frac{\norm{\widetilde E_B(s)}}{s_-}, \qquad \sup_{s\in I}\norm{\widetilde E_B(s)}\longrightarrow0 . \]
Step 5: identification of the branch. The tuple \((\sigma_B,\tilde\sigma_B,a_B,c_B)(s)\) is nonnegative and lies in the box of Theorem SB.1(iii) by (SB.31) and \(\Fw\le\sw2\). Its third and fourth residuals vanish by (SB.43), and its first residual is
\[ \begin{aligned} \Big|\sigma_B-\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1}\big)\Big| &=\Big|\frac1B\Tr\big(\Sigmaout(\mathcal P-(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1})\big)\Big|\\ &\le\frac{\Tr\Sigmaout}{B}\,\frac{\norm{E_B(s)}}{s_-}\le\frac{C_\Sigma\gamma_\star}{s_-}\norm{E_B(s)}, \end{aligned} \]
with the same bound for the second residual in terms of \(\widetilde E_B\). Both residuals tend to zero uniformly in \(s\in I\). By Theorem SB.1(iii), applied with \((\nu,\tilde\nu)=(\nu_{\mathrm{out}},\nu_{\mathrm{in}})\in\mathcal K\),
\[ \sup_{s\in I}\Big(\abs{\sigma_B-\sigma}+\abs{\tilde\sigma_B-\tilde\sigma}+\abs{a_B-\tilde s_1}+\abs{c_B-s_1}\Big)(-s)\longrightarrow0 . \]
The conclusion follows from the resolvent identity. For every \(s\in I\),
\[ \begin{aligned} &\norm{(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1}-(s\Id+\tilde s_1\Sigmaout)^{-1}}\\ &\qquad=\norm{(s\Id+a_B\Sigmaout)^{-1}\,(\tilde s_1-a_B)\Sigmaout\,(s\Id+\tilde s_1\Sigmaout)^{-1}} \le\frac{C_\Sigma\abs{a_B-\tilde s_1}}{s_-^2}, \end{aligned} \]
and together with (SB.45) and the triangle inequality,
\[ \sup_{s\in I}\norm{\EE R(-s)-\Rbar(-s)}\le\sup_{s\in I}\Big(\frac{\norm{E_B(s)}}{s_-}+\frac{C_\Sigma\abs{a_B(s)-\tilde s_1(-s)}}{s_-^2}\Big)\longrightarrow0 . \]
The input side is identical.
