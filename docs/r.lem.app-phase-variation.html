Lemma SI.5 (Sum and integral)
Let \(p,q,a>0\) and \(f_a(x)\defeq x^{-p}e^{-ax^{-q}}\) for \(x>0\), \(f_a(0)\defeq0\). The function \(f_a\) has a unique maximum, at \(x=(qa/p)^{1/q}\), with value \(M_{p,q}a^{-p/q}\). For every integer \(N\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.20} \begin{gathered} \Big|\sum_{i=1}^Nf_a(i)-\int_0^Nf_a(x)\,\dif x\Big|\le2M_{p,q}a^{-p/q},\\ \int_0^Nf_a(x)\,\dif x=\frac{a^{(1-p)/q}}q\int_{a/N^q}^\infty y^{(p-1)/q-1}e^{-y}\,\dif y . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Proof.
The derivative is
\[ f_a'(x)=x^{-p-1}e^{-ax^{-q}}\big(-p+qa\,x^{-q}\big), \]
which changes sign once, from positive to negative, at \(x=(qa/p)^{1/q}\). Evaluating there gives the maximum. Since \(f_a\) tends to \(0\) at both ends of \((0,\infty)\), its total variation is twice the maximum. On each interval \([i-1,i]\),
\[ \Big|f_a(i)-\int_{i-1}^if_a(x)\,\dif x\Big|\le\int_{i-1}^i\abs{f_a(i)-f_a(x)}\,\dif x\le\int_{i-1}^i\abs{f_a'(y)}\,\dif y , \]
and summing over \(i\le N\) bounds the difference by the total variation. The substitution \(y=ax^{-q}\) gives the formula for the integral.
□
