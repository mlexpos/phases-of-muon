Lemma SB.4 (Rank-two deletion)
Let \(M=M_0+\mathsf h\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v\) with \(M_0\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), \(\mathsf h\in\R\), \(\mathsf u\in\R^{\Nout}\), \(\mathsf v\in\R^{\Nin}\), and \(s>0\). Write \(R_0=R_s(M_0)\), \(\widetilde R_0=\widetilde R_s(M_0)\), \(\mathsf X_0=\mathsf X_s(M_0)\), \(R=R_s(M)\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.12} \mathsf U\defeq\mathsf u^\T R_0\mathsf u,\qquad \mathsf V\defeq\mathsf v^\T\widetilde R_0\mathsf v,\qquad \mathsf C\defeq\mathsf u^\T\mathsf X_0\mathsf v,\qquad \Lambda\defeq(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2+s\mathsf h^2\,\mathsf U\mathsf V . \end{equation}
Then \(\Lambda>0\), and with
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.13} \alpha_1\defeq\frac{\mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)}{\Lambda},\qquad \alpha_2\defeq\frac{\mathsf h^2\,\mathsf U}{\Lambda},\qquad \alpha_3\defeq-\frac{s\mathsf h^2\,\mathsf V}{\Lambda}, \end{equation}
one has
\begin{align} R-R_0&=\alpha_3\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(R_0\mathsf u)-\alpha_1\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v) -\alpha_1\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(R_0\mathsf u)+\alpha_2\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v) , \tag{SB.14}\\ \mathsf h\,RM\mathsf v&=-\alpha_3\,R_0\mathsf u+\alpha_1\,\mathsf X_0\mathsf v . \tag{SB.15} \end{align}
Moreover, if \(\Lambda'\) denotes the quantity (SB.12) computed from \(M\) in place of \(M_0\) and \(-\mathsf h\) in place of \(\mathsf h\), then \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\), and consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.16} \Lambda^{-1}\le\Big(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt s}\,\abs{\mathsf h}\,\mathsf N\Big)^2,\qquad \mathsf N\defeq\norm{\mathsf u}^2+\norm{\mathsf v}^2 . \end{equation}
The same holds at \(z\in\mathbb S\) in place of \(s\), with \(x\defeq\Ree z\), in the following form. Define \(R_0,\widetilde R_0,\mathsf X_0,R\) and \(\mathsf U,\mathsf V,\mathsf C,\Lambda,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) by the formulas above with \(z\) in place of \(s\); they are complex. Then \(\Lambda\ne0\), (SB.14) and (SB.15) hold, \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.17} \abs\Lambda^{-1}\le\Big(1+\frac{\abs{\mathsf h}\,\mathsf N}{\sqrt x}\Big)^2 . \end{equation}
Proof.
For \(K\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) let
\[ \mathcal L(K)\defeq\begin{pmatrix}s\Id_{\Nout}&K\\K^\T&-\Id_{\Nin}\end{pmatrix}. \]
Block inversion with the Schur complement \(s\Id+KK^\T\) of the block \(-\Id\) gives, for every \(K\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SB.18} \mathcal L(K)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}R_s(K)&\mathsf X_s(K)\\ \mathsf X_s(K)^\T&-\Id_{\Nin}+K^\T R_s(K)K\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}R_s(K)&\mathsf X_s(K)\\ \mathsf X_s(K)^\T&-s\widetilde R_s(K)\end{pmatrix}, \end{equation}
where the second form uses Lemma SB.3. The rank-one change of \(M\) is a rank-two change of \(\mathcal L\):
\[ \mathcal L(M)=\mathcal L(M_0)+\mathsf h\big(\tilde{\mathsf u}\tensor\tilde{\mathsf v}+\tilde{\mathsf v}\tensor\tilde{\mathsf u}\big)=\mathcal L(M_0)+PSP^\T, \]
with
\[ \tilde{\mathsf u}\defeq\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf u\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \tilde{\mathsf v}\defeq\begin{pmatrix}0\\ \mathsf v\end{pmatrix},\qquad P\defeq\big[\tilde{\mathsf u}\ \ \tilde{\mathsf v}\big],\qquad S\defeq\mathsf h\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \]
Assume first \(\mathsf h\ne0\). By (SB.18),
\[ W\defeq P^\T\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}P=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf U&\mathsf C\\ \mathsf C&-s\mathsf V\end{pmatrix},\qquad S^{-1}+W=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf U&\mathsf C+\mathsf h^{-1}\\ \mathsf C+\mathsf h^{-1}&-s\mathsf V\end{pmatrix}. \]
The determinant of \(S^{-1}+W\) is expressed through \(\Lambda\):
\[ \det(S^{-1}+W)=-s\mathsf U\mathsf V-\big(\mathsf C+\mathsf h^{-1}\big)^2 =-\frac{s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V+(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2}{\mathsf h^2}=-\frac{\Lambda}{\mathsf h^2}. \]
Positivity of \(\Lambda\) follows from the positive definiteness of \(R_0\) and \(\widetilde R_0\). Indeed \(\mathsf U\ge0\) and \(\mathsf V\ge0\), with \(\mathsf U=0\) only if \(\mathsf u=0\) and \(\mathsf V=0\) only if \(\mathsf v=0\). If \(\mathsf u=0\) or \(\mathsf v=0\), then \(\mathsf C\) vanishes and \(\Lambda=1\); otherwise \(\Lambda\ge s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V>0\). In particular \(S^{-1}+W\) is invertible, and the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury formula [87, Section 2.1.4] gives
\[ \mathcal L(M)^{-1}=\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}-\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}P\,\Theta\,P^\T\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}, \]
where
\[ \Theta\defeq(S^{-1}+W)^{-1}=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\begin{pmatrix}s\mathsf h^2\mathsf V&\mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)\\ \mathsf h(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)&-\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_3&\alpha_1\\ \alpha_1&-\alpha_2\end{pmatrix}. \]
The two identities are read off from the blocks of the Woodbury identity. By (SB.18), the columns of \(\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}P\) are
\[ \mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}\tilde{\mathsf u}=\begin{pmatrix}R_0\mathsf u\\ \mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}\tilde{\mathsf v}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf X_0\mathsf v\\ -s\widetilde R_0\mathsf v\end{pmatrix}. \]
The upper-left block of the Woodbury identity is therefore
\[ R-R_0=-\big[R_0\mathsf u\ \ \mathsf X_0\mathsf v\big]\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_3&\alpha_1\\ \alpha_1&-\alpha_2\end{pmatrix}\big[R_0\mathsf u\ \ \mathsf X_0\mathsf v\big]^\T , \]
which, expanded, is (SB.14). The upper-right block is
\[ \begin{aligned} RM={}&\mathsf X_0+\alpha_3\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u)+s\alpha_1\,(R_0\mathsf u)\tensor(\widetilde R_0\mathsf v)\\ &-\alpha_1\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(\mathsf X_0^\T\mathsf u)-s\alpha_2\,(\mathsf X_0\mathsf v)\tensor(\widetilde R_0\mathsf v) . \end{aligned} \]
Multiplying on the right by \(\mathsf v\) and using the definitions of \(\mathsf C\) and \(\mathsf V\) in (SB.12) gives
\[ RM\mathsf v=R_0\mathsf u\,(\alpha_3\mathsf C+s\alpha_1\mathsf V)+\mathsf X_0\mathsf v\,(1-\alpha_1\mathsf C-s\alpha_2\mathsf V). \]
The two scalar coefficients simplify by the definitions (SB.13):
\[ \alpha_3\mathsf C+s\alpha_1\mathsf V=\frac{s\mathsf h\mathsf V}{\Lambda}\big(-\mathsf h\mathsf C+1+\mathsf h\mathsf C\big)=\frac{s\mathsf h\mathsf V}{\Lambda}=-\frac{\alpha_3}{\mathsf h}, \]
\[ 1-\alpha_1\mathsf C-s\alpha_2\mathsf V=\frac{\Lambda-\mathsf h\mathsf C(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)-s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U\mathsf V}{\Lambda}=\frac{(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)^2-\mathsf h\mathsf C(1+\mathsf h\mathsf C)}{\Lambda}=\frac{1+\mathsf h\mathsf C}{\Lambda}=\frac{\alpha_1}{\mathsf h}. \]
Multiplying \(RM\mathsf v\) by \(\mathsf h\) gives (SB.15). If \(\mathsf h=0\), then \(M=M_0\), so \(R=R_0\) and all three coefficients vanish; both identities hold.
The relation \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\) is a determinant identity. We factor
\[ \mathcal L(M)=\mathcal L(M_0)\big(\Id+\mathcal L(M_0)^{-1}PSP^\T\big). \]
Sylvester’s identity \(\det(\Id+XY)=\det(\Id+YX)\) [88, Section 0.8.5], applied to the second factor, gives
\[ \begin{aligned} \det\mathcal L(M)&=\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,\det(\Id_2+SW) =\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,\det(S)\det(S^{-1}+W)\\ &=\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,(-\mathsf h^2)\Big(-\frac{\Lambda}{\mathsf h^2}\Big) =\det\mathcal L(M_0)\,\Lambda . \end{aligned} \]
Both determinants are nonzero, because the block inverse (SB.18) exists for every \(K\). Exchanging the roles of \(M\) and \(M_0\) and replacing \(\mathsf h\) by \(-\mathsf h\) is the same situation, because \(M_0=M-\mathsf h\,\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf v\); it gives
\[ \det\mathcal L(M_0)=\det\mathcal L(M)\,\Lambda' , \]
and the two relations together give \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\).
The inverse bound follows from the norm bounds of Lemma SB.3 applied to \(M\). The primed quantities satisfy
\[ 0\le\mathsf U'\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf u}^2}{s},\qquad 0\le\mathsf V'\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf v}^2}{s},\qquad \abs{\mathsf C'}\le\frac{\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}}{2\sqrt s}\le\frac{\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}, \]
the last step by \(2\norm{\mathsf u}\norm{\mathsf v}\le\mathsf N\). Using \((1+x)^2+y^2\le(1+x+y)^2\) for \(x,y\ge0\) in the last step,
\[ \Lambda^{-1}=\Lambda'=(1-\mathsf h\mathsf C')^2+s\mathsf h^2\mathsf U'\mathsf V' \le\Big(1+\frac{\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N}{2\sqrt s}\Big)^2 \le\Big(1+\frac{3\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N}{4\sqrt s}\Big)^2 . \]
At \(z\in\mathbb S\) the argument is the same with \(s\) replaced by \(z\), except for the nonvanishing of \(\Lambda\) and the inverse bound. The block inverse (SB.18) holds with \(z\) in place of \(s\), since \(z\Id+KK^\T\) is invertible by Lemma SB.3. Sylvester’s identity is used first: directly from the definitions,
\[ \Id_2+SW=\begin{pmatrix}1+\mathsf h\mathsf C&-z\mathsf h\mathsf V\\ \mathsf h\mathsf U&1+\mathsf h\mathsf C\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det(\Id_2+SW)=\Lambda , \]
so \(\det\mathcal L(M)=\Lambda\det\mathcal L(M_0)\) with both determinants nonzero, and \(\Lambda\ne0\). For \(\mathsf h\ne0\) this gives \(\det(S^{-1}+W)=-\Lambda/\mathsf h^2\ne0\), and the Woodbury step, the reading of the blocks and the relation \(\Lambda\Lambda'=1\) are unchanged. For the inverse bound, Lemma SB.3 at \(z\) applied to \(M\) gives \(\abs{\mathsf U'}\le\norm{\mathsf u}^2/x\), \(\abs{\mathsf V'}\le\norm{\mathsf v}^2/x\) and \(\abs{\mathsf C'}\le\mathsf N/(4\sqrt x)\), and with \(\abs z\le\frac{\sqrt5}{2}x\) and \(\norm{\mathsf u}^2\norm{\mathsf v}^2\le\mathsf N^2/4\), writing \(t\defeq\abs{\mathsf h}\mathsf N/\sqrt x\),
\[ \abs\Lambda^{-1}=\abs{\Lambda'}\le\abs{1-\mathsf h\mathsf C'}^2+\abs z\,\mathsf h^2\abs{\mathsf U'}\abs{\mathsf V'} \le\Big(1+\frac t4\Big)^2+\frac{\sqrt5}{8}\,t^2\le(1+t)^2 , \]
the last step by \((1+a)^2+b^2\le(1+a+b)^2\) with \(a=t/4\), \(b=(\sqrt5/8)^{1/2}t\le\frac34t\).

theorem lem_app_de_deletion {m n : ℕ} (M₀ : Mat m n) (h : ℝ) (u : Vec m) (v : Vec n)
    (M : Mat m n) (s : ℝ) (hM : M = M₀ + h • outer u v) (hs : 0 < s) :
    0 < delLambda M₀ h u v s ∧
    Rs M s - Rs M₀ s =
      (alpha3 M₀ h u v s) • outer (Rs M₀ s *ᵥ u) (Rs M₀ s *ᵥ u)
        - (alpha1 M₀ h u v s) • outer (Rs M₀ s *ᵥ u) (Xs M₀ s *ᵥ v)
        - (alpha1 M₀ h u v s) • outer (Xs M₀ s *ᵥ v) (Rs M₀ s *ᵥ u)
        + (alpha2 M₀ h u v s) • outer (Xs M₀ s *ᵥ v) (Xs M₀ s *ᵥ v) ∧
    h • (Rs M s *ᵥ (M *ᵥ v)) =
      (-alpha3 M₀ h u v s) • (Rs M₀ s *ᵥ u) + (alpha1 M₀ h u v s) • (Xs M₀ s *ᵥ v) ∧
    delLambda M₀ h u v s * delLambda M (-h) u v s = 1 ∧
    (delLambda M₀ h u v s)⁻¹ ≤ (1 + 3 / (4 * Real.sqrt s) * |h| * (sqNorm u + sqNorm v)) ^ 2 := by
