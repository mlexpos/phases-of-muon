Proposition SF.12 (Zero ridge as the momentum vanishes)
In the square case write \(\gamma=n+y\) with an integer \(n\ge0\) and \(y\in[0,1)\), and put \(y^2C_0(y)\defeq0\) at \(y=0\). Then
\[ \lim_{\bmom\downarrow0}C_\bmom(\gamma)=C^\sharp(\gamma)\defeq\frac{n\,C_0(1)+y^2C_0(y)}{\gamma^2}+\frac{n(n-1)/2+ny}{\sqrt\pi\,\gamma^2}. \]
In particular \(C^\sharp(\gamma)=C_0(\gamma)\) for \(\gamma\le1\), while for \(\gamma\ge1\)
\[ \gamma^2\big(C^\sharp(\gamma)-C_0(\gamma)\big)=(n-1)\,C_0(1)+y^2C_0(y)+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)/2+(n-1)y}{\sqrt\pi}, \]
which is strictly positive for every \(\gamma>1\). Finally \(C^\sharp(\gamma)\to1/(2\sqrt\pi)\) as \(\gamma\to\infty\).
Proof.
The proof is dominated convergence in the participation representation. By Lemma SF.8 at \(\ridge=0\), in the square case where \(-\mathsf W'=1\) and \(\mathsf W(\gamma)=0\),
\[ C_\bmom(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma^2}\int_0^\gamma\mathsf i_\bmom(m)\,\dif m\qquad(\bmom\in(0,1)). \]
Domination. For \(\bmom\le\frac12\), Lemma SF.7 gives \(\mathsf g_\bmom<4\) and \(\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m)\le\sqrt{2\pi}\,m+\mathsf g'_\bmom<\sqrt{2\pi}\,\gamma+4\pi\), so \(0\le\mathsf i_\bmom\le4+\frac12(\sqrt{2\pi}\gamma+4\pi)\) on \((0,\gamma)\).
Pointwise limit. Fix a non-integer \(m\in(k,k+1)\) with \(m<\gamma\), and put \(x_j\defeq\emaw j^2Q_m\), so that \(\sum_j\Phi(x_j)=m\) and \(x_{j+1}=\bmom^2x_j\). Take a subsequence \(\bmom\downarrow0\) along which \(x_k\to L\in[0,\infty]\). If \(L=\infty\), then \(x_j\ge x_k\to\infty\) for all \(j\le k\), so \(\liminf\sum_j\Phi(x_j)\ge k+1>m\), which is impossible. If \(L=0\), then
\[ \sum_{j\ge k}\Phi(x_j)\le\sum_{j\ge k}x_j\le\frac{x_k}{1-\bmom^2}\longrightarrow0, \]
so \(\limsup\sum_j\Phi(x_j)\le k<m\), which is also impossible. Hence \(L\in(0,\infty)\). Then \(x_j=\bmom^{-2(k-j)}x_k\to\infty\) for \(j<k\), and \(\sum_{j>k}x_j\le x_k\bmom^2/(1-\bmom^2)\to0\), so \(\Phi(L)=m-k\). Since \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing, \(L=\Phi^{-1}(m-k)\), independently of the subsequence. By Lemma SF.4(iv),
\[ \mathsf i_\bmom(m)=\sum_j\Big[\sqrt{x_j}\,\Jfun(x_j)+\tfrac12\Lambda_0(x_j)\Big]. \]
For \(j<k\), \(\sqrt{x_j}\Jfun(x_j)\le x_j^{-1/2}\to0\) and \(0\le\sqrt{2\pi}-\Lambda_0(x_j)\le2x_j^{-1/2}\to0\). For \(j>k\), \(\sqrt x\,\Jfun(x)+\frac12\Lambda_0(x)\le2\sqrt x\), so
\[ \sum_{j>k}\Big[\sqrt{x_j}\,\Jfun(x_j)+\tfrac12\Lambda_0(x_j)\Big]\le2\sum_{j>k}\sqrt{x_j}\le\frac{2\sqrt{x_k}\,\bmom}{1-\bmom}\longrightarrow0 . \]
The term \(j=k\) converges to \(\mathsf i_0(m-k)=\sqrt L\,\Jfun(L)+\frac12\Lambda_0(L)\), the integrand at \(\bmom=0\) and level \(m-k\). Hence \(\mathsf i_\bmom(m)\to\frac k2\sqrt{2\pi}+\mathsf i_0(m-k)\) for almost every \(m\in(0,\gamma)\).
Evaluation. By dominated convergence,
\[ \lim_{\bmom\downarrow0}C_\bmom(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma^2}\Big[\sum_{k<n}\Big(\frac k2\sqrt{2\pi}+\int_0^1\mathsf i_0\Big)+\frac n2\sqrt{2\pi}\,y+\int_0^y\mathsf i_0\Big]. \]
For \(0<y<1\), Lemma SF.8 at \(\bmom=0\) and \(\gin=\gout=y\) gives \(\int_0^y\mathsf i_0=\pi y^2C_0(y)/\sqrt2\), and the proof of Proposition SF.9 gives \(\int_0^1\mathsf i_0=\pi C_0(1)/\sqrt2\). Substituting these and \(\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt{2\pi}/(2\pi)=\pi^{-1/2}\) gives the formula for \(C^\sharp\). For \(\gamma\le1\) one has \(n=0\) and \(y=\gamma\), or \(n=1\) and \(y=0\), and \(C^\sharp=C_0\). For \(\gamma\ge1\), subtracting \(\gamma^2C_0(\gamma)=C_0(1)+(n+y-1)/\sqrt\pi\) (Proposition SF.9) and using \(n(n-1)/2-n+1=(n-1)(n-2)/2\) gives the displayed difference. Every term is nonnegative; the first is positive if \(n\ge2\), and the second is positive if \(n=1\) and \(y>0\). Finally \(C^\sharp(\gamma)=n^2/(2\sqrt\pi\gamma^2)+O(n/\gamma^2)\to1/(2\sqrt\pi)\).
□
