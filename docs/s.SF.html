This section specializes the theorems of Section SA, Section SB, Section SC, Section SD and Section SE to isotropic data, \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), which is the setting of Section 3. Both spectral measures are then point masses, the two trace equations of the fixed-point system become algebraic, the drift kernel \(\mathfrak d_{ij}\) and the volatility coefficient \(\mathfrak v_{ij}\) each take one value, and the deterministic mode-energy equations close on the risk alone. The closed equation is the main text’s scalar recursion (8).

SF.1 Main results in brief

The main text uses the following results of this section. Write \(\gamma=N/B\) in the square case \(\Nin=\Nout=N\), and \(B_{\rm eff}=B(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)\).
  1. (1)
    The risk follows a scalar equation (Proposition SF.4 and Corollary SF.1). With isotropic data every mode has the same drift and volatility, so the mode-energy equations close on the risk:
    \[ \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif\macrotime}=-2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\macrostep^2}{2B}\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}} , \]
    and the risk of the actual algorithm tracks its solution on a positive time interval. This is the main text’s recursion (8).
  2. (2)
    The drift constant ((SF.21) and Proposition SF.5). At zero ridge the drift is a single number \(C_\bmom(\gamma)\), given by an explicit integral over the fixed-point root.
  3. (3)
    Its two batch limits (Theorem SF.4, Theorem SF.7, Theorem SF.11 and Proposition SF.12). At large batch, \(C_\bmom\approx\frac{8}{3\pi}\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/(2N)}\): momentum acts as the effective batch \(B_{\rm eff}\). At small batch with momentum, \(C_\bmom\to1/(2\sqrt\pi)\), a constant, whereas without momentum \(C_0\approx B/(\sqrt\pi N)\to0\); the limit \(\bmom\downarrow0\) is therefore discontinuous at zero ridge.
  4. (4)
    The volatility constant (Definition SF.1, Lemma SF.10, Theorem SF.8 and Theorem SF.11). Without momentum the zero-ridge volatility is \(\min\{B,N\}\), the rank of the gradient. With momentum it is \(N V_\bmom\) at large batch, with an explicit factor \(1<V_\bmom<(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)\), and \(N^2/(2B)\) at small batch.
  5. (5)
    Momentum as a rescaled step (Remark SF.1). At large batch, momentum at step \(\macrostep\) is no momentum at step \(\macrostep\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/B}\), with the volatility reduced by the factor \(c_\bmom=\sw2V_\bmom<1\) (\(0.826\ldots\) at \(\bmom=\frac12\), tending to \(0.7776\ldots\) as \(\bmom\uparrow1\)).
  6. (6)
    A small ridge at small batch (Theorem SF.13 and Theorem SF.14). With momentum and \(B_{\rm eff}<N\), a ridge \(\ridge\) limits the number of past batches the update whitens to about \(\log(1/\ridge)/(2\log(1/\bmom))\). The drift and the volatility then follow explicit crossover laws between the fixed-ridge and the zero-ridge regimes. This is the mechanism by which a few Newton–Schulz steps can beat exact whitening (Appendix C).
The remaining statements support these. Section SF.3, Section SF.4, Section SF.5 and Section SF.6 specialize the general theorems of Section SA, Section SB, Section SC, Section SD and Section SE; nothing there is reproved, and each proof displays the specialization. The later subsections carry their own proofs: the participation representation of the drift (Section SF.8), the exact constant for \(N\ge B\) and at aspect ratio one (Proposition SF.9 and Theorem SF.6), the spectral form at \(\bmom=0\) (Proposition SF.7), the lag profile of the volatility (Theorem SF.9, Theorem SF.10 and Theorem SF.12), and the order of the limits in \(\ridge\), \(\bmom\) and \(\gamma\) (Proposition SF.11).

SF.2 Standing setup

Throughout the section the features are Gaussian as in Assumption S1, with \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\). Every eigenvalue \(\mu_i\) and \(\lambda_j\) equals \(1\), and any orthonormal bases \((u_i)\) of \(\R^{\Nout}\) and \((v_j)\) of \(\R^{\Nin}\) are eigenbases. At each dimension we write
\[ \gin\defeq\frac{\Nin}{B},\qquad \gout\defeq\frac{\Nout}{B} \]
for the two aspect ratios; along a sequence of dimensions they converge, by Assumption S2, and the limits are the aspect ratios of that assumption. The spectral measures (SA.41) are the point masses
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.1} \nu_{\mathrm{out}}=\gout\,\delta_1,\qquad \nu_{\mathrm{in}}=\gin\,\delta_1 . \end{equation}
The pairing of (SA.2) is the Frobenius pairing. With identity covariances,
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.2} \langle V,W\rangle_\Sigma=\Tr\big(VW^\T\big),\qquad \cR(t)=\tfrac12\norm{\Dt}_\F^2=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\CMscr Q_{ij}(t),\qquad \Ahat=\frac{\Dt}{\norm{\Dt}_\F}, \end{equation}
by (SA.4), (SA.9) and (SA.10). In particular the diffuseness \(\norm{\Ahat}\) of Assumption S3 is the ratio \(\norm{\Dt}/\norm{\Dt}_\F\) of the operator and Frobenius norms of the error. The covariance bound of that assumption holds with \(C_\Sigma=1\).
The momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), the normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\) and the EMA weights are those of Definition SA.2. For \(s>0\), \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)(-s)\) denotes the solution of the weighted system (SB.2) for the EMA weights and the measures (SF.1), given by Theorem SB.1, and
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.3} \psi(s)\defeq q(-s)\,\Fw\big(q(-s)\big)\qquad(s>0) \end{equation}
is the common value of the coupling (SB.4). At \(\bmom=0\) we write, as in Definition SI.2,
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.4} \Phi(v)\defeq v\Jfun(v)=\EE\frac{vg^2}{1+vg^2}\qquad(v\ge0,\ g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)), \end{equation}
so that \(\psi(s)=\Phi(q(-s))\) at \(\bmom=0\).

SF.3 The isotropic closure

Proposition SF.1 (Isotropic closure)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\gin,\gout>0\) and \(s>0\), and let \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)=(\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)(-s)\) be the solution of (SB.2) for the measures (SF.1).
  1. (i)
    The branch satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.5} \sigma=\frac{\gout}{s+\tilde s_1},\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac{\gin}{s+s_1},\qquad \tilde s_1\sigma=s_1\tilde\sigma=\psi(s), \end{equation}
    and the two anchors and their consequences
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.6} \psi(s)=\gout-s\sigma=\gin-s\tilde\sigma,\qquad \tilde\sigma-\sigma=\frac{\gin-\gout}{s},\qquad (s+\tilde s_1)(s+s_1)=\frac{s\,\gin\gout}{q}. \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    The number \(q=q(-s)\) is the unique \(q>0\) with \(q\Fw(q)<\min(\gin,\gout)\) and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.7} s\,q=\big(\gout-q\Fw(q)\big)\big(\gin-q\Fw(q)\big). \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    At \(\bmom=0\), with \(\xi\defeq(2q)^{-1/2}=(2s\sigma\tilde\sigma)^{-1/2}\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.8} \psi(s)=\Phi(q)=f(\xi)=1-\sqrt\pi\,\xi\,e^{\xi^2}\erfc(\xi). \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is the system (SB.2) at point masses. For \(\nu=\gout\delta_1\) the first trace equation is
\[ \sigma=\int\frac{x}{\tilde s_1x+s}\,\gout\delta_1(dx)=\frac{\gout}{\tilde s_1+s}, \]
and the second is the same computation for \(\gin\delta_1\). The third identity of (SF.5) is the coupling (SB.4). Multiplying the first identity by \(s+\tilde s_1\) gives
\[ s\sigma+\tilde s_1\sigma=\gout , \]
which is the first anchor, and the second anchor is the same computation on the input side. Subtracting the anchors gives the second identity of (SF.6). The product of the first two identities of (SF.5) and \(q=s\sigma\tilde\sigma\) give
\[ (s+\tilde s_1)(s+s_1)=\frac{\gin\gout}{\sigma\tilde\sigma}=\frac{s\,\gin\gout}{q}. \]
Part (ii) eliminates \(\sigma\) and \(\tilde\sigma\). By the anchors,
\[ \sigma=\frac{\gout-\psi(s)}{s},\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac{\gin-\psi(s)}{s}, \]
and inserting both into \(q=s\sigma\tilde\sigma\) gives (SF.7). Since \(\tilde s_1\ge0\), the first identity of (SF.5) gives \(\sigma>0\), so \(\psi(s)<\gout\); likewise \(\psi(s)<\gin\), and \(q>0\).
Conversely, let \(q'>0\) satisfy \(q'\Fw(q')<\min(\gin,\gout)\) and (SF.7), and put \(\psi'\defeq q'\Fw(q')\) and
\[ \sigma'\defeq\frac{\gout-\psi'}{s},\qquad \tilde\sigma'\defeq\frac{\gin-\psi'}{s},\qquad a'\defeq\frac{\psi'}{\sigma'},\qquad c'\defeq\frac{\psi'}{\tilde\sigma'} . \]
These four numbers are nonnegative. The tuple solves the system (SB.2) at \(z=-s\). Indeed the first trace equation holds because
\[ s+a'=s+\frac{s\,\psi'}{\gout-\psi'}=\frac{s\,\gout}{\gout-\psi'}=\frac{\gout}{\sigma'} , \]
the second holds by the same computation, the equation \(q'=s\sigma'\tilde\sigma'\) is (SF.7), and
\[ s\,\tilde\sigma'\,\Fw(q')=\frac{s\,\tilde\sigma'\,\psi'}{q'}=\frac{\psi'}{\sigma'}=a', \]
with the equation for \(c'\) symmetric. The uniqueness in Theorem SB.1(i) gives \(q'=q\).
Part (iii) is Proposition SB.1: at \(\bmom=0\) the only weight is \(\emaw0=1\), so \(\Fw=\Jfun\) and \(\psi(s)=q\Jfun(q)=\Phi(q)\).
□
The no-momentum form of (SF.5) at \(z=-s\) is the system
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.9} \begin{gathered} \sigma(z)=\frac{\gout}{\tilde s_1(z)-z},\qquad \tilde\sigma(z)=\frac{\gin}{s_1(z)-z},\\ \tilde s_1(z)\sigma(z)=s_1(z)\tilde\sigma(z)=f(\xi(z)),\qquad \xi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-2z\sigma(z)\tilde\sigma(z)}} , \end{gathered} \end{equation}
which is the specialization of the system of Figure 2. The companion denominator of \(\tilde\sigma\) is \(s_1-z\).

SF.4 The drift

Theorem SF.1 (Isotropic drift)
Work under Assumption S3 with \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), let \(\Gtilde=\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\) be the frozen-state update, and let \(\varepsilon_B\to0\) be the sequence of Theorem SC.2.
  1. (i)
    Every kernel \(\mathfrak d_{ij}\) of Corollary SC.1 equals \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\defeq\mathfrak d(1,1)\), and, with \(z=\ridge+u^2\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.10} \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty q(-z)\,\Lw\big(q(-z)\big)\,du, \qquad 0\le\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\le\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\ridge}} . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    At \(\bmom=0\), with \(z=\ridge+u^2\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.11} \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty\psi(z)\,du =\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty\big(\gout-z\,\sigma(-z)\big)\,du . \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    The candidate (SC.3) is a multiple of the error, and the expected update is
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.12} \Gtildebar_\ridge=\frac{\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,\Dt,\qquad \EE\,\Gtilde=\frac{\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,\Dt+E_\ridge,\qquad \sup_{\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]}\norm{E_\ridge}_\F=\varepsilon_B . \end{equation}
  4. (iv)
    The total drift is
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.13} \begin{gathered} \sum_{i,j}\cD_{ij}(t)=\big\langle\Dt,\EE\,\Gtilde\big\rangle_\F =2\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\cR(t)}+\langle\Dt,E_\ridge\rangle_\F,\\ \abs{\langle\Dt,E_\ridge\rangle_\F}\le\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\varepsilon_B . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
The theorem is Corollary SC.1 and Theorem SC.2 at \(\mu_i=\lambda_j=1\). By Corollary SC.1(i), \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)=\mathfrak d(1,1)\) for every pair. At \(\mu=\lambda=1\) the denominator of the kernel (SC.4) is the product computed in (SF.6), so that
\[ \frac{z\,\Lw(q)}{(z+\tilde s_1)(z+s_1)}=\frac{z\,\Lw(q)\,q}{z\,\gin\gout}=\frac{q\,\Lw(q)}{\gin\gout}, \]
with the branch evaluated at \(-z\). Inserting this in (SC.4) gives the integral of (SF.10). The bound is (SC.14) at \(\mu=\lambda=1\).
Part (ii) is part (i) with \(\Lw=\Jfun\). Then \(q\Jfun(q)=\psi(z)\), and \(\psi(z)=\gout-z\sigma(-z)\) by (SF.6).
Part (iii) evaluates the candidate. With identity covariances, \(\Rbar(-s)=(s+\tilde s_1)^{-1}\Id_{\Nout}\) and \(\Rtbar(-s)=(s+s_1)^{-1}\Id_{\Nin}\), so the candidate (SC.2) is
\[ T(s)=\frac{s\,\Lw(q)}{(s+\tilde s_1)(s+s_1)}\,\frac{\Dt}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}} =\frac{q\,\Lw(q)}{\gin\gout}\,\frac{\Dt}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}} . \]
Integrating against \(\frac2\pi\,du\) at \(s=\ridge+u^2\) and comparing with (SF.10) gives
\[ \Gtildebar_\ridge=\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\,\frac{\Dt}{\sqrt{2\cR(t)}}=\frac{\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,\Dt . \]
The second and third statements of (SF.12) are the definition \(E_\ridge\defeq\EE\Gtilde-\Gtildebar_\ridge\) and (SC.8).
Part (iv) pairs part (iii) with the error. The first identity of (SF.13) is (SA.23) with \(\mu_i\lambda_j=1\). By (SF.12) and (SF.2),
\[ \big\langle\Dt,\EE\,\Gtilde\big\rangle_\F=\frac{\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,\norm{\Dt}_\F^2+\langle\Dt,E_\ridge\rangle_\F =2\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\cR(t)}+\langle\Dt,E_\ridge\rangle_\F , \]
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with \(\norm{\Dt}_\F=\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\) bounds the last term.
□
Part (iii) says that in the isotropic case the deterministic equivalent of the expected regularized update is a scalar multiple of the error itself, not only diagonal in some eigenbasis. The projected drift is therefore \(\cD_{ij}(t)=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\CMscr Q_{ij}(t)/\sqrt{\cR(t)}\) up to the error of (SC.12), for every pair of unit vectors \((u_i,v_j)\).

SF.5 The volatility

Theorem SF.2 (Isotropic volatility)
Work under Assumption S3 with \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), let \(I=[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\), let \({\mathsf S}_t(\ridge)=\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\) be the frozen update of (SD.15), and let \(e_B\to0\) be the sequence of Theorem SD.1.
  1. (i)
    (Same time.) For every row \(i\) and every column \(j\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.14} \sup_{\ridge\in I}\Big|\EE\norm{{\mathsf S}_t(\ridge)^\T u_i}^2-\frac{\psi(\ridge)}{\gout}\Big|\le\ridge_+e_B,\qquad \sup_{\ridge\in I}\Big|\EE\norm{{\mathsf S}_t(\ridge)v_j}^2-\frac{\psi(\ridge)}{\gin}\Big|\le\ridge_+e_B', \end{equation}
    where \(e_B'\defeq\sup_{s\in I}\norm{\EE\widetilde R(-s)-\Rtbar(-s)}\to0\), and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.15} \EE\sup_{\ridge\in I}\Big|\frac1B\norm{{\mathsf S}_t(\ridge)}_\F^2-\psi(\ridge)\Big|^2\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    (Integrated.) There is a number \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\ge0\), depending on the dimension, \(\bmom\) and \(\ridge\), such that the mode coefficients of Proposition SD.3 and the row coefficients of Theorem SD.7 are
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.16} \mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)}{\Nout\Nin},\qquad \mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)}{\Nout},\qquad \zeta_\ridge=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)}{B}\,\delta_{(1,1)} , \end{equation}
    for all \(i,j\), and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=\sum_{i,j}\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=B\,\overline\Gamma(\ridge,\ridge)\) with \(\overline\Gamma=\overline\Gamma[\Id_{\Nout},\Id_{\Nin}]\) of (SD.54).
  3. (iii)
    (Lag expansion.) With the deterministic lag covariances \(\overline C_\ell=\overline C_\ell[\Id_{\Nout},\Id_{\Nin}]\) of Theorem SD.4,
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.17} \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\,\psi(\ridge)+2B\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_\ell(\ridge,\ridge),\qquad \abs{\overline C_\ell(\ridge,\ridge)}\le\mathsf K_\star\bmom^\ell , \end{equation}
    with \(\mathsf K_\star\) of (SD.39) at \(C_\Sigma=1\). At \(\bmom=0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.18} \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\,\psi(\ridge)=B\,\Phi\big(q(-\ridge)\big)=B\big(\gout-\ridge\,\sigma(-\ridge)\big). \end{equation}
  4. (iv)
    (Actual coefficient.) The actual integrated covariance \(\Gamma=\Gamma[\Id_{\Nout},\Id_{\Nin}]\) of (SD.17) satisfies \(\sup_{\ridge\in I}\abs{\Gamma(\ridge,\ridge)-\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)/B}\to0\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is Theorem SD.1 at \(\mu_i=1\) and \(\lambda_j=1\). By (SF.5), the same-time coefficient (SD.22) is the same for every row,
\[ \volraw_i(\ridge)=\frac{\tilde s_1(-\ridge)}{\ridge+\tilde s_1(-\ridge)}=\frac{\tilde s_1\sigma}{\gout}=\frac{\psi(\ridge)}{\gout}, \]
and (SD.24) is the first bound of (SF.14). The column bound of (SD.24) (Theorem SD.1(ii)), with the column coefficient
\[ \frac{s_1(-\ridge)}{\ridge+s_1(-\ridge)}=\frac{s_1\tilde\sigma}{\gin}=\frac{\psi(\ridge)}{\gin}, \]
gives the second bound. The candidate total of (SD.22) is
\[ \overline Z(\ridge)=\frac1B\Tr\Id_{\Nout}-\ridge\,\sigma(-\ridge)=\gout-\ridge\,\sigma(-\ridge)=\psi(\ridge), \]
by (SF.6), and \(\norm{\cdot}_\Sigma=\norm{\cdot}_\F\), so (SD.25) is (SF.15).
Part (ii) is Proposition SD.3 and Proposition SD.4 with one eigenvalue on each side. The only atom of the measures of (SD.78) is \((1,1)\), with multiplicities \(r_1=\Nout\) and \(t_1=\Nin\). Put \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\defeq B\,\zeta_\ridge(\{(1,1)\})\). Then (SD.80) gives the first identity of (SF.16), and Proposition SD.4 gives
\[ \mathfrak v_i(\ridge)=\Nin\,\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)=\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)}{\Nout}. \]
The atom of \(\zeta_\ridge\) at \((1,1)\) is, by (SD.78) with \(P_1=\Id_{\Nout}\) and \(P_1'=\Id_{\Nin}\), equal to \(\overline\Gamma(\ridge,\ridge)\), and summing the first identity of (SF.16) over the \(\Nout\Nin\) pairs gives \(\sum_{i,j}\mathfrak v_{ij}=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\). Nonnegativity is Theorem SD.8(iii).
Part (iii) expands \(\overline\Gamma\). By (SD.54) at equal ridges,
\[ \overline\Gamma(\ridge,\ridge)=\overline C_0(\ridge,\ridge)+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_\ell(\ridge,\ridge), \]
and \(\overline C_0(\ridge,\ridge)=\overline Z(\ridge)=\psi(\ridge)\) by Theorem SD.4(v) and part (i). The bound on the lag terms is Theorem SD.4(ii) with \(\norm F=\norm G=1\). At \(\bmom=0\) the lag terms vanish by the same statement, and the three forms of (SF.18) are (SF.4) and (SF.6).
Part (iv) is Theorem SD.4(v).

The main text writes \(\mathfrak v_{ij}=\mathfrak v/N^2\) in the square case \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) (Section 3). By (SF.16) this is the convention \(\mathfrak v=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\) in general, with \(N^2\) replaced by \(\Nout\Nin\). Summed over the pairs, the quadratic term of the total recursion (SA.22) is therefore \(\frac{\lr^2}2\sum_{i,j}\mathfrak v_{ij}=\frac{\lr^2}2\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\), which is the volatility term \(\frac{\eta^2}2\mathfrak v\) of (8). Each lag term is given in closed form by Corollary SD.3 at the single eigenvalue pair \((1,1)\),

which is (SD.92); no closed form of their sum is claimed, and Proposition SF.10 evaluates the lag coefficients at zero ridge in participation coordinates.
Proposition SF.2 (Zero-ridge isotropic volatility without momentum)
Let \(\bmom=0\) and \(m_\gamma\defeq\min\{1,\gin,\gout\}\). Then \(\psi(\ridge)\to m_\gamma\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\), and
\[ \lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\,m_\gamma=\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}. \]
Proof
Proof.
The function \(\psi\) lies below \(m_\gamma\). Indeed \(\psi<\min(\gin,\gout)\) by Proposition SF.1(ii), and \(\psi<1\) by Lemma SF.1(ii).
The function \(\psi\) cannot stay a fixed distance below \(m_\gamma\). Suppose that \(\psi(\ridge_k)\le m_\gamma-\delta\) for some \(\delta>0\) along a sequence \(\ridge_k\downarrow0\). Then (SF.7) gives
\[ q(-\ridge_k)=\frac{(\gout-\psi(\ridge_k))(\gin-\psi(\ridge_k))}{\ridge_k}\ge\frac{\delta^2}{\ridge_k}\longrightarrow\infty , \]
and \(\psi(\ridge_k)=\Phi(q(-\ridge_k))\to1\ge m_\gamma\) by Lemma SF.1(iv), a contradiction. Hence \(\psi(\ridge)\to m_\gamma\).
The limit of the volatility follows from (SF.18), which gives \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\psi(\ridge)\) at \(\bmom=0\). Since \(\gin=\Nin/B\) and \(\gout=\Nout/B\), the product \(B\,m_\gamma\) is \(\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}\).
□
The main text’s value \(\min\{B,N\}\) is, in general, the exact identity \(\norm{\Qridge0(\Gt)}_\F^2=\rank\Gt\le\min\{B,\Nout,\Nin\}\) of Lemma SA.2(i) for a gradient of full rank. No statement of this appendix is made at \(\ridge=0\).
Definition SF.1 (Zero-ridge isotropic volatility)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) and let \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) be the number of Theorem SF.2(ii) at momentum \(\bmom\). The zero-ridge isotropic volatility at momentum \(\bmom\) is
\[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge), \]
the limit being taken at this fixed \(\bmom\), whenever it exists.
Like \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\), the number \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\) depends on the dimension. The limit is taken at a fixed momentum \(\bmom\). At \(\bmom=0\) it exists, and
\[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}=\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}, \]
by Proposition SF.2. For \(\bmom>0\) the next proposition gives the limit of the same-time part and, in the square case, the existence of the limit.
Proposition SF.3 (Zero-ridge limits with momentum)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) and \(\gin,\gout>0\), put \(\gamma_\wedge\defeq\min(\gin,\gout)\) and \(\gamma_\vee\defeq\max(\gin,\gout)\), and let \(K_\bmom\) be the unique \(q>0\) with \(q\Fw(q)=\gamma_\wedge\).
  1. (i)
    As \(\ridge\downarrow0\), \(\psi(\ridge)\to\gamma_\wedge\), and for every \(\ridge>0\)
    \[ \gamma_\wedge-\psi(\ridge)\le\frac{\ridge\,K_\bmom}{\gamma_\vee-\gamma_\wedge}\quad(\gin\ne\gout),\qquad \gamma_\wedge-\psi(\ridge)\le\sqrt{\ridge\,K_\bmom}\quad(\gin=\gout). \]
    In particular the same-time row and column coefficients \(\psi(\ridge)/\gout\) and \(\psi(\ridge)/\gin\) of (SF.14) tend to \(\gamma_\wedge/\gout\) and \(\gamma_\wedge/\gin\), and the same-time total \(B\psi(\ridge)\) of (SF.15) tends to \(\min\{\Nin,\Nout\}\).
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\). For \(\ell\ge0\) let \(\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)\) be the row lag coefficient of (SG.2) for the spectrum \(\mu_1=\dots=\mu_N=1\), which is the same for every row. Then \(\overline C_{1,0}(\ridge)=\psi(\ridge)/\gamma\); for every \(\ell\ge0\) the limit \(\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)\) exists and is nonnegative, with \(\overline C_{1,0}(0)=1\); and the limit of Definition SF.1 exists and equals
    \[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=N\Big(1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\Big)\ge N . \]
  3. (iii)
    If moreover \(N>B\), then \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\ge N>B=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}\).
Proof
Proof.
For part (i), \(K_\bmom\) is well defined because every EMA weight is positive, so that \(q\mapsto q\Fw(q)\) is an increasing bijection of \([0,\infty)\) by Lemma SB.1(iii). By Proposition SF.1(ii), \(q(-\ridge)\Fw(q(-\ridge))=\psi(\ridge)<\gamma_\wedge\), so \(q(-\ridge)<K_\bmom\), and (SF.7) gives
\[ \big(\gout-\psi(\ridge)\big)\big(\gin-\psi(\ridge)\big)=\ridge\,q(-\ridge)\le\ridge\,K_\bmom . \]
Both factors are positive. For \(\gin\ne\gout\) the larger one is at least \(\gamma_\vee-\gamma_\wedge\), and for \(\gin=\gout\) the two coincide; this gives the two bounds, and \(\psi(\ridge)\to\gamma_\wedge\). The coefficients are those of Theorem SF.2(i), and \(B\gamma_\wedge=\min\{\Nin,\Nout\}\).
Part (ii) is Proposition SG.4 at \(\mu_1=\dots=\mu_N=1\), which is the case \(\Sigmaout=\Sigmain=\Id_N\) of the setup of the power-law section; the kernel (SG.16) depends on the row only through \(\mu_i\), so every row has the same coefficients. The zero-lag coefficient is the raw coefficient \(\volraw_1(\ridge)\) (Theorem SD.7(iv)), which is \(\psi(\ridge)/\gamma\) by the proof of Theorem SF.2(i). By Theorem SF.2(ii) the row coefficient is \(\mathfrak v_1(\ridge)=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)/N\), and by Proposition SG.4(ii) it converges to \(1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\ge1\).
Part (iii) combines (ii) with Proposition SF.2, which gives \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}=\min\{B,N\}=B\).

The limits \(\ridge\downarrow0\) and \(\bmom\downarrow0\) therefore do not commute for the volatility when \(N>B\), by (iii), and they do not commute for the same-time total when \(B<\min\{\Nin,\Nout\}\): its zero-ridge limit is \(\min\{\Nin,\Nout\}\) for every \(\bmom>0\) and \(\min\{B,\Nin,\Nout\}\) at \(\bmom=0\). SF.6 The scalar equation and the dynamics

Proposition SF.4 (Scalar reduction)
Let \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\ridge>0\) and \(\macrostep>0\), and write \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) for the numbers of Theorem SF.1 and Theorem SF.2.
  1. (i)
    The deterministic mode-energy equations (SE.7) close on the risk: if \((\fQ_{ij})\) solves them on an interval on which \(\fRisk>0\), then \(\fRisk=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\fQ_{ij}\) solves
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.19} \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif\macrotime}=-2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\macrostep^2}{2B}\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}},\qquad \fRisk(0)=\cR(0), \end{equation}
    and (SF.19) has at most one solution with \(\fRisk>0\) on any interval \([0,T]\).
  2. (ii)
    Under Assumption S3 and for \(\ridge\in[\ridge_-,\ridge_+]\), one step of size \(\lr\ge0\) from a frozen state, \(\Delta^+=\Dt-\lr\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\), satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.20} \begin{gathered} \EE\,\cR(\Delta^+)-\cR(t)=-2\lr\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\cR(t)}+\frac{\lr^2B}{2}\,\psi(\ridge)+r_t,\\ \abs{r_t}\le\lr\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\varepsilon_B+\frac{\lr^2B}{2}\,\gamma_\star\ridge_+e_B , \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    with \(\varepsilon_B\) of Theorem SC.2 and \(e_B\) of Theorem SD.1.
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) sums (SE.7) over the pairs. By Theorem SF.1(i), \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) for every pair, and by Theorem SF.2(ii), \(\sum_{i,j}\mathfrak v_{ij}=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\). The risk of (SE.7) is \(\fRisk=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\fQ_{ij}\), because \(\mu_i\lambda_j=1\). Summing (SE.7) therefore gives
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\big(2B^2\fRisk\big)=-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,2B^2\fRisk+\macrostep^2B\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}, \]
and dividing by \(2B^2\) gives (SF.19). The initial value is \(\frac12\sum_{i,j}\CMscr Q_{ij}(0)=\cR(0)\) by (SF.2). The right side of (SF.19) is a continuously differentiable function of \(\fRisk\) on \((0,\infty)\), so the Picard–Lindelöf theorem [98, Theorem 2.2] gives uniqueness.
Part (ii) is Proposition SC.2 with the quadratic term (SD.26), both at \(C_\Sigma=1\). By (SC.15) and (SC.16), the linear term differs from
\[ \frac{\lr}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\sum_{i,j}\mathfrak d_{ij}\,\CMscr Q_{ij}(t)=\frac{\lr\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}}\,2\cR(t)=2\lr\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\cR(t)} \]
by at most \(\lr\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\,\varepsilon_B\). By (SD.26) and \(\overline Z(\ridge)=\psi(\ridge)\) (Theorem SF.2(i)), the quadratic term differs from \(\frac{\lr^2B}2\psi(\ridge)\) by at most \(\frac{\lr^2B}2\gamma_\star\ridge_+e_B\).
□
With \(\lr=\macrostep/B\) and \(t=B\macrotime\), the chain rule turns (SF.19) into
\[ \frac{\dif\fRisk}{\dif t}=-2\lr\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\lr^2}{2}\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}} , \]
which is the equation (SI.11) of the common-drift model of Proposition SI.2 with \(\mathfrak d=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) and \(\mathfrak v_{\rm tot}=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\), and the continuous-time form of the main text’s recursion (8). In the deterministic row model of Definition SI.1 the isotropic case is the case of \(\Nout\) rows with \(\mu_i=1\), \(\mathfrak d_i=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) and \(\mathfrak v_i=\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}/\Nout\). In the frozen one-step identity (SF.20) the quadratic term is the same-time value \(B\psi(\ridge)\), which equals \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\) at \(\bmom=0\); over many steps the integrated coefficient \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}\) replaces it (Theorem SD.2(i)), and the next corollary is the statement that does this for the algorithm.
Corollary SF.1 (Isotropic dynamics)
Let \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\) and \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), fix \(\macrostep>0\), \(\ridge>0\), \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\gamma_\star<\infty\), \(E_\star<\infty\), \(c_M<\infty\) and \(0<r_0\le r_1<\infty\), and along a sequence of dimensions with \(B\to\infty\) assume:
  1. (a)
    Assumption S2 holds and \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\);
  2. (b)
    the algorithm of Definition SA.2 is run with momentum \(\bmom\), normalized ridge \(\ridge\) and step \(\lr_t=\macrostep/B\);
  3. (c)
    \(W_0\) is deterministic, \(\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2\le E_\star\), \(r_0\le\cR(0)\le r_1\) and \(\norm{\Delta_0}/\norm{\Delta_0}_\F\to0\);
  4. (d)
    the initial buffer \(M^\circ_{-1}\) is independent of all batches and \(\EE\norm{M^\circ_{-1}}_\F^2\le c_MB\).
Let \(T_\star\) be given by (SE.10) at \(C_\Sigma=1\). Then (SF.19) has a unique solution \(\fRisk\) on \([0,T_\star]\), and
  1. (i)
    with probability tending to one, \(t_{\rm stop}>BT_\star\) and \(\inf_{t\le BT_\star}\cR(t)\ge r_0/2\);
  2. (ii)

  3. (iii)
    on the event of (i), \(\sup_{t\le BT_\star}\norm{\Dt}/\norm{\Dt}_\F\to0\) in probability.
Proof
Proof.
Hypotheses (a)–(d) are {{(H1)}}–{{(H4)}} of Theorem SE.1 at identity covariances. In {{(H1)}} the features are Gaussian by the standing setup, and the covariances are deterministic with \(\norm{\Sigmain}=\norm{\Sigmaout}=1\), so \(C_\Sigma=1\). In {{(H3)}} the diffuseness is \(\norm{\widehat A_0}=\norm{\Delta_0}/\norm{\Delta_0}_\F\) by (SF.2). By Proposition SE.1 the equations (SE.7) have a unique solution on \([0,T_\star]\) with \(\fRisk\ge r_0/2\), and by Proposition SF.4(i) its risk is the unique solution of (SF.19). Conclusions (i), (ii) and (iii) are Theorem SE.1(i), the risk statement of (iii), and (iv).
□

SF.7 The drift constant at zero ridge

Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\gin,\gout>0\), and for \(t>0\) let \(q(-t)\) be the branch of Proposition SF.1 at momentum \(\bmom\). The zero-ridge drift constant at momentum \(\bmom\) is
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.21} C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\defeq\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty q(-u^2)\,\Lw\big(q(-u^2)\big)\,du,\qquad C_\bmom(\gamma)\defeq C_\bmom(\gamma,\gamma). \end{equation}
It is the integral (SF.10) at \(\ridge=0\). At \(\bmom=0\), \(q\Jfun(q)=\psi\) by (SF.8), so \(C_0\) is the integral (SF.11) at \(\ridge=0\). The square case \(C_\bmom(N/B)\) is the constant of (9). The branch, and hence the integrand, depends on \((\gin,\gout)\) and \(\bmom\), and this dependence is suppressed.
Lemma SF.1 (The Gaussian scalar)
For \(v>0\):
  1. (i)
    \(\Phi(v)=f\big((2v)^{-1/2}\big)\);
  2. (ii)
    \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing, with \(\Phi'(v)=\EE\,g^2(1+vg^2)^{-2}\in(0,1]\), and \(0<\Phi(v)<1\);
  3. (iii)
    \(0\le v-\Phi(v)\le3v^2\);
  4. (iv)
    \(\Phi(v)/v\to1\) as \(v\downarrow0\) and \(\Phi(v)\to1\) as \(v\to\infty\).
In particular, for real \(\xi>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.22} \Big|f(\xi)-\frac{1}{2\xi^2}\Big|\le\frac{3}{4\xi^4}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is Proposition SB.1. Parts (ii) and (iv) are Lemma SB.1 for the single weight \(w=(1,0,0,\dots)\), for which \(r_w=\Phi\) and \(\#\{j:w_j>0\}=1\): part (iii) of that lemma gives the monotonicity, the derivative and the limit \(1\) at infinity, and \(\Phi(v)/v=\Jfun(v)\to\Jfun(0)=1\) by its part (i). The derivative is at most \(\EE g^2=1\), and \(\Phi<1\) because the integrand of (SF.4) is below \(1\). Part (iii) is the identity
\[ v-\Phi(v)=\EE\Big[vg^2-\frac{vg^2}{1+vg^2}\Big]=\EE\frac{v^2g^4}{1+vg^2}\in\big[0,\,v^2\EE g^4\big]=\big[0,\,3v^2\big]. \]
The bound (SF.22) is (iii) at \(v=1/(2\xi^2)\), by (i).

Proposition SF.5 (The zero-ridge constant)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) and \(\gin,\gout>0\). Put \(K_0\defeq1\), and for \(\bmom>0\) let \(K_\bmom\) be the unique \(q>0\) with \(q\Fw(q)=\min(\gin,\gout)\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(t>0\),
    \[ 0<q(-t)\,\Lw\big(q(-t)\big)\le\min\Big(K_\bmom,\frac{\gin\gout}{t}\Big), \]
    so the integral (SF.21) converges and \(0<C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)<\infty\). Moreover \(C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)=C_\bmom(\gout,\gin)\), and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.23} C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\,q(-t)\,\Lw\big(q(-t)\big)\,\dif t . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) be the kernel (SF.10) at momentum \(\bmom\), for the measures (SF.1). Then, at this fixed \(\bmom\), \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\to C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\).
Proof
Proof.
For part (i), \(K_\bmom\) is well defined: for \(\bmom>0\) every EMA weight is positive, so by Lemma SB.1(iii) the map \(q\mapsto q\Fw(q)\) is a continuous strictly increasing bijection of \([0,\infty)\). The integrand is positive because \(q(-t)>0\) by Proposition SF.1(ii) and \(\Lw>0\). By Lemma SB.1(i), \(\Jfun\le1\), so
\[ \Lw(q)=\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j\,\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\le\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j=1,\qquad q\,\Lw(q)\le q . \]
The first identity of (SF.5) gives \(\sigma(-t)\le\gout/t\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\le\gin/t\), so that
\[ q(-t)=t\,\sigma(-t)\,\tilde\sigma(-t)\le\frac{\gin\gout}{t}. \]
At \(\bmom=0\), \(q\Jfun(q)=\Phi(q)<1=K_0\) by Lemma SF.1(ii). For \(\bmom>0\), Proposition SF.1(ii) gives \(q(-t)\Fw(q(-t))<\min(\gin,\gout)=K_\bmom\Fw(K_\bmom)\), so \(q(-t)<K_\bmom\) by monotonicity, and \(q\Lw(q)\le q<K_\bmom\). The integrand of (SF.21) is therefore at most \(\min(K_\bmom,\gin\gout/u^2)\). The equation (SF.7) is symmetric in \((\gin,\gout)\) for every \(\bmom\), so \(q(-t)\) is unchanged when the two ratios are exchanged, which gives the symmetry. The substitution \(t=u^2\) gives (SF.23).
Part (ii) is a limit under the integral. By Theorem SF.1(i),
\[ \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty q(-\ridge-u^2)\,\Lw\big(q(-\ridge-u^2)\big)\,du . \]
The function \(t\mapsto q(-t)\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\) by Theorem SB.1(iv), and \(\Lw\) is continuous by Lemma SB.1(ii), so the integrand converges to that of (SF.21) for every \(u>0\). By part (i) it is at most \(\min(K_\bmom,\gin\gout/u^2)\). Dominated convergence gives the limit.
□
The statements of Section SC, Section SD and Section SE hold at a fixed positive ridge, so \(C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\) is the drift of the zero-ridge algorithm only as a formal limit (Corollary SC.1 and Remark SE.1): part (ii) is a statement about the deterministic kernel, and no statement of the appendix concerns the algorithm at \(\ridge=0\).
The bounds, the symmetry and the form (SF.23) hold at every \(\bmom\in[0,1)\). The limit of part (ii) is taken at a fixed \(\bmom\). The limits \(\ridge\downarrow0\) and \(\bmom\downarrow0\) commute at a positive ridge (Proposition SF.11(i)) but not at zero ridge: in the square case \(\lim_{\bmom\downarrow0}C_\bmom(\gamma)\) differs from \(C_0(\gamma)\) for every \(\gamma>1\) (Proposition SF.12). The asymptotics of \(C_\bmom\) at every momentum are in Section SF.11. The spectral form, the measure pair, the limiting laws of Section SF.9 and the value at aspect ratio one are proved at \(\bmom=0\). In the rest of this subsection, \(\bmom=0\), and we write \(C(\gin,\gout)\defeq C_0(\gin,\gout)\) and \(C(\gamma)\defeq C_0(\gamma)\); the same convention holds in Section SF.9 and Section SF.10. For \(t>0\) the branch at \(\bmom=0\) satisfies, by (SF.6) and (SF.8),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.24} q(-t)\Jfun\big(q(-t)\big)=\psi(t)=\gout-t\sigma(-t)=\gin-t\tilde\sigma(-t)=\Phi\big(t\sigma(-t)\tilde\sigma(-t)\big)=f\big(\xi(-t)\big), \end{equation}

The spectral form. The constant is a half-moment of a spectral measure when the branch is a Stieltjes transform. For a finite nonnegative measure \(\nu\) on \([0,\infty)\) we write its transform on the negative axis as
\[ S_\nu(-t)\defeq\int\frac{\nu(\dif x)}{x+t}\qquad(t>0). \]
Definition SF.2 (Isotropic measure pair)
Let \(\gin,\gout>0\). An isotropic measure pair at \((\gin,\gout)\) is a pair of finite nonnegative measures \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}\) and \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}\) on \([0,\infty)\), of masses \(\gout\) and \(\gin\), whose transforms \(\sigma(-t)\defeq S_{\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}}(-t)\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\defeq S_{\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}}(-t)\) satisfy, for every \(t>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.25} \gout-t\,\sigma(-t)=\gin-t\,\tilde\sigma(-t)=\Phi\big(t\,\sigma(-t)\,\tilde\sigma(-t)\big). \end{equation}
A pair says that the no-momentum isotropic closure is the transform of two measures on the half-line. For every \(\gin,\gout>0\) exactly one pair exists, by Proposition SF.6 below. At \(\gin=\gout=\gamma\) a pair is the measure of the scalar measure hypothesis of Definition SI.2 in the phase-comparison section: a measure \(\mu_\gamma\) of that definition gives the pair \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}=\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}=\mu_\gamma\), and conversely the two measures of a pair coincide when \(\gin=\gout\), by Lemma SF.3(iii). That hypothesis therefore holds at every \(\gamma>0\). The next two lemmas are the facts about such pairs that the existence proof and the asymptotic statements use.
Lemma SF.2 (Negative-axis transforms)
  1. (i)
    Let \(\nu_j\) \((j\ge1)\) and \(\nu\) be probability measures on \([0,\infty)\) with \(S_{\nu_j}(-t)\to S_\nu(-t)\) for every \(t>0\). Then \(\nu_j\to\nu\) weakly. If moreover \(\sup_j\int x\,\nu_j(\dif x)<\infty\) and \(\int x\,\nu(\dif x)<\infty\), then
    \[ \int\sqrt x\,\nu_j(\dif x)\longrightarrow\int\sqrt x\,\nu(\dif x). \]
  2. (ii)
    Two finite nonnegative measures on \([0,\infty)\) with equal masses and equal transforms at every \(-t\), \(t>0\), are equal.
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) first extends the convergence off the negative axis. For \(k\ge1\) let \(D_k\defeq\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{dist}(z,[0,\infty))>1/k\}\), a connected open set. For a probability measure \(\nu\) on \([0,\infty)\) the function \(S_\nu(z)\defeq\int(x-z)^{-1}\nu(\dif x)\) is analytic on \(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\), because for \(\abs{z-z_0}<\operatorname{dist}(z_0,[0,\infty))\) the expansion
\[ \frac{1}{x-z}=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(z-z_0)^n}{(x-z_0)^{n+1}} \]
converges uniformly in \(x\ge0\); and \(\abs{S_\nu(z)}\le k\) on \(D_k\). The points \(-t\) with \(t>1/k\) lie in \(D_k\) and accumulate at \(-2/k\in D_k\). Vitali’s convergence theorem [99, §5.21], whose hypotheses are the uniform bound on \(D_k\) and the convergence on this set, gives that \(S_{\nu_j}\) converges at every point of \(D_k\) to an analytic function on \(D_k\). That function equals \(S_\nu\) on the negative axis in \(D_k\), so it equals \(S_\nu\) on \(D_k\) by the identity theorem [99, §2.6]. Since \(\bigcup_kD_k=\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\),
\[ S_{\nu_j}(z)\longrightarrow S_\nu(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\R). \]
Theorem 2.4.4(c) of [80], applied to the deterministic measures \(\nu_j\) with the limit \(S_\nu\), the Stieltjes transform of the probability measure \(\nu\), gives \(\nu_j\to\nu\) weakly.
The half-moments converge by truncation. For \(L>0\) and \(x>L\) one has \(\sqrt x\le x/\sqrt L\), so
\[ 0\le\int\sqrt x\,\nu_j(\dif x)-\int\min(\sqrt x,\sqrt L)\,\nu_j(\dif x)\le\int_{(L,\infty)}\sqrt x\,\nu_j(\dif x)\le\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\sup_k\int x\,\nu_k(\dif x), \]
and the same bound holds for \(\nu\). The truncated integrand is bounded and continuous, so its integrals converge by weak convergence, and letting \(L\to\infty\) proves the claim.
Part (ii) is part (i) for a constant sequence. If the common mass is zero there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, after dividing both measures by their mass, the constant sequence \(\nu_j\defeq\nu'\) has the transforms of \(\nu\), so \(\nu'\to\nu\) weakly by part (i), that is, \(\nu'=\nu\).
□
Lemma SF.3 (Moments and atoms)
Let \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}})\) be an isotropic measure pair at \((\gin,\gout)\).
  1. (i)
    The transforms are the branch: \(\sigma(-t)\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\) in (SF.25) are those of Proposition SF.1 at \(\bmom=0\), and \(\psi(t)=\int\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+t}\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\).
  2. (ii)
    The first moments are
    \[ \int\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)=\int\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}(\dif\lambda)=\gin\gout, \qquad\text{and}\qquad \int\sqrt\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\le\gout\sqrt{\gin}. \]
  3. (iii)
    \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}-\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}=(\gin-\gout)\,\delta_0\).
  4. (iv)
    If \(\gin>1\) and \(\gout>1\), then \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}=(\gout-1)\delta_0+\eta\) and \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}=(\gin-1)\delta_0+\eta\) for a probability measure \(\eta\) on \((0,\infty)\).
  5. (v)
    For every finite nonnegative measure \(\nu\) on \([0,\infty)\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.26} \int\sqrt x\,\nu(\dif x)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\int\frac{x}{x+t}\,\nu(\dif x)\,\dif t . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
For part (i), fix \(t>0\) and put \(q'\defeq t\sigma(-t)\tilde\sigma(-t)\). The measures have positive masses, so \(\sigma(-t),\tilde\sigma(-t)>0\), and (SF.25) gives \(\Phi(q')=\gout-t\sigma(-t)<\gout\) and likewise \(\Phi(q')<\gin\). Inserting \(\sigma(-t)=(\gout-\Phi(q'))/t\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)=(\gin-\Phi(q'))/t\) in the definition of \(q'\) gives
\[ t\,q'=\big(\gout-\Phi(q')\big)\big(\gin-\Phi(q')\big), \]
which is (SF.7) at \(\bmom=0\). By the uniqueness in Proposition SF.1(ii), \(q'=q(-t)\), and then the anchors (SF.6) identify \(\sigma(-t)\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\). The formula for \(\psi\) follows from \(\gout-t\sigma(-t)=\int\big(1-\frac{t}{\lambda+t}\big)\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\).
Part (ii) reads the first moment off the large-\(t\) behaviour of \(\psi\). By dominated convergence, \(t\sigma(-t)\to\gout\) and \(t\tilde\sigma(-t)\to\gin\) as \(t\to\infty\). Hence \(q(-t)\to0\) and \(t\,q(-t)\to\gin\gout\), and by Lemma SF.1(iii),
\[ \abs{t\,\psi(t)-t\,q(-t)}\le3t\,q(-t)^2\longrightarrow0 , \]
so \(t\psi(t)\to\gin\gout\). On the other hand,
\[ t\,\psi(t)=\int\frac{t\lambda}{\lambda+t}\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda) \]
increases to \(\int\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\) by monotone convergence. The same argument with \(\psi(t)=\gin-t\tilde\sigma(-t)\) gives the first moment of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}\). The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives
\[ \int\sqrt\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\le\Big(\gout\int\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\Big)^{1/2}=\gout\sqrt{\gin}. \]
Part (iii) compares two measures of equal mass. By (SF.6), \(\tilde\sigma(-t)-\sigma(-t)=(\gin-\gout)/t\), so the measures \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}+\gout\delta_0\) and \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}+\gin\delta_0\) have the same transform at every \(-t\):
\[ \tilde\sigma(-t)+\frac{\gout}{t}=\sigma(-t)+\frac{\gin}{t}. \]
Both have mass \(\gin+\gout\), so they are equal by Lemma SF.2(ii).
Part (iv) identifies the mass away from zero. By Lemma SF.1(ii), \(\psi<1\), so by (SF.7)
\[ q(-t)=\frac{(\gout-\psi(t))(\gin-\psi(t))}{t}\ge\frac{(\gout-1)(\gin-1)}{t}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(t\downarrow0), \]
and \(\psi(t)=\Phi(q(-t))\to1\) by Lemma SF.1(iv). By part (i) and dominated convergence, \(\psi(t)\to\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}((0,\infty))\) as \(t\downarrow0\). Hence \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}((0,\infty))=1\), the atom of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}\) at \(0\) is \(\gout-1\), and \(\eta\) is the restriction of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}\) to \((0,\infty)\). Part (iii) gives the decomposition of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}\).
Part (v) is a scalar integral. For \(x>0\) the substitution \(t=xv^2\) gives
\[ \int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\,\frac{x}{x+t}\,\dif t=2\sqrt x\int_0^\infty\frac{\dif v}{1+v^2}=\pi\sqrt x , \]
and for \(x=0\) both sides vanish. Integrating against \(\nu(\dif x)\) gives (SF.26).

Proposition SF.6 (Existence of the isotropic measure pair)
For every \(\gin>0\) and \(\gout>0\) there is exactly one isotropic measure pair \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}})\) at \((\gin,\gout)\).
Proof
Proof.
Uniqueness is Lemma SF.3(i) with Lemma SF.2(ii). By the former, the transforms of any pair at \((\gin,\gout)\) are the branch \(\sigma(-t)\) and \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\) of Proposition SF.1 at \(\bmom=0\). The masses are fixed, so two pairs have equal masses and equal transforms.
Existence: the pair is a limit of expected Gram spectral measures. Fix dimensions with \(B\to\infty\), \(\Nout/B\to\gout\) and \(\Nin/B\to\gin\), identity covariances, \(\bmom=0\), and the frozen states \(\Ahat=k^{-1/2}\sum_{l\le k}u_l\tensor v_l\) with \(k=\min(\Nout,\Nin)\). Then \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\) and \(\norm{\Ahat}=k^{-1/2}\to0\), so Assumption S3 holds, and \(\Mt=\Gt\). Let \(\zeta_B\) be the expected spectral measure of \(\Gt\Gt^\T\) normalized by \(B\),
\[ \int\phi\,\dif\zeta_B\defeq\frac1B\,\EE\Tr\phi\big(\Gt\Gt^\T\big),\qquad \zeta_B([0,\infty))=\frac{\Nout}{B},\qquad S_{\zeta_B}(-t)=\frac1B\,\EE\Tr R(-t). \]
By Corollary SB.1 with \(\Sigmaout=\Id_{\Nout}\), and the continuity of the branch in the ratios (Theorem SB.1(ii)),
\[ S_{\zeta_B}(-t)\longrightarrow\sigma(-t)\qquad(t>0), \]
where \(\sigma\) is the branch at the limiting ratios. The first moments are bounded: by Lemma SA.4 with identity covariances and \(\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F=\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\),
\[ \int x\,\zeta_B(\dif x)=\frac1B\,\EE\norm{\Gt}_\F^2=\frac1B+\frac{\Nout\Nin+2\Nout+2\Nin+3}{B^2}\longrightarrow\gin\gout . \]
The normalized measures \(\zeta_B/\zeta_B([0,\infty))\) are therefore tight, since \(\zeta([L,\infty))\le L^{-1}\int x\,\dif\zeta\), and by Prokhorov’s theorem [83, Theorem 5.1] a subsequence converges weakly. The limit times \(\gout\) is a measure \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}\) of mass \(\gout\) whose transform is \(\sigma(-t)\), because \(x\mapsto(x+t)^{-1}\) is bounded and continuous on \([0,\infty)\). The same argument for \(\Gt^\T\Gt\), along a further subsequence, gives \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}\) of mass \(\gin\) with transform \(\tilde\sigma(-t)\). By (SF.24), the two transforms satisfy (SF.25).

Proposition SF.7 (Spectral form of the constant)
Let \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}})\) be the isotropic measure pair at \((\gin,\gout)\) of Proposition SF.6. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.27} C(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\gin\gout}\int\sqrt\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda) =\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\gin\gout}\int\sqrt\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}(\dif\lambda). \end{equation}

Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.28} C(\gin,\gout)=\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt2\,\gin\gout}\int\sqrt x\,\widehat{\mathfrak m}_b(\dif x). \end{equation}
In particular, at \(b=\gin\gout\),
\[ C(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\gin\gout}}\int\sqrt x\,\widehat{\mathfrak m}_{\gin\gout}(\dif x). \]
Proof
Proof.
By (SF.23), Lemma SF.3(i) and the half-moment formula (SF.26),
\[ C(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\int\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+t}\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)\,\dif t =\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\gin\gout}\int\sqrt\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda). \]
The second form of (SF.27) holds because the two measures differ by an atom at \(0\) (Lemma SF.3(iii)), where \(\sqrt\lambda\) vanishes. For the rescaled measure,
\[ S_{\widehat{\mathfrak m}_b}(-t)=\int\frac{\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)}{\lambda/b+t}=b\int\frac{\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)}{\lambda+bt}=b\,\sigma(-bt), \]
the density is the change of variables \(x=\lambda/b\), and \(\sqrt\lambda=\sqrt b\,\sqrt{\lambda/b}\) gives (SF.28).

SF.8 The participation representation

In this subsection the momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) is arbitrary. The drift integral (SF.10) is an integral of one scalar root. This subsection reparametrizes the root by its participation level and integrates by parts once, which isolates the main term of the drift.
We write \(r_\bmom(q)\defeq q\Fw(q)\) for the function of Lemma SB.1(iii) at the EMA weights, and \(\sw k=\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j^k\) for \(k\ge2\), so that \(\sw k=(1-\bmom)^k/(1-\bmom^k)\) for \(\bmom>0\) and \(\sw k=1\) at \(\bmom=0\). Put
\[ \gamma_\wedge\defeq\min(\gin,\gout),\qquad \gamma_\vee\defeq\max(\gin,\gout), \]
\[ \mathsf W(m)\defeq\sqrt{(\gin-m)(\gout-m)}\qquad(0\le m\le\gamma_\wedge), \]
and, for \(q\ge0\) and \(s>0\),
\[ \Lambda_\bmom(q)\defeq\int_0^qx^{-1/2}\Lw(x)\,\dif x,\qquad \mathsf G_\bmom(s)\defeq q(-s)\,\Lw\big(q(-s)\big), \]
where \(q(-s)\) is the root of Proposition SF.1(ii) at momentum \(\bmom\). By Theorem SF.1(i), for \(\ridge>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.29} \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^\infty\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge+u^2)\,\dif u , \end{equation}
and we put \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(0)\defeq C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)\), the same integral at \(\ridge=0\) ((SF.21)), which is its limit by Proposition SF.5(ii). When the momentum and the aspect ratios have to be shown we write \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge;\bmom,\gin,\gout)\). The square case is \(\gin=\gout=\gamma\).
Lemma SF.4 (Scalar facts)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\).
  1. (i)
    For \(q\ge0\),
    \[ r_\bmom(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\Phi(\emaw j^2q),\qquad r_\bmom'(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\emaw j^2\,\Phi'(\emaw j^2q)>0 . \]
    For \(\bmom>0\), \(r_\bmom\) is an increasing bijection of \([0,\infty)\) onto itself; at \(\bmom=0\), \(r_0=\Phi\) is an increasing bijection of \([0,\infty)\) onto \([0,1)\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(x>0\), \(\Phi(x)\le\min(1,x)\), the derivative \(\Phi'\) is decreasing, and
    \[ 1-\Phi(x)\le\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x^{-1/2},\qquad x\,\Phi'(x)\le\min\Big(x,\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x^{-1/2}\Big). \]
  3. (iii)
    For \(x>0\), \(\Jfun(x)\le\min(1,1/x)\) and \(-3\le\Jfun'(x)\le0\).
  4. (iv)
    For \(q,z\ge0\),
    \[ \Lambda_\bmom(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\Lambda_0(\emaw j^2q),\qquad \Lambda_0(z)\le2\sqrt z, \]
    for \(z>0\),
    \[ 0\le\sqrt{2\pi}-\Lambda_0(z)\le2z^{-1/2}, \]
    and \(\Lambda_0(z)\uparrow\sqrt{2\pi}\) as \(z\to\infty\).
  5. (v)
    \(\Fw\le\sw2\) and \(\Lw\le1\), and \(q\mapsto q\Lw(q)\) is strictly increasing.
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is termwise. Each term of \(r_\bmom(q)=\sum_j\emaw j^2q\,\Jfun(\emaw j^2q)\) is \(\Phi(\emaw j^2q)\) by (SF.4). The derivative and its positivity are Lemma SB.1(iii), with \(\EE g^2(1+vg^2)^{-2}=\Phi'(v)\) from Lemma SF.1(ii). By the same lemma, \(r_\bmom\) increases to the number of positive weights, which is infinite for \(\bmom>0\) and equal to \(1\) at \(\bmom=0\).
Part (ii) uses the Gaussian density. The bound \(\Phi\le1\) is Lemma SF.1(ii), and \(\Phi(x)=x\Jfun(x)\le x\) because \(\Jfun\le1\). The derivative \(\Phi'(x)=\EE g^2(1+xg^2)^{-2}\) is decreasing in \(x\). Since the standard Gaussian density is at most \((2\pi)^{-1/2}\),
\[ 1-\Phi(x)=\EE\frac1{1+xg^2}\le\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_\R\frac{\dif y}{1+xy^2}=\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x^{-1/2}. \]
Finally \(x\Phi'(x)=\EE[xg^2(1+xg^2)^{-2}]\) is at most \(\EE[xg^2]=x\), and at most \(\EE[(1+xg^2)^{-1}]=1-\Phi(x)\) because \(xg^2/(1+xg^2)\le1\).
Part (iii) reads off the integrand: \(g^2/(1+xg^2)\) is at most \(g^2\) and at most \(1/x\), and \(\Jfun'(x)=-\EE g^4(1+xg^2)^{-2}\in[-\EE g^4,0]=[-3,0]\).
Part (iv) is a substitution. In the \(j\)-th term of \(\Lw\) the substitution \(y=\emaw j^2x\) gives
\[ \int_0^qx^{-1/2}\emaw j\Jfun(\emaw j^2x)\,\dif x=\int_0^{\emaw j^2q}y^{-1/2}\Jfun(y)\,\dif y=\Lambda_0(\emaw j^2q). \]
The bound \(\Lambda_0(z)\le\int_0^zy^{-1/2}\dif y=2\sqrt z\) uses \(\Jfun\le1\). The full integral \(\int_0^\infty y^{-1/2}\Jfun(y)\,\dif y=\sqrt{2\pi}\) is computed in the proof of Lemma SG.2, and by (iii)
\[ \sqrt{2\pi}-\Lambda_0(z)=\int_z^\infty y^{-1/2}\Jfun(y)\,\dif y\le\int_z^\infty y^{-3/2}\,\dif y=2z^{-1/2}. \]
Part (v) uses \(\Jfun\le1\) again: \(\Fw\le\sum_j\emaw j^2=\sw2\) and \(\Lw\le\sum_j\emaw j=1\). Finally \(q\Lw(q)=\sum_{j:\,\emaw j>0}\Phi(\emaw j^2q)/\emaw j\), a sum of strictly increasing functions.

Lemma SF.5 (Root comparison)
Let \(r\) be continuous and strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\) with \(r(0)=0\), let \(\gin,\gout,s>0\), and put \(D_r\defeq\{q\ge0:\ r(q)<\gamma_\wedge\}\).
  1. (i)
    The function
    \[ G_r(q)\defeq s\,q-\big(\gout-r(q)\big)\big(\gin-r(q)\big) \]
    is strictly increasing on the interval \(D_r\) and has exactly one zero \(q_r(s)\) there. The map \(s\mapsto q_r(s)\) is continuous and strictly decreasing.
  2. (ii)
    Let \(r_1,r_2\) be two such functions, with zeros \(q_1=q_{r_1}(s)\) and \(q_2=q_{r_2}(s)\) at the same \(s\). If \(r_1(q_i)\le r_2(q_i)\) for \(i=1\) or for \(i=2\), then \(q_2\le q_1\).
For \(r=r_\bmom\) the zero is the root of Proposition SF.1(ii): \(q_{r_\bmom}(s)=q(-s)\).
Proof
Proof.
For part (i), the map \(x\mapsto(\gout-x)(\gin-x)\) is positive and decreasing on \([0,\gamma_\wedge)\), so \(G_r\) is strictly increasing on \(D_r\), and \(G_r(0)=-\gin\gout<0\). If \(\sup D_r<\infty\), then \(r(q)\to\gamma_\wedge\) as \(q\uparrow\sup D_r\) by continuity, and \(G_r\) tends to \(s\sup D_r>0\). Otherwise \(G_r(q)\ge sq-\gin\gout\to\infty\). In both cases \(G_r\) has exactly one zero in \(D_r\). For \(s<s'\) the function at \(s'\) exceeds the function at \(s\) by \((s'-s)q>0\) at \(q=q_r(s)>0\), so the zero at \(s'\) is smaller. For continuity, fix \(s\) and \(0<\delta<q_r(s)\). At \(s\) the function is negative at \(q_r(s)-\delta\), and it is positive at \(q_r(s)+\delta\) unless that point lies outside \(D_r\). For a fixed \(q\) the value \(G_r(q)\) is continuous in \(s\), so the same signs hold at every \(s'\) near \(s\), and the zero at \(s'\) lies within \(\delta\) of \(q_r(s)\).
For part (ii) with \(i=2\), \(r_1(q_2)\le r_2(q_2)<\gamma_\wedge\), so \(q_2\in D_{r_1}\), and since \((\gout-x)(\gin-x)\) decreases in \(x\),
\[ G_{r_1}(q_2)\le G_{r_2}(q_2)=0 . \]
Hence \(q_2\le q_1\) by part (i). With \(i=1\), either \(r_2(q_1)\ge\gamma_\wedge\), and then \(q_1\ge\sup D_{r_2}>q_2\), or \(q_1\in D_{r_2}\) and \(G_{r_2}(q_1)\ge G_{r_1}(q_1)=0\), which gives \(q_1\ge q_2\).
The last sentence is the uniqueness of Proposition SF.1(ii).

The next lemma makes the participation scale explicit. For \(\bmom>0\) the level \(r_\bmom(q)\) counts the ages \(j\) with \(\emaw j^2q\ge1\), up to explicit errors.
Lemma SF.6 (Participation count)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) and put
\[ \mathsf n_\bmom(q)\defeq\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)^2q\big)}{2\log(1/\bmom)},\qquad \mathsf c_\bmom\defeq\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{1-\bmom},\qquad \mathsf c_\bmom'\defeq1+\frac1{1-\bmom^2}, \]
\[ \mathsf e_\bmom^-\defeq\bmom^2\,\Phi'(1),\qquad \mathsf e_\bmom^+\defeq\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{1-\bmom}+\frac1{1-\bmom^2}. \]
  1. (i)
    If \((1-\bmom)^2q\ge1\), then \(\mathsf n_\bmom(q)-\mathsf c_\bmom<r_\bmom(q)\le\mathsf n_\bmom(q)+\mathsf c_\bmom'\). Moreover \(r_\bmom\big((1-\bmom)^{-2}\big)\le\mathsf c'_\bmom\).
  2. (ii)
    For every \(q>0\), \(0<q\,r_\bmom'(q)\le\mathsf e_\bmom^+\). If \((1-\bmom)^2q\ge1\), then \(q\,r_\bmom'(q)\ge\mathsf e_\bmom^-\).
The value \(\Phi'(1)=\EE[g^2/(1+g^2)^2]=0.15568\ldots\) enters only \(\mathsf e^-_\bmom\).
Proof
Proof.
Put \(x_j\defeq\emaw j^2q=(1-\bmom)^2q\,\bmom^{2j}\). Then \(r_\bmom(q)=\sum_j\Phi(x_j)\) and \(q\,r_\bmom'(q)=\sum_jx_j\Phi'(x_j)\) by Lemma SF.4(i).
For part (i), let \((1-\bmom)^2q\ge1\) and \(n\defeq\lfloor\mathsf n_\bmom(q)\rfloor\). Since \((1-\bmom)^2q=\bmom^{-2\mathsf n_\bmom(q)}\), we have \(x_j=\bmom^{2(j-\mathsf n_\bmom(q))}\), so \(x_j\ge1\) exactly for \(j\le n\), and
\[ x_j^{-1/2}=\bmom^{\mathsf n_\bmom(q)-j}\le\bmom^{n-j}\qquad(j\le n). \]
The lower bound uses \(\Phi(x)\ge1-\sqrt{\pi/2}\,x^{-1/2}\) (Lemma SF.4(ii)) on the terms \(j\le n\):
\[ r_\bmom(q)\ge\sum_{j\le n}\Big(1-\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,x_j^{-1/2}\Big)\ge n+1-\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\sum_{k\ge0}\bmom^k =n+1-\mathsf c_\bmom>\mathsf n_\bmom(q)-\mathsf c_\bmom . \]
The upper bound uses \(\Phi\le1\) on the terms \(j\le n\) and \(\Phi(x)\le x\) on the others. Since \(x_{n+1}<1\) and \(x_j=\bmom^{2(j-n-1)}x_{n+1}\),
\[ r_\bmom(q)\le n+1+\sum_{j>n}x_j<n+1+\frac1{1-\bmom^2}\le\mathsf n_\bmom(q)+\mathsf c_\bmom' . \]
At \(q=(1-\bmom)^{-2}\) one has \(\mathsf n_\bmom(q)=0\), which gives the second claim.
For part (ii), split the sum at \(x_j=1\) and use \(x\Phi'(x)\le\min(x,\sqrt{\pi/2}\,x^{-1/2})\). The numbers \(x_j^{-1/2}\) over the terms with \(x_j\ge1\), taken from the largest index down, are at most \(1,\bmom,\bmom^2,\dots\), and the numbers \(x_j\) over the terms with \(x_j<1\), taken from the smallest index up, are below \(1,\bmom^2,\bmom^4,\dots\). The two geometric sums give \(\mathsf e_\bmom^+\), and positivity is Lemma SF.4(i). If \(x_0\ge1\), then \(x_{n+1}\in[\bmom^2,1)\), and since \(\Phi'\) is decreasing,
\[ q\,r_\bmom'(q)\ge x_{n+1}\Phi'(x_{n+1})\ge\bmom^2\Phi'(1)=\mathsf e_\bmom^- . \]

Lemma SF.7 (Two constants)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\).
  1. (i)
    If \(y_0>0\) and \(y_j=y_0\bmom^j\), then \(\sum_{j\ge0}\min(y_j,1/y_j)<2/(1-\bmom)\).
  2. (ii)
    \(\displaystyle\mathsf g_\bmom\defeq\sup_{q>0}\sqrt q\,\Lw(q)<\frac2{1-\bmom}\).
  3. (iii)
    \(\displaystyle\mathsf g'_\bmom\defeq\sup_{q>0}\Big|\Lambda_\bmom(q)-\sqrt{2\pi}\,r_\bmom(q)\Big|<\frac{2\pi}{1-\bmom}\).
At \(\bmom=0\), \(\sqrt q\,\Jfun(q)\le1\) and \(\abs{\Lambda_0(q)-\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Phi(q)}\le\pi\) for every \(q>0\).
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) splits the sum at the last index with \(y_j\ge1\), and the bound is uniform in \(y_0\). If there is no such index, every \(y_j<1\) and the sum is \(y_0/(1-\bmom)<1/(1-\bmom)\). Otherwise let \(j_0\) be that index and \(y\defeq y_{j_0}\). Then \(1\le y<1/\bmom\), because \(y_{j_0+1}=\bmom y<1\), and
\[ \sum_{j\le j_0}\frac1{y_j}=\frac1y\sum_{k=0}^{j_0}\bmom^k\le\frac1{(1-\bmom)\,y},\qquad \sum_{j>j_0}y_j=\frac{\bmom y}{1-\bmom}. \]
The function \(y\mapsto1/y+\bmom y\) is convex and equals \(1+\bmom\) at both ends of \([1,1/\bmom]\). Hence
\[ \sum_{j\ge0}\min\Big(y_j,\frac1{y_j}\Big)\le\frac{1+\bmom}{1-\bmom}<\frac2{1-\bmom} \]
for every \(y_0>0\), and the suprema in (ii) and (iii) inherit the strict inequality.
Part (ii) is part (i) with \(y_j=\emaw j\sqrt q\). By Lemma SF.4(iii),
\[ \sqrt q\,\Lw(q)=\sum_jy_j\,\Jfun(y_j^2)\le\sum_j\min\Big(y_j,\frac1{y_j}\Big). \]
Part (iii) rests on a pointwise bound. Put \(\varphi\defeq\Lambda_0-\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Phi\). By Lemma SF.4(i),(iv),
\[ \Lambda_\bmom(q)-\sqrt{2\pi}\,r_\bmom(q)=\sum_j\varphi(\emaw j^2q). \]
We claim that \(\abs{\varphi(z)}\le\pi\min(\sqrt z,z^{-1/2})\). For \(z\le1\) the two terms \(\Lambda_0(z)\le2\sqrt z\) and \(\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Phi(z)\le\sqrt{2\pi}\,z\le\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sqrt z\) are nonnegative, so \(\abs{\varphi(z)}\le\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sqrt z<\pi\sqrt z\). For \(z\ge1\),
\[ \varphi(z)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\big(1-\Phi(z)\big)-\big(\sqrt{2\pi}-\Lambda_0(z)\big) \]
is a difference of two nonnegative terms, bounded by \(\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\pi/2}\,z^{-1/2}=\pi z^{-1/2}\) and by \(2z^{-1/2}\) (Lemma SF.4(ii),(iv)), so \(\abs{\varphi(z)}\le\pi z^{-1/2}\). Part (i) with \(y_j=\emaw j\sqrt q\) gives the bound.
At \(\bmom=0\) the same pointwise bounds give \(\sqrt q\,\Jfun(q)\le\min(\sqrt q,1/\sqrt q)\le1\) and \(\abs{\varphi(q)}\le\pi\).

Lemma SF.8 (Participation identity)
Let \(\gin,\gout>0\), and assume either \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), or \(\bmom=0\) and \(\gamma_\wedge<1\). For \(0\le m\le\gamma_\wedge\) put \(Q_m\defeq r_\bmom^{-1}(m)\),
\[ \mathsf i_\bmom(m)\defeq\sqrt{Q_m}\,\Lw(Q_m)+\tfrac12\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m), \]
\[ \widetilde{\mathcal I}(H)\defeq\int_0^H\big(-\mathsf W'(m)\big)\,\mathsf i_\bmom(m)\,\dif m+\tfrac12\mathsf W(H)\,\Lambda_\bmom(Q_H), \]
and put \(m_\ridge\defeq\psi(\ridge)\) for \(\ridge>0\) and \(m_0\defeq\gamma_\wedge\). Then for every \(\ridge\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.30} \widetilde{\mathcal I}(m_\ridge)\le\int_0^\infty\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge+u^2)\,\dif u\le \widetilde{\mathcal I}(m_\ridge)+\mathsf W(m_\ridge)\sqrt{Q_{m_\ridge}}\,\Lw(Q_{m_\ridge}), \end{equation}
and the last term vanishes at \(\ridge=0\), so that equality holds there. If \(\bmom=0\) and \(\gamma_\wedge\ge1\), the same holds with \(0\le m<1\), \(Q_m=\Phi^{-1}(m)\), \(m_0\defeq1\) and \(\Lambda_0(Q_1)\defeq\sqrt{2\pi}\); the boundary term of \(\widetilde{\mathcal I}(1)\) is then \(\frac12\mathsf W(1)\sqrt{2\pi}\).
Proof
Proof.
The proof changes variables to the participation level and integrates by parts once. Let \(m_\star\) be \(\gamma_\wedge\) in the first two cases and \(1\) in the last. In the first two cases \(Q_{\gamma_\wedge}<\infty\), by Lemma SF.4(i) for \(\bmom>0\) and because \(\Phi^{-1}(\gamma_\wedge)<\infty\) for \(\gamma_\wedge<1\). The map \(m\mapsto Q_m\) is continuously differentiable on \((0,m_\star)\) with \(\dif Q_m/\dif m=1/r_\bmom'(Q_m)\), by Lemma SF.4(i). Put
\[ s(m)\defeq\frac{\mathsf W(m)^2}{Q_m}\qquad(0<m<m_\star). \]
It is a continuously differentiable, strictly decreasing bijection of \((0,m_\star)\) onto \((0,\infty)\): \(\mathsf W\) decreases and \(Q_m\) increases, \(s(m)\to\infty\) as \(m\downarrow0\), and \(s(m)\to0\) as \(m\uparrow m_\star\), because \(\mathsf W(\gamma_\wedge)=0\) in the first two cases and \(Q_m\to\infty\) in the last. Since
\[ s(m)\,Q_m=\mathsf W(m)^2=\big(\gout-r_\bmom(Q_m)\big)\big(\gin-r_\bmom(Q_m)\big), \]
Lemma SF.5(i) gives \(q(-s(m))=Q_m\) and \(\psi(s(m))=m\). In particular \(s(m_\ridge)=\ridge\) for \(\ridge>0\).
The function \(\mathsf G_\bmom\) is nonincreasing, because \(q(-s)\) decreases in \(s\) (Lemma SF.5(i)) and \(q\Lw(q)\) increases in \(q\) (Lemma SF.4(v)). The ridge is removed first. The substitution \(s=\ridge+u^2\) gives
\[ \int_0^\infty\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge+u^2)\,\dif u=\int_\ridge^\infty\mathsf G_\bmom(s)\,\frac{\dif s}{2\sqrt{s-\ridge}} . \]
The kernel \(\frac1{2\sqrt{s-\ridge}}-\frac1{2\sqrt s}\) is nonnegative on \((\ridge,\infty)\) with integral \(\sqrt\ridge\), and \(\mathsf G_\bmom\le\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge)\) there. Hence the integral lies between
\[ \mathcal I_\star(\ridge)\defeq\int_\ridge^\infty\mathsf G_\bmom(s)\,\frac{\dif s}{2\sqrt s} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathcal I_\star(\ridge)+\sqrt\ridge\,\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge), \]
and, at \(s(m_\ridge)=\ridge\),
\[ \sqrt\ridge\,\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge)=\frac{\mathsf W(m_\ridge)}{\sqrt{Q_{m_\ridge}}}\,Q_{m_\ridge}\Lw(Q_{m_\ridge}) =\mathsf W(m_\ridge)\sqrt{Q_{m_\ridge}}\,\Lw(Q_{m_\ridge}). \]
The change of variables \(s=s(m)\), \(m\in(0,m_\ridge)\), transforms \(\mathcal I_\star(\ridge)\). Differentiating \(s=\mathsf W^2/Q_m\),
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathsf G_\bmom(s(m))\,\frac{-s'(m)}{2\sqrt{s(m)}} &=Q_m\Lw(Q_m)\Big(\frac{-\mathsf W'(m)}{\sqrt{Q_m}}+\frac{\mathsf W(m)}{2Q_m^{3/2}\,r_\bmom'(Q_m)}\Big)\\ &=\sqrt{Q_m}\,\Lw(Q_m)\big(-\mathsf W'(m)\big)+\frac{\mathsf W(m)}2\,\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m), \end{aligned} \]
since \(\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m)=Q_m^{-1/2}\Lw(Q_m)/r_\bmom'(Q_m)\). An integration by parts on \([0,H]\), with \(\Lambda_\bmom(Q_0)=0\), turns the last term into
\[ \int_0^H\frac{\mathsf W(m)}2\,\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m)\,\dif m =\tfrac12\mathsf W(H)\Lambda_\bmom(Q_H)+\int_0^H\big(-\mathsf W'(m)\big)\,\tfrac12\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m)\,\dif m , \]
so that \(\mathcal I_\star(\ridge)=\widetilde{\mathcal I}(m_\ridge)\) for \(\ridge>0\). At \(\ridge=0\) the boundary term \(\sqrt\ridge\,\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge)\) is absent, and we let \(H\uparrow m_\star\) by monotone convergence. In the first two cases \(\mathsf W(m_\star)=0\) and \(\Lambda_\bmom(Q_{m_\star})<\infty\), so the boundary term of \(\widetilde{\mathcal I}\) vanishes. In the last case \(\Lambda_0(Q_H)\to\sqrt{2\pi}\) by Lemma SF.4(iv), which gives the boundary term \(\frac12\mathsf W(1)\sqrt{2\pi}\).
□
The main term of \(\widetilde{\mathcal I}\) is \(\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}2\int_0^H\mathsf W\), because \(\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m)\) is \(\sqrt{2\pi}\,m\) up to the bounded error of Lemma SF.7(iii). This gives the drift as a fixed function of the participation level.
Theorem SF.3 (Master drift estimate)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\gin,\gout>0\) and \(\ridge\ge0\), with the convention \(\psi(0)\defeq\gamma_\wedge\).
  1. (i)
    The drift satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.31} \Big|\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)-\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^{\psi(\ridge)}\mathsf W(m)\,\dif m\Big| \le\frac{\sqrt2\,(\mathsf g_\bmom+\mathsf g'_\bmom/2)}{\pi\sqrt{\gin\gout}} <\frac{2.32}{(1-\bmom)\sqrt{\gin\gout}} . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    In the square case, with \(\Xi_0(x)\defeq\pi^{-1/2}\big(x-\tfrac12x^2\big)\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.32} \Big|\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)-\Xi_0\Big(\frac{\psi(\ridge)}\gamma\Big)\Big|<\frac{2.32}{(1-\bmom)\gamma} . \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
For part (i) write \(H\defeq\psi(\ridge)=m_\ridge\) and \(\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,m+k(m)\), so that \(\abs k\le\mathsf g'_\bmom\) by Lemma SF.7(iii) and \(r_\bmom(Q_m)=m\). An integration by parts gives
\[ \int_0^Hm\big(-\mathsf W'(m)\big)\,\dif m+H\,\mathsf W(H)=\int_0^H\mathsf W(m)\,\dif m . \]
By Lemma SF.8, therefore,
\[ \int_0^\infty\mathsf G_\bmom(\ridge+u^2)\,\dif u=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}2\int_0^H\mathsf W(m)\,\dif m+R, \]
\[ R=\int_0^H\big(-\mathsf W'(m)\big)\Big[\sqrt{Q_m}\,\Lw(Q_m)+\frac{k(m)}2\Big]\dif m+\frac12\mathsf W(H)\,k(H)+E_\ridge, \]
where \(0\le E_\ridge\le\mathsf g_\bmom\,\mathsf W(H)\) by (SF.30), whose last term is at most \(\mathsf g_\bmom\,\mathsf W(H)\).
Since \(-\mathsf W'\ge0\) and \(0\le\sqrt{Q_m}\Lw(Q_m)\le\mathsf g_\bmom\),
\[ \abs R\le\Big(\mathsf g_\bmom+\frac{\mathsf g'_\bmom}2\Big)\big(\mathsf W(0)-\mathsf W(H)\big)+\Big(\mathsf g_\bmom+\frac{\mathsf g'_\bmom}2\Big)\mathsf W(H) =\Big(\mathsf g_\bmom+\frac{\mathsf g'_\bmom}2\Big)\sqrt{\gin\gout} . \]
Multiplying by \(\sqrt2/(\pi\gin\gout)\) ((SF.29)) and using \(\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt{2\pi}/(2\pi)=\pi^{-1/2}\) gives the first inequality of (SF.31). By Lemma SF.7(ii),(iii), \(\mathsf g_\bmom+\mathsf g'_\bmom/2<(2+\pi)/(1-\bmom)\), and \(\sqrt2(2+\pi)/\pi=2.3146\ldots\).
Part (ii) is part (i) at \(\gin=\gout=\gamma\), where
\[ \frac1{\gamma^2}\int_0^{\psi}(\gamma-m)\,\dif m=\frac\psi\gamma-\frac12\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big)^2 . \]

SF.9 Asymptotics in the aspect ratios

In this subsection and the next, \(\bmom=0\) and \(C=C_0\), as in Section SF.7. The small-aspect limit is a Marchenko–Pastur-type law, which we write down explicitly with its atom at zero.
Lemma SF.9 (The limiting law)
For \(r>0\) put
\[ a_r\defeq\frac{(1-\sqrt r)^2}{r},\qquad b_r\defeq\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{r},\qquad \omega_r\defeq\Big(1-\frac1r\Big)_+ . \]
The measure
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.33} \mathsf M_r(\dif x)\defeq\omega_r\,\delta_0(\dif x)+\frac{\sqrt{(b_r-x)(x-a_r)}}{2\pi x}\,\mathbf 1_{(a_r,b_r)}(x)\,\dif x \end{equation}
is a probability measure with first moment \(1/r\). For \(t>0\) its transform \(m_r(t)\defeq S_{\mathsf M_r}(-t)\) is the unique positive root \(m\) of
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.34} t\,m^2+\Big(t+\frac1r-1\Big)m-1=0 , \end{equation}
namely, with \(A_r\defeq1+1/r\) and \(D_r\defeq2/\sqrt r\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.35} m_r(t)=\frac{1-1/r-t+\sqrt{(A_r+t)^2-D_r^2}}{2t}. \end{equation}
Proof
Proof.
One integral carries the whole computation. Write \(a=a_r\), \(b=b_r\), \(A=A_r=(a+b)/2\) and \(D=D_r=(b-a)/2\), and for \(t\ge0\) put
\[ I(t)\defeq\int_a^b\frac{\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}}{x+t}\,\dif x . \]
The substitution \(x=A+D\cos\vartheta\), \(\vartheta\in[0,\pi]\), gives \(\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}=D\sin\vartheta\). With \(c\defeq A+t\ge D\) and the identity
\[ \frac{D^2\sin^2\vartheta}{c+D\cos\vartheta}=c-D\cos\vartheta-\frac{c^2-D^2}{c+D\cos\vartheta}, \]
together with \(\int_0^\pi(c+D\cos\vartheta)^{-1}\dif\vartheta=\pi/\sqrt{c^2-D^2}\) for \(c>D\), obtained by the substitution \(v=\tan(\vartheta/2)\), this gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.36} I(t)=\pi\Big(A+t-\sqrt{(A+t)^2-D^2}\Big), \end{equation}
for \(t>0\), and for \(t=0\) by monotone convergence.
The mass and the first moment follow. Since \(A^2-D^2=(1-1/r)^2\), the continuous part of \(\mathsf M_r\) has mass
\[ \frac{I(0)}{2\pi}=\frac{1+1/r-\abs{1-1/r}}{2}=\min\Big(1,\frac1r\Big), \]
and adding \(\omega_r\) gives \(1\). The first moment is
\[ \frac1{2\pi}\int_a^b\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}\,\dif x=\frac{D^2}{2\pi}\int_0^\pi\sin^2\vartheta\,\dif\vartheta=\frac{D^2}{4}=\frac1r . \]
The transform is a difference of two values of \(I\). By the partial fractions \(\frac{1}{x(x+t)}=\frac1t\big(\frac1x-\frac1{x+t}\big)\),
\[ m_r(t)=\frac{\omega_r}{t}+\frac{I(0)-I(t)}{2\pi t}, \]
and inserting (SF.36) with \(2\omega_r-\abs{1-1/r}=1-1/r\) gives (SF.35). Since
\[ \Big(t+\frac1r-1\Big)^2+4t=(A+t)^2-D^2 , \]
(SF.35) is the root of (SF.34) with the positive square root. The product of the two roots is \(-1/t<0\), so it is the only positive root.
□
Theorem SF.4 (Small aspect ratios)
Let \(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\to0\) with \(r_j\defeq\gout^{(j)}/\gin^{(j)}\to r\in(0,\infty)\), and let \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}^{(j)})\) be the isotropic measure pair at \((\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)})\) of Proposition SF.6. Let \(\mathsf P_j\) be the image of the probability measure \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)}/\gout^{(j)}\) under \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda/\gout^{(j)}\), and write \(\mathsf m_j(t)\defeq S_{\mathsf P_j}(-t)\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(t>0\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.37} \Big|t\,\mathsf m_j(t)^2+\Big(t+\frac1{r_j}-1\Big)\mathsf m_j(t)-1\Big|\le\frac{3\gout^{(j)}}{r_j^2t^2}. \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    \(\mathsf P_j\to\mathsf M_r\) weakly, and \(\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x)\to J_r\defeq\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf M_r(\dif x)>0\).
  3. (iii)
    The constant satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.38} \begin{gathered} C\big(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\big)=\frac{r_j}{\sqrt{2\gout^{(j)}}}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x) \sim\frac{r}{\sqrt{2\gout^{(j)}}}\,J_r,\\ J_r=\int_{a_r}^{b_r}\frac{\sqrt{(b_r-x)(x-a_r)}}{2\pi\sqrt x}\,\dif x . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    In particular \(J_1=8/(3\pi)\), and \(C(\gamma)\sim8/(3\pi\sqrt{2\gamma})\) as \(\gamma\downarrow0\).
Proof
Proof.
We drop the index \(j\) from \(\gin,\gout,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}},\sigma,\tilde\sigma\) and keep it on \(r_j\), \(\mathsf P_j\) and \(\mathsf m_j\). Fix \(t>0\).
The transform of \(\mathsf P_j\) is the branch at \(-\gout t\):
\[ \mathsf m_j(t)=\frac{1}{\gout}\int\frac{\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)}{\lambda/\gout+t}=\sigma(-\gout t), \]
and by (SF.6) the companion is
\[ \tilde\sigma(-\gout t)=\sigma(-\gout t)+\frac{\gin-\gout}{\gout t}=\mathsf m_j(t)+\frac{1/r_j-1}{t}. \]
Both transforms are bounded by the mass over \(\gout t\): since \(\mathsf P_j\) is a probability measure, \(\mathsf m_j(t)\le1/t\), and \(\tilde\sigma(-\gout t)\le\gin/(\gout t)=1/(r_jt)\). The argument of \(\Phi\) in the closure is therefore small:
\[ v_j\defeq\gout t\,\sigma(-\gout t)\,\tilde\sigma(-\gout t)=\gout\,\mathsf m_j\Big(t\,\mathsf m_j+\frac1{r_j}-1\Big)\le\frac{\gout}{r_jt}. \]
For part (i), the closure (SF.25) at \(\gout t\) reads
\[ \gout\big(1-t\,\mathsf m_j\big)=\Phi(v_j)=v_j-e_j,\qquad 0\le e_j\le3v_j^2, \]
by Lemma SF.1(iii). Dividing by \(\gout\) and inserting \(v_j\),
\[ t\,\mathsf m_j^2+\Big(t+\frac1{r_j}-1\Big)\mathsf m_j-1=\frac{e_j}{\gout}, \]
and \(e_j/\gout\le3v_j^2/\gout\le3\gout/(r_j^2t^2)\).
For part (ii), the residual (SF.37) tends to zero and \(\mathsf m_j(t)\in[0,1/t]\). Every limit point \(m\) of \((\mathsf m_j(t))_j\) is therefore a nonnegative root of (SF.34), and \(m\ne0\) because the constant term is \(-1\). By Lemma SF.9, \(m=m_r(t)\), so \(\mathsf m_j(t)\to S_{\mathsf M_r}(-t)\) for every \(t>0\). The first moments are bounded: by Lemma SF.3(ii),
\[ \int x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x)=\frac{1}{\gout^2}\int\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)=\frac{\gin\gout}{\gout^2}=\frac1{r_j}, \]
and \(\mathsf M_r\) has first moment \(1/r\). Lemma SF.2(i) gives the weak convergence and the convergence of the half-moments. The limit \(J_r\) is positive because \(\mathsf M_r\) gives positive mass \(\min(1,1/r)\) to \((a_r,b_r)\subset(0,\infty)\).
For part (iii), (SF.27) and the change of variables \(\lambda=\gout x\) give
\[ C(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\gin\gout}\,\gout^{3/2}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x) =\frac{r_j}{\sqrt{2\gout}}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P_j(\dif x), \]
and part (ii) gives the asymptotic equivalence. The atom of \(\mathsf M_r\) at \(0\) does not contribute to \(J_r\). For \(r=1\), \(a_1=0\) and \(b_1=4\), so
\[ J_1=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^4\sqrt{4-x}\,\dif x=\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{16}{3}=\frac{8}{3\pi}. \]
□
Proposition SF.8 (Reciprocity)
  1. (i)
    Let \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}})\) be the isotropic measure pair at \((\gin,\gout)\), let \(b=\gin\gout\), and let \(\mathsf P\) be the image of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}/\gout\) under \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda/\gout\). Then \(\mathsf P\) is the image of \(\widehat{\mathfrak m}_b/\gout\) under \(x\mapsto\gin x\). If \(\widehat{\mathfrak m}_b\) has a density \(\widehat\rho\), then \(\mathsf P\) has the density \(\widehat\rho(x/\gin)/(\gin\gout)\). Its transform and the constant are
    \[ S_{\mathsf P}(-t)=\sigma(-\gout t)=\frac{1}{\gin\gout}\,S_{\widehat{\mathfrak m}_b}\Big(-\frac{t}{\gin}\Big),\qquad C(\gin,\gout)=\frac{\sqrt{\gout}}{\sqrt2\,\gin}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf P(\dif x). \]
  2. (ii)
    For every \(r>0\), \(J_{1/r}=r^{3/2}J_r\), and the right side of (SF.38) is unchanged when \(\gin\) and \(\gout\) are exchanged (with \(r\) replaced by \(1/r\)).
  3. (iii)
    For every \(r>0\) the endpoint pairs \(\{(1\pm\sqrt r)^2/r\}\) and \(\{(1\pm1/\sqrt r)^2\}\) are equal.
Proof
Proof.
For part (i), the image of \(\lambda\) under the two maps is \(\lambda/(\gin\gout)\) followed by \(\gin\lambda/(\gin\gout)=\lambda/\gout\). The density is the change of variables with the Jacobian \(1/\gin\) and the mass normalization \(1/\gout\). The transform is the first computation of the proof of Theorem SF.4 together with \(S_{\widehat{\mathfrak m}_b}(-t/\gin)=b\,\sigma(-\gout t)\) from Proposition SF.7, and the constant is the computation of Theorem SF.4(iii) with \(r_j=\gout/\gin\), since \(r_j/\sqrt{\gout}=\sqrt{\gout}/\gin\).
For part (ii), the endpoints at \(1/r\) are \(a_{1/r}=r\,a_r\) and \(b_{1/r}=r\,b_r\). Writing \(\rho_r\) for the density of the continuous part of \(\mathsf M_r\), direct substitution gives
\[ \rho_{1/r}(ry)=\frac{\sqrt{r^2(b_r-y)(y-a_r)}}{2\pi ry}=\rho_r(y), \]
so that
\[ J_{1/r}=\int\sqrt x\,\rho_{1/r}(x)\,\dif x=\int\sqrt{ry}\,\rho_r(y)\,r\,\dif y=r^{3/2}J_r . \]
Exchanging the ratios replaces \((r,\gout)\) by \((1/r,\gin)\) in (SF.38), and with \(\gout=r\gin\),
\[ \frac{1/r}{\sqrt{2\gin}}\,J_{1/r}=\frac{\sqrt r\,J_r}{\sqrt{2\gin}}=\frac{r\,J_r}{\sqrt{2\gout}} . \]
Part (iii) is the identity \((1\pm\sqrt r)^2/r=(1/\sqrt r\pm1)^2\), whose right side is \((1\pm1/\sqrt r)^2\) for the same choice of sign.

Theorem SF.5 (Large aspect ratios)
Let \(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\to\infty\), without a condition on their ratio, and let \((\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)},\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{in}}^{(j)})\) be the isotropic measure pair at \((\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)})\) of Proposition SF.6. For \(j\) with \(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}>1\) write \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}^{(j)}=(\gout^{(j)}-1)\delta_0+\eta_j\) as in Lemma SF.3(iv), let \(\mathsf E_j\) be the image of the probability measure \(\eta_j\) under \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda/(\gin^{(j)}\gout^{(j)})\), and put \(\epsilon_j\defeq1/\gin^{(j)}+1/\gout^{(j)}\). Let \(\mathsf X\) be the law of \(g^2\) for \(g\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), which has density, first moment and half-moment
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.39} \frac{e^{-x/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\,\mathbf 1_{x>0},\qquad \int x\,\mathsf X(\dif x)=1,\qquad \int\sqrt x\,\mathsf X(\dif x)=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}. \end{equation}
Then \(\mathsf E_j\to\mathsf X\) weakly,
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.40} 0\le\sqrt{\frac2\pi}-\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf E_j(\dif x)\le\frac4\pi\sqrt{\epsilon_j}, \end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.41} C\big(\gin^{(j)},\gout^{(j)}\big)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\gin^{(j)}\gout^{(j)}}}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf E_j(\dif x)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\gin^{(j)}\gout^{(j)}}} . \end{equation}
In particular \(C(\gamma)\sim1/(\sqrt\pi\,\gamma)\) as \(\gamma\to\infty\).
Proof
Proof.
We drop the index \(j\) from the aspect ratios and the branch, write \(b=\gin\gout\), and fix \(j\) with \(\gin,\gout>1\). For \(t>0\) put
\[ \mathsf s_j(t)\defeq S_{\mathsf E_j}(-t),\qquad \mathsf H_j(t)\defeq1-t\,\mathsf s_j(t)=\int\frac{x}{x+t}\,\mathsf E_j(\dif x)\in[0,1]. \]
The closure at \(bt\) is a closure for \(\mathsf E_j\). By the decomposition of \(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}\),
\[ bt\,\sigma(-bt)=(\gout-1)+t\,\mathsf s_j(t)=\gout-\mathsf H_j(t), \]
and by Lemma SF.3(iv) the same computation on the input side gives \(bt\,\tilde\sigma(-bt)=\gin-\mathsf H_j(t)\). The closure (SF.25) at \(bt\) therefore reads
\[ \mathsf H_j(t)=\Phi\big(v_j(t)\big),\qquad v_j(t)\defeq bt\,\sigma(-bt)\,\tilde\sigma(-bt)=\frac1t\Big(1-\frac{\mathsf H_j(t)}{\gout}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\mathsf H_j(t)}{\gin}\Big). \]
The argument \(v_j(t)\) is close to \(1/t\). Since \(0\le\mathsf H_j\le1\) and \((1-x)(1-y)\ge1-x-y\) for \(x,y\ge0\),
\[ 0\le\frac1t-v_j(t)\le\frac{\epsilon_j}{t}. \]
The Gaussian law is the fixed point at \(v=1/t\). Indeed
\[ \Phi(1/t)=\EE\frac{g^2}{g^2+t}=1-t\,S_{\mathsf X}(-t)=\int\frac{x}{x+t}\,\mathsf X(\dif x). \]
Since \(\Phi\) is increasing with slope at most \(1\) and takes values in \([0,1]\) (Lemma SF.1(ii)),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.42} 0\le\Phi(1/t)-\mathsf H_j(t)\le\min\Big(1,\frac{\epsilon_j}{t}\Big),\qquad \abs{\mathsf s_j(t)-S_{\mathsf X}(-t)}\le\frac{\epsilon_j}{t^2}. \end{equation}
The law \(\mathsf X\) has the stated density by the change of variables \(x=u^2\) in the Gaussian density, first moment \(\EE g^2=1\), and half-moment \(\EE\abs g=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). The first moment of \(\mathsf E_j\) is
\[ \int x\,\mathsf E_j(\dif x)=\frac1b\int\lambda\,\eta_j(\dif\lambda)=\frac1b\int\lambda\,\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{out}}(\dif\lambda)=1 , \]
by Lemma SF.3(ii). Since \(\epsilon_j\to0\), (SF.42) and Lemma SF.2(i) give \(\mathsf E_j\to\mathsf X\) weakly.
The quantitative bound integrates (SF.42). By the half-moment formula (SF.26) for \(\mathsf X\) and for \(\mathsf E_j\),
\[ \sqrt{\frac2\pi}-\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf E_j(\dif x)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\big(\Phi(1/t)-\mathsf H_j(t)\big)\,\dif t , \]
whose integrand lies between \(0\) and \(t^{-1/2}\min(1,\epsilon_j/t)\). Splitting at \(t=\epsilon_j\),
\[ \int_0^{\epsilon_j}t^{-1/2}\,\dif t+\epsilon_j\int_{\epsilon_j}^\infty t^{-3/2}\,\dif t=2\sqrt{\epsilon_j}+2\sqrt{\epsilon_j}, \]
which proves (SF.40).
Finally, (SF.27), the vanishing of \(\sqrt\lambda\) on the atom, and the change of variables \(\lambda=bx\) give
\[ C(\gin,\gout)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,b}\int\sqrt\lambda\,\eta_j(\dif\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt2\,b}\int\sqrt x\,\mathsf E_j(\dif x), \]
which is the identity of (SF.41); the equivalence follows from (SF.40), since \(\sqrt{2/\pi}/\sqrt{2b}=1/\sqrt{\pi b}\).
□
For aspect ratios at least one the participation representation of Section SF.8 gives the constant exactly.
Proposition SF.9 (The constant for \(N\ge B\))
For every \(\gamma\ge1\),
\[ C(\gamma)=\frac{\gamma-1}{\sqrt\pi\,\gamma^2}+\frac{C(1)}{\gamma^2}. \]
Proof
Proof.
Apply Lemma SF.8 at \(\bmom=0\), \(\gin=\gout=\gamma\ge1\) and \(\ridge=0\). In the square case \(-\mathsf W'=1\) and \(\mathsf W(1)=\gamma-1\), and at \(\bmom=0\) the integrand \(\mathsf G_0(u^2)\) is \(\psi(u^2)\) by (SF.24), so
\[ \int_0^\infty\psi(u^2)\,\dif u=\mathcal I_1+\frac{(\gamma-1)\sqrt{2\pi}}2,\qquad \mathcal I_1\defeq\int_0^1\mathsf i_0(m)\,\dif m . \]
The levels \(Q_m=\Phi^{-1}(m)\), and hence \(\mathcal I_1\), do not depend on \(\gamma\). At \(\gamma=1\) the second term vanishes, and (SF.21) gives \(\mathcal I_1=\pi C(1)/\sqrt2\). Multiplying by \(\sqrt2/(\pi\gamma^2)\) and using \(\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt{2\pi}/(2\pi)=\pi^{-1/2}\) gives the formula.

Theorem SF.6 (The constant at aspect ratio one)
The constant (SF.21) at \(\gin=\gout=1\) is
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.43} C(1)=\frac{1+\log(1+\sqrt2)/\sqrt2}{2\sqrt\pi}, \end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.44} C(1)>\frac{3}{4\sqrt\pi}>\Big[\pi\Big(1+\frac{9\pi}{32}\Big)\Big]^{-1/2}. \end{equation}
The constant \(C(1)\) of Definition SI.2, whose hypothesis holds at \(\gamma=1\) by Proposition SF.6, is the constant (SF.43), by Proposition SF.7 and the remark following Definition SF.2. Numerically \(C(1)\approx0.4579\), while the interpolation of (9) at \(N/B=1\) is \([\pi(1+9\pi/32)]^{-1/2}\approx0.4111\).
Proof
Proof.
The proof changes variables from \(t\) to the closure variable \(\xi\) and then evaluates a Gaussian double integral.
At \(\gin=\gout=1\) the two transforms coincide, \(\sigma=\tilde\sigma\), by (SF.6), and the closure (SF.24) reads
\[ \psi(t)=1-t\,\sigma(-t)=\Phi\big(t\,\sigma(-t)^2\big). \]
Put \(\xi(t)\defeq(2t\sigma(-t)^2)^{-1/2}\) and, for \(\xi>0\),
\[ \bar f(\xi)\defeq1-f(\xi)=\EE\frac{2\xi^2}{V^2+2\xi^2}\qquad(V\sim\mathcal N(0,1)). \]
By Lemma SF.1(i), \(\psi(t)=f(\xi(t))\), so that \(t\sigma(-t)=\bar f(\xi(t))\). Dividing the square of this identity by \(t\sigma(-t)^2=1/(2\xi(t)^2)\) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.45} \sqrt t=\sqrt2\,\xi(t)\,\bar f(\xi(t)). \end{equation}
The map \(\xi\mapsto\sqrt2\,\xi\bar f(\xi)\) is a smooth increasing bijection of \((0,\infty)\). Indeed \(\bar f(\xi)=1-\Phi(1/(2\xi^2))\) is smooth and strictly increasing, by Lemma SF.1(ii) and Lemma SB.1(ii), and it tends to \(0\) as \(\xi\downarrow0\) and to \(1\) as \(\xi\to\infty\), by Lemma SF.1(iv). By (SF.45), \(t\mapsto\xi(t)\) is its inverse composed with \(\sqrt t\).
The constant becomes an integral in \(\xi\). By (SF.23) at \(\gin=\gout=1\) and the substitution \(t=2\xi^2\bar f(\xi)^2\), for which \(t^{-1/2}\,\dif t=2\sqrt2\,(\xi\bar f)'\,\dif\xi\),
\[ C(1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi}\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\psi(t)\,\dif t =\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\big(1-\bar f(\xi)\big)\big(\xi\bar f(\xi)\big)'\,\dif\xi . \]
Two integrations by parts simplify the integrand. Since
\[ (1-\bar f)(\xi\bar f)'=\big[(1-\bar f)\,\xi\bar f\big]'+\xi\bar f\bar f',\qquad \xi\bar f\bar f'=\tfrac12\big[\xi(\bar f^2-1)\big]'+\tfrac12\big(1-\bar f^2\big), \]
and the boundary terms vanish,
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.46} C(1)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\big(1-\bar f(\xi)^2\big)\,\dif\xi . \end{equation}
The boundary terms are at most \(\xi\) and \(\xi\) in absolute value near \(0\), and at most \(1/(2\xi)\) and \(1/\xi\) near infinity, because \(1-\bar f(\xi)=f(\xi)=\Phi(1/(2\xi^2))\le1/(2\xi^2)\) by Lemma SF.1(iii).
The square of \(\bar f\) is a Gaussian double expectation. Let \(U,V\) be independent standard Gaussian variables, and put \(\mathsf a\defeq\abs U/\sqrt2\) and \(\mathsf b\defeq\abs V/\sqrt2\). Then \(\bar f(\xi)=\EE[\xi^2/(\xi^2+\mathsf a^2)]\), and independence gives
\[ 1-\bar f(\xi)^2=\EE\Big[1-\frac{\xi^4}{(\xi^2+\mathsf a^2)(\xi^2+\mathsf b^2)}\Big] =\EE\Big[\frac{(\mathsf a^2+\mathsf b^2)\xi^2+\mathsf a^2\mathsf b^2}{(\xi^2+\mathsf a^2)(\xi^2+\mathsf b^2)}\Big]. \]
For \(\mathsf a\ne\mathsf b\), which holds with probability one, partial fractions give
\[ \frac{(\mathsf a^2+\mathsf b^2)\xi^2+\mathsf a^2\mathsf b^2}{(\xi^2+\mathsf a^2)(\xi^2+\mathsf b^2)} =\frac{1}{\mathsf a^2-\mathsf b^2}\Big(\frac{\mathsf a^4}{\xi^2+\mathsf a^2}-\frac{\mathsf b^4}{\xi^2+\mathsf b^2}\Big), \]
and with \(\int_0^\infty(\xi^2+c^2)^{-1}\dif\xi=\pi/(2c)\),
\[ \int_0^\infty\frac{(\mathsf a^2+\mathsf b^2)\xi^2+\mathsf a^2\mathsf b^2}{(\xi^2+\mathsf a^2)(\xi^2+\mathsf b^2)}\,\dif\xi =\frac\pi2\,\frac{\mathsf a^3-\mathsf b^3}{\mathsf a^2-\mathsf b^2}=\frac\pi2\,\frac{\mathsf a^2+\mathsf a\mathsf b+\mathsf b^2}{\mathsf a+\mathsf b}. \]
Inserting this in (SF.46) gives
\[ C(1)=\frac12\,\EE\frac{\mathsf a^2+\mathsf a\mathsf b+\mathsf b^2}{\mathsf a+\mathsf b} =\frac{1}{2\sqrt2}\,\EE\frac{U^2+\abs{UV}+V^2}{\abs U+\abs V}. \]
The expectation is computed in polar coordinates. Write \((\abs U,\abs V)=R(\cos\vartheta,\sin\vartheta)\). By the Gaussian density in polar coordinates, \(R\) has density \(re^{-r^2/2}\) on \((0,\infty)\), \(\vartheta\) is uniform on \([0,\pi/2]\), and the two are independent. An integration by parts gives
\[ \EE R=\int_0^\infty r^2e^{-r^2/2}\,\dif r=\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2/2}\,\dif r=\sqrt{\frac\pi2}, \]
and the ratio is \(R(1+\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta)/(\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta)\), so
\[ C(1)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt2}\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\cdot\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1+\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta}{\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta}\,\dif\vartheta =\frac{1}{2\sqrt\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1+\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta}{\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta}\,\dif\vartheta . \]
Since \(1+\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta=\frac12\big((\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta)^2+1\big)\) and \(\int_0^{\pi/2}(\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta)\,\dif\vartheta=2\), the last integral is
\[ 1+\frac12\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\dif\vartheta}{\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta} =1+\frac{1}{2\sqrt2}\int_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}\frac{\dif\vartheta'}{\sin\vartheta'} =1+\frac{1}{2\sqrt2}\log\frac{\tan(3\pi/8)}{\tan(\pi/8)}=1+\frac{\log(1+\sqrt2)}{\sqrt2}, \]
using \(\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta=\sqrt2\sin(\vartheta+\pi/4)\) with \(\vartheta'=\vartheta+\pi/4\), the antiderivative \(\log\tan(\vartheta'/2)\) of \(1/\sin\vartheta'\), and \(\tan(3\pi/8)=1+\sqrt2=1/\tan(\pi/8)\). This proves (SF.43).
For (SF.44), the derivative of \(\log(t+\sqrt{1+t^2})\) is \((1+t^2)^{-1/2}\), so
\[ \log(1+\sqrt2)=\int_0^1\frac{\dif t}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}>\frac{1}{\sqrt2}, \]
because the integrand exceeds \(1/\sqrt2\) on \([0,1)\). Hence \(C(1)>(1+\frac12)/(2\sqrt\pi)=3/(4\sqrt\pi)\). The second inequality is equivalent to \(16/9<1+9\pi/32\), that is, to \(\pi>224/81\), which holds because \(224/81<3<\pi\).

Lemma SF.12 (Lag lower bound)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\Nin=\Nout=N\), \(\gamma=N/B\), \(\varepsilon\ge0\) and \(\ell\ge1\). Put \(m_\varepsilon\defeq\psi(\varepsilon)\) for \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(m_0\defeq\gamma\). Then
\[ \overline C_{1,\ell}(\varepsilon)\ge\frac{\mathsf k_\bmom}{\gamma^3}\int_{\mathsf c'_\bmom}^{\,m_\varepsilon-\ell-\mathsf c''_\bmom} \big(\gamma-\eta-\mathsf c''_\bmom\big)\big(\gamma-\eta-\ell-\mathsf c''_\bmom\big)\,\dif\eta, \]
where the right side is zero if the range is empty. For \(\varepsilon>0\) the right side is at least \(\frac{\mathsf k_\bmom}{\gamma^3}(\gamma-m_\varepsilon)^2\big(m_\varepsilon-\ell-\mathsf c''_\bmom-\mathsf c'_\bmom\big)_+\).
Proof
Proof.
The proof bounds the determinant by one, changes variables to the two roots, and integrates over a thin region where the participation count is effective. Write \(r=r_\bmom\) and \(Q\defeq r^{-1}(m_\varepsilon)\), so that \(Q=q(-\varepsilon)\) for \(\varepsilon>0\).
Change of variables. By Lemma SF.11,
\[ \overline C_{1,\ell}(\varepsilon)\ge\frac4{\pi^2\gamma^3}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty b_\ell\,q\,q'\,\dif x\,\dif y,\qquad q=q(-\varepsilon-x^2),\quad q'=q(-\varepsilon-y^2). \]
In the square case the closure (SF.7) inverts as \(s(q)=(\gamma-r(q))^2/q\), so each axis is parametrized by its root \(q\in(0,Q)\), with \(x=\sqrt{s(q)-\varepsilon}\). Since \(\sqrt{s(q)}=(\gamma-r(q))\,q^{-1/2}\) and \(r'>0\),
\[ \Big|\frac{\dif x}{\dif q}\Big|\ge\Big|\frac{\dif\sqrt{s(q)}}{\dif q}\Big|\ge\frac{\gamma-r(q)}{2\,q^{3/2}} . \]
By (SG.15), \(b_\ell=\bmom^\ell\big(r(q)-r(\bmom^{2\ell}q')\big)/\big(q-\bmom^{2\ell}q'\big)\). The substitution \(p=\bmom^{2\ell}q'\) in the second variable therefore gives
\[ \overline C_{1,\ell}(\varepsilon)\ge\frac1{\pi^2\gamma^3}\iint D(q,p)\,\big(\gamma-r(q)\big)\big(\gamma-r(p\,\bmom^{-2\ell})\big)\,\dif q\,\dif p, \qquad D(q,p)\defeq\frac{r(q)-r(p)}{(q-p)\sqrt{qp}}\ge0, \]
over the region \(r(q)<m_\varepsilon\), \(r(p\,\bmom^{-2\ell})<m_\varepsilon\).
Restriction. Keep only \(p\ge(1-\bmom)^{-2}\) and \(q=p\,e^\delta\) with \(\delta\in[0,2]\). By Lemma SF.6(ii), \(r'(x)\ge\mathsf e^-_\bmom/x\) for \(x\ge p\), so \(r(q)-r(p)\ge\mathsf e^-_\bmom\delta\), and with \((e^\delta-1)/\delta\le e^\delta\),
\[ D(q,p)\ge\frac{\mathsf e^-_\bmom\,\delta}{p^2(e^\delta-1)\,e^{\delta/2}}\ge\frac{\mathsf e^-_\bmom\,e^{-3}}{p^2} . \]

\[ r(q)<r(p)+\mathsf c''_\bmom,\qquad r(p\,\bmom^{-2\ell})<r(p)+\ell+\mathsf c''_\bmom . \]
Put \(\eta\defeq r(p)\) and keep only \(\eta\le m_\varepsilon-\ell-\mathsf c''_\bmom\). Then both constraints of the region hold, and the two brackets are at least \(\gamma-\eta-\mathsf c''_\bmom\) and \(\gamma-\eta-\ell-\mathsf c''_\bmom\), which are nonnegative. \[ \int_p^{pe^2}D(q,p)\,\dif q\ge\frac{\mathsf e^-_\bmom\,e^{-3}(e^2-1)}{p} . \]

The condition \(p\ge(1-\bmom)^{-2}\) holds when \(\eta\ge\mathsf c'_\bmom\), because \(r((1-\bmom)^{-2})\le\mathsf c'_\bmom\) (Lemma SF.6(i)). Collecting the factors gives the bound.
The case \(\varepsilon>0\). The integrand decreases in \(\eta\), and at the upper endpoint it is \((\gamma-m_\varepsilon+\ell)(\gamma-m_\varepsilon)\ge(\gamma-m_\varepsilon)^2\).

Lemma SF.13 (Participation slope)
For every \(y>0\),
\[ \mathsf e_\bmom(y)\le\frac14+\frac1{2\log(1/\bmom)}\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom . \]
Proof
Proof.
The slope is a lattice sum of the logistic density \(\mathsf l(v)\defeq e^v/(1+e^v)^2\), which is unimodal, with maximum \(\frac14\) and integral \(1\) over \(\R\). Put \(x_j\defeq\emaw j^2y\). By Lemma SF.4(i) and the identity \(t/(1+t)^2=\mathsf l(\log t)\) for \(t>0\),
\[ \mathsf e_\bmom(y)=\sum_{j\ge0}x_j\Phi'(x_j)=\sum_{j\ge0}\EE\frac{x_jg^2}{(1+x_jg^2)^2} =\EE\sum_{j\ge0}\mathsf l\Big(\log\big((1-\bmom)^2yg^2\big)-2j\log(1/\bmom)\Big). \]
For fixed \(g\ne0\) the arguments of \(\mathsf l\) lie on a lattice of spacing \(2\log(1/\bmom)\).
A lattice sum of a unimodal function is at most its maximum plus its integral divided by the spacing. Let \(F\ge0\) be unimodal with mode \(v_*\), and let the points \(p\) form a lattice of spacing \(h\). Each point \(p<v_*\) other than the largest such point satisfies
\[ F(p)\le\frac1h\int_p^{p+h}F , \]
because \(F\) is nondecreasing on \([p,p+h]\). Each point \(p>v_*\) other than the smallest such point satisfies \(F(p)\le h^{-1}\int_{p-h}^pF\) for the same reason. These cells are disjoint. The two remaining points \(p_-\le v_*\le p_+=p_-+h\) satisfy \(F\ge\min(F(p_-),F(p_+))\) on \([p_-,p_+]\), so that
\[ F(p_-)+F(p_+)\le\max F+\frac1h\int_{p_-}^{p_+}F . \]
Summing gives \(\sum_pF(p)\le\max F+h^{-1}\int_\R F\). With \(F=\mathsf l\) and \(h=2\log(1/\bmom)\) the inner sum is at most \(\frac14+1/(2\log(1/\bmom))\) for every \(g\ne0\), and taking the expectation proves the lemma.

Lemma SF.15 (Kernel bounds)

    \(\max(0,r_\wedge-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)\le\mathcal X(y,z)\le r_\wedge\).
  4. (iv)

  5. (v)

Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is partial fractions. By Lemma SF.4(i) and \(\Fw(y)=r(y)/y\),
\[ r(y)=\sum_j\EE\frac{yd_j^2}{1+yd_j^2},\qquad \Fw(y)=\sum_j\EE\frac{d_j^2}{1+yd_j^2}. \]
The difference quotient of the first series is the series of \(\mathcal Z/\sqrt{yz}\). The second form follows from \(z\,r(y)-y\,r(z)=yz\big(\Fw(y)-\Fw(z)\big)\) and
\[ \frac{d_j^2}{1+yd_j^2}-\frac{d_j^2}{1+zd_j^2}=\frac{(z-y)\,d_j^4}{(1+yd_j^2)(1+zd_j^2)} . \]
For (ii), nonnegativity is read from (i). The first bound uses \(\abs{r(y)-r(z)}\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\delta\) (Lemma SF.14(iv)) and \(\sqrt{yz}/\abs{y-z}=1/(2\sinh(\delta/2))\). The second bounds every factor \((1+\cdots)^{-1}\) in (i) by one, which leaves \(\sqrt{yz}\sum_j\emaw j^2\,\EE g^2=\sw2\sqrt{yz}\). The bound \(\delta/(2\sinh(\delta/2))\le1\) is \(\sinh x\ge x\). Since \(2\sinh(\delta/2)=e^{\delta/2}(1-e^{-\delta})\), the bound \(\delta/(2\sinh(\delta/2))\le(1+\delta)e^{-\delta/2}\) is equivalent to \((1+\delta)e^{-\delta}\le1\), that is to \(e^\delta\ge1+\delta\).
For (iii), take \(z<y\), so that \(y=ze^\delta\); the case \(y<z\) is symmetric. Then
\[ \mathcal X(y,z)=r(z)-\frac{r(y)-r(z)}{e^\delta-1},\qquad 0\le\frac{r(y)-r(z)}{e^\delta-1}\le\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\,\delta}{e^\delta-1}\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom , \]
and \(r(z)=r_\wedge\). Nonnegativity of \(\mathcal X\) is read from (i).
For (iv), put \(v(y)\defeq\big(yd^2,\sqrt y\,d\big)/(1+yd^2)\in\R^2\) for a real number \(d\). Then (i) reads
\[ \mathcal X(y,z)+\mathcal Z(y,z)=\sum_j\EE\big\langle v(y),v(z)\big\rangle\Big|_{d=d_j},\qquad \abs{v(y)}^2=\frac{yd^2}{1+yd^2}, \]
and the second identity summed over \(j\) and averaged over \(g\) is \(r(y)\). The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in \(j\) and \(g\) gives (iv).
For (v), part (iii) gives \(\gamma-\mathcal X\ge\gamma-r_\wedge=\mathsf A_\vee\). With \(\mathcal Z\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\) from (ii), this gives \(\gamma-\mathcal X-\mathcal Z\ge\mathsf A_\vee-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\). By (iv) and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality,
\[ \gamma-\mathcal X-\mathcal Z\ge\gamma-\tfrac12\big(r(y)+r(z)\big)=\tfrac12\big(\mathsf A(y)+\mathsf A(z)\big)\ge\frac{\mathsf A_\vee}2 . \]
The determinant factors as
\[ \gamma^2\widehat\Delta=(\gamma-\mathcal X-\mathcal Z)(\gamma-\mathcal X+\mathcal Z), \]
and \(\gamma-\mathcal X+\mathcal Z\ge\mathsf A_\vee\), which gives the lower bound. For the upper bound, (iii) gives \(0\le\gamma-\mathcal X\le\gamma-r_\wedge+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom=\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\), so that \(\gamma^2\widehat\Delta\le(\gamma-\mathcal X)^2\le(\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)^2\).

In the representation below, the two roots of the lag kernel are the levels \(y\) and \(z_\ell\), and the determinant depends only on the pair \((y,z)\).
Proposition SF.10 (Lag coefficients in participation coordinates)
Let \(\ridge\ge0\) and let \(\ell\ge1\) be an integer. Then \(\widehat\Delta(y,z)>0\) whenever \(y<Q_{\psi(\ridge)}\) and \(z_\ell<Q_{\psi(\ridge)}\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.55} \overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)=\frac1{\pi^2\gamma^3}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty D(y,z)\, \mathbf 1\{y<Q_{\psi(\ridge)},\ z_\ell<Q_{\psi(\ridge)}\}\, \frac{\mathsf j(y)\,\mathsf u_\ridge(y)\;\mathsf j(z_\ell)\,\mathsf u_\ridge(z_\ell)}{\widehat\Delta(y,z)}\,\dif y\,\dif z . \end{equation}
The lag \(\ell\) enters (SF.55) only through the weight \(\mathsf j(z_\ell)\mathsf u_\ridge(z_\ell)\) and the constraint \(z_\ell<Q_{\psi(\ridge)}\).
Proof
Proof.
The proof writes the two scalars of the isotropic lag kernel as divided differences of \(r\) at the pair \((y,z)\), and then changes variables from the spectral parameters to the roots.
The kernel at a pair of roots. By Lemma SF.11(ii), the lag kernel at \((s,s')\) is \(b_\ell\,qq'/(\gamma^3\Delta_\ell)\) with \(q=q(-s)\) and \(q'=q(-s')\). Put \(y\defeq q\) and \(z\defeq\bmom^{2\ell}q'\). The divided-difference forms (SG.15) and \(\Fw(x)=r(x)/x\) give
\[ \sqrt{qq'}\,b_\ell=\sqrt{yz}\,\frac{r(y)-r(z)}{y-z}=\mathcal Z(y,z),\qquad qq'\,e_\ell=yz\,\frac{\Fw(y)-\Fw(z)}{z-y}=\mathcal X(y,z). \]
The determinant of Lemma SF.11 is therefore \(\Delta_\ell=\widehat\Delta(y,z)\), which is positive by Lemma SF.11(i).
Change of variables. In the square case the closure (SF.7) inverts as \(s=\mathsf s(q)\defeq\mathsf A(q)^2/q\). The map \(\mathsf s\) is continuously differentiable and strictly decreasing from \((0,Q_\gamma)\) onto \((0,\infty)\), with
\[ \mathsf s'(y)=\frac{-2y\,r'(y)\,\mathsf A(y)-\mathsf A(y)^2}{y^2}=-\frac{\mathsf A(y)\,\mathsf j(y)}{y^2}, \]
and \(q(-\mathsf s(y))=y\) by the uniqueness of Proposition SF.1(ii). For \(\ridge>0\) one has \(\mathsf s(Q_{\psi(\ridge)})=\ridge\), by the closure at \(s=\ridge\). Hence \(x=\sqrt{\mathsf s(y)-\ridge}\) is a decreasing bijection of \((0,Q_{\psi(\ridge)})\) onto \((0,\infty)\) for every \(\ridge\ge0\), with
\[ \Big|\frac{\dif x}{\dif y}\Big|=\frac{\mathsf A(y)\,\mathsf j(y)}{2y^2\sqrt{\mathsf A(y)^2/y-\ridge}}=\frac{\mathsf j(y)\,\mathsf u_\ridge(y)}{2y^{3/2}} . \]
Apply this substitution in both variables of (SF.52), with \(y=q(-\ridge-x^2)\) and \(y'=q(-\ridge-x'^2)\). Since the kernel is \(b_\ell\,yy'/(\gamma^3\widehat\Delta)\),
\[ \begin{aligned} \overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)&=\frac4{\pi^2}\iint_{y,y'<Q_{\psi(\ridge)}}\frac{b_\ell\,yy'}{\gamma^3\,\widehat\Delta}\cdot \frac{\mathsf j(y)\mathsf u_\ridge(y)\,\mathsf j(y')\mathsf u_\ridge(y')}{4\,(yy')^{3/2}}\,\dif y\,\dif y'\\ &=\frac1{\pi^2\gamma^3}\iint_{y,y'<Q_{\psi(\ridge)}}\frac{b_\ell\,\mathsf j(y)\mathsf u_\ridge(y)\,\mathsf j(y')\mathsf u_\ridge(y')}{\widehat\Delta\,\sqrt{yy'}}\,\dif y\,\dif y' . \end{aligned} \]
The lag shift. Substitute \(y'=\bmom^{-2\ell}z=z_\ell\). Then
\[ \frac{b_\ell\,\dif y'}{\sqrt{yy'}}=\bmom^\ell\,\frac{r(y)-r(z)}{y-z}\cdot\frac{\bmom^{-2\ell}\,\dif z}{\bmom^{-\ell}\sqrt{yz}}=D(y,z)\,\dif z , \]
which is (SF.55).

The next lemma averages the divided difference \(D\) over the intermediate participation level. Under this averaging the level enters only through the base point of a fixed random map, and \(r'\) is absorbed by the change of variables \(\dif\eta=r'(s)\,\dif s\).
Lemma SF.16 (Layer averaging)
Let \(\nu_\star\) be the measure on \(\{+,-\}\times(0,\infty)^2\) whose density in \((a,c)\) on each of the two copies is
\[ \frac1{\pi^2}\cdot\frac1{2\sinh\big((a+c)/2\big)} , \]
write \(\EE_\star\) for the integral against it, and put \(\tau\defeq a+c\),
\[ \Pi_+(s;a,c)\defeq(se^a,se^{-c}),\qquad \Pi_-(s;a,c)\defeq(se^{-c},se^a). \]
  1. (i)
    \(\nu_\star\) is a probability measure, and for every nonnegative function \(\Psi\) on \((0,\infty)^2\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.56} \frac1{\pi^2}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty D(y,z)\,\Psi(y,z)\,\dif y\,\dif z=\int_0^\infty\EE_\star\big[\Psi\big(\Pi(Q_\eta;\cdot)\big)\big]\,\dif\eta . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    The moments \(\mathsf M_k\defeq\EE_\star\tau^k\) and \(\mathsf M_e\defeq\EE_\star e^{\tau/4}\) are
    \[ \mathsf M_1=\frac{28\,\zeta(3)}{\pi^2}=3.41023\ldots,\quad \mathsf M_2=2\pi^2, \]
    \[ \mathsf M_3=\frac{1488\,\zeta(5)}{\pi^2}=156.333\ldots,\quad \mathsf M_e=2+\frac{16\,\mathrm G}{\pi^2}=3.48491\ldots, \]
    where \(\mathrm G=0.91596\ldots\) is Catalan’s constant.
  3. (iii)
    If \((y,z)=\Pi(s;a,c)\), then \(\abs{\log(y/s)}\le\tau\), \(\abs{\log(z/s)}\le\tau\), \(\abs{\mathsf A(y)-\mathsf A(s)}\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\), \(\abs{\mathsf A(z)-\mathsf A(s)}\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\) and \(z\ge se^{-\tau}\).
Proof
Proof.
For (i), write the divided difference as an integral of \(r'\) over the intermediate level,
\[ D(y,z)=\frac1{\abs{y-z}\sqrt{yz}}\int_{\min(y,z)}^{\max(y,z)}r'(s)\,\dif s , \]
and exchange the order of integration. For fixed \(s\), the region \(z<s<y\) is the image of \((0,\infty)^2\) under \(\Pi_+(s;\cdot)\), with \(\dif y\,\dif z=yz\,\dif a\,\dif c\) and
\[ \frac{yz}{\abs{y-z}\sqrt{yz}}=\frac{e^{(a-c)/2}}{e^a-e^{-c}}=\frac1{2\sinh(\tau/2)} . \]
The region \(y<s<z\) is the image under \(\Pi_-(s;\cdot)\), with the same factor. Finally \(\eta=r(s)\) is a continuously differentiable increasing bijection of \((0,\infty)\) onto itself (Lemma SF.4(i)), with \(\dif\eta=r'(s)\,\dif s\) and \(s=Q_\eta\). This gives (SF.56), once \(\nu_\star\) is shown to have mass one, which is the case \(k=0\) below.
For (ii), the density depends on \((a,c)\) only through \(\tau\), and the set \(\{a+c\in\dif\tau\}\) has length \(\tau\,\dif\tau\). Hence, for \(k\ge0\),
\[ \EE_\star\tau^k=\frac2{\pi^2}\int_0^\infty\frac{\tau^{k+1}}{2\sinh(\tau/2)}\,\dif\tau . \]
Expanding \(1/(2\sinh(\tau/2))=\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-(n+1/2)\tau}\) and integrating term by term gives, for \(p>0\),
\[ \int_0^\infty\frac{\tau^p}{2\sinh(\tau/2)}\,\dif\tau=\Gamma(p+1)\sum_{n\ge0}\big(n+\tfrac12\big)^{-p-1}=2^{p+1}\,\Gamma(p+1)\big(1-2^{-p-1}\big)\,\zeta(p+1) . \]
At \(p=1,2,3,4\) this is \(\pi^2/2\), \(14\zeta(3)\), \(\pi^4\) and \(744\zeta(5)\). Multiplying by \(2/\pi^2\) gives \(\EE_\star1=1\) and the stated \(\mathsf M_1,\mathsf M_2,\mathsf M_3\). For \(\mathsf M_e\) the same expansion gives
\[ \mathsf M_e=\frac2{\pi^2}\sum_{n\ge0}\int_0^\infty\tau\,e^{-(n+1/4)\tau}\,\dif\tau=\frac2{\pi^2}\sum_{n\ge0}\big(n+\tfrac14\big)^{-2}=\frac{32}{\pi^2}\sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{(4n+1)^2}, \]
and \(\sum_{n\ge0}(4n+1)^{-2}=\pi^2/16+\mathrm G/2\), the sum of \(\sum_{n\ge0}(2n+1)^{-2}=\pi^2/8\) and \(\mathrm G=\sum_{n\ge0}\big((4n+1)^{-2}-(4n+3)^{-2}\big)\), halved.
For (iii), \((y,z)\) is \((se^a,se^{-c})\) or \((se^{-c},se^a)\) with \(a,c\ge0\), which gives the bounds on the logarithms and on \(z\). Lemma SF.14(iv) turns them into the bounds on \(\mathsf A\).

Lemma SF.17 (Low levels)
For \(\eta>0\) let \((y,z)=\Pi(Q_\eta;a,c)\) as in Lemma SF.16, and put \(\sigma\defeq\log\big((1-\bmom)^2Q_\eta\big)\). Then

and \(\mathsf L_k\defeq\int_0^\infty\EE_\star\big[\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)^k\big]\,\dif\eta\) satisfies \(\mathsf L_k\le\bar{\mathsf L}_k\) for \(k=1,2\), where
\[ \bar{\mathsf L}_1\defeq\frac1{2\log(1/\bmom)}\Big(\frac{\mathsf M_1+1}{1-\bmom^2}+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\mathsf M_2}2\Big),\qquad \bar{\mathsf L}_2\defeq\frac1{4\log^2(1/\bmom)}\Big(\frac{\mathsf M_2+2\mathsf M_1+2}{1-\bmom^2}+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\mathsf M_3}3\Big). \]
Proof
Proof.
The function \(\mathsf n^-_\bmom\) is nonincreasing and \(z\ge Q_\eta e^{-\tau}\) (Lemma SF.16(iii)), which gives the first claim.
For the moments, change variables from \(\eta\) to \(\sigma\). Since \(\eta=r(Q_\eta)\),
\[ \dif\eta=r'(Q_\eta)\,\dif Q_\eta=\mathsf e_\bmom(Q_\eta)\,\dif\sigma . \]
Two bounds on the slope are used. For \(\sigma<0\), \(\mathsf e_\bmom(s)\le\sw2\,s=e^\sigma/(1-\bmom^2)\), because \(r'\le\sw2\) (Lemma SF.4(i),(ii)) and \(\sw2/(1-\bmom)^2=1/(1-\bmom^2)\). For \(\sigma\ge0\), \(\mathsf e_\bmom\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\) (Lemma SF.13). Hence, for \(\tau\ge0\) and \(k=1,2\),
\[ \int_\R(\tau-\sigma)_+^k\,\mathsf e_\bmom\,\dif\sigma\le\frac1{1-\bmom^2}\int_0^\infty(\tau+v)^ke^{-v}\,\dif v+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\,\frac{\tau^{k+1}}{k+1}, \]
with \(\int_0^\infty(\tau+v)e^{-v}\dif v=\tau+1\) and \(\int_0^\infty(\tau+v)^2e^{-v}\dif v=\tau^2+2\tau+2\). Dividing by \((2\log(1/\bmom))^k\) and integrating against \(\nu_\star\) with Lemma SF.16(ii) gives the bounds.

Theorem SF.9 (Lag sum at zero ridge)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\ge\gamma^\circ_\bmom\). Then
\[ -\mathsf K^-_\bmom\le\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)-\frac\gamma4\le\mathsf K^+_\bmom . \]
Proof
Proof.
The proof sums over the lag first, then averages over the participation level, and compares the integrand with \(\mathsf A(s)/2\) pointwise. The errors are linear or quadratic in \(\tau\) and \(\mathsf n^-_\bmom\), which have finite moments.
Step 1: the summed representation. By Proposition SF.10 at \(\ridge=0\), summed over \(\ell\), and Lemma SF.16(i),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.58} \sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)=\frac1\gamma\int_0^\gamma\EE_\star\big[\Psi\big(\Pi(Q_\eta;\cdot)\big)\big]\,\dif\eta, \end{equation}
where
\[ \Psi(y,z)\defeq\mathbf 1\{y<Q_\gamma\}\,\frac{\mathsf j(y)\,\mathsf T(z)}{\gamma^2\,\widehat\Delta(y,z)},\qquad \mathsf T(z)\defeq\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathbf 1\{z_\ell<Q_\gamma\}\,\mathsf j(z_\ell). \]
The range of \(\eta\) is \((0,\gamma)\), because \(\Psi(y,z)\ne0\) forces \(y<Q_\gamma\) and \(z<z_1<Q_\gamma\), and the base point \(s=Q_\eta\) lies between \(y\) and \(z\). Write \(x\defeq\mathsf A(s)=\gamma-\eta\). Then
\[ \frac1\gamma\int_0^\gamma\frac x2\,\dif\eta=\frac\gamma4 . \]
Step 2: bounds on \(\mathsf T\). Put \(L_z\defeq\gamma-\mathsf n_\bmom(z)+\mathsf c_\bmom\) and \(E_z\defeq\mathsf c^+_\bmom+\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)\). A term of \(\mathsf T(z)\) with \(z_\ell<Q_\gamma\) has \(r(z_\ell)<\gamma\), and Lemma SF.14(ii) gives \(r(z_\ell)\ge\mathsf n_\bmom(z)+\ell-\mathsf c_\bmom\). Hence \(\ell<L_z\) and, by Lemma SF.13,
\[ \mathsf j(z_\ell)\le\gamma-r(z_\ell)+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\le L_z-\ell+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom . \]
By Lemma SF.14(iii), \(L_z\le\mathsf A(z)+E_z\le\mathsf A_\vee+E_z\). If \(\mathsf T(z)>0\), then \(L_z>1\), and since \(\sum_{1\le\ell<L}(L-\ell)\le L^2/2\) and \(t\mapsto t^2/2+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom t\) increases on \([0,\infty)\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.59} \mathsf T(z)\le\frac{L_z^2}2+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom L_z\le\frac{(\mathsf A_\vee+E_z)^2}2+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\mathsf A_\vee+E_z) ; \end{equation}
the bound also holds when \(\mathsf T(z)=0\). Conversely, every \(1\le\ell<\mathsf A(z)\) has \(r(z_\ell)\le r(z)+\ell<\gamma\) by Lemma SF.14(i), and \(\mathsf j(z_\ell)\ge\mathsf A(z_\ell)\ge\mathsf A(z)-\ell\). Comparing the decreasing summand with an integral,
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.60} \mathsf T(z)\ge\sum_{1\le\ell<\mathsf A(z)}\big(\mathsf A(z)-\ell\big)\ge\frac{(\mathsf A(z)-1)_+^2}2 . \end{equation}
Step 3: upper bound. On the set \(\{\Psi>0\}\) one has \(\mathsf A(y)>0\), and \(\mathsf T(z)>0\) forces \(r(\bmom^{-2}z)<\gamma\), so that \(\mathsf A(z)>\Phi(1)\) by Lemma SF.14(v). Thus \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\Phi(1)\) there. With \(\mathsf j(y)\le\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\), (SF.59) and the lower bound of Lemma SF.15(v),
\[ \Psi\le\frac{(\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)\big((\mathsf A_\vee+E_z)^2/2+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\mathsf A_\vee+E_z)\big)}{\mathsf A_\vee\max(\mathsf A_\vee-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom,\mathsf A_\vee/2)} . \]
Suppose first that \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\). Then
\[ \frac{\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\mathsf A_\vee-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}=1+\frac{3\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\mathsf A_\vee-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}\le1+\frac{6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\mathsf A_\vee}\le4 . \]
The second factor divided by \(\mathsf A_\vee\) is \(\mathsf A_\vee/2+E_z+E_z^2/(2\mathsf A_\vee)+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom E_z/\mathsf A_\vee\). Applying the factor \(1+6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom/\mathsf A_\vee\) to its first term and the factor \(4\) to the rest, and using \(2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom/\mathsf A_\vee\le1\) and \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\Phi(1)\),
\[ \Psi\le\frac{\mathsf A_\vee}2+3\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+4\Big(E_z+\frac{E_z^2}{2\mathsf A_\vee}+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+\frac{2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom E_z}{\mathsf A_\vee}\Big) \le\frac{\mathsf A_\vee}2+11\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+8E_z+\frac{2E_z^2}{\Phi(1)} . \]
Suppose instead that \(\Phi(1)\le\mathsf A_\vee<2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\). Then \(\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom<4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\), the second factor of the numerator is at most \(6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom^2+4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom E_z+E_z^2/2\), and the denominator is at least \(\Phi(1)^2/2\), so that
\[ \Psi\le\frac{8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\Phi(1)^2}\Big(6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom^2+4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom E_z+\frac{E_z^2}2\Big). \]
In both cases, and also where \(\Psi=0\),
\[ \Psi\le\frac{\mathsf A_\vee}2+\mathsf K_0+\mathsf K_1E_z+\mathsf K_2E_z^2 . \]
Now \(\mathsf A_\vee\le x+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\) by Lemma SF.16(iii), and \(E_z^2\le2(\mathsf c^+_\bmom)^2+2\,\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)^2\). Therefore
\[ \Psi\le\frac x2+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}2+\mathsf K_0+\mathsf K_1\mathsf c^+_\bmom+2\mathsf K_2(\mathsf c^+_\bmom)^2+\mathsf K_1\,\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)+2\mathsf K_2\,\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)^2 . \]
Integrate against \(\gamma^{-1}\dif\eta\,\nu_\star\) over \(\eta\in(0,\gamma)\). The first term gives \(\gamma/4\), the second \(\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\mathsf M_1/2\), the constants give themselves, and the last two terms give at most \((\mathsf K_1\bar{\mathsf L}_1+2\mathsf K_2\bar{\mathsf L}_2)/\gamma\) by Lemma SF.17, which is at most \(\mathsf K_1\bar{\mathsf L}_1+2\mathsf K_2\bar{\mathsf L}_2\) because \(\gamma\ge1\).
Step 4: lower bound. By (SF.60), \(\mathsf j(y)\ge\mathsf A(y)\) and the upper bound of Lemma SF.15(v),
\[ \Psi\ge\mathbf 1\{y<Q_\gamma\}\,\frac{\mathsf A(y)\,(\mathsf A(z)-1)_+^2}{2(\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)^2} . \]
Put \(\upsilon\defeq\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\tau+1)\). Since \(\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\ge1\), Lemma SF.16(iii) gives
\[ \mathsf A(y)\ge x-\upsilon,\qquad \mathsf A(z)-1\ge x-\upsilon,\qquad \mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\le x+\upsilon . \]
If \(x>\upsilon\), then \(\mathsf A(y)>0\), that is \(y<Q_\gamma\), and with \(t\defeq\upsilon/x<1\),
\[ \Psi\ge\frac{(x-\upsilon)^3}{2(x+\upsilon)^2}=\frac x2\,(1-t)\Big(\frac{1-t}{1+t}\Big)^2\ge\frac x2\,(1-t)(1-2t)^2 . \]
For \(t<\frac15\) this is at least \(\frac x2(1-3t)(1-2t)\ge\frac x2(1-5t)=\frac{x-5\upsilon}2\). For \(t\ge\frac15\), and for \(x\le\upsilon\), the right side \((x-5\upsilon)/2\) is negative. Hence \(\Psi\ge(x-5\upsilon)/2\) everywhere, and integration as in Step 3, with \(\EE_\star\upsilon=\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\mathsf M_1+1)\), gives
\[ \sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\ge\frac\gamma4-\frac52\,\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\mathsf M_1+1) . \]

\[ \mathcal G(x)\defeq\begin{cases}1-x+x\log x,&0\le x\le1,\\ 0,&x\ge1,\end{cases} \]
with \(\mathcal G(0)=1\), and
\[ \mathsf K_3\defeq14\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+\frac{32\,\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom^2}{\Phi(1)},\qquad \mathsf K_4\defeq4+\frac{8\,\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\Phi(1)},\qquad \mathsf K_5\defeq4+\frac{4+8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\Phi(1)}+\frac{8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+32\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom^2}{\Phi(1)^2}, \]

Theorem SF.10 (Lag profile at zero ridge)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\ge\gamma^\circ_\bmom\). For every integer \(\ell\ge1\),
\[ -\frac{4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\mathsf M_1+1)}\gamma\Big(1+\log^+\frac\gamma{4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}\Big)\le\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)-\mathcal G\Big(\frac\ell\gamma\Big)\le\frac{\mathsf K^{\rm pr}_\bmom(1+\log\gamma)}\gamma, \]
\[ \overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\le\mathsf K^{\rm dec}_\bmom\,e^{-\delta_\bmom(\ell-\gamma)} . \]
If \(\ell_\gamma/\gamma\to x\in[0,\infty)\) along integer pairs \((N,B)\), then \(\overline C_{1,\ell_\gamma}(0)\to\mathcal G(x)\). At the corner the profile vanishes quadratically,
\[ \mathcal G(x)=\tfrac12(1-x)^2+\tfrac16(1-x)^3+O\big((1-x)^4\big)\qquad(x\uparrow1), \]
and beyond it the lag coefficients decay exponentially in \(\ell-\gamma\).
Proof
Proof.
The proof is the unsummed version of the proof of Theorem SF.9: the same pointwise bounds, with \(\mathsf T(z)\) replaced by a single weight, compare the integrand with \((1-\ell/x)_+\), whose integral over the level is exactly \(\mathcal G(\ell/\gamma)\).
Step 1: representation. By Proposition SF.10 at \(\ridge=0\) and Lemma SF.16(i),
\[ \overline C_{1,\ell}(0)=\frac1\gamma\int_0^\gamma\EE_\star[\Psi_\ell]\,\dif\eta,\qquad \Psi_\ell\defeq\mathbf 1\{y<Q_\gamma,\ z_\ell<Q_\gamma\}\,\frac{\mathsf j(y)\,\mathsf j(z_\ell)}{\gamma^2\,\widehat\Delta(y,z)}, \]
with \((y,z)=\Pi(Q_\eta;\cdot)\). With \(x=\gamma-\eta\), direct integration gives
\[ \frac1\gamma\int_0^\gamma\Big(1-\frac\ell x\Big)_+\dif\eta=\frac1\gamma\int_\ell^\gamma\Big(1-\frac\ell x\Big)\dif x=\mathcal G\Big(\frac\ell\gamma\Big). \]
One more integral recurs below. For \(Y>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.61} \frac1\gamma\int_0^\gamma\min\Big(1,\frac Yx\Big)\dif\eta\le\frac Y\gamma\Big(1+\log^+\frac\gamma Y\Big), \end{equation}
and the right side is nondecreasing in \(Y\).
Step 2: upper bound. As in Steps 2 and 3 of the proof of Theorem SF.9, \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\Phi(1)\) on \(\{\Psi_\ell>0\}\), and
\[ \mathsf j(z_\ell)\,\mathbf 1\{z_\ell<Q_\gamma\}\le(\mathsf A_\vee+E_z-\ell)_++2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom , \]
so that
\[ \Psi_\ell\le\frac{(\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)\big((\mathsf A_\vee+E_z-\ell)_++2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\big)}{\mathsf A_\vee\max(\mathsf A_\vee-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom,\mathsf A_\vee/2)} . \]
If \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\), this is at most
\[ \Big(1+\frac{6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\mathsf A_\vee}\Big)\Big[\Big(1-\frac\ell{\mathsf A_\vee}\Big)_++\frac{E_z+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\mathsf A_\vee}\Big]\le\Big(1-\frac\ell{\mathsf A_\vee}\Big)_++\frac{14\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+4E_z}{\mathsf A_\vee} . \]
If \(\mathsf A_\vee<2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\), it is at most \(8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+E_z)/(\Phi(1)\mathsf A_\vee)\). Hence
\[ \Psi_\ell\le\Big(1-\frac\ell{\mathsf A_\vee}\Big)_++\frac{\mathsf K_3+\mathsf K_4E_z}{\mathsf A_\vee} . \]
The first term is compared with its value at \(x\). The function \(A\mapsto(1-\ell/A)_+\) is nondecreasing, and \((1-\ell/(x+\delta))_+-(1-\ell/x)_+\le\min(1,\delta/x)\) for \(\delta\ge0\); with \(\mathsf A_\vee\le x+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\) this gives
\[ \Big(1-\frac\ell{\mathsf A_\vee}\Big)_+\le\Big(1-\frac\ell x\Big)_++\min\Big(1,\frac{1+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}x\Big). \]
The second term is controlled by
\[ \frac1{\mathsf A_\vee}\le\frac1{\Phi(1)}\min\Big(1,\frac{2(\Phi(1)+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau)}x\Big): \]
if \(\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\le x/2\), then \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\mathsf A(y)\ge x/2\), and otherwise the minimum is one. Hence
\[ \Psi_\ell\le\Big(1-\frac\ell x\Big)_++\min\Big(1,\frac{1+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}x\Big)+\frac{\mathsf K_3+\mathsf K_4\mathsf c^+_\bmom}{\Phi(1)}\min\Big(1,\frac{2(\Phi(1)+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau)}x\Big)+\frac{\mathsf K_4\,\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)}{\Phi(1)} . \]
Integrate with (SF.61). For \(Y=1+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\ge1\) the logarithm is at most \(\log\gamma\). For \(Y=2(\Phi(1)+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau)\ge2\Phi(1)\) it is at most \(\log\gamma+\log(1/(2\Phi(1)))\), and \(1+a+b\le(1+a)(1+b)\) for \(a,b\ge0\). Then take \(\EE_\star\), and bound the last term by Lemma SF.17.
Step 3: lower bound. If \(\mathsf A(z)>\ell\), then \(r(z_\ell)\le r(z)+\ell<\gamma\) and \(\mathsf j(z_\ell)\ge\mathsf A(z)-\ell\). With Lemma SF.15(v),
\[ \Psi_\ell\ge\mathbf 1\{y<Q_\gamma\}\,\frac{\mathsf A(y)\,(\mathsf A(z)-\ell)_+}{(\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)^2} . \]
With \(\upsilon=\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\tau+1)\) as before, \(\mathsf A(y)\ge x-\upsilon\), \(\mathsf A(z)-\ell\ge x-\ell-\upsilon\) and \(\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\le x+\upsilon\). If \(x-\ell>4\upsilon\), put \(t\defeq\upsilon/x<\frac14\). Then \(y<Q_\gamma\), and
\[ \Psi_\ell\ge\frac{1-t}{(1+t)^2}\Big(1-\frac\ell x-t\Big)\ge(1-2t)(1-t)\Big(1-\frac\ell x-t\Big)\ge(1-3t)\Big(1-\frac\ell x-t\Big)\ge1-\frac\ell x-4t . \]
If \(x-\ell\le4\upsilon\), then \((1-\ell/x)_+\le\min(1,4\upsilon/x)\). Hence
\[ \Psi_\ell\ge\Big(1-\frac\ell x\Big)_+-\min\Big(1,\frac{4\upsilon}x\Big) \]
in all cases. Integrate with (SF.61), using \(4\upsilon\ge4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\) in the logarithm and \(\EE_\star[4\upsilon]=4\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\mathsf M_1+1)\).
Step 4: decay. Keeping the indicator \(\mathbf 1\{\ell<L_z\}\) in Step 2, and using \(L_z-\ell+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\le\mathsf A_\vee+E_z+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\) in both cases of \(\mathsf A_\vee\), gives
\[ \Psi_\ell\le\mathsf K_5\,(1+E_z)\,\mathbf 1\{\ell<L_z\} . \]
Also \(L_z\le\mathsf A_\vee+E_z\le\gamma+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau+\mathsf c^+_\bmom+\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)\). With \(\delta=\delta_\bmom\), the bounds \(\mathbf 1\{\ell<L_z\}\le e^{\delta(L_z-\ell)}\) and \((1+\mathsf c^+_\bmom+n)e^{\delta n}\le(1+\mathsf c^+_\bmom+\delta^{-1})e^{2\delta n}\) for \(n\ge0\) give
\[ \Psi_\ell\le\mathsf K_5\Big(1+\mathsf c^+_\bmom+\frac1\delta\Big)e^{\delta\mathsf c^+_\bmom}\,e^{-\delta(\ell-\gamma)}\,e^{\tau/8}\,e^{(\tau-\sigma)_+/8}, \]
because \(\delta\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\le\frac18\) and \(2\delta\,\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)\le(\tau-\sigma)_+/8\), with \(\sigma=\log((1-\bmom)^2Q_\eta)\) (Lemma SF.17). Finally, with \(\dif\eta=\mathsf e_\bmom\,\dif\sigma\) and the two bounds on \(\mathsf e_\bmom\) of the proof of Lemma SF.17,
\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\gamma\EE_\star\big[e^{\tau/8}e^{(\tau-\sigma)_+/8}\big]\dif\eta &\le\gamma\,\mathsf M_e+\EE_\star\Big[e^{\tau/8}\int_\R\big(e^{(\tau-\sigma)_+/8}-1\big)\mathsf e_\bmom\,\dif\sigma\Big]\\ &\le\mathsf M_e\Big(\gamma+\frac8{7(1-\bmom^2)}+8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\Big), \end{aligned} \]
where the part \(\sigma<0\) of the inner integral is at most \(8e^{\tau/8}/(7(1-\bmom^2))\) and the part \(0\le\sigma<\tau\) is at most \(8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom e^{\tau/8}\). Dividing by \(\gamma\ge1\) gives the decay bound.

Theorem SF.11 (Zero ridge, large aspect ratios)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) and \(\gin,\gout>0\), put \(\iota\defeq\gamma_\wedge/\gamma_\vee\in(0,1]\) and
\[ \mathsf F(\iota)\defeq\frac1\iota\int_0^\iota\sqrt{(1-x)(\iota-x)}\,\dif x . \]
  1. (i)
    The zero-ridge constant satisfies
    \[ \Big|C_\bmom(\gin,\gout)-\frac{\mathsf F(\iota)}{\sqrt\pi}\Big|\le\frac{2.32}{(1-\bmom)\sqrt{\gin\gout}}, \]
    where \(\mathsf F(1)=\frac12\) and, for \(\iota<1\),
    \[ \mathsf F(\iota)=\frac1\iota\Big[\frac{(1+\iota)\sqrt\iota}4+\frac{(1-\iota)^2}8\log\frac{1-\sqrt\iota}{1+\sqrt\iota}\Big]. \]
    In particular, in the square case,
    \[ \Big|C_\bmom(\gamma)-\frac1{2\sqrt\pi}\Big|\le\frac{2.32}{(1-\bmom)\gamma}\qquad(\gamma>0). \]
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\ge\gamma^\circ_\bmom\). The zero-ridge volatility of Definition SF.1 satisfies, with \(\mathsf K^\pm_\bmom\) of (SF.57),
    \[ \frac{1-2\mathsf K^-_\bmom}\gamma\le\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}}{N\gamma}-\frac12\le\frac{1+2\mathsf K^+_\bmom}\gamma . \]
    In particular \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=\frac{N^2}{2B}\big(1+O_\bmom(B/N)\big)\), and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}/(N\gamma)\to\frac12\) as \(\gamma\to\infty\) at every fixed \(\bmom\in(0,1)\).
The square-case limit \(1/(2\sqrt\pi)\) involves no logarithm and does not depend on \(\bmom\). The zero-ridge drift therefore does not decay with the aspect ratio, in contrast with \(C_0(\gamma)\sim1/(\sqrt\pi\gamma)\) (Theorem SF.5); only the rate depends on \(\bmom\). At zero ridge the same-time total is \(\min\{\Nin,\Nout\}\) (Proposition SF.3(i)). By part (ii) the integrated total is \(N^2/(2B)\) at leading order, half of \(N\) times the aspect ratio, for every \(\bmom\in(0,1)\); the leading constant does not depend on \(\bmom\), and the constants \(\mathsf K^\pm_\bmom\) of the error do. Without momentum the integrated total is \(\min\{B,N\}\) (Proposition SF.2).
Proof
Proof.
For (i), apply Theorem SF.3 at \(\ridge=0\), where \(\psi(0)=\gamma_\wedge\), and substitute \(m=\gamma_\vee x\):
\[ \frac1{\sqrt\pi\,\gin\gout}\int_0^{\gamma_\wedge}\sqrt{(\gamma_\vee-m)(\gamma_\wedge-m)}\,\dif m =\frac{\gamma_\vee^2}{\sqrt\pi\,\gamma_\vee^2\iota}\int_0^\iota\sqrt{(1-x)(\iota-x)}\,\dif x=\frac{\mathsf F(\iota)}{\sqrt\pi} . \]
The closed form follows by differentiating the antiderivative
\[ \frac{2x-1-\iota}4\sqrt{(1-x)(\iota-x)}-\frac{(1-\iota)^2}8\log\Big|2x-1-\iota+2\sqrt{(1-x)(\iota-x)}\Big| \qquad(0\le x<\iota<1) \]
and evaluating it at \(x=\iota\) and \(x=0\), using \((1-\sqrt\iota)^2/(1-\iota)=(1-\sqrt\iota)/(1+\sqrt\iota)\). At \(\iota=1\) the integral is \(\int_0^1(1-x)\,\dif x=\frac12\).
For (ii), Proposition SF.3(ii) gives \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}/N=1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\), that is
\[ \frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}}{N\gamma}-\frac12=\frac1\gamma\Big(1+2\Big(\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)-\frac\gamma4\Big)\Big), \]
and Theorem SF.9 bounds the inner bracket.

Lemma SF.18 (Ridge excess)
Let \(\gamma\ge\gamma^\circ_\bmom\) and \(\ridge>0\). Then
\[ \sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C^{\,\sharp}_{\ell}(\ridge)\le\frac{\mathsf K^\flat_{\rm ex}+\mathsf K^\sharp_{\rm ex}\,\psi(\ridge)}\gamma . \]
Proof
Proof.
The ridge factor exceeds one only within a bounded distance of the top level \(\log Q_{\psi(\ridge)}\) in logarithmic scale, and the lag lattice meets that window once per period of length \(2\log(1/\bmom)\); there are about \(\psi(\ridge)\) periods. Write \(\psi=\psi(\ridge)\), \(b\defeq\log(1/\bmom)\), \(U\defeq\log Q_\psi\), \(u\defeq\log y\), \(v\defeq\log z\) and \(v_\ell\defeq v+2b\ell=\log z_\ell\). Then \(D(y,z)\,\dif y\,\dif z=\mathcal Z(y,z)\,\dif u\,\dif v\).
The ridge factor. The closure at \(s=\ridge\) gives \(\ridge\,Q_\psi=(\gamma-\psi)^2\), and \(\mathsf A(y)\ge\gamma-\psi\) for \(y<Q_\psi\). Hence \(\ridge y/\mathsf A(y)^2\le y/Q_\psi=e^{-(U-u)}\), and
\[ \mathsf u_\ridge(e^u)\le1+\varpi(U-u),\qquad \varpi(\zeta)\defeq\big(1-e^{-\zeta}\big)^{-1/2}-1\le\frac1{e^\zeta-1}\qquad(\zeta>0). \]
The substitution \(\sqrt{1-e^{-\zeta}}=t\) gives \(\varpi(\zeta)\,\dif\zeta=2\,\dif t/(1+t)\), so that
\[ \int_0^\infty\varpi=2\log2,\qquad \int_0^\infty\zeta\,\varpi(\zeta)\,\dif\zeta\le\int_0^\infty\frac\zeta{e^\zeta-1}\,\dif\zeta=\frac{\pi^2}6 . \]
Moreover \(\varpi\) is decreasing, and \(\varpi(1)<0.6\).
The other factors. On the support of \(\overline C^{\,\sharp}_\ell\) one has \(z_1\le z_\ell<Q_\psi<Q_\gamma\), so \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\Phi(1)\) by Lemma SF.14(v). Also \(\mathsf j(y)\le\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\) and \(\mathsf j(z_\ell)\le\mathsf A(z_\ell)+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\le\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\). The two cases \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\) and \(\mathsf A_\vee<2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\) of Lemma SF.15(v) give
\[ \frac{\mathsf j(y)\,\mathsf j(z_\ell)}{\gamma^2\,\widehat\Delta(y,z)}\le\mathsf K_6 . \]
The same bound at \(z_\ell=e^{v_\ell}\) gives
\[ \mathsf u_\ridge(y)\,\mathsf u_\ridge(z_\ell)-1\le\varpi(U-u)+\big(1+\varpi(U-u)\big)\,\varpi(U-v_\ell), \]
and summing over \(\ell\) gives
\[ \sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C^{\,\sharp}_{\ell}(\ridge)\le\frac{\mathsf K_6}{\pi^2\gamma}\iint_{u<U}\mathcal Z\,\Big[\varpi(U-u)\,\mathsf n(v)+\big(1+\varpi(U-u)\big)\,\mathsf W_\ridge(v)\Big]\,\dif u\,\dif v, \]
\[ \mathsf n(v)\defeq\#\{\ell\ge1:\ v_\ell<U\}\le\frac{(U-v)_+}{2b},\qquad \mathsf W_\ridge(v)\defeq\sum_{\ell\ge1,\ v_\ell<U}\varpi(U-v_\ell). \]
The gaps \(U-v_\ell\) form an arithmetic progression of step \(2b\) starting at some \(\zeta_0(v)\in(0,2b]\), and \(\varpi\) is decreasing, so that
\[ \mathsf W_\ridge(v)\le\Big[\varpi\big(\zeta_0(v)\big)+\frac{\log2}b\Big]\mathbf 1\{v<U-2b\} . \]
The bracket is a \(2b\)-periodic function of \(v\) whose integral over one period is at most \(\int_0^\infty\varpi+2\log2=4\log2\).
The term with \(\mathsf n\). By Lemma SF.15(ii), \(\mathcal Z\le\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\,\mathsf h(\abs{u-v})\) with \(\mathsf h(w)\defeq w/(2\sinh(w/2))\le1\), and the computation in the proof of Lemma SF.16(ii) gives \(\int_\R\mathsf h=\pi^2\) and \(\int_\R\abs w\,\mathsf h(w)\,\dif w=28\,\zeta(3)\). \[ \frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{2b}\Big(\pi^2\int_0^\infty\zeta\varpi(\zeta)\,\dif\zeta+28\,\zeta(3)\int_0^\infty\varpi\Big)\le\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{2b}\Big(\frac{\pi^4}6+56\,\zeta(3)\log2\Big). \]
The term with \(\mathsf W_\ridge\), for \(v\ge V_0\defeq-2\log(1-\bmom)\). Put \(I(v)\defeq\int_{u<U}\mathcal Z\,(1+\varpi(U-u))\,\dif u\). The term with \(\mathsf W_\ridge\), for \(v<V_0\). Put \(w_1\defeq V_0-v>0\); we show \(I(v)\le\mathsf K_{\rm J}(1+w_1)e^{-w_1/2}\). On \(u<V_0\), Lemma SF.15(ii) and \(\sw2e^{V_0}=1/(1-\bmom^2)\) give
\[ \mathcal Z\le\sw2e^{(u+v)/2}=\frac{e^{-(V_0-u)/2}\,e^{-w_1/2}}{1-\bmom^2} . \]
Splitting \(1+\varpi\) into \(1\), \(\varpi\) on \(U-u\ge1\) (where \(\varpi<0.6\)) and \(\varpi\) on \(U-u<1\) (where \(e^{-(V_0-u)/2}\le1\) and \(\int\varpi\le2\log2\)), the part \(u<V_0\) of \(I(v)\) is at most \((2+1.2+2\log2)\,e^{-w_1/2}/(1-\bmom^2)\). The function \(f(w)\defeq(1+w)e^{-w/2}\) is unimodal on \((0,\infty)\), with \(\sup f=2e^{-1/2}<1.214\) and \(\int_0^\infty f=6\). On each, \(\mathsf W_\ridge\) has integral at most \(4\log2\), and \(I\le\mathsf K_{\rm J}\sup f\) over the corresponding interval of \(w_1\). For a unimodal \(f\ge0\) the sum of its suprema over consecutive intervals of length \(h\) is at most \(3\sup f+h^{-1}\int f\): an interval that neither contains the mode nor is adjacent to the interval containing it has its supremum at the end nearest the mode, which is at most \(h^{-1}\) times the integral of \(f\) over the next interval towards the mode, and the three remaining intervals contribute at most \(\sup f\) each. With \(h=2b\) this part is at most \(4\log2\;\mathsf K_{\rm J}\,(3.64+3/b)\).
Adding the three parts and multiplying by \(\mathsf K_6/(\pi^2\gamma)\) gives the lemma.

Theorem SF.12 (Integrated volatility at positive ridge)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\Nin=\Nout=N\), \(\gamma=N/B\ge\gamma^\circ_\bmom\) and \(\ridge>0\), and put \(\psi=\psi(\ridge)\in(0,\gamma)\). Then
\[ -\frac\psi\gamma\,\mathsf K^\circ_\bmom\le\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)-\frac\gamma2\,\mathcal P\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big)\le\frac{\mathsf K^\flat_\bmom+\mathsf K^\sharp_\bmom\psi}\gamma , \]
and the integrated volatility of Theorem SF.2(ii) satisfies
\[ -\frac{2\psi\,\mathsf K^\circ_\bmom}\gamma\le\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)}N-\frac\psi\gamma-\gamma\,\mathcal P\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big)\le\frac{2(\mathsf K^\flat_\bmom+\mathsf K^\sharp_\bmom\psi)}\gamma . \]
The function \(\mathcal P\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), with \(\mathcal P(x)=x^2-x^3/3+O(x^4)\) as \(x\downarrow0\) and \(\mathcal P(1)=\frac12\). It interpolates between the fixed-ridge regime, where \(\gamma\,\mathcal P(\psi/\gamma)\) is close to \(\psi^2/\gamma\) (Corollary SF.2(iii)), and the zero-ridge rate \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}/(N\gamma)\to\frac12\) of Theorem SF.11(ii) (Theorem SF.14).
Proof
Proof.
The main part \(\overline C^{\,\circ}_\ell\) is treated as at zero ridge, with the participation cut at \(\psi\), and the excess is Lemma SF.18.
Step 0: the target integral. For \(0\le X\le a\) put \(\mathsf f_a(X)\defeq X-X^2/(2a)\); then \(0\le\partial_X\mathsf f_a\le1\) and \(0\le\partial_a\mathsf f_a\le\frac12\). For \(0\le\eta\le\psi\) put \(X\defeq\psi-\eta\), \(x\defeq\gamma-\eta\) and \(\mathsf H(\eta)\defeq\mathsf f_x(X)\). With \(p\defeq\psi/\gamma\) and \(c\defeq1-p\), the substitution \(\eta=\gamma y'\) turns \(\gamma^{-1}\int_0^\psi\mathsf H\) into \(\gamma\int_0^p\big[(p-y')-(p-y')^2/(2(1-y'))\big]\dif y'\), and the substitution \(1-y'=t'\) gives
\[ \int_0^p\frac{(p-y')^2}{2(1-y')}\,\dif y'=\frac12\int_c^1\Big(t'-2c+\frac{c^2}{t'}\Big)\dif t'=\frac{1-c^2}4-c(1-c)-\frac{c^2}2\log c . \]
Hence
\[ \frac1\gamma\int_0^\psi\mathsf H(\eta)\,\dif\eta=\gamma\Big[\frac{p^2}2-\frac{1-c^2}4+c(1-c)+\frac{c^2}2\log c\Big]=\frac\gamma2\,\mathcal P(p) . \]
Step 1: the summed representation of the main part. As in Step 1 of the proof of Theorem SF.9,
\[ \sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C^{\,\circ}_{\ell}(\ridge)=\frac1\gamma\int_0^\psi\EE_\star\Psi_\ridge\,\dif\eta, \]
where
\[ \Psi_\ridge\defeq\mathbf 1\{y<Q_\psi\}\,\frac{\mathsf j(y)\,\mathsf T_\ridge(z)}{\gamma^2\widehat\Delta(y,z)},\qquad \mathsf T_\ridge(z)\defeq\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathbf 1\{z_\ell<Q_\psi\}\,\mathsf j(z_\ell). \]
The range is \(\eta<\psi\) because \(s\) lies between \(y\) and \(z\), both below \(Q_\psi\). On \(\{\Psi_\ridge>0\}\), \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\Phi(1)\) by Lemma SF.14(v), since \(z_1<Q_\psi<Q_\gamma\).
Step 2: the main part, upper bound. The terms of \(\mathsf T_\ridge(z)\) have \(\ell<L^\ridge_z\defeq\psi-\mathsf n_\bmom(z)+\mathsf c_\bmom=L_z-(\gamma-\psi)\), by Lemma SF.14(ii) and \(r(z_\ell)<\psi\), and each is at most \(L_z-\ell+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\). For an integer \(n<L^\ridge_z\le L_z\),
\[ \sum_{\ell=1}^n(L_z-\ell)\le nL_z-\frac{n^2}2\le L^\ridge_zL_z-\frac{(L^\ridge_z)^2}2 , \]
so that
\[ \mathsf T_\ridge(z)\le\mathsf b(L^\ridge_z)+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom L^\ridge_z,\qquad \mathsf b(\zeta)\defeq\zeta\Big(\frac\zeta2+\gamma-\psi\Big), \]
where \(\mathsf b\) is increasing on \([0,\infty)\). By Lemma SF.14(iii),(iv) and Lemma SF.16(iii), \(L^\ridge_z\le X+Y\) with \(Y\defeq\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau+\mathsf c^+_\bmom+\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)\). Since \(\gamma-\psi=x-X\),
\[ \mathsf b(X+Y)=x\,\mathsf H+Yx+\frac{Y^2}2 . \]
From \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\mathsf A(y)\ge x-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\) and \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge\Phi(1)\),
\[ \frac x{\mathsf A_\vee}\le1+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}{\Phi(1)},\qquad \frac{x\,\mathsf H}{\mathsf A_\vee}=\mathsf H+\mathsf H\,\frac{x-\mathsf A_\vee}{\mathsf A_\vee}\le\mathsf H+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\Big(1+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}{\Phi(1)}\Big), \]
using \(\mathsf H\le X\le x\). Hence
\[ \frac{\mathsf T_\ridge(z)}{\mathsf A_\vee}\le\mathsf H+\mathsf E_1,\qquad \mathsf E_1\defeq\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\Big(1+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}{\Phi(1)}\Big)+\Big(1+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}{\Phi(1)}\Big)Y+\frac{Y^2}{2\Phi(1)}+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\Big(1+\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau}{\Phi(1)}\Big)+\frac{2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom Y}{\Phi(1)} . \]
By Lemma SF.15(v) and \(\mathsf j(y)\le\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\),
\[ \Psi_\ridge\le\frac{\mathsf A_\vee+2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom}{\max(\mathsf A_\vee-\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom,\mathsf A_\vee/2)}\cdot\frac{\mathsf T_\ridge(z)}{\mathsf A_\vee} . \]
If \(\mathsf A_\vee\ge2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\), this is at most \((1+6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom/\mathsf A_\vee)(\mathsf H+\mathsf E_1)\le\mathsf H+6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(1+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau/\Phi(1))+4\mathsf E_1\). If \(\mathsf A_\vee<2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\), it is at most \((8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom/\Phi(1))(\mathsf H+\mathsf E_1)\); since \(8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom/\Phi(1)\ge1\) and \(\mathsf H\le x\le\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau<2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau\), it is at most \(\mathsf H+(8\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom/\Phi(1))(2\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom\tau+\mathsf E_1)\). Put \(\mathsf R\defeq1+\tau^2+\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)^2\), which dominates \(1\), \(\tau\), \(\mathsf n^-_\bmom(z)\), their squares and their product. Then
\[ Y\le\big(\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+\mathsf c^+_\bmom+1\big)\mathsf R,\qquad Y^2\le3\big(\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom^2+(\mathsf c^+_\bmom)^2+1\big)\mathsf R,\qquad \tau Y\le\big(\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom+\mathsf c^+_\bmom+1\big)\mathsf R, \]
hence \(\mathsf E_1\le\mathsf K_7\mathsf R\), and in both cases
\[ \Psi_\ridge\le\mathsf H+\mathsf K_8\,\mathsf R . \]
Integrating over \(\eta\in(0,\psi)\), with \(\int_0^\psi\EE_\star\mathsf R\,\dif\eta\le\psi(1+\mathsf M_2)+\bar{\mathsf L}_2\) by Lemma SF.16(ii) and Lemma SF.17, gives
\[ \sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C^{\,\circ}_{\ell}(\ridge)\le\frac\gamma2\,\mathcal P\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big)+\frac{\mathsf K_8\big(\psi(1+\mathsf M_2)+\bar{\mathsf L}_2\big)}\gamma . \]
Step 3: the main part, lower bound. Put \(X_z\defeq\psi-r(z)\le\mathsf A(z)\). Every \(1\le\ell<X_z\) has \(r(z_\ell)\le r(z)+\ell<\psi\) by Lemma SF.14(i), and \(\mathsf j(z_\ell)\ge\mathsf A(z)-\ell\). With \(n\defeq\lceil X_z\rceil-1\ge X_z-1\) and \(\mathsf A=\mathsf A(z)\),
\[ \mathsf T_\ridge(z)\ge\sum_{\ell=1}^n(\mathsf A-\ell)=\int_0^n(\mathsf A-t)\,\dif t-\frac n2\ge\mathsf A\,\mathsf f_{\mathsf A}(X_z)-\frac32\,\mathsf A , \]
where the last step uses \(n\ge X_z-1\), \(\partial_X\mathsf f_{\mathsf A}\le1\) and \(n\le\mathsf A\); the final bound also holds when \(0<X_z<1\), where \(\mathsf A\,\mathsf f_{\mathsf A}(X_z)<\mathsf A\). Using \(\mathsf A(y)\mathsf A(z)\le\mathsf A_\vee^2\) and Lemma SF.15(v),
\[ \Psi_\ridge\ge\mathbf 1\{y<Q_\psi\}\Big[\frac{\mathsf A(y)\,\mathsf A(z)}{(\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)^2}\,\mathsf f_{\mathsf A(z)}(X_z)-\frac32\Big]. \]
Put \(\upsilon\defeq\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\tau+1)\). If \(X\le\upsilon\), then \(\mathsf H\le X\le\upsilon\) and \(\mathsf H-6\upsilon-\frac32<0\le\Psi_\ridge\). Otherwise \(X_z\ge X-\upsilon>0\) and \(\psi-r(y)\ge X-\upsilon>0\), so that \(y<Q_\psi\), and \(\mathsf A(z)\ge x-\upsilon\). Since \(\mathsf f_a(X)\) is nondecreasing in \(a\) and in \(X\), moving first \(X\) to \(X-\upsilon\) at \(a=x\) and then \(a\) to \(x-\upsilon\) at \(X-\upsilon\) gives
\[ \mathsf f_{\mathsf A(z)}(X_z)\ge\mathsf f_{x-\upsilon}(X-\upsilon)\ge\mathsf H-\upsilon-\frac\upsilon2\ge\mathsf H-2\upsilon . \]
The ratio \(\mathsf A(y)\mathsf A(z)/(\mathsf A_\vee+\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom)^2\) lies between \(((x-\upsilon)/(x+\upsilon))^2\ge1-4\upsilon/x\) and \(1\). If \(\mathsf H\ge2\upsilon\) and \(4\upsilon\le x\), the product is at least \((1-4\upsilon/x)(\mathsf H-2\upsilon)\ge\mathsf H-6\upsilon\), because \(\mathsf H\le x\). If \(\mathsf H<2\upsilon\) or \(4\upsilon>x\ge\mathsf H\), then \(\mathsf H-6\upsilon<0\). In all cases
\[ \Psi_\ridge\ge\mathsf H-6\upsilon-\frac32 , \]
and integration over \((0,\psi)\) gives \(\sum_\ell\overline C^{\,\circ}_\ell(\ridge)\ge\frac\gamma2\mathcal P(\psi/\gamma)-\frac\psi\gamma\big(6\bar{\mathsf e}_\bmom(\mathsf M_1+1)+\frac32\big)\). Finally \(\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)\ge\overline C^{\,\circ}_\ell(\ridge)\).
Step 4: the excess and the total. Lemma SF.18 bounds \(\sum_\ell\overline C^{\,\sharp}_\ell(\ridge)\), and together with Step 2 it gives the upper bound. For the volatility, (SG.2) at \(\mu_1=\dots=\mu_N=1\), Theorem SF.2(ii) and Proposition SF.3(ii) give
\[ \frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)}N=\overline C_{1,0}(\ridge)+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge),\qquad \overline C_{1,0}(\ridge)=\frac\psi\gamma , \]
and the second display of the theorem follows from the first.

Large aspect ratios at a fixed ridge. At a fixed positive ridge only about
\begin{equation}\tag{SF.64} \widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)\defeq\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)^2\gin\gout/\ridge\big)}{2\log(1/\bmom)} =\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge)+\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)\sqrt{\gin\gout}\big)}{\log(1/\bmom)} \end{equation}
participation levels are available, and the drift is this count divided by \(\sqrt{\pi\gin\gout}\). In the notation of Lemma SF.6, \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)=\mathsf n_\bmom(\gin\gout/\ridge)\).
Theorem SF.13 (Fixed ridge, large aspect ratios)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\ridge>0\) and \(\gin,\gout>0\), write \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}=\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)\), and assume
\[ \widehat{\mathfrak p}\ge\frac{2\mathsf c'_\bmom}{\log(1/\bmom)\,\gamma_\wedge},\qquad \widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom\le\frac{\gamma_\wedge}2 . \]
Put \(e_\bmom\defeq\max\big(\mathsf c'_\bmom,\ \mathsf c_\bmom+2(\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom)/(\log(1/\bmom)\gamma_\wedge)\big)\). Then
\[ \big|\psi(\ridge)-\widehat{\mathfrak p}\big|\le e_\bmom,\qquad \Big|\sqrt{\gin\gout}\;\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)-\frac{\widehat{\mathfrak p}}{\sqrt\pi}\Big| \le\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\Big(e_\bmom+\frac{(\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom)^2}{\gamma_\wedge}\Big)+\frac{2.32}{1-\bmom}. \]
If moreover \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\), then
\[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\ge B\,\psi(\ridge)+\mathsf k_\bmom B\Big(1-\frac{\psi(\ridge)}\gamma\Big)^2\big(\psi(\ridge)-\mathsf c''_\bmom-\mathsf c'_\bmom-1\big)_+^2 . \]
Proof
Proof.
Let \(H\defeq\psi(\ridge)\), so that \(Q_H=q(-\ridge)\) and the closure reads \(\ridge\,Q_H=\mathsf W(H)^2\). For \(0\le m\le\gamma_\wedge\),
\[ \sqrt{\gin\gout}\Big(1-\frac m{\gamma_\wedge}\Big)\le\mathsf W(m)\le\sqrt{\gin\gout} . \]
Suppose first \((1-\bmom)^2Q_H\ge1\). By Lemma SF.6(i), \(H\in(\mathsf n_\bmom(Q_H)-\mathsf c_\bmom,\ \mathsf n_\bmom(Q_H)+\mathsf c'_\bmom]\), where
\[ \mathsf n_\bmom(Q_H)=\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)^2\mathsf W(H)^2/\ridge\big)}{2\log(1/\bmom)} =\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\frac{\log\big(\mathsf W(H)/\sqrt{\gin\gout}\big)}{\log(1/\bmom)}\le\widehat{\mathfrak p} . \]
The upper bound gives \(H\le\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom\le\gamma_\wedge/2\). Then \(\log(1-H/\gamma_\wedge)\ge-2H/\gamma_\wedge\), so
\[ H>\widehat{\mathfrak p}-\mathsf c_\bmom-\frac{2(\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom)}{\log(1/\bmom)\,\gamma_\wedge} . \]
Suppose instead \((1-\bmom)^2Q_H<1\). Then \(H\le r_\bmom((1-\bmom)^{-2})\le\mathsf c'_\bmom\), and
\[ \ridge=\frac{\mathsf W(H)^2}{Q_H}>(1-\bmom)^2\,\mathsf W(H)^2\ge(1-\bmom)^2\gin\gout\Big(1-\frac{\mathsf c'_\bmom}{\gamma_\wedge}\Big)^2 , \]
so \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}<-\log(1-\mathsf c'_\bmom/\gamma_\wedge)/\log(1/\bmom)\le2\mathsf c'_\bmom/(\log(1/\bmom)\gamma_\wedge)\), which the hypothesis excludes. This proves the bound on \(\psi(\ridge)\).
For the drift, the bounds on \(\mathsf W\) give
\[ \frac{\sqrt{\gin\gout}}{\gin\gout}\int_0^H\mathsf W(m)\,\dif m\in\Big[H-\frac{H^2}{\gamma_\wedge},\ H\Big], \]
whose distance to \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}\) is at most \(e_\bmom+(\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom)^2/\gamma_\wedge\). Theorem SF.3, multiplied by \(\sqrt{\gin\gout}\), gives the drift bound.
For the volatility, Proposition SF.3(ii) gives \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=N\big(\overline C_{1,0}(\ridge)+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)\big)\) with \(N\overline C_{1,0}(\ridge)=NH/\gamma=BH\). By Lemma SF.12 with \(\varepsilon=\ridge\) and \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}(X-\ell)_+\ge\int_1^\infty(X-x)_+\dif x=(X-1)_+^2/2\), where \(X\defeq H-\mathsf c''_\bmom-\mathsf c'_\bmom\),
\[ 2N\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(\ridge)\ge\frac{N\mathsf k_\bmom}{\gamma^3}(\gamma-H)^2(X-1)_+^2=\mathsf k_\bmom B\Big(1-\frac H\gamma\Big)^2(X-1)_+^2 . \]

Corollary SF.2 (Fixed-ridge laws)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\).
  1. (i)
    Along \(\gamma_\wedge\to\infty\) and \(\ridge>0\) with \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)\to\infty\) and \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)/\gamma_\wedge\to0\),
    \[ \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\sim\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)^2\gin\gout/\ridge\big)}{2\sqrt\pi\,\log(1/\bmom)\,\sqrt{\gin\gout}}, \]
    and \(\psi(\ridge)-\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)\) stays bounded.
  2. (ii)
    In the square case, for fixed \(\ridge>0\), the quantity
    \[ \gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)-\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\Big[\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge)+\frac{\log((1-\bmom)\gamma)}{\log(1/\bmom)}\Big] \]
    stays bounded as \(\gamma\to\infty\). In particular \(\gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\to\infty\).
  3. (iii)
    In the square case, along \(\gamma\to\infty\) and \(\ridge>0\) with \(\psi(\ridge)\to\infty\) and \(\psi(\ridge)/\gamma\to0\), and in particular at a fixed \(\ridge>0\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\,\psi(\ridge)^2\Big(1+O_\bmom\Big(\frac1{\psi(\ridge)}+\frac{\psi(\ridge)}\gamma\Big)\Big). \]
Proof
Proof.
In parts (i) and (ii) the hypotheses of Theorem SF.13 hold eventually, because \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)\to\infty\) and \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)=o(\gamma_\wedge)\); in part (ii) \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)\) is the bracket, which is of order \(\log\gamma\). Then \(e_\bmom\) stays bounded and \((\widehat{\mathfrak p}+\mathsf c'_\bmom)^2/\gamma_\wedge=o(\widehat{\mathfrak p})\), and the theorem gives both statements.
For part (iii), at a fixed ridge the bound \(\abs{\psi(\ridge)-\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)}\le e_\bmom\) of Theorem SF.13, with \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)\) of order \(\log\gamma\), gives \(\psi(\ridge)\to\infty\) and \(\psi(\ridge)/\gamma\to0\). The function \((\mathcal P(x)-x^2)/x^3\) is continuous on \((0,1]\) and tends to \(-\frac13\) as \(x\downarrow0\), by (SF.63) and \(\log(1-x)=-x-x^2/2-x^3/3+O(x^4)\). Hence \(\abs{\mathcal P(x)-x^2}\le C x^3\) on \([0,1]\) for an absolute constant \(C\), and
\[ N\gamma\,\mathcal P\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big)=B\gamma^2\mathcal P\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big)=B\psi^2\Big(1+O\Big(\frac\psi\gamma\Big)\Big) \qquad(\psi=\psi(\ridge)). \]
For \(\gamma\ge\gamma^\circ_\bmom\) the second display of Theorem SF.12, multiplied by \(N\) and with \(N/\gamma=B\), gives \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)=B\psi+B\gamma^2\mathcal P(\psi/\gamma)+O_\bmom\big(B(1+\psi)\big)\), which is the claim.
□
At a fixed ridge the drift therefore decays like \(\log\gamma/\gamma\), not like \(1/\gamma\), with leading term \(\log\gamma/(\sqrt\pi\log(1/\bmom)\gamma)\). The same-time resolved rank is \(B\psi(\ridge)=B\,\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)+O(B)\). It differs from \(B\,\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge)\) by \(B\log((1-\bmom)\sqrt{\gin\gout})/\log(1/\bmom)\), which is not negligible at a fixed ridge. The integrated total is \(B\psi(\ridge)^2\) at leading order, with an error of order \(B(1+\psi(\ridge))\) (Corollary SF.2(iii), Theorem SF.12); the explicit lower bound of Theorem SF.13 holds at every \(\gamma\).
The crossover. When \(\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge)\) is of the order of \(\gamma\), the resolved fraction \(\psi/\gamma\) converges, and (SF.32) turns this into a limit for the drift itself.
Theorem SF.14 (Crossover)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) be fixed, let \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\to\infty\), and let \(\ridge_\gamma\in(0,1)\) satisfy \(\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge_\gamma)/\gamma\to c\in(0,\infty)\). Then \(\psi(\ridge_\gamma)/\gamma\to c\wedge1\), the same-time total satisfies \(B\psi(\ridge_\gamma)/N\to c\wedge1\),
\[ \mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge_\gamma)\longrightarrow\Xi(c)\defeq\frac{(c\wedge1)\big(1-\tfrac12(c\wedge1)\big)}{\sqrt\pi}, \]
and, with \(\mathcal P\) of (SF.63),
\[ \frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge_\gamma)}{N\gamma}\longrightarrow\mathcal P(c\wedge1). \]
The function \(\Xi\) interpolates between the two other regimes. As \(c\to0\), \(\Xi(c)\sim c/\sqrt\pi\), the fixed-ridge law of Corollary SF.2 with \(\psi\approx c\gamma\); for \(c\ge1\), \(\Xi(c)=1/(2\sqrt\pi)\), the zero-ridge limit of Theorem SF.11. It reaches this value at \(c=1\), that is at \(\ridge\approx\bmom^{2\gamma}\), and it is continuously differentiable there. In particular \(\gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge_\gamma)\to\infty\). The order-one object in the window is \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) itself. The volatility limit \(\mathcal P(c\wedge1)\) interpolates in the same way: \(\mathcal P(c)\sim c^2\) as \(c\to0\), which is the fixed-ridge law \(B\psi^2\) of Corollary SF.2(iii) with \(\psi\approx c\gamma\), and \(\mathcal P(c\wedge1)=\frac12\) for \(c\ge1\), which is the zero-ridge law of Theorem SF.11(ii).
Proof
Proof.
Write \(H\defeq\psi(\ridge_\gamma)\) and \(\mathfrak p\defeq\mathfrak p_\bmom(\ridge_\gamma)\to\infty\). The case \((1-\bmom)^2Q_H<1\) is excluded for large \(\gamma\): as in the proof of Theorem SF.13, it forces \(H\le\mathsf c'_\bmom\) and \(\ridge_\gamma>(1-\bmom)^2(\gamma-\mathsf c'_\bmom)^2\), hence \(\mathfrak p<-\log((1-\bmom)(\gamma-\mathsf c'_\bmom))/\log(1/\bmom)<0\) eventually. Since \(Q_H=(\gamma-H)^2/\ridge_\gamma\), Lemma SF.6(i) gives
\[ \mathfrak p+\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)(\gamma-H)\big)}{\log(1/\bmom)}-\mathsf c_\bmom<H\le\mathfrak p+\frac{\log\big((1-\bmom)\gamma\big)}{\log(1/\bmom)}+\mathsf c'_\bmom . \]
If \(c<1\), the upper bound gives \(H\le c\gamma+o(\gamma)\), so eventually \(H\le(1-\delta)\gamma\) with \(\delta\defeq(1-c)/2\), and then the lower bound gives \(H>\mathfrak p+\log((1-\bmom)\delta\gamma)/\log(1/\bmom)-\mathsf c_\bmom=c\gamma+o(\gamma)\). If \(c\ge1\) and \(H\le(1-\delta)\gamma\) along a subsequence for some \(\delta\in(0,1)\), the lower bound gives \(H>(1-\delta/2)\gamma\) eventually along it, a contradiction; since \(H<\gamma\), \(H/\gamma\to1\). The drift limit follows from (SF.32) and the continuity of \(\Xi_0\), and the same-time total is \(B\psi/N=\psi/\gamma\).
For the volatility, Theorem SF.12 applies for large \(\gamma\), since \(\gamma\ge\gamma^\circ_\bmom\) eventually. Dividing its second display by \(\gamma\) and using \(H<\gamma\),
\[ \Big|\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge_\gamma)}{N\gamma}-\mathcal P\Big(\frac H\gamma\Big)\Big| \le\frac H{\gamma^2}+\frac{2\big(\mathsf K^\flat_\bmom+(\mathsf K^\sharp_\bmom+\mathsf K^\circ_\bmom)H\big)}{\gamma^2}\longrightarrow0 , \]
and \(\mathcal P(H/\gamma)\to\mathcal P(c\wedge1)\) because \(\mathcal P\) is continuous on \([0,1]\).

The order of the limits. The limits \(\bmom\downarrow0\), \(\ridge\downarrow0\) and \(\gamma\to\infty\) do not commute. At a fixed positive ridge the limit \(\bmom\downarrow0\) is continuous but does not commute with \(\gamma\to\infty\); at zero ridge it is discontinuous as soon as \(\gamma>1\).
Proposition SF.11 (Order of limits at positive ridge)
  1. (i)
    For fixed \(\gin,\gout>0\) and \(\ridge>0\), \(\lim_{\bmom\downarrow0}\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge;\bmom,\gin,\gout)=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge;0,\gin,\gout)\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(\gamma\ge2\) and \(\ridge\ge0\),
    \[ \Big|\gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge;0,\gamma,\gamma)-\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\Big|\le\frac{\sqrt2\,\big(1+\sqrt{2\pi}/2+\sqrt\ridge\big)}{\pi\gamma} . \]
  3. (iii)
    For every \(\ridge>0\), \(\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\lim_{\bmom\downarrow0}\gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge;\bmom,\gamma,\gamma)=\pi^{-1/2}\), whereas for every fixed \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge;\bmom,\gamma,\gamma)\to\infty\) as \(\gamma\to\infty\).
Part (iii) has no version uniform in \(\bmom\). The error bound of (SF.32), multiplied by \(\gamma\), stays bounded as \(\bmom\downarrow0\), so the non-uniformity lies in the main term. By Theorem SF.13, at fixed \(\bmom>0\) the product \(\gamma\,\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{iso}}\) grows like \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)/\sqrt\pi\), and \(\widehat{\mathfrak p}_\bmom(\ridge)/\log\gamma\to1/\log(1/\bmom)\), a rate that vanishes as \(\bmom\downarrow0\). Part (iii) is stated for \(\ridge>0\); at \(\ridge=0\) the inner limit is given by Proposition SF.12 below and is not \(C_0\).
Proof
Proof.

\[ \int_0^\infty\mathsf G_0(\ridge+u^2)\,\dif u\ge\frac{(\gamma-1)\sqrt{2\pi}}2-\sqrt\ridge . \]

Proposition SF.12 (Zero ridge as the momentum vanishes)
In the square case write \(\gamma=n+y\) with an integer \(n\ge0\) and \(y\in[0,1)\), and put \(y^2C_0(y)\defeq0\) at \(y=0\). Then
\[ \lim_{\bmom\downarrow0}C_\bmom(\gamma)=C^\sharp(\gamma)\defeq\frac{n\,C_0(1)+y^2C_0(y)}{\gamma^2}+\frac{n(n-1)/2+ny}{\sqrt\pi\,\gamma^2}. \]
In particular \(C^\sharp(\gamma)=C_0(\gamma)\) for \(\gamma\le1\), while for \(\gamma\ge1\)
\[ \gamma^2\big(C^\sharp(\gamma)-C_0(\gamma)\big)=(n-1)\,C_0(1)+y^2C_0(y)+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)/2+(n-1)y}{\sqrt\pi}, \]
which is strictly positive for every \(\gamma>1\). Finally \(C^\sharp(\gamma)\to1/(2\sqrt\pi)\) as \(\gamma\to\infty\).
The zero-ridge drift constant is therefore discontinuous at \(\bmom=0\) for every \(\gamma>1\), and the limits \(\bmom\downarrow0\) and \(\ridge\downarrow0\) do not commute there; for example \(C^\sharp(2)=0.37000\ldots\) while \(C_0(2)=0.25552\ldots\). For \(\bmom>0\) every row eventually participates at zero ridge (\(\psi\to\gamma\), Proposition SF.3(i)), whereas at \(\bmom=0\) at most one unit of participation is available (\(\Phi<1\)). As \(\bmom\downarrow0\) the extra participation is supplied by the ages \(j\ge1\) at ever larger roots.
Proof
Proof.
The proof is dominated convergence in the participation representation. By Lemma SF.8 at \(\ridge=0\), in the square case where \(-\mathsf W'=1\) and \(\mathsf W(\gamma)=0\),
\[ C_\bmom(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma^2}\int_0^\gamma\mathsf i_\bmom(m)\,\dif m\qquad(\bmom\in(0,1)). \]
Domination. For \(\bmom\le\frac12\), Lemma SF.7 gives \(\mathsf g_\bmom<4\) and \(\Lambda_\bmom(Q_m)\le\sqrt{2\pi}\,m+\mathsf g'_\bmom<\sqrt{2\pi}\,\gamma+4\pi\), so \(0\le\mathsf i_\bmom\le4+\frac12(\sqrt{2\pi}\gamma+4\pi)\) on \((0,\gamma)\).
Pointwise limit. Fix a non-integer \(m\in(k,k+1)\) with \(m<\gamma\), and put \(x_j\defeq\emaw j^2Q_m\), so that \(\sum_j\Phi(x_j)=m\) and \(x_{j+1}=\bmom^2x_j\). Take a subsequence \(\bmom\downarrow0\) along which \(x_k\to L\in[0,\infty]\). If \(L=\infty\), then \(x_j\ge x_k\to\infty\) for all \(j\le k\), so \(\liminf\sum_j\Phi(x_j)\ge k+1>m\), which is impossible. If \(L=0\), then
\[ \sum_{j\ge k}\Phi(x_j)\le\sum_{j\ge k}x_j\le\frac{x_k}{1-\bmom^2}\longrightarrow0, \]
so \(\limsup\sum_j\Phi(x_j)\le k<m\), which is also impossible. Hence \(L\in(0,\infty)\). Then \(x_j=\bmom^{-2(k-j)}x_k\to\infty\) for \(j<k\), and \(\sum_{j>k}x_j\le x_k\bmom^2/(1-\bmom^2)\to0\), so \(\Phi(L)=m-k\). Since \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing, \(L=\Phi^{-1}(m-k)\), independently of the subsequence. By Lemma SF.4(iv),
\[ \mathsf i_\bmom(m)=\sum_j\Big[\sqrt{x_j}\,\Jfun(x_j)+\tfrac12\Lambda_0(x_j)\Big]. \]
For \(j<k\), \(\sqrt{x_j}\Jfun(x_j)\le x_j^{-1/2}\to0\) and \(0\le\sqrt{2\pi}-\Lambda_0(x_j)\le2x_j^{-1/2}\to0\). For \(j>k\), \(\sqrt x\,\Jfun(x)+\frac12\Lambda_0(x)\le2\sqrt x\), so
\[ \sum_{j>k}\Big[\sqrt{x_j}\,\Jfun(x_j)+\tfrac12\Lambda_0(x_j)\Big]\le2\sum_{j>k}\sqrt{x_j}\le\frac{2\sqrt{x_k}\,\bmom}{1-\bmom}\longrightarrow0 . \]
The term \(j=k\) converges to \(\mathsf i_0(m-k)=\sqrt L\,\Jfun(L)+\frac12\Lambda_0(L)\), the integrand at \(\bmom=0\) and level \(m-k\). Hence \(\mathsf i_\bmom(m)\to\frac k2\sqrt{2\pi}+\mathsf i_0(m-k)\) for almost every \(m\in(0,\gamma)\).

\[ \lim_{\bmom\downarrow0}C_\bmom(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi\gamma^2}\Big[\sum_{k<n}\Big(\frac k2\sqrt{2\pi}+\int_0^1\mathsf i_0\Big)+\frac n2\sqrt{2\pi}\,y+\int_0^y\mathsf i_0\Big]. \]
For \(0<y<1\), Lemma SF.8 at \(\bmom=0\) and \(\gin=\gout=y\) gives \(\int_0^y\mathsf i_0=\pi y^2C_0(y)/\sqrt2\), and the proof of Proposition SF.9 gives \(\int_0^1\mathsf i_0=\pi C_0(1)/\sqrt2\). Substituting these and \(\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt{2\pi}/(2\pi)=\pi^{-1/2}\) gives the formula for \(C^\sharp\). For \(\gamma\le1\) one has \(n=0\) and \(y=\gamma\), or \(n=1\) and \(y=0\), and \(C^\sharp=C_0\). For \(\gamma\ge1\), subtracting \(\gamma^2C_0(\gamma)=C_0(1)+(n+y-1)/\sqrt\pi\) (Proposition SF.9) and using \(n(n-1)/2-n+1=(n-1)(n-2)/2\) gives the displayed difference. SF.12 Remarks

Remark SF.1 (Momentum as a change of batch and of step)
In the oversampled square case \(N<B\), with \(\gamma=N/B\) small, the small-aspect theorems describe momentum at the level of the scalar equation (SF.19), read with its formal zero-ridge coefficients. For every step \(\macrostep\), with \(\macrostep'\defeq\macrostep/\sqrt{\sw2}=\macrostep\sqrt{(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)}\),
\[ -2\macrostep\,C_\bmom\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\macrostep^2}{2B}\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom} =-2\macrostep'\big(\sqrt{\sw2}\,C_\bmom\big)\sqrt{\fRisk}+\frac{\macrostep'^2}{2B}\big(\sw2\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\big). \]
On the drift, \(\sqrt{\sw2}\,C_\bmom(\gamma)=C_0(\gamma)(1+O(\gamma))\) by Theorem SF.7(iii): momentum acts as the effective batch \(B/\sw2=B(1+\bmom)/(1-\bmom)\). On the volatility the factor is \(V_\bmom\), not \(1/\sw2\): \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}=N\) and \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=NV_\bmom(1+O(\gamma))\) (Theorem SF.8), so that \(\sw2\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=\sw2V_\bmom\,\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},0}(1+O(\gamma))\) with \(\sw2V_\bmom\in(\sw2,1)\) (Lemma SF.10). Momentum at step \(\macrostep\) is therefore no momentum at step \(\macrostep'\) with the volatility multiplied by \(\sw2V_\bmom<1\). The step-rescaling reading is only approximate in the oversampled isotropic case: momentum carries less noise than the rescaled step, by the factor \(\sw2V_\bmom\), which is \(0.826\ldots\) at \(\bmom=\frac12\) and tends to \(0.7776\ldots\) as \(\bmom\uparrow1\). The main text writes \(B_{\rm eff}\defeq B/\sw2\) for the effective batch and \(c_\bmom\defeq\sw2V_\bmom\) for this factor, so that \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}=c_\bmom NB_{\rm eff}/B\,\big(1+O(\gamma)\big)\). The factor \(1/\sw2\) for the volatility of Corollary SG.1(ii) belongs to a different regime, the resolved rows of a power-law spectrum with \(\gamma\to\infty\); that regime and the oversampled isotropic one are disjoint, and neither statement specializes to the other.
