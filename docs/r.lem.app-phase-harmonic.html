Lemma SI.8 (Harmonic sums)
For \(a>0\) and \(N\ge1\) let \(H_N(a)\defeq\sum_{i=1}^Ni^{-a}\). Then
\[ \int_1^{N+1}x^{-a}\,\dif x\le H_N(a)\le1+\int_1^Nx^{-a}\,\dif x , \]
and \(H_N(a)\ge N^{1-a}\) for \(a\le1\). Consequently, as \(N\to\infty\), \(H_N(a)\sim N^{1-a}/(1-a)\) for \(0<a<1\), \(H_N(1)\sim\log N\) with \(H_N(1)\ge\log(N+1)\), and \(0\le\zeta(a)-H_N(a)\le N^{1-a}/(a-1)\) for \(a>1\), where \(\zeta(a)=\sum_{i\ge1}i^{-a}\). Moreover \(\zeta(2)<2\).
Proof.
The function \(x\mapsto x^{-a}\) is decreasing, so \(i^{-a}\) lies between its integrals over \([i,i+1]\) and \([i-1,i]\); summing gives the two bounds, and evaluating the integrals gives the asymptotics. For \(a\le1\) every term is at least \(N^{-a}\). The tail bound is Lemma SI.6. Finally \(\zeta(2)<1+\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\dif x=2\).
□
