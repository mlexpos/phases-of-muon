Theorem SI.3 (Hitting times)
Assume (M1) and \(\mathcal S>0\), fix \(\epsilon>0\), and take \(\lr=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S\), so that \(\bar k=\sqrt\epsilon\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(a>0\), \(H_Q(a)\ge H_F(a)\) and
    \[ t_a\ge\frac1\lr\int_0^{H_F(a)}\sqrt{F(s)}\,\dif s . \]
    If \(H_Q(a)<\infty\), then \(t_a=t(H_Q(a))\) with \(t(\cdot)\) of Theorem SI.2.
  2. (ii)
    Fix \(c_0>1\) with \(F_0>c_0\epsilon\), let \(s_c\) be the time with \(F(s_c)=c_0\epsilon\), and put
    \[ C_\ast\defeq\max_{0\le s\le s_c}\frac{(k*\sqrt F)(s)}{\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}},\qquad m_\ast\defeq\frac{C_\ast}{1-1/(2\sqrt{c_0})} . \]
    Then \(0\le C_\ast\le\sqrt{F_0/(c_0\epsilon)}\) and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.9} Q(s)\le F(s)+m_\ast\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}\qquad(0\le s\le s_c). \end{equation}
    Consequently, for every \(T\ge c_0+m_\ast\sqrt{c_0}\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.10} \frac1\lr\int_0^{H_F(T\epsilon)}\sqrt{F(s)}\,\dif s\ \le\ t_{T\epsilon}\ \le\ \frac1\lr\int_0^{s_c}\sqrt{F(s)+m_\ast\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}}\,\dif s . \end{equation}
Proof.
For (i), the convolution in (SI.4) is nonnegative, so \(Q\ge F\). Hence \(\{Q\le a\}\subseteq\{F\le a\}\), and taking infima gives \(H_Q(a)\ge H_F(a)\). If the first set is nonempty, it is closed by continuity and contains its infimum. The clock is a strictly increasing bijection, so the first time at which \(\fRisk\) reaches \(a\) is \(t(H_Q(a))\). Using \(\sqrt Q\ge\sqrt F\) in the integral defining \(t(\cdot)\) and \(H_Q(a)\ge H_F(a)\) gives the lower bound. If the first set is empty, then \(t_a=\infty\) and the bound holds.
For (ii), the time \(s_c\) exists and is unique because \(F\) is continuous and strictly decreasing to zero. The ratio defining \(C_\ast\) is continuous on \([0,s_c]\), since \(F\ge c_0\epsilon>0\) there, and attains its maximum. Because \(\sqrt F\le\sqrt{F_0}\) and \(\int k=\sqrt\epsilon\),
\[ (k*\sqrt F)(s)\le\sqrt{F_0\epsilon},\qquad \frac{(k*\sqrt F)(s)}{\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}}\le\sqrt{\frac{F_0}{F(s)}}\le\sqrt{\frac{F_0}{c_0\epsilon}} . \]
The function \(\mathsf B\defeq F+m_\ast\sqrt{\epsilon F}\) is a supersolution on \([0,s_c]\). The inequality \(\sqrt{1+x}\le1+x/2\) for \(x\ge0\) gives
\[ \sqrt{\mathsf B(v)}\le\sqrt{F(v)}+\frac{m_\ast}2\sqrt\epsilon . \]
Hence, for \(0\le s\le s_c\), using \(F(s)\ge c_0\epsilon\) in the last step,
\[ F(s)+(k*\sqrt{\mathsf B})(s)\le F(s)+(k*\sqrt F)(s)+\frac{m_\ast\epsilon}2 \le F(s)+\sqrt{\epsilon F(s)}\Big(C_\ast+\frac{m_\ast}{2\sqrt{c_0}}\Big)=\mathsf B(s). \]
Both \(Q\) and \(\mathsf B\) are at least \(F\ge c_0\epsilon\) on \([0,s_c]\), so Lemma SI.3 with \(m=c_0\epsilon\) gives (SI.9). At \(s_c\) it gives
\[ Q(s_c)\le\epsilon\,(c_0+m_\ast\sqrt{c_0})\le T\epsilon , \]
so \(H_Q(T\epsilon)\le s_c\). Part (i) and the clock integral, bounded with (SI.9), give (SI.10).
□
