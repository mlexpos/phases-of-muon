Lemma SG.6 (Finite participation)
  1. (i)
    \(0\le S_\alpha(V)-S_N(V)\le1/N\) and \(\abs{VS_N'(V)-\mathsf T(V)}\le1/(2N)\) for \(V>0\).
  2. (ii)
    There is \(C\) such that, for \(M\ge1\), \(B\ge2\) and \(M/\gamma\le p\le1-M/\gamma\),
    \[ \Big|\log\frac{V_N(p)}{V_\alpha(p)}\Big|\le\frac{C}{BM}. \]
  3. (iii)
    \(V_N(p)\asymp(1-p)^{-1}\) for \(p\ge1/2\), uniformly in \(N\).
  4. (iv)
    If \(\alpha>1\), \(Bm\to\infty\) and \(m/\gamma\to0\), then \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\sim(Bm/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) and \(m\Lambda_N'(m)/\Lambda_N(m)\to\alpha\), locally uniformly in \(m\) on compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\) when \(m\) is held fixed.
  5. (v)
    If \(\alpha>1\), then \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\asymp(Bm)^\alpha\) for \(1/B\le m\le\gamma/2\) and \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\asymp Bm\) for \(0<m\le1/B\).
Proof.
Part (i) is a Riemann-sum comparison. The function \(t\mapsto V(V+t^\alpha)^{-1}\) decreases from \(1\) on \([0,1]\), so \(S_N(V)\) is a lower Riemann sum of \(S_\alpha(V)\) that differs from the upper one by at most \(1/N\). The function \(t\mapsto Vt^\alpha(V+t^\alpha)^{-2}\) vanishes at \(0\), increases to its maximum \(1/4\) and then decreases, so its total variation is at most \(1/2\), and its Riemann sum differs from its integral by at most \(1/(2N)\).
Part (ii) follows from (i) and Lemma SG.5(ii). By (i), \(S_\alpha(V_N(p))\in[p,p+1/N]\), so \(V_N(p)=V_\alpha(p')\) with \(p\le p'\le p+1/N\), and
\[ \log\frac{V_\alpha(p')}{V_\alpha(p)}=\int_p^{p'}\frac{\dif p''}{\mathsf T(V_\alpha(p''))}\le\frac1N\sup_{p''\in[p,p']}\frac{C}{p''(1-p'')}. \]
On the stated range, \(p''\ge M/\gamma\) and \(1-p''\ge M/\gamma-1/N\ge M/(2\gamma)\), so \(p''(1-p'')\ge M/(4\gamma)\), and the right side is at most \(4C\gamma/(NM)=4C/(BM)\).
Part (iii) bounds the deficit. Since \(1-S_N(V)=N^{-1}\sum_j(j/N)^\alpha(V+(j/N)^\alpha)^{-1}\) and \(N^{-1}\sum_j(j/N)^\alpha\) is bounded above and below, \(1-S_N(V)\asymp\min(1,1/V)\) for \(V\) bounded below, which inverts to the claim.
Part (iv) inverts (i) at small participation. With \(p=m/\gamma\), \(S_\alpha(V_N(p))=p(1+O(1/(Np)))=p(1+O(1/(Bm)))\), so \(V_N(p)\sim(p/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) by Lemma SG.5(iii), and (SG.42) gives \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\sim(Np/K_\alpha)^\alpha=(Bm/K_\alpha)^\alpha\). For the derivative, \(\Lambda_N'/\Lambda_N=\gamma^{-1}/(VS_N'(V))\) at \(V=V_N(p)\), and by (i) and Lemma SG.5(ii) at small \(V\), \(VS_N'(V)=\mathsf T(V)+O(N^{-1})=\alpha^{-1}p\,(1+o(1))\), so \(m\Lambda_N'(m)/\Lambda_N(m)\to\alpha\).
Part (v) compares the sum \(Bm_N(\Lambda)=\sum_jn^\alpha(n^\alpha+j^\alpha)^{-1}\), \(n=(c_N\Lambda)^{1/\alpha}\), with integrals. If \(n\le1\), comparison with \(\sum_jj^{-\alpha}<\infty\) gives \(Bm_N\asymp n^\alpha\). If \(1\le n\le N\), the terms \(j\le n\) give at least \(\lfloor n\rfloor/2\), and splitting the sum at \(n\) and comparing the terms \(j>n\) with \(\int_n^\infty n^\alpha t^{-\alpha}\dif t=n/(\alpha-1)\) gives at most \(C_\alpha n\). The case \(n>N\) does not occur when \(m\le\gamma/2\), because then every term would exceed \(1/2\). Since \(Bm_N=Bm\), these give \(c_N\Lambda\asymp Bm\) for \(Bm\le1\) and \(c_N\Lambda=n^\alpha\asymp(Bm)^\alpha\) for \(Bm\ge1\), the bounded transition region included.
□
