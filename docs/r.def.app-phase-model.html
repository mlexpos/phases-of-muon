Definition SI.1 (Deterministic row model)
Let the index set \(\mathcal I\) be \(\{1,\dots,N\}\) for an integer \(N\ge1\), or \(\N\). The deterministic row model consists of the following four items.
  1. (M1)
    Adopted coefficients. Numbers \(\mu_i>0\), \(\mathfrak d_i>0\), \(\mathfrak v_i\ge0\) and initial row energies \(\fQ_i(0)\ge0\), \(i\in\mathcal I\), and a constant step \(\lr>0\) are fixed, with
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.1} F_0\defeq\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mu_i\fQ_i(0)\in(0,\infty),\qquad \mathcal S\defeq\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\frac{\mu_i\mathfrak v_i}{2\mathfrak d_i}<\infty . \end{equation}
    They do not depend on time or on the state. An array carrying the superscript \(\text{s-SVD}\) or \(\text{s-SGD}\), such as \(\mathfrak d_i^{\text{s-SVD}}\) or \(\mathfrak v_i^{\text{s-SGD}}\), is an array adopted in this sense, and not a coefficient proved for an algorithm.
  2. (M2)
    Kernel replacement. For an update matrix \({\mathsf S}\) computed at a state \(\Delta\) of positive risk \(\cR(\Delta)\), the conditional row pairing and the conditional raw second moment of the row are replaced by
    \[ \EE\big[\langle\Delta^\T u_i,{\mathsf S}^\T u_i\rangle\,\big|\,\Delta\big]\ \leadsto\ \frac{\mathfrak d_i\,\norm{\Delta^\T u_i}^2}{\sqrt{\cR(\Delta)}},\qquad \EE\big[\norm{{\mathsf S}^\T u_i}^2\,\big|\,\Delta\big]\ \leadsto\ \mathfrak v_i . \]
  3. (M3)
    Dynamical closure. The row energies \(\fQ_i(t)\) solve
    \begin{equation}\tag{SI.2} \frac{\dif\fQ_i}{\dif t}=-\frac{2\lr\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\fRisk(t)}}\,\fQ_i(t)+\lr^2\mathfrak v_i,\qquad \fRisk(t)\defeq\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mu_i\fQ_i(t), \end{equation}
    from the initial values of (M1), on an interval starting at \(0\) on which \(\fRisk\) is continuous and positive.
  4. (M4)
    Transfer. A statement about the stochastic algorithm is drawn from the model only through an approximation theorem with its own regime, horizon and observable.
