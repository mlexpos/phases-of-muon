Proposition SI.6 (Limits of the ratio)
Write \(\mathsf R\defeq t^{\text{s-SGD}}_{T\epsilon}/t^{\text{s-SVD}}_{T\epsilon}\), and consider sequences with \(N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and \(B_N\ge N\) in the indicated windows. The constants in \(\asymp\) and \(O\) depend only on \(\alpha\), \(\texp\) and \(T\).
  1. (i)
    The range \(\texp<1\) (window of Theorem SI.8). With \(\Xi_N\defeq\epsilon N^{p-1}\) and \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}=(p-1)/\alpha\),
    \[ \mathsf R\asymp\begin{cases} \Xi_N^{-1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})},&0<\alpha<1,\\ (\log N)\,\Xi_N^{-1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})},&\alpha=1,\\ N^{\alpha-1}\,\Xi_N^{-1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})},&1<\alpha<2,\\ (N/\log N)\,\Xi_N^{-1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})},&\alpha=2,\\ \epsilon^{-1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})},&\alpha>2. \end{cases} \]
    Hence \(\mathsf R\to0\) for \(0<\alpha<1\) along sequences with \(\Xi_N\to\infty\), and \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) for \(\alpha>2\). For \(\alpha=1\) and \(\epsilon_N=N^{-r_{\text{s-SVD}}}(\log N)^{b}\) with \(b>0\), \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) if \(b<2r_{\text{s-SVD}}\) and \(\mathsf R\to0\) if \(b>2r_{\text{s-SVD}}\). For \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(\epsilon_N=N^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<p-1\), put \(\omega_c\defeq r_{\text{s-SVD}}(2-\alpha)\in(0,p-1)\); then \(\mathsf R\to0\) if \(\omega<\omega_c\) and \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) if \(\omega>\omega_c\). For \(\alpha=2\) and \(\epsilon_N=(\log N)^{-\omega}\) with \(\omega>0\), \(\mathsf R\to0\) if \(\omega<2r_{\text{s-SVD}}\) and \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) if \(\omega>2r_{\text{s-SVD}}\). All these sequences lie in the window.
  2. (ii)
    \(\alpha>2\), \(\texp\ge1\) (window of Theorem SI.9). \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) if \(\texp\le\alpha+1\), and \(\mathsf R\to\pi\zeta(\alpha)J(p,\alpha)/(\zeta(\alpha/2)J(p,\alpha/2))\in(0,\infty)\) if \(\texp>\alpha+1\); this limit exceeds \(\pi/2\) if \(\alpha\ge4\).
  3. (iii)
    \(1<\alpha\le2\), \(\texp\ge1\) (window of Theorem SI.9). \(\mathsf R\to0\) if \(\texp>\alpha+1\). If \(\texp=\alpha+1\) and \(1<\alpha<2\), then \(\mathsf R\to0\). If \(\alpha=2\), \(\texp=3\) and \(\log(1/\epsilon)/\log N\to c_\infty\), then \(0\le c_\infty\le1\) and \(\mathsf R\to\pi\zeta(2)c_\infty/(8J(5,1))\).
  4. (iv)
    \(0<\alpha\le1\), \(\texp\ge1\) (low-exponent window). \(\mathsf R\to0\); more precisely \(\mathsf R\) is \(O(\log(1/\epsilon)^{-2})\), \(O(\epsilon^{(\texp-1)/(p-1)})\), \(O(\sqrt\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon))\) and \(O(\sqrt\epsilon)\) for \(\texp=1\), \(1<\texp<\alpha+1\), \(\texp=\alpha+1\) and \(\texp>\alpha+1\) respectively.
  5. (v)
    \(1<\alpha\le2\), \(1\le\texp<\alpha+1\) (window of Theorem SI.9). Put
    \[ e_\texp\defeq\frac{\alpha+1-\texp}{2(p-1)},\qquad \texp_c(\alpha)\defeq\frac{\alpha^2-2\alpha+3}{3-\alpha} , \]
    so that \(1<\texp_c(\alpha)<\alpha+1\) for \(1<\alpha<2\), and \(\texp_c(2)=3\).
    1. (a)
      If \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(\texp_c(\alpha)\le\texp<\alpha+1\), then \(\mathsf R\to0\) along every sequence with \(N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and \(\epsilon N\to\infty\).
    2. (b)
      If \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(1\le\texp<\texp_c(\alpha)\), put \(\omega_c\defeq(2-\alpha)(p-1)/(\alpha+1-\texp)\in(0,1)\). For \(\epsilon_N=N^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<1\), \(\mathsf R\to0\) if \(\omega<\omega_c\) and \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) if \(\omega>\omega_c\). For \(\epsilon_N=1/\log N\), \(\mathsf R\to0\).
    3. (c)
      If \(\alpha=2\) and \(1\le\texp<3\), then \(\mathsf R\to\infty\) for \(\epsilon_N=N^{-\omega}\) with \(0<\omega<1\), and \(\mathsf R\to0\) for \(\epsilon_N=1/\log N\).
    All the sequences named in (b) and (c) lie in the window for large \(N\).
Proof of Proposition SI.6.
For (i), Corollary SI.3 gives \(\mathsf R\asymp D_N\epsilon^{-1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})}\), and \(\epsilon=\Xi_NN^{-(p-1)}\) with \((p-1)/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})=\alpha/2\) gives
\[ \mathsf R\asymp D_N\,N^{\alpha/2}\,\Xi_N^{-1/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})} . \]
The equivalents (SI.31) give the five rows; for \(\alpha>2\), \(D_N\) has a positive limit. For \(0<\alpha<1\) the first row tends to \(0\) when \(\Xi_N\to\infty\), and for \(\alpha>2\) the last row tends to infinity because \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). For \(\alpha=1\), \(p-1=r_{\text{s-SVD}}\), so \(\Xi_N=(\log N)^b\to\infty\) and \(\mathsf R\asymp(\log N)^{1-b/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})}\). For \(1<\alpha<2\), \(\Xi_N=N^{p-1-\omega}\to\infty\) and
\[ \mathsf R\asymp N^{\alpha-1}N^{-(p-1-\omega)/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})}=N^{(\omega-\omega_c)/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})} , \]
with \(\omega_c/(p-1)=(2-\alpha)/\alpha\in(0,1)\). For \(\alpha=2\), \(\Xi_N=N^{p-1}(\log N)^{-\omega}\to\infty\) and \(\mathsf R\asymp(\log N)^{\omega/(2r_{\text{s-SVD}})-1}\). Positive powers of \(N\) dominate powers of \(\log N\), which gives each limit and membership in the window.
For (ii), \(D_N\to\pi\zeta(\alpha)/\zeta(\alpha/2)>0\). The factor in (SI.38) tends to infinity for \(\texp\le\alpha+1\), and for \(\texp>\alpha+1\) the equivalent of Corollary SI.3 gives the limit. If \(\alpha\ge4\), \(\zeta(\alpha)>1\) and \(\zeta(\alpha/2)\le\zeta(2)<2\), so \(D_N\) has limit above \(\pi/2\), and \(J(p,\alpha)\ge J(p,\alpha/2)\) as in the proof of Corollary SI.2.
For (iii), \(D_N\to0\) by (SI.31), which gives the saturated limit. At \(\texp=\alpha+1\), \(\epsilon N\ge E_*\ge1\) gives \(\log(1/\epsilon)\le\log N\). For \(1<\alpha<2\), \(D_N\log N\asymp N^{\alpha/2-1}\log N\to0\). For \(\alpha=2\), \(2\sqrt{8\alpha}=8\) and \(D_N\sim\pi\zeta(2)/\log N\), which gives the limit, with \(0\le c_\infty\le1\).
For (iv), (SI.29) and (SI.27) give the identity \(D_N=\frac\pi2k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}\). At \(\texp=1\), \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}=1\) and the height condition reads \(k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}C_{\text{s-SVD}}\log(C_{\text{s-SVD}}/\sqrt\epsilon)\le\sqrt\epsilon\), so \(k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}=O(\sqrt\epsilon/\log(1/\epsilon))\) and \(\mathsf R=O(\log(1/\epsilon)^{-2})\) by (SI.38). For \(\texp>1\), \(r_{\text{s-SVD}}>1\) and \(I_{r_{\text{s-SVD}}}(H)\ge I_{r_{\text{s-SVD}}}(1)>0\) once \(H\ge1\), so \(k_{N,\text{s-SVD}}=O(\sqrt\epsilon)\). In the branch \(1<\texp<\alpha+1\) the remaining exponent is \(\frac12-\frac{\alpha+1-\texp}{2(p-1)}=\frac{\texp-1}{p-1}>0\), and the last two branches give \(O(\sqrt\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon))\) and \(O(\sqrt\epsilon)\).
For (v), the rows \(1\le\texp<\alpha+1\) of (SI.38) have the common form
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.57} \mathsf R\asymp D_N\,\epsilon^{-e_\texp}\,\ell_\epsilon,\qquad \ell_\epsilon\defeq\begin{cases}1/\log(1/\epsilon),&\texp=1,\\ 1,&1<\texp<\alpha+1,\end{cases} \end{equation}
since \(e_1=\frac12\). By (SI.31), \(D_N\asymp N^{\alpha/2-1}\) for \(1<\alpha<2\) and \(D_N\asymp1/\log N\) for \(\alpha=2\). The position of \(\texp\) relative to the curve \(\texp_c\) is the sign of one exponent. Expanding,
\[ (\alpha-2)(p-1)+(\alpha+1-\texp)=(\alpha-2)(\alpha-1)+(\alpha-2)\texp+\alpha+1-\texp=\alpha^2-2\alpha+3-(3-\alpha)\texp , \]
so that
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.58} \frac\alpha2-1+e_\texp=\frac{(3-\alpha)\big(\texp_c(\alpha)-\texp\big)}{2(p-1)} . \end{equation}
The bounds on \(\texp_c\) follow from
\[ \texp_c(\alpha)-1=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{3-\alpha},\qquad \alpha+1-\texp_c(\alpha)=\frac{2\alpha(2-\alpha)}{3-\alpha} . \]
For (a), \(\texp\ge\texp_c(\alpha)>1\), so \(\ell_\epsilon=1\), and (SI.57) gives
\[ \mathsf R\asymp N^{\alpha/2-1}\epsilon^{-e_\texp}=(\epsilon N)^{-e_\texp}\,N^{\alpha/2-1+e_\texp} . \]
By (SI.58) the exponent of \(N\) is at most \(0\), and \((\epsilon N)^{-e_\texp}\to0\) because \(e_\texp>0\) and \(\epsilon N\to\infty\).
For (b) with \(\epsilon_N=N^{-\omega}\), (SI.57) gives
\[ \mathsf R\asymp N^{\alpha/2-1+\omega e_\texp}\,\ell_{\epsilon_N},\qquad \ell_{\epsilon_N}\in\Big\{1,\frac1{\omega\log N}\Big\} . \]
The exponent of \(N\) is negative exactly when \(\omega<(1-\alpha/2)/e_\texp=\omega_c\). By (SI.58), \(\omega_c<1\) exactly when \(\texp<\texp_c(\alpha)\). A positive or negative power of \(N\) dominates the factor \(\ell_{\epsilon_N}\), which gives both limits. For \(\epsilon_N=1/\log N\),
\[ \mathsf R\asymp N^{\alpha/2-1}(\log N)^{e_\texp}\ell_{\epsilon_N}\longrightarrow0 . \]
For (c), \(D_N\asymp1/\log N\), and (SI.57) gives
\[ \mathsf R\asymp\frac{N^{\omega e_\texp}\ell_{\epsilon_N}}{\log N}\longrightarrow\infty\quad(\epsilon_N=N^{-\omega}),\qquad \mathsf R\asymp(\log N)^{e_\texp-1}\ell_{\epsilon_N}\longrightarrow0\quad(\epsilon_N=1/\log N), \]
where the second limit uses \(e_\texp=(3-\texp)/(2(1+\texp))\le\frac12\) for \(\texp\ge1\).
Every named sequence has \(N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon_N\downarrow0\) and \(\epsilon_NN\to\infty\) (\(N^{1-\omega}\) or \(N/\log N\)), so it lies in the window of Theorem SI.9 for large \(N\).
□
