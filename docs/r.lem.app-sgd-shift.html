Lemma SJ.2 (Small shifts)
Let \(X\) have an even density \(f\), and put \(h(x)\defeq\EE\sign(X+x)\) for real \(x\).
  1. (i)
    For every real \(x\), with oriented integrals,
    \[ h(x)=2\int_0^xf(y)\,\dif y . \]
  2. (ii)
    If \(a>0\), \(\alpha>0\), \(L_f\ge0\) and \(\abs{f(y)-f(0)}\le L_f\abs y^\alpha\) for \(\abs y\le a\), then
    \[ \abs{h(x)-2xf(0)}\le\frac{2L_f}{\alpha+1}\abs x^{\alpha+1}\qquad(\abs x\le a). \]
  3. (iii)
    If \(f\) is continuously differentiable on \([-a,a]\) with a \(K_f\)-Lipschitz derivative, then \(\abs{h(x)-2xf(0)}\le\frac{K_f}3\abs x^3\) for \(\abs x\le a\).
  4. (iv)
    If \(X\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma>0\), then for every real \(x\)
    \begin{equation}\tag{SJ.6} \Big|h(x)-\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac x\sigma\Big|\le\frac{\abs x^3}{3\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma^3}. \end{equation}
Proof.
The identity (i) splits the law at the shifted origin. Symmetry puts mass \(\frac12\) on each half-line, so
\[ h(x)=\PP(X>-x)-\PP(X<-x)=1-2\,\PP(X<-x)=2\int_{-x}^0f(y)\,\dif y=2\int_0^xf(y)\,\dif y . \]
The bound (ii) integrates the modulus of \(f\) in (i):
\[ \abs{h(x)-2xf(0)}\le2\Big|\int_0^x\abs{f(y)-f(0)}\,\dif y\Big|\le2L_f\int_0^{\abs x}y^\alpha\,\dif y=\frac{2L_f}{\alpha+1}\abs x^{\alpha+1}. \]
For (iii), evenness forces \(f'(0)=0\). The Lipschitz bound then gives \(\abs{f'(y)}\le K_f\abs y\), and integrating once,
\[ \abs{f(y)-f(0)}\le\frac{K_f}2\,y^2\qquad(\abs y\le a). \]
This is (ii) with \(\alpha=2\) and \(L_f=K_f/2\).
For (iv), the Gaussian density \(f_\sigma(y)=f_\sigma(0)e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2)}\) satisfies, by \(0\le1-e^{-u}\le u\) for \(u\ge0\),
\[ \abs{f_\sigma(y)-f_\sigma(0)}\le\frac{f_\sigma(0)}{2\sigma^2}\,y^2\qquad(y\in\R). \]
This is (ii) with \(\alpha=2\), \(L_f=f_\sigma(0)/(2\sigma^2)\) and every \(a\), and \(2f_\sigma(0)=\sqrt{2/\pi}/\sigma\).
□
