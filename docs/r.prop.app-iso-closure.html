Proposition SF.1 (Isotropic closure)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\gin,\gout>0\) and \(s>0\), and let \((\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)=(\sigma,\tilde\sigma,\tilde s_1,s_1,q)(-s)\) be the solution of (SB.2) for the measures (SF.1).
  1. (i)
    The branch satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.5} \sigma=\frac{\gout}{s+\tilde s_1},\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac{\gin}{s+s_1},\qquad \tilde s_1\sigma=s_1\tilde\sigma=\psi(s), \end{equation}
    and the two anchors and their consequences
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.6} \psi(s)=\gout-s\sigma=\gin-s\tilde\sigma,\qquad \tilde\sigma-\sigma=\frac{\gin-\gout}{s},\qquad (s+\tilde s_1)(s+s_1)=\frac{s\,\gin\gout}{q}. \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    The number \(q=q(-s)\) is the unique \(q>0\) with \(q\Fw(q)<\min(\gin,\gout)\) and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.7} s\,q=\big(\gout-q\Fw(q)\big)\big(\gin-q\Fw(q)\big). \end{equation}
  3. (iii)
    At \(\bmom=0\), with \(\xi\defeq(2q)^{-1/2}=(2s\sigma\tilde\sigma)^{-1/2}\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SF.8} \psi(s)=\Phi(q)=f(\xi)=1-\sqrt\pi\,\xi\,e^{\xi^2}\erfc(\xi). \end{equation}
Proof.
Part (i) is the system (SB.2) at point masses. For \(\nu=\gout\delta_1\) the first trace equation is
\[ \sigma=\int\frac{x}{\tilde s_1x+s}\,\gout\delta_1(dx)=\frac{\gout}{\tilde s_1+s}, \]
and the second is the same computation for \(\gin\delta_1\). The third identity of (SF.5) is the coupling (SB.4). Multiplying the first identity by \(s+\tilde s_1\) gives
\[ s\sigma+\tilde s_1\sigma=\gout , \]
which is the first anchor, and the second anchor is the same computation on the input side. Subtracting the anchors gives the second identity of (SF.6). The product of the first two identities of (SF.5) and \(q=s\sigma\tilde\sigma\) give
\[ (s+\tilde s_1)(s+s_1)=\frac{\gin\gout}{\sigma\tilde\sigma}=\frac{s\,\gin\gout}{q}. \]
Part (ii) eliminates \(\sigma\) and \(\tilde\sigma\). By the anchors,
\[ \sigma=\frac{\gout-\psi(s)}{s},\qquad \tilde\sigma=\frac{\gin-\psi(s)}{s}, \]
and inserting both into \(q=s\sigma\tilde\sigma\) gives (SF.7). Since \(\tilde s_1\ge0\), the first identity of (SF.5) gives \(\sigma>0\), so \(\psi(s)<\gout\); likewise \(\psi(s)<\gin\), and \(q>0\).
Conversely, let \(q'>0\) satisfy \(q'\Fw(q')<\min(\gin,\gout)\) and (SF.7), and put \(\psi'\defeq q'\Fw(q')\) and
\[ \sigma'\defeq\frac{\gout-\psi'}{s},\qquad \tilde\sigma'\defeq\frac{\gin-\psi'}{s},\qquad a'\defeq\frac{\psi'}{\sigma'},\qquad c'\defeq\frac{\psi'}{\tilde\sigma'} . \]
These four numbers are nonnegative. The tuple solves the system (SB.2) at \(z=-s\). Indeed the first trace equation holds because
\[ s+a'=s+\frac{s\,\psi'}{\gout-\psi'}=\frac{s\,\gout}{\gout-\psi'}=\frac{\gout}{\sigma'} , \]
the second holds by the same computation, the equation \(q'=s\sigma'\tilde\sigma'\) is (SF.7), and
\[ s\,\tilde\sigma'\,\Fw(q')=\frac{s\,\tilde\sigma'\,\psi'}{q'}=\frac{\psi'}{\sigma'}=a', \]
with the equation for \(c'\) symmetric. The uniqueness in Theorem SB.1(i) gives \(q'=q\).
Part (iii) is Proposition SB.1: at \(\bmom=0\) the only weight is \(\emaw0=1\), so \(\Fw=\Jfun\) and \(\psi(s)=q\Jfun(q)=\Phi(q)\).
□
