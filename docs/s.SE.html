This section proves that the algorithm of Definition SA.2, run at the constant step \(\lr=\macrostep/B\), follows the deterministic mode-energy equations of the main text on a positive interval of macroscopic time \(\macrotime=t/B\). The equations are (6) in continuous time, with the drift kernel \(\mathfrak d_{ij}\) of Corollary SC.1 and the volatility coefficient \(\mathfrak v_{ij}\) of Proposition SD.3. The comparison is made in the bounded-Lipschitz distance of spectral energy measures, which place the projected risk \(\CMscr Q_{ij}\) of the eigenpair \((u_i,v_j)\) at the point \((\mu_i,\lambda_j)\) of the spectral rectangle. Theorem SE.1 is the main result. The half-anisotropic case \(\Sigmain=\Id\) is Corollary SE.1, and Corollary SE.2 passes to dimension-free measure equations when the coefficients and the initial data converge.
The coefficients of the drift and volatility sections are computed in the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3: a stationary buffer of independent batches, all computed at one deterministic state whose normalized error has vanishing operator norm. The algorithm differs from it in three ways. Its state moves; its buffer retains the batches of earlier states and an initial buffer; and its ridge follows the risk. The proof compares the algorithm, on blocks of \(\lfloor B^{1/8}\rfloor\) consecutive steps, with the frozen-state model at the state of the start of the block. It controls the difference of the expected block increments by a causal expansion in the step, the fluctuations by Gaussian concentration and a martingale argument, and the persistence of a small normalized operator norm by a matrix martingale; it then shows that the risk stays bounded below on a positive interval and passes from fixed tests to the bounded-Lipschitz distance.

SE.1 Standing setup

The algorithm at constant step. Throughout this section the algorithm of Definition SA.2 is run with momentum \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\) and the constant step \(\lr_t=\lr=\macrostep/B\) with \(\macrostep>0\). The features are Gaussian as in Assumption S1, and the whitened samples of the batch of step \(t\) are \((z_{t,a},w_{t,a})_{a\le B}\). For a matrix \(E\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) we write the gradient of that batch at \(E\) as
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.1} G^\circ_{t+1}[E]\defeq\frac1B\sum_{a=1}^B\big(z_{t,a}^\T\Sigmaout^{1/2}E\,\Sigmain^{1/2}w_{t,a}\big)\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z_{t,a}\tensor w_{t,a})\Sigmain^{1/2} . \end{equation}
It is linear in \(E\), its mean is \(\Sigmaout E\Sigmain\) by (SA.5), and the minibatch gradient of (SA.9) is \(G^\circ_{t+1}=G^\circ_{t+1}[\Dt]\). For a matrix \(\Delta\) we write \(\cR(\Delta)\defeq\frac12\norm{\Delta}_\Sigma^2\), so that \(\cR(t)=\cR(\Dt)\) by (SA.4), and \(\ridgephys(\Delta)\defeq2\cR(\Delta)\ridge\) for the physical ridge. We write \({\mathsf S}_t\) for the update applied at step \(t\), the matrix \(\Gtilde^\circ\) of (SA.15). Before the stopping time \(t_{\rm stop}\) of Definition SA.2 the algorithm is
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.2} M^\circ_t=\bmom M^\circ_{t-1}+(1-\bmom)\,G^\circ_{t+1}[\Dt],\qquad {\mathsf S}_t=\Qridge{\ridgephys(\Dt)}(M^\circ_t),\qquad \Delta_{t+1}=\Dt-\frac{\macrostep}{B}\,{\mathsf S}_t . \end{equation}
The letter \({\mathsf S}_t\) always denotes this actual update; the frozen updates of the volatility section, written there \({\mathsf S}_{t'}(\ridge)\) with their ridge, are written \(V_r\) in this section.
Normalized error and diffuseness. For \(\Delta\) with \(\cR(\Delta)>0\) we put
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.3} \widehat A(\Delta)\defeq\frac{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Delta\Sigmain^{1/2}}{\sqrt{2\cR(\Delta)}},\qquad \delta(\Delta)\defeq\norm{\widehat A(\Delta)} , \end{equation}
so that \(\norm{\widehat A(\Delta)}_\F=1\) by (SA.9) and \(\widehat A(\Dt)=\Ahat\). We call \(\delta(\Delta)\) the diffuseness of \(\Delta\). We write \(\mathscr S_B\) for the set of all matrices \(\widehat A(\Delta)\) with \(\cR(\Delta)>0\); it is the set of normalized states of the frozen-state model.
Spectral energy measures. Let \(\mathbb K\defeq[0,C_\Sigma]^2\). For \(\Delta\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) the spectral energy measure of \(\Delta\) is the positive measure
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.4} \chi(\Delta)\defeq\sum_{i,j}\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta)\,\delta_{(\mu_i,\lambda_j)}\quad\text{on }\mathbb K . \end{equation}
For bounded functions \(f,g\) on \([0,C_\Sigma]\) we write \(\mathcal W_{f,g}X\defeq f(\Sigmaout)\,X\,g(\Sigmain)\), a linear map on \(\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) which is selfadjoint for the Frobenius pairing \(\langle V,W\rangle=\Tr(VW^\T)\) and has norm at most \(\norm f_\infty\norm g_\infty\) for the Frobenius norm. Expanding in the eigenbases,
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.5} \int f\tensor g\,\dif\chi(\Delta)=\sum_{i,j}f(\mu_i)g(\lambda_j)\CMscr Q_{ij}(\Delta)=\langle\Delta,\mathcal W_{f,g}\Delta\rangle , \end{equation}
where \((f\tensor g)(\mu,\lambda)\defeq f(\mu)g(\lambda)\). In particular \(\chi(\Delta)(\mathbb K)=\norm\Delta_\F^2\) and \(\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\chi(\Delta)=2\cR(\Delta)\) by Lemma SA.1. For a finite signed measure \(m\) on \(\mathbb K\) we use the total variation \(\norm m_{\mathrm{TV}}\) and the bounded-Lipschitz norm \(\norm m_\BL\) of the volatility section (the definition following (SD.78)), in which the test functions \(\phi\) satisfy \(\norm\phi_\infty\le1\) and \(\abs{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}\le\abs{x-y}_1\). For a bounded function \(\phi\) on \(\mathbb K\), \(\phi\,m\) denotes the measure with density \(\phi\) with respect to \(m\).
Coefficients. The drift kernel is the function \(\mathfrak d\) of (SC.4) at the ridge \(\ridge\), with \(\mathfrak d_{ij}=\mathfrak d(\mu_i,\lambda_j)\) (Corollary SC.1). The forcing measure is the measure \(\zeta\defeq\zeta_\ridge\) of (SD.78), and the mode coefficients are \(\mathfrak v_{ij}\defeq\mathfrak v_{ij}(\ridge)\) of (SD.80), so that \(\zeta=B^{-1}\sum_{i,j}\mathfrak v_{ij}\,\delta_{(\mu_i,\lambda_j)}\) by Proposition SD.3. Both are deterministic, depend on the dimension, on \(\bmom\), \(\ridge\) and the covariances, and do not depend on the state. By (SC.14) and Theorem SD.8(iii), with \(\mathsf K_\star\) the constant of (SD.39) at \(\ridge_-=\ridge\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.6} \begin{gathered} 0\le\mathfrak d\le\mathfrak d_{\max}\defeq\frac{C_\Sigma^2}{\sqrt{2\ridge}}\ \text{on }\mathbb K,\qquad \abs{\mathfrak d(x)-\mathfrak d(y)}\le\frac{C_\Sigma}{\sqrt{2\ridge}}\abs{x-y}_1,\\ 0\le\zeta(\mathbb K)\le m_\zeta\defeq\gamma_\star+\frac{2\mathsf K_\star\bmom}{1-\bmom}. \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Conventions. The past \(\cF_t\) is that of Definition SA.1, and \(\EE_t=\EE[\,\cdot\mid\cF_t]\). Conditional expectations given \(\cF_t\) are computed by integrating the batches of the steps \(t,t+1,\dots\) with the past held fixed, since these batches are independent of the past. Comparisons with the frozen-state model use auxiliary batches, independent of everything else and drawn anew for each comparison; \(\EE_t\) integrates them as well. At \(\bmom=0\) we use the convention \(\bmom^0=1\), so that \(\bmom^\ell=0\) for \(\ell\ge1\). A constant \(C\) depends only on \((\macrostep,\ridge,\bmom,\gamma_\star,C_\Sigma)\) and on the compact sets of states named in the statement where it appears; it never depends on the dimension, on the time or on the block, and it may change from line to line.

SE.2 The deterministic equations

Definition SE.1 (Deterministic mode-energy equations)
For \(i\le\Nout\) and \(j\le\Nin\), the deterministic mode-energy equations are
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.7} \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\big(B^2\fQ_{ij}(\macrotime)\big)=-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{ij}}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\macrotime)}}\,B^2\fQ_{ij}(\macrotime)+\macrostep^2B\,\mathfrak v_{ij},\qquad \fRisk(\macrotime)\defeq\frac12\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_j\,\fQ_{ij}(\macrotime), \end{equation}
with the initial values \(\fQ_{ij}(0)=\CMscr Q_{ij}(0)=(u_i^\T\Delta_0v_j)^2\). Their spectral energy measure is \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)\defeq\sum_{i,j}\fQ_{ij}(\macrotime)\,\delta_{(\mu_i,\lambda_j)}\).
Dividing (SE.7) by \(B^2\) gives
\[ \frac{\dif\fQ_{ij}}{\dif\macrotime}=-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{ij}}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}\,\fQ_{ij}+\frac{\macrostep^2}{B}\,\mathfrak v_{ij}. \]
With \(\lr=\macrostep/B\) and \(B\) steps per unit of \(\macrotime\), the change over one step is \(-2\lr\,\mathfrak d_{ij}\fQ_{ij}/\sqrt{\fRisk}+\lr^2\mathfrak v_{ij}\), which is the recursion (6) with the kernels of Corollary SC.1 and Proposition SD.3. The variables \(B^2\fQ_{ij}\) are the ones in which both terms are of order one. The total \(\sum_{i,j}\fQ_{ij}=\norm{\Delta}_\F^2\) is bounded and is spread over at most \(\gamma_\star^2B^2\) pairs, so a typical \(B^2\fQ_{ij}\) is of order one; the drift rate \(2\macrostep\mathfrak d_{ij}/\sqrt{\fRisk}\) is bounded by (SE.6) where the risk is bounded below; and since \(\zeta=B^{-1}\sum_{i,j}\mathfrak v_{ij}\delta_{(\mu_i,\lambda_j)}\) has mass at most \(m_\zeta\), the forcing \(B\mathfrak v_{ij}\) of a typical pair is of order one as well.
The measure form. For a positive measure \(\chi\) on \(\mathbb K\) with \(\fRisk(\chi)\defeq\frac12\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\chi(\mu,\lambda)>0\) we define the signed measure
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.8} \mathsf F_B(\chi)\defeq-\frac{2\macrostep}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}\,\mathfrak d\,\chi+\macrostep^2\zeta . \end{equation}
The atom of \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)\) at a point \((\mu,\lambda)\) is the sum of \(\fQ_{ij}(\macrotime)\) over the pairs with \((\mu_i,\lambda_j)=(\mu,\lambda)\), and the atom of \(\macrostep^2\zeta\) there is the sum of \(\macrostep^2\mathfrak v_{ij}/B\) over the same pairs. Summing the divided equations over these pairs therefore gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.9} \chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)=\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(0)+\int_0^\macrotime\mathsf F_B\big(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\sigma)\big)\,\dif\sigma , \end{equation}
with \(\fRisk(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime))=\fRisk(\macrotime)\).
Constants and the admissible set. Fix \(E_\star<\infty\) and \(0<r_0\le r_1<\infty\). With the constants of (SE.6) put
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.10} \begin{gathered} C_E\defeq\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}(E_\star+1)}{\sqrt{r_0/2}}+\macrostep^2m_\zeta,\qquad C_{\cR}\defeq2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}\sqrt{r_1+1}+\frac{\macrostep^2C_\Sigma^2m_\zeta}{2},\\ T_\star\defeq\frac{\min(r_0,1)}{8\,(1+C_E+C_{\cR})} , \end{gathered} \end{equation}
which depend only on \((\macrostep,\ridge,\bmom,\gamma_\star,C_\Sigma,E_\star,r_0,r_1)\). The admissible measures are
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.11} \mathfrak M\defeq\Big\{\chi\ \text{positive on }\mathbb K:\ \chi(\mathbb K)\le E_\star+1,\ \frac{r_0}{2}\le\fRisk(\chi)\le r_1+\frac12\Big\}. \end{equation}
Lemma SE.1 (The field)
There is \(C_{\mathsf F}\), depending only on \((\macrostep,\ridge,C_\Sigma,E_\star,r_0,r_1)\) and on \(m_\zeta\), such that for \(\chi,\chi'\in\mathfrak M\):
  1. (i)
    \(\abs{\mathsf F_B(\chi)(\mathbb K)}\le\norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le C_E\) and \(\frac12\big|\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\mathsf F_B(\chi)\big|\le C_{\cR}\);
  2. (ii)
    \(\norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)-\mathsf F_B(\chi')}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le C_{\mathsf F}\norm{\chi-\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\);
  3. (iii)
    \(\norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)-\mathsf F_B(\chi')}_\BL\le C_{\mathsf F}\norm{\chi-\chi'}_\BL\).
The same statements hold, with \(C_E\), \(C_{\cR}\), \(C_{\mathsf F}\) depending also on the bounds, on every set defined as \(\mathfrak M\) with other positive bounds on the mass and the risk.
Proof
Proof.
The mass bound is (SE.6). On \(\mathfrak M\),
\[ \norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi)}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}\,\chi(\mathbb K)}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}+\macrostep^2\zeta(\mathbb K)\le\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}(E_\star+1)}{\sqrt{r_0/2}}+\macrostep^2m_\zeta=C_E . \]
The risk bound uses \(\mathfrak d\le\mathfrak d_{\max}\) inside the risk integral. Since \(\int\mu\lambda\,\mathfrak d\,\dif\chi\le\mathfrak d_{\max}\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\chi=2\mathfrak d_{\max}\fRisk(\chi)\) and \(\mu\lambda\le C_\Sigma^2\) on \(\mathbb K\),
\[ \frac12\Big|\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\mathsf F_B(\chi)\Big|\le\frac{\macrostep}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}\,2\mathfrak d_{\max}\fRisk(\chi)+\frac{\macrostep^2C_\Sigma^2}{2}\zeta(\mathbb K) \le2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_{\max}\sqrt{r_1+1}+\frac{\macrostep^2C_\Sigma^2m_\zeta}{2}=C_{\cR} . \]
The Lipschitz bounds follow from one decomposition. The forcing cancels in the difference, and
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.12} \mathsf F_B(\chi)-\mathsf F_B(\chi')=-\frac{2\macrostep}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}\,\mathfrak d\,(\chi-\chi') -2\macrostep\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi')}}\Big)\mathfrak d\,\chi' . \end{equation}
For \(\fRisk,\fRisk'\ge r_0/2\) the mean value theorem gives
\[ \Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\fRisk'}}\Big|\le\frac12\Big(\frac{r_0}{2}\Big)^{-3/2}\abs{\fRisk-\fRisk'} . \]
In total variation, \(\norm{\mathfrak d(\chi-\chi')}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\mathfrak d_{\max}\norm{\chi-\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\) and \(\abs{\fRisk(\chi)-\fRisk(\chi')}\le\frac12C_\Sigma^2\norm{\chi-\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\), which with \(\norm{\mathfrak d\chi'}_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\mathfrak d_{\max}(E_\star+1)\) proves (ii).
In the bounded-Lipschitz norm we use that multiplication by a bounded Lipschitz function maps the unit ball of test functions into a multiple of it. If \(\norm\phi_\infty\le1\) and \(\phi\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz for \(\abs\cdot_1\), then by (SE.6)
\[ \norm{\phi\,\mathfrak d}_\infty\le\mathfrak d_{\max},\qquad \abs{\phi\mathfrak d(x)-\phi\mathfrak d(y)}\le\Big(\mathfrak d_{\max}+\frac{C_\Sigma}{\sqrt{2\ridge}}\Big)\abs{x-y}_1 , \]
so \(\abs{\int\phi\,\mathfrak d\,\dif(\chi-\chi')}\le c_1\norm{\chi-\chi'}_\BL\) with \(c_1\defeq2\mathfrak d_{\max}+C_\Sigma/\sqrt{2\ridge}\). The function \(\mu\lambda\) has supremum \(C_\Sigma^2\) and \(\abs\cdot_1\)-Lipschitz constant \(C_\Sigma\) on \(\mathbb K\), so \(\abs{\fRisk(\chi)-\fRisk(\chi')}\le\frac12(2C_\Sigma^2+C_\Sigma)\norm{\chi-\chi'}_\BL\). Inserting these two bounds in (SE.12), with \(\abs{\int\phi\,\mathfrak d\,\dif\chi'}\le\mathfrak d_{\max}(E_\star+1)\), proves (iii). The last sentence is the same computation with the other bounds.
□
Proposition SE.1 (Well-posedness)
At a fixed dimension, let \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), and let \(\Delta_0\) satisfy \(\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2\le E_\star\) and \(r_0\le\cR(\Delta_0)\le r_1\). Then:
  1. (i)
    the system (SE.7) has a unique solution on \([0,T_\star]\), and \(\fQ_{ij}(\macrotime)\ge0\) for all \(i,j\) and \(\macrotime\le T_\star\);
  2. (ii)
    for \(\macrotime\le T_\star\), \(\abs{\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)(\mathbb K)-\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2}\le C_E\macrotime\) and \(\abs{\fRisk(\macrotime)-\cR(\Delta_0)}\le C_{\cR}\macrotime\); in particular \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)\in\mathfrak M\);
  3. (iii)
    \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}\) satisfies (SE.9) on \([0,T_\star]\).
Proof
Proof.
The vector field is locally Lipschitz where the risk is positive. On the open set \(\mathcal O\defeq\{(e_{ij})\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}:\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\lambda_je_{ij}>0\}\), the right side of (SE.7) in the variables \(e_{ij}=B^2\fQ_{ij}\) is a product of linear functions of \(e\) and of \(x\mapsto x^{-1/2}\) evaluated at a positive linear function of \(e\), hence continuously differentiable. The Picard–Lindelöf theorem [98, Theorem 2.2] gives a unique solution on a maximal interval \([0,T_{\max})\) in \(\mathcal O\).
The solution is nonnegative by variation of constants. Along the solution put \(a_{ij}(\macrotime)\defeq2\macrostep\mathfrak d_{ij}/\sqrt{\fRisk(\macrotime)}\), a continuous function on \([0,T_{\max})\). Then
\[ B^2\fQ_{ij}(\macrotime)=e^{-\int_0^\macrotime a_{ij}}\,B^2\fQ_{ij}(0)+\macrostep^2B\,\mathfrak v_{ij}\int_0^\macrotime e^{-\int_\sigma^\macrotime a_{ij}}\,\dif\sigma\ge0 , \]
because \(\fQ_{ij}(0)\ge0\) and \(\mathfrak v_{ij}\ge0\) by Proposition SD.3.
The measure equation (SE.9) holds on \([0,T_{\max})\) by the summation over atoms explained before (SE.10). Testing it with \(1\) and with \(\mu\lambda/2\) gives
\[ \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)(\mathbb K)=\mathsf F_B(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime))(\mathbb K),\qquad \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\fRisk(\macrotime)=\frac12\int\mu\lambda\,\dif\mathsf F_B(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)). \]
The solution cannot leave \(\mathfrak M\) before \(T_\star\). The initial measure satisfies \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(0)(\mathbb K)=\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2\le E_\star\) and \(\fRisk(0)=\cR(\Delta_0)\in[r_0,r_1]\), so it lies in the interior of \(\mathfrak M\). Let \(\macrotime_1\) be the first time in \([0,T_{\max})\) at which \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}\) reaches the boundary of \(\mathfrak M\), and suppose \(\macrotime_1\le T_\star\). On \([0,\macrotime_1]\) the measure lies in \(\mathfrak M\), so Lemma SE.1(i) bounds both derivatives above, and
\[ \abs{\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime_1)(\mathbb K)-\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2}\le C_ET_\star\le\frac18,\qquad \abs{\fRisk(\macrotime_1)-\cR(\Delta_0)}\le C_{\cR}T_\star\le\frac{\min(r_0,1)}{8}. \]
Then \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime_1)(\mathbb K)\le E_\star+\frac18\), \(\fRisk(\macrotime_1)\ge\frac78r_0\) and \(\fRisk(\macrotime_1)\le r_1+\frac18\), so \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime_1)\) is in the interior of \(\mathfrak M\), a contradiction. Hence the solution stays in \(\mathfrak M\) on \([0,\min(T_{\max},T_\star))\), with the bounds of (ii).
The solution exists up to \(T_\star\). On \([0,\min(T_{\max},T_\star))\) the vector \((e_{ij})\) stays in the set \(\{e\ge0:\sum_{ij}e_{ij}\le B^2(E_\star+1),\ \sum\mu_i\lambda_je_{ij}\ge B^2r_0\}\), which is a compact subset of \(\mathcal O\). A solution that stays in a compact subset of the domain on a bounded interval extends beyond it [98, Corollary 2.15], so \(T_{\max}>T_\star\). This proves (i), (ii) and (iii).
□

SE.3 The main theorem

The actual spectral energy measure at macroscopic time \(\macrotime\) is \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)\defeq\chi(\Delta_{\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor})\).
Theorem SE.1 (Dynamical comparison)
Fix \(\macrostep>0\), \(\ridge>0\), \(\bmom\in[0,1)\), \(\gamma_\star<\infty\), \(C_\Sigma\ge1\), \(E_\star<\infty\), \(c_M<\infty\) and \(0<r_0\le r_1<\infty\). Along a sequence of dimensions with \(B\to\infty\) assume:
  1. (H1)
    Assumption S1 and Assumption S2 hold with deterministic covariances, \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\) and \(\norm{\Sigmain},\norm{\Sigmaout}\le C_\Sigma\);
  2. (H2)
    the algorithm of Definition SA.2 is run with momentum \(\bmom\), normalized ridge \(\ridge\) and step \(\lr_t=\macrostep/B\);
  3. (H3)
    \(W_0\) is deterministic, \(\norm{\Delta_0}_\F^2\le E_\star\), \(r_0\le\cR(0)\le r_1\), and \(\norm{\widehat A_0}\to0\);
  4. (H4)
    the initial buffer \(M^\circ_{-1}\) is independent of all batches and \(\EE\norm{M^\circ_{-1}}_\F^2\le c_MB\).
Let \(T_\star\) be given by (SE.10) and let \((\fQ_{ij})\) be the solution of (SE.7) of Proposition SE.1. Then the following hold.
  1. (i)
    With probability tending to one,
    \[ t_{\rm stop}>BT_\star,\qquad \inf_{t\le BT_\star}\cR(t)\ge r_0/2,\qquad \sup_{t\le BT_\star}\norm{\Dt}_\F^2\le E_\star+1. \]
  2. (ii)
    \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)}_\BL\to0\) in probability.
  3. (iii)
    \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\mu\lambda\,(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime))}_\BL\to0\) and \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\abs{\cR(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor)-\fRisk(\macrotime)}\to0\) in probability.
  4. (iv)
    On the event of (i), \(\sup_{t\le BT_\star}\norm{\Ahat}\to0\) in probability.
The statement is about aggregates. It compares the actual and deterministic spectral energy measures in a weak topology, and their risks; it does not assert that \(B^2\CMscr Q_{ij}(t)\) is close to \(B^2\fQ_{ij}(t/B)\) for every pair. The equations depend on the dimension through the coefficients and the initial data, and no convergence of these is assumed; Corollary SE.2 adds it.
Corollary SE.1 (Half-anisotropic input)
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem SE.1 with \(\Sigmain=\Id_{\Nin}\). Let \(\mathfrak d_i\defeq\mathfrak d(\mu_i,1)\) and let \(\mathfrak v_i\defeq\mathfrak v_i(\ridge)\) be the row coefficient of (SD.75). Then the row equations
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.13} \frac{\dif}{\dif\macrotime}\big(B\fQ_i(\macrotime)\big)=-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_i}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\macrotime)}}\,B\fQ_i(\macrotime)+\macrostep^2\mathfrak v_i,\qquad \fRisk(\macrotime)=\frac12\sum_i\mu_i\fQ_i(\macrotime),\qquad \fQ_i(0)=\norm{\Delta_0^\T u_i}^2, \end{equation}
have a unique solution on \([0,T_\star]\), given by \(\fQ_i=\sum_j\fQ_{ij}\) with \((\fQ_{ij})\) the solution of (SE.7). The row measures \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)\defeq\sum_i\CMscr Q_i(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor)\,\delta_{\mu_i}\) and \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)\defeq\sum_i\fQ_i(\macrotime)\,\delta_{\mu_i}\) on \([0,C_\Sigma]\) satisfy \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}_{\mathrm{row}}(\macrotime)}_\BL\to0\) in probability, and the same holds for the measures weighted by \(\mu\).
Dividing (SE.13) by \(B\) gives \(\dif\fQ_i/\dif\macrotime=-2\macrostep\mathfrak d_i\fQ_i/\sqrt{\fRisk}+\macrostep^2\mathfrak v_i/B\), which is the row recursion (14) with \(B\) steps per unit of \(\macrotime\). The kernel \(\mathfrak d_i\) is the common value of the \(\mathfrak d_{ij}\) (Corollary SC.2), and the volatility \(\mathfrak v_i=\sum_j\mathfrak v_{ij}\) is their sum (Proposition SD.4).
Corollary SE.2 (Continuum equation)
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem SE.1, and write \(\mathfrak d_B\), \(\zeta_B\), \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_B\), \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}_B\) for the objects at the dimension indexed by \(B\).
  1. (i)
    Every sequence of dimensions has a subsequence along which \(\mathfrak d_B\to\mathfrak d_\infty\) uniformly on \(\mathbb K\), \(\zeta_B\to\zeta_\infty\) weakly and \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_B(0)\to\chi_0\) weakly.
  2. (ii)
    Along any such subsequence, the measure equation
    \begin{equation}\tag{SE.14} \chi(\macrotime)=\chi_0+\int_0^\macrotime\Big(-\frac{2\macrostep\,\mathfrak d_\infty\,\chi(\sigma)}{\sqrt{\fRisk(\chi(\sigma))}}+\macrostep^2\zeta_\infty\Big)\dif\sigma \end{equation}
    has a unique solution \(\chi\) on \([0,T_\star]\) among paths with values in \(\mathfrak M\) that are continuous for the bounded-Lipschitz norm, and
    \[ \sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}_B(\macrotime)-\chi(\macrotime)}_\BL\to0,\qquad \sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_B(\macrotime)-\chi(\macrotime)}_\BL\to0\ \text{in probability}. \]
  3. (iii)
    Along any such subsequence, \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\abs{\cR(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor)-\fRisk(\chi(\macrotime))}\to0\) in probability.
Convergence of the forcing measures with the dimension is not proved in the volatility section; it is a hypothesis of (ii), and (i) says that it always holds along a subsequence. If the three sequences converge along the whole sequence of dimensions, the conclusions of (ii) and (iii) hold along it.
Remark SE.1 (Scope)
Theorem SE.1 is a statement about spectral aggregates in a weak topology; it makes no claim about the accuracy of an individual random mode or row. It is proved at a fixed normalized ridge \(\ridge>0\), which is the convention of Definition SA.2; it covers a fixed physical ridge only on the time ranges described in Remark SA.1, and it says nothing about the endpoint \(\ridge=0\), the exact SignSVD map. It holds on the interval \([0,T_\star]\), whose length is fixed by the initial risk, energy and the coefficient bounds, and not for all times. It concerns the regularized map \(\Qridge\ridge\) itself; no statement is made about a Newton–Schulz approximation of it.
Remark SE.2 (No momentum)
At \(\bmom=0\) the buffer is the current gradient ((SA.16)), the initial buffer plays no role, the drift kernel is that of Remark SC.1 with \(\Lw=\Jfun\), and the forcing measure is the same-time measure \(\zeta=\overline{\mathfrak C}_0(\ridge,\ridge)\) of Remark SD.3. Theorem SE.1 then compares the regularized SignSVD algorithm with (6) for these kernels. The proof below applies without change; every factor \(\bmom^\ell\) with \(\ell\ge1\) is zero.
Organization of the proof. Section SE.4 defines the frozen chains attached to a state and collects finite estimates on the regularized map. SE.4 Frozen chains and finite filter estimates

Definition SE.2 (Frozen chains)
Let \(t\ge0\), let \(D,m\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) with \(\cR(D)>0\), and let \(G^\ast_j[\cdot]\), \(j\ge0\), be the gradients (SE.1) of auxiliary batches. The stationary chain at \((t,D)\) is
\[ N_{-1}\defeq\sum_{j\ge0}\emaw j\,G^\ast_j[D],\qquad N_r\defeq\bmom N_{r-1}+(1-\bmom)\,G^\circ_{t+r+1}[D],\qquad V_r\defeq\Qridge{\ridgephys(D)}(N_r), \]
for \(r\ge0\). The same-buffer chain at \((t,D,m)\) is
\[ \widehat N_{-1}\defeq m,\qquad \widehat N_r\defeq\bmom\widehat N_{r-1}+(1-\bmom)\,G^\circ_{t+r+1}[D],\qquad \widehat V_r\defeq\Qridge{\ridgephys(D)}(\widehat N_r)\qquad(r\ge0). \]
At a step \(t<t_{\rm stop}\) of the algorithm, the chains at time \(t\) are those with \(D=\Dt\) and \(m=M^\circ_{t-1}\). The mean frozen update at \(D\) is \(\overline V(D)\defeq\EE V_0\).
Both chains use the actual batches of the steps \(t,t+1,\dots\); the stationary chain replaces the actual past by an auxiliary past computed at the state \(D\), and the same-buffer chain keeps the actual buffer.
Lemma SE.2 (Laws of the frozen chains)
Let \(D,m\) be deterministic with \(\cR(D)>0\), and put \(C_M\defeq C_\Sigma^2(\gamma_\star+2)^2\).
  1. (i)
    The sequence \((N_r/\sqrt{2\cR(D)})_{r\ge-1}\) has the law of the buffers \((M_{t'})_{t'\ge t-1}\) of the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3 at the normalized state \(\widehat A(D)\), with the samples indexed by time, and \(V_r=\Qridge\ridge(N_r/\sqrt{2\cR(D)})\) is its update. In particular \((V_r)_{r\ge0}\) is stationary, \(\overline V(D)\) is the expected frozen update \(\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\) at \(\widehat A(D)\), and the lag covariances, the integrated covariance measure \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{int}}(\ridge)\) and the finite-time measures \(\mathfrak F_T(\ridge)\) of the volatility section at the state \(\widehat A(D)\) are those of \((V_r)\).
  2. (ii)
    For \(r\ge-1\), \(\widehat N_r-N_r=\bmom^{r+1}(m-N_{-1})\).
  3. (iii)
    \(\EE\norm{N_{-1}}_\F^2\le2\cR(D)\,C_MB\).
  4. (iv)
    At a step \(t<t_{\rm stop}\), the statements (i)–(iii) hold conditionally on \(\cF_t\) with \(D=\Dt\) and \(m=M^\circ_{t-1}\).
Proof
Proof.
For (i), write \(d_a=z_a^\T\widehat A(D)w_a\). By (SE.1), \(G^\circ_{t+1}[D]=\sqrt{2\cR(D)}\,G_{t+1}\), where \(G_{t+1}\) is the batch (SA.26) built from the same samples at the normalized state \(\widehat A(D)\). The auxiliary batches and the batches of the steps \(t,t+1,\dots\) are independent, so \(N_r/\sqrt{2\cR(D)}\) is the time-indexed buffer of the frozen-state model. By Proposition SA.3(ii), \(\Qridge{\ridgephys(D)}(N_r)=\Qridge\ridge(N_r/\sqrt{2\cR(D)})\). Stationarity is Lemma SA.5(ii), and the covariances of the volatility section are defined from these updates in Definition SD.1 and Theorem SD.8(iv).
Part (ii) follows by subtracting the two recursions: the difference satisfies \(\widehat N_r-N_r=\bmom(\widehat N_{r-1}-N_{r-1})\) and starts from \(m-N_{-1}\), as in the proof of Proposition SA.6. Part (iii) is (i) together with the bound \(B^{-1}\EE\norm{\Mt}_\F^2\le C_M\) of Lemma SD.3. Part (iv) holds because the batches of the steps \(t,t+1,\dots\) and the auxiliary batches are independent of the past.

Lemma SE.3 (Moments of one batch)
Let \(E\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic, put \(A_E\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}E\Sigmain^{1/2}\) and \(d_a\defeq z_a^\T A_Ew_a\) for the samples of one batch, and let \(G^\circ[E]\) be the gradient (SE.1) of that batch.
  1. (i)
    \(\EE\norm{G^\circ[E]}_\F^2\le C_MB\norm{A_E}_\F^2\).
  2. (ii)
    \(\big(\EE\norm{G^\circ[E]}^2\big)^{1/2}\le C\log(2B)\norm{A_E}_\F\) and \(\big\|\max_{a\le B}\abs{d_a}\big\|_6\le C\log(2B)\norm{A_E}_\F\).
  3. (iii)
    The sample \(X_a\defeq B^{-1}d_a\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z_a\tensor w_a)\Sigmain^{1/2}\) has rank one and \((\EE\norm{X_a}_*^2)^{1/2}\le7C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\norm{A_E}_\F\).
The same bounds hold conditionally on \(\cF_t\) for the gradient \(G^\circ_{t+1}[E]\) of the batch of step \(t\) at a matrix \(E\) determined by the past \(\cF_t\).
Proof
Proof.
All three bounds are homogeneous of degree one in \(E\), so we may assume \(\norm{A_E}_\F=1\); then \(E\neq0\), \(\widehat A(E)=A_E\) and \(G^\circ[E]\) is the normalized batch of (SA.10) at the state \(E\), up to the factor \(\sqrt{2\cR(E)}=1\).
For (i), Lemma SA.4 and Lemma SA.6(iv) give
\[ \EE\norm{G^\circ[E]}_\F^2=\norm{\EE G^\circ[E]}_\F^2+\mathsf V_B\le C_\Sigma^2+\frac{C_\Sigma^2}{B}\big(\Nout\Nin+2\Nout+2\Nin+3\big)\le C_\Sigma^2(\gamma_\star+2)^2B . \]
For (ii), write \(Y\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}[z_1\cdots z_B]\) and \(X\defeq\Sigmain^{1/2}[w_1\cdots w_B]\), so that
\[ G^\circ[E]=\frac1B\,Y\diag(d_1,\dots,d_B)X^\T,\qquad \norm{G^\circ[E]}\le\frac1B\norm Y\norm X\max_a\abs{d_a}. \]
The matrix \([z_1\cdots z_B]\) has independent standard Gaussian entries, so by [100, Theorem 4.4.5] its operator norm exceeds \(C(\sqrt{\Nout}+\sqrt B+u)\) with probability at most \(2e^{-u^2}\); integrating this tail gives \(\norm{\norm Y}_6\le C\sqrt B\), and likewise \(\norm{\norm X}_6\le C\sqrt B\). For the residuals, Lemma SA.7(i) gives \(\norm{d_a}_{2p}\le((2p-1)!!)^{1/p}\le2p\) for every integer \(p\ge1\). Choosing \(2p\) to be the least even integer at least \(\max(6,\log B)\),
\[ \big\|\max_a\abs{d_a}\big\|_6\le\big\|\max_a\abs{d_a}\big\|_{2p}\le\Big(\sum_{a\le B}\EE\abs{d_a}^{2p}\Big)^{1/(2p)}\le B^{1/(2p)}\,2p\le C\log(2B). \]
Hölder’s inequality with the three exponents \(6\) gives
\[ \EE\norm{G^\circ[E]}^2\le\frac1{B^2}\norm{\norm Y}_6^2\norm{\norm X}_6^2\big\|\max_a\abs{d_a}\big\|_6^2\le C\log^2(2B). \]
For (iii), \(X_a=d_a\,\mathsf u_a\tensor\mathsf v_a\) with \(\mathsf u_a=\Sigmaout^{1/2}z_a/\sqrt B\) and \(\mathsf v_a=\Sigmain^{1/2}w_a/\sqrt B\) as in (SB.26), so \(\norm{X_a}_*=\abs{d_a}\norm{\mathsf u_a}\norm{\mathsf v_a}\), and Lemma SB.6 gives the bound. The conditional statement holds because the batch of step \(t\) is independent of the past.

The regularized map is controlled in three norms through the symmetric dilation \(\mathcal D(K)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&K\\K^\T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) of the proof of Lemma SA.2. We write \(\norm\cdot_{S_1}=\norm\cdot_*\) and \(\norm\cdot_{S_\infty}=\norm\cdot\), and \(\mathsf X_s(K)=(KK^\T+s\Id)^{-1}K\) as in (SB.10).
Lemma SE.4 (Schatten sensitivity of the filter)
Let \(K,K'\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), \(s>0\), \(\ridge'>0\) and \(p\in\{1,\infty\}\). Then
  1. (i)
    \(\norm{\mathsf X_s(K)-\mathsf X_s(K')}_{S_p}\le s^{-1}\norm{K-K'}_{S_p}\);
  2. (ii)
    \(\norm{\Qridge{\ridge'}(K)-\Qridge{\ridge'}(K')}_{S_p}\le\ridge'^{-1/2}\norm{K-K'}_{S_p}\);
  3. (iii)
    \(\norm{\partial_{\ridge'}\Qridge{\ridge'}(K)-\partial_{\ridge'}\Qridge{\ridge'}(K')}_*\le\frac12\ridge'^{-3/2}\norm{K-K'}_*\);
  4. (iv)
    if \(\ridge_1,\ridge_2\ge\ridge_\circ>0\), then \(\norm{\Qridge{\ridge_1}(K)-\Qridge{\ridge_2}(K)}\le\abs{\ridge_1-\ridge_2}/(2\ridge_\circ)\) and the same bound multiplied by \(\sqrt{\min(\Nout,\Nin)}\) holds in Frobenius norm.
Proof
Proof.
The mixed resolvent is a block of a resolvent of the dilation. For \(c>0\), \(\mathcal D(K)^2=\diag(KK^\T,K^\T K)\), and by Lemma SB.3
\[ \mathcal D(K)\big(\mathcal D(K)^2+c^2\Id\big)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathsf X_{c^2}(K)\\ \mathsf X_{c^2}(K)^\T&0\end{pmatrix}. \]
For a real symmetric \(T\) the left side is a sum of two resolvents,
\[ T(T^2+c^2\Id)^{-1}=\frac12\Big[(T-ic\Id)^{-1}+(T+ic\Id)^{-1}\Big], \]
each of operator norm at most \(c^{-1}\). The resolvent identity \((T-ic)^{-1}-(S-ic)^{-1}=(T-ic)^{-1}(S-T)(S-ic)^{-1}\) and the ideal property \(\norm{XYZ}_{S_p}\le\norm X\norm Y_{S_p}\norm Z\) [81, Proposition IV.2.4 and (IV.40)] give, for symmetric \(T,S\),
\[ \norm{T(T^2+c^2)^{-1}-S(S^2+c^2)^{-1}}_{S_p}\le c^{-2}\norm{T-S}_{S_p}. \]
A matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&Y\\Y^\T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has the singular values of \(Y\), each twice, so its \(S_p\) norm is \(2^{1/p}\norm Y_{S_p}\), with \(2^{1/\infty}=1\). Applying the last display with \(T=\mathcal D(K)\), \(S=\mathcal D(K')\) and \(c^2=s\), the factor \(2^{1/p}\) appears on both sides and cancels, which proves (i).
Part (ii) integrates (i). By Lemma SA.3, \(\Qridge{\ridge'}(K)=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\mathsf X_{\ridge'+u^2}(K)\,\dif u\), so
\[ \norm{\Qridge{\ridge'}(K)-\Qridge{\ridge'}(K')}_{S_p}\le\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{\dif u}{\ridge'+u^2}\,\norm{K-K'}_{S_p}=\ridge'^{-1/2}\norm{K-K'}_{S_p}. \]
Part (iii) is the same argument for the derivative in the ridge. For a symmetric \(T\), partial fractions give
\[ -T(T^2+c^2)^{-2}=\frac{i}{4c}\Big[(T-ic)^{-2}-(T+ic)^{-2}\Big], \]
and \((T-ic)^{-2}-(S-ic)^{-2}=(T-ic)^{-1}\big[(T-ic)^{-1}-(S-ic)^{-1}\big]+\big[(T-ic)^{-1}-(S-ic)^{-1}\big](S-ic)^{-1}\) has nuclear norm at most \(2c^{-3}\norm{T-S}_*\). Hence the difference of \(-T(T^2+c^2)^{-2}\) at \(T\) and \(S\) has nuclear norm at most \(c^{-4}\norm{T-S}_*\). The derivative of the integrand of Lemma SA.3 in the ridge is the upper right block of \(-\mathcal D(K)(\mathcal D(K)^2+(\ridge'+u^2))^{-2}\); with the block factor cancelled as before,
\[ \norm{\partial_{\ridge'}\Qridge{\ridge'}(K)-\partial_{\ridge'}\Qridge{\ridge'}(K')}_*\le\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{\dif u}{(\ridge'+u^2)^2}\,\norm{K-K'}_*=\frac{1}{2\ridge'^{3/2}}\norm{K-K'}_*. \]
Part (iv) is (SD.6) for the filter \(\omega_\ridge\) of (SD.2) together with the first two bounds of (SD.4).

Lemma SE.5 (Second-order filter estimates)
Let \(0<\ell_-\le\ell_+<\infty\). There is \(C\), depending only on \((\ell_-,\ell_+)\), such that for \(\ell\in[\ell_-,\ell_+]\) and all \(N,N',R\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\), with \(D\Qridge\ell(N)[R]\) the derivative of \(\Qridge\ell\) at \(N\) in the direction \(R\):
  1. (i)
    \(\norm{D\Qridge\ell(N)[R]}_\F\le\ell^{-1/2}\norm R_\F\);
  2. (ii)
    \(\norm{\Qridge\ell(N+R)-\Qridge\ell(N)-D\Qridge\ell(N)[R]}_\F\le C\norm R\norm R_\F\);
  3. (iii)
    \(\norm{D\Qridge\ell(N)[R]-D\Qridge\ell(N')[R]}_\F\le C\norm{N-N'}\norm R_\F\);
  4. (iv)
    \(\norm{\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N)}\le C\), \(\norm{\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N)}_\F+\norm{\partial_\ell^2\Qridge\ell(N)}_\F\le C\sqrt{\min(\Nout,\Nin)}\), and \(\norm{\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N)-\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N')}_\F\le C\norm{N-N'}_\F\).
The map \((N,\ell)\mapsto\Qridge\ell(N)\) is smooth on \(\ell>0\), by the integral representation of Lemma SA.3, so the derivatives in the statement exist.
Proof
Proof.
Part (i) is the Lipschitz estimate (SA.13) read as a bound on directional derivatives.
Parts (ii) and (iii) come from the second derivative of the dilated resolvents. By the proof of Lemma SE.4, \(\Qridge\ell(N)\) is the upper right block of
\[ f_\ell(\mathcal D(N))=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\Big[(\mathcal D(N)-ia\Id)^{-1}+(\mathcal D(N)+ia\Id)^{-1}\Big]\dif u,\qquad a=\sqrt{\ell+u^2}. \]
For \(\mathsf G=(H\mp ia)^{-1}\) with \(H\) symmetric, the second derivative in the directions \(H_1,H_2\) is \(\mathsf GH_1\mathsf GH_2\mathsf G+\mathsf GH_2\mathsf GH_1\mathsf G\), whose Frobenius norm is at most \(2a^{-3}\norm{H_1}\norm{H_2}_\F\). Since \(\norm{\mathcal D(R)}=\norm R\), \(\norm{\mathcal D(R)}_\F=\sqrt2\norm R_\F\) and \(\int_0^\infty(\ell+u^2)^{-3/2}\dif u=\ell^{-1}\), the second derivative of \(\Qridge\ell\) satisfies
\[ \norm{D^2\Qridge\ell(N)[R_1,R_2]}_\F\le\frac{4\sqrt2}{\pi\ell}\norm{R_1}\norm{R_2}_\F . \]
Taylor’s formula with integral remainder along \(N+\vartheta R\), \(\vartheta\in[0,1]\), gives (ii), and the same formula for \(\vartheta\mapsto D\Qridge\ell(N'+\vartheta(N-N'))[R]\) gives (iii).
Part (iv) concerns singular-value functions. The ridge derivatives of \(\Qridge\ell=\omega_\ell^\sharp\) are \(\phi_1^\sharp\) and \(\phi_2^\sharp\) in the notation (SD.1), with the odd functions
\[ \phi_1(x)=-\frac{x}{2(x^2+\ell)^{3/2}},\qquad \phi_2(x)=\frac{3x}{4(x^2+\ell)^{5/2}}, \]
which are bounded by constants depending on \(\ell_-\), and \(\phi_1\) has derivative \(\phi_1'(x)=(2x^2-\ell)/(2(x^2+\ell)^{5/2})\), bounded by \(\ell^{-3/2}\). Lemma SD.1 gives the three bounds.

The last finite tool bounds a covariance by single-coordinate sensitivities.
Lemma SE.6 (Covariance inequality)
Let \(F,G\) be square-integrable functions of countably many independent random coordinates \((\xi_l)_{l\ge1}\), and let \(F^{[l]}\), \(G^{[l]}\) denote \(F\), \(G\) with \(\xi_l\) replaced by an independent copy. Then
\[ \abs{\Cov(F,G)}\le\sum_{l\ge1}\norm{F-F^{[l]}}_2\,\norm{G-G^{[l]}}_2 . \]
Proof
Proof.
Let \(\mathcal A_l\) be generated by \(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_l\) and \(D_lF\defeq\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_l]-\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_{l-1}]\). By Lévy’s upward theorem [101, Theorem 14.2], \(\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_l]\to F\) in \(L^2\), so \(F-\EE F=\sum_lD_lF\) in \(L^2\), and likewise for \(G\). Distinct martingale differences are orthogonal, so \(\Cov(F,G)=\sum_l\EE[D_lF\,D_lG]\). Since the copy of \(\xi_l\) is independent, \(\EE[F^{[l]}\mid\mathcal A_l]=\EE[F\mid\mathcal A_{l-1}]\), hence \(D_lF=\EE[F-F^{[l]}\mid\mathcal A_l]\) and \(\norm{D_lF}_2\le\norm{F-F^{[l]}}_2\) by Jensen’s inequality. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to each term proves the claim.

SE.5 Finite mean and susceptibility of the frozen update

The drift section identifies the expected frozen update for diffuse states. Along the algorithm, diffuseness is a conclusion and not a hypothesis, so the comparison needs bounds on the expected update that hold for every normalized state. This subsection proves them. We work in the frozen-state model of Definition SA.3 with \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\), covariances of norm at most \(C_\Sigma\) and \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), and without any condition on \(\norm{\Ahat}\). We use the notation of the drift section: the samples \((\mathsf h_{ja},\mathsf u_{ja},\mathsf v_{ja})\) of (SB.26), the deleted buffers \(M_0^{(ja)}\), the coefficients \(\alpha_{1,ja},\alpha_{2,ja},\alpha_{3,ja}\), the conditioning \(\mathcal G_{ja}\), the expected mixed resolvent \(\mathcal H(s)=\EE\,\mathsf X_s(\Mt)\) and its deleted versions \(\mathcal H_j(s)\), the quantities \(\phi_j,b_j,\mathcal C_j\) of (SC.26), and the expected resolvents and traces \(\mathcal P(s),\mathcal P_j(s),\sigma_B(s),\tilde\sigma_B(s),q_B(s)\) of (SB.30).
For the derivative in the ridge we also need complex spectral parameters. Let
\[ \mathbb S\defeq\{z\in\mathbb C:\ x\defeq\Ree z>0,\ \abs{\Img z}\le x/2\}, \]
and for \(z\in\mathbb S\) and real \(K\) put \(R_z(K)\defeq(KK^\T+z\Id)^{-1}\), \(\widetilde R_z(K)\defeq(K^\T K+z\Id)^{-1}\), \(\mathsf X_z(K)\defeq R_z(K)K\) and \(\mathcal H(z)\defeq\EE\,\mathsf X_z(\Mt)\); at real \(z=s\) these are the objects of (SB.10).
Lemma SE.7 (Finite mean and susceptibility)
In the frozen-state model with \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\), \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\) and \(\max(\Nin,\Nout)\le\gamma_\star B\), and with no condition on \(\norm{\Ahat}\):
  1. (i)
    for every compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\) there is \(C_I\) with \(\norm{\mathcal H(z)}_\F\le C_I\) for \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(\Ree z\in I\), and \(\norm{\mathcal H(z)}_\F\le C/\Ree z\) for \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(\Ree z\ge1\);
  2. (ii)
    for real \(s\), \(\norm{\frac{\dif}{\dif s}\mathcal H(s)}_\F\le C_I\) for \(s\in I\) and \(\norm{\frac{\dif}{\dif s}\mathcal H(s)}_\F\le C/s^2\) for \(s\ge2\);
  3. (iii)
    for \(0<\ridge_-\le\ridge'\le\ridge_+\), \(\norm{\EE\,\Qridge{\ridge'}(\Mt)}_\F+\norm{\EE\,\partial_{\ridge'}\Qridge{\ridge'}(\Mt)}_\F\le C\), with \(C\) depending also on \((\ridge_-,\ridge_+)\);
  4. (iv)
    for the fixed ridge \(\ridge\) there are \(C\) and a sequence \(r_B\to0\), depending only on \((\ridge,\gamma_\star,C_\Sigma)\) and on the sequence of dimensions, such that \(\norm{\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)}\le C\norm{\Ahat}+r_B\) for every normalized state.
The constants depend only on \((\gamma_\star,C_\Sigma)\) and on \(I\) where indicated, not on \(\Ahat\), on the dimension or on the weights within the class (SB.27). Consequently, for the stationary chain of Definition SE.2 at a deterministic \(D\) with \(r_-\le\cR(D)\le r_+\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.15} \begin{gathered} \norm{\overline V(D)}_\F\le C,\qquad \big\|\EE\,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_0)\big|_{\ell=\ridgephys(D)}\big\|_\F\le C,\\ \norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\overline V(D)\Sigmain^{1/2}}\le C\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}D\Sigmain^{1/2}}+Cr_B , \end{gathered} \end{equation}
with \(C\) depending also on \((r_-,r_+)\).
Part (i) is Theorem SC.1(ii) on the sector \(\mathbb S\), which is what the Cauchy estimate of (ii) requires; part (iv) is the operator-norm statement on which the persistence of diffuseness rests.
Proof
Proof.
(i). By Theorem SC.1(ii), in the form (SC.7), for every \(s_\circ>0\) there is \(C_\circ\) depending only on \((s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\) with \(\norm{\mathcal H(z)}_\F\le C_\circ/\Ree z\) for \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(\Ree z\ge s_\circ\), in every frozen-state model with the stated hypotheses and weights in the class (SB.27). The case \(s_\circ=1\) is the second bound of (i), and the case \(s_\circ=\min I\) gives the first with \(C_I=C_\circ/\min I\).
(ii). At fixed dimension \(z\mapsto\mathcal H(z)\) is holomorphic on \(\Ree z>0\), since \(z\mapsto\mathsf X_z(\Mt)\) is. For real \(s>0\) the circle \(\abs{\zeta-s}=s/4\) lies in \(\mathbb S\), because on it \(\Ree\zeta\ge3s/4\) and \(\abs{\Img\zeta}\le s/4\le\Ree\zeta/3\). Cauchy’s estimate for the derivative gives
\[ \Big\|\frac{\dif}{\dif s}\mathcal H(s)\Big\|_\F\le\frac4s\max_{\abs{\zeta-s}=s/4}\norm{\mathcal H(\zeta)}_\F . \]
For \(s\ge2\) every point of the circle has real part at least \(3s/4\ge1\), so (i) bounds the maximum by \(4C/(3s)\), which gives \(C/s^2\). For \(s\in I\) the circle has real parts in the compact \([\frac34\min I,\frac54\max I]\), and (i) gives \(C_I\).
(iii). By Lemma SA.3 and differentiation of its integrand in the ridge,
\[ \EE\,\Qridge{\ridge'}(\Mt)=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\mathcal H(\ridge'+u^2)\,\dif u,\qquad \EE\,\partial_{\ridge'}\Qridge{\ridge'}(\Mt)=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{\dif\mathcal H}{\dif s}(\ridge'+u^2)\,\dif u . \]
Split both integrals at \(u=2\). On \([0,2]\) the parameter lies in \(I\defeq[\ridge_-,\ridge_++4]\), where (i) and (ii) give \(C_I\); on \([2,\infty)\) it is at least \(u^2\ge4\), where they give \(C/u^2\) and \(C/u^4\). Both integrals are therefore bounded.
(iv). We work on the real axis. The targets of Lemma SB.12 are centered at the expected traces \((\sigma_B,\tilde\sigma_B,q_B)\), and its sequence \(e_B(I)\) depends only on \((I,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\), so it serves for every normalized state, every dimension and all weights in the class. For a sample of age \(j\), with \(\check x_j=\emaw j^2q_B\) and the targets \(\check\alpha_{1,j},\check\alpha_{2,j}\) of (SB.33), put
\[ \mathsf e^{(1)}_B(s)\defeq\sup_j\frac{\norm{\alpha_{1,j}-\check\alpha_{1,j}}_2}{\emaw j},\qquad \mathsf e^{(2)}_B(s)\defeq\sup_j\frac{\norm{\alpha_{2,j}-\check\alpha_{2,j}}_2}{\emaw j^2}. \]
Then \(\sup_{s\in I}(\mathsf e^{(1)}_B+\mathsf e^{(2)}_B)\le2e_B(I)\) on every compact \(I\), and for \(s\ge1\), by Lemma SC.2, \(\abs{f_x(d)}\le\abs d\), \(0\le\psi_x(d)\le d^2\) and \(\sigma_B\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star/s\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.16} \mathsf e^{(1)}_B(s)\le C,\qquad\mathsf e^{(2)}_B(s)\le\frac Cs . \end{equation}
The proof of Proposition SC.3, run with these targets and with the expected deleted resolvent \(\mathcal P_j(s)\) kept in place of \(\Rbar(-s)\), gives an equation for \(\mathcal H(s)\) in which every error is displayed with its dependence on \(\norm{\Ahat}\). In the source term, the replacement of \(\alpha_{1,j}\) costs \(C_\Sigma\emaw j\mathsf e^{(1)}_B/s\), and (SB.22) with \(\norm{f_x'''}_\infty\le36\check x_j\le36q_B\) (Lemma SC.7) costs \(36C_\Sigma\emaw jq_B\norm{\Ahat}^2/s\). In the Gram term, the centered part costs \(C\emaw j^2(\mathsf e^{(2)}_B+6\sigma_B\norm{\Ahat})/\sqrt s\), by Lemma SB.7 and (SB.23) with \(\norm{\psi''}_\infty\le6\). The deletion mean and covariance terms cost \(C\emaw j^3/(s^2\sqrt B)\) and \(C\emaw j^2/(s^{3/2}\sqrt B)\), by (SC.21) and Lemma SC.5. Summing over \(j\), with \(\jmath_j\defeq\EE f'_{\check x_j}(d_j)\in[-1,1]\) and \(\mathsf Y_B(s)=\Id+b_B(s)\Sigmain\) as before,
\[ \mathcal H(s)\,\mathsf Y_B(s)=\sum_j\emaw j\,\jmath_j\,\mathcal P_j(s)\,\EE\Gt+E_1(s), \]
\[ \norm{E_1(s)}_\F\le C\Big[\frac{\mathsf e^{(1)}_B(s)}{s}+\frac{q_B(s)\norm{\Ahat}^2}{s}+\frac{\mathsf e^{(2)}_B(s)+\sigma_B(s)\norm{\Ahat}}{\sqrt s}+\frac{1}{s\sqrt B}\Big]\qquad(s\ge\ridge), \]
with \(C\) depending on \(\ridge\). The source has small operator norm: since \(\norm{\mathcal P_j(s)}\le1/s\), \(\abs{\jmath_j}\le1\), \(\sum_j\emaw j\le1\) and \(\norm{\EE\Gt}=\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Ahat\Sigmain^{1/2}}\le C_\Sigma\norm{\Ahat}\), its operator norm is at most \(C_\Sigma\norm{\Ahat}/s\). Since \(b_B(s)\ge0\), \(\norm{\mathsf Y_B(s)^{-1}}\le1\), and with \(q_B,\sigma_B\le C/s\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.17} \norm{\mathcal H(s)}\le C\Big(\frac1s+\frac1{s^{3/2}}+\frac1{s^2}\Big)\norm{\Ahat}+\mathsf e_B(s),\qquad \mathsf e_B(s)\defeq C\Big[\frac{\mathsf e^{(1)}_B(s)}{s}+\frac{\mathsf e^{(2)}_B(s)}{\sqrt s}+\frac{1}{s\sqrt B}\Big], \end{equation}
for \(s\ge\ridge\). Here \(\norm{\Ahat}^2\le\norm{\Ahat}\) was used. The error \(\mathsf e_B\) tends to zero uniformly on every compact interval and over all normalized states, and \(\mathsf e_B(s)\le C/s\) for \(s\ge1\) by (SE.16).
The operator norm of the expected update is the integral of (SE.17). Splitting at a cutoff \(\mathsf R\ge1\),
\[ \norm{\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)}\le\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\norm{\mathcal H(\ridge+u^2)}\,\dif u \le C\norm{\Ahat}+\frac2\pi\Big[\mathsf R\sup_{s\in[\ridge,\ridge+\mathsf R^2]}\mathsf e_B(s)+\frac{C}{\mathsf R}\Big]. \]
Let \(r_B\) be the infimum over \(\mathsf R\ge1\) of the bracket. It does not depend on the state, and it tends to zero: for fixed \(\mathsf R\) the first term vanishes as the dimension grows, and the second is small for large \(\mathsf R\). This proves (iv).

SE.6 Causal response of the frozen chain

Over a block, the algorithm differs from the frozen chain at the start of the block because its state moves by a quantity of order the step. This subsection computes the first-order effect. The expansion is causal: the gradient of a batch acts on updates computed from strictly earlier batches, so the target batch can be deleted from the buffer while the direction in which the buffer is differentiated stays independent of it.
The tangent. Fix \(t\ge0\), a deterministic \(D\) with \(\cR(D)>0\) and an initial buffer \(m_0\), which will be either the auxiliary buffer \(N_{-1}\) or a matrix \(m\). For \(\varepsilon\ge0\) consider the chain
\[ \begin{gathered} \Delta^\varepsilon_0=D,\qquad M^\varepsilon_{-1}=m_0,\qquad M^\varepsilon_r=\bmom M^\varepsilon_{r-1}+(1-\bmom)\,G^\circ_{t+r+1}[\Delta^\varepsilon_r],\\ {\mathsf S}^\varepsilon_r=\Qridge{\ridgephys(\Delta^\varepsilon_r)}(M^\varepsilon_r),\qquad \Delta^\varepsilon_{r+1}=\Delta^\varepsilon_r-\varepsilon {\mathsf S}^\varepsilon_r . \end{gathered} \]
For each \(r\) it is defined for all \(\varepsilon\) small enough that the risks \(\cR(\Delta^\varepsilon_k)\), \(k\le r\), stay positive. At \(\varepsilon=0\) the state is \(D\) at every time, and the buffers and updates are those of the frozen chain with initial buffer \(m_0\), which we write \(N^{m_0}_r\) and \(V^{m_0}_r\): the stationary chain of Definition SE.2 for \(m_0=N_{-1}\), and the same-buffer chain for \(m_0=m\). At \(\varepsilon=\lr\), \(D=\Dt\) and \(m_0=M^\circ_{t-1}\) it is the algorithm from step \(t\). We write \(\dot\Delta_r,\dot M_r,\dot {\mathsf S}_r\) for the derivatives at \(\varepsilon=0\), and \(\dot V_r\), respectively \(\dot{\widehat V}_r\), for \(\dot {\mathsf S}_r\) when \(m_0=N_{-1}\), respectively \(m_0=m\).

Proof.

Proposition SE.2 (Causal resolvent reduction)

\[ \Big|\EE\big\langle\Pi,D\mathsf X_s(N_r)\big[\varpi G^\circ_{t+j+1}[E]\big]\big\rangle+\frac{\varpi^2}{B}\sum_{a\le B}\EE\big[F_s(T_a)\,g(E)\big]\Big|\le\frac{C\sqrt B\,\varpi}{s}, \]

Proof.

The norms used are: \(\norm{R_0},\norm{\widetilde R_0}\le1/s\) and \(\norm{\mathsf X_0}\le(2\sqrt s)^{-1}\) (Lemma SB.3); \(\norm e_{p,*}\le C_p\norm{C_E}_\F\le C_pC_\Sigma c_E\sqrt B\); and the Bessel bounds (SC.22), which give \(C\norm\cdot_{2,*}/\sqrt B\) for the quadratic structures \(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf u\), \(\mathsf v\tensor\mathsf v\) and \(C\norm\cdot_{2,*}/B\) for the mixed ones.
Coefficient errors. Replace \(\partial_d\alpha_1,\partial_d\alpha_2,\partial_d\alpha_3\) by \(\varpi f_x'(d)\), \(\varpi^2\sigma\psi_x'(d)\), \(-s\varpi^2\tilde\sigma\psi_x'(d)\). By Lemma SE.10 and Hölder’s inequality, the errors multiplied by \(e\) have conditional \(L^2\) norms at most \(C\varpi\), \(C\varpi^2/s\) and \(C\varpi^2\). With the exterior norms, the four error matrices have Frobenius norms at most
\[ \frac1s\cdot\frac1{2\sqrt s}\cdot\frac{C\varpi^2}{\sqrt B},\qquad s\cdot\frac1{s^2}\cdot\frac{C\varpi}{B},\qquad \frac1{4s}\cdot\frac{C\varpi}{B},\qquad s\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt s}\cdot\frac1s\cdot\frac{C\varpi^2}{s\sqrt B}, \]
and summing over the \(B\) samples gives at most \(C(\varpi^2\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}+\varpi s^{-1})\le C\sqrt B\varpi/s\), using \(\varpi^2\le\varpi\) and \(s^{-3/2}\le s_\circ^{-1/2}s^{-1}\).
Mixed and centered target terms. The mixed terms carry \(\varpi f_x'(d)e\), with conditional \(L^2\) norm at most \(\varpi\norm e_{2,*}\le C\varpi\sqrt B\) since \(\abs{f_x'}\le1\); each has Frobenius norm at most \(C\varpi\sqrt B/(sB)\), and the \(B\) samples give \(C\sqrt B\varpi/s\). In the quadratic terms we split \(\mathsf u\tensor\mathsf u=B^{-1}\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z\tensor z-\Id)\Sigmaout^{1/2}+B^{-1}\Sigmaout\) and \(\mathsf v\tensor\mathsf v=B^{-1}\Sigmain^{1/2}(w\tensor w-\Id)\Sigmain^{1/2}+B^{-1}\Sigmain\). The centered parts are bounded by Lemma SB.7: the scalars \(s\varpi^2\tilde\sigma\psi_x'(d)e\) and \(s\varpi^2\sigma\psi_x'(d)e\) have conditional \(L^2\) norms at most \(C\varpi^2\sqrt B\), since \(s\sigma,s\tilde\sigma\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\) and \(\abs{\psi_x'(d)}\le2\abs d\); each centered part has Frobenius norm at most \(C\varpi^2\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}/B\), and the \(B\) samples give \(C\varpi^2\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}\le C\sqrt B\varpi/s\).
Identity parts. What remains is, for each sample,
\[ -\frac{s\varpi^2}{B}\,\EE_*\big[e\,\psi_x'(d)\big]\Big\{\tilde\sigma\,R_0\Sigmaout\mathsf X_0+\sigma\,\mathsf X_0\Sigmain\widetilde R_0\Big\}. \]
By Lemma SE.11, \(\EE_*[e\psi_x'(d)]=g(E)b(x)+\rho\) with \(\abs\rho\le24xC_\Sigma c_E\delta\le C\). Pairing with \(\Pi\), the term with \(g(E)b(x)\) is \(-\varpi^2B^{-1}F_s(T)g(E)\). The term with \(\rho\) is at most \((s\varpi^2/B)\cdot C\cdot(\tilde\sigma+\sigma)\,c_\Pi\,C_\Sigma\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\,s^{-3/2}\), because \(\norm{R_0\Sigmaout\mathsf X_0}_\F,\norm{\mathsf X_0\Sigmain\widetilde R_0}_\F\le C_\Sigma\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\,s^{-3/2}/2\); with \(s\sigma,s\tilde\sigma\le C\) and the \(B\) samples this is at most \(C\varpi^2\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}\le C\sqrt B\varpi/s\).
Pairing the error matrices with \(\Pi\) costs the factor \(c_\Pi\). Taking expectations and summing the contributions proves the claim.

Lemma SE.12 (The scalar of the minor)
Let \(s\ge s_\circ>0\), \(\varpi\in[0,1]\), and let \(F_s(T)\) be defined by (SE.24) for an arbitrary real matrix \(T\), with its own traces. Then
\[ \abs{F_s(T)}\le C\sqrt B\,s^{-3/2},\qquad \abs{F_s(T)-F_s(T')}\le C s^{-2}\big(\pi_\Pi+B^{-1/2}\big)\norm{T-T'}_*, \]
with \(C\) depending only on \((s_\circ,c_\Pi,\gamma_\star,C_\Sigma)\).
Proof
Proof.
The size bound uses \(\abs b\le2\), \(s\sigma,s\tilde\sigma\le C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\) and \(\abs{\langle\Pi,R\Sigmaout\mathsf X\rangle}\le c_\Pi\norm{R\Sigmaout\mathsf X}_\F\le c_\Pi C_\Sigma\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\,s^{-3/2}/2\), and the same for the second pairing.
The sensitivity is obtained by changing one factor at a time; put \(E\defeq T-T'\). By Lemma SB.9, \(\norm{R_s(T)-R_s(T')}_*\le2s^{-3/2}\norm E_*\), the same for \(\widetilde R_s\), and by Lemma SE.4(i), \(\norm{\mathsf X_s(T)-\mathsf X_s(T')}_*\le s^{-1}\norm E_*\). With trace duality \(\abs{\Tr(XY)}\le\norm X\norm Y_*\),
\[ \big|\langle\Pi,R\Sigmaout\mathsf X\rangle-\langle\Pi,R'\Sigmaout\mathsf X'\rangle\big| \le\pi_\Pi C_\Sigma\Big(\frac{2}{s^{3/2}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt s}+\frac1s\cdot\frac1s\Big)\norm E_*=\frac{2\pi_\Pi C_\Sigma}{s^2}\norm E_*, \]
and the same for the second pairing; with the bounded prefactors this contributes \(C\pi_\Pi s^{-2}\norm E_*\). The traces satisfy \(\abs{\sigma-\sigma'}\le C_\Sigma B^{-1}\norm{R_s(T)-R_s(T')}_*\le2C_\Sigma B^{-1}s^{-3/2}\norm E_*\), and the same for \(\tilde\sigma\); multiplied by \(s\) and by the pairing of size \(C\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}\) this contributes \(CB^{-1/2}s^{-2}\norm E_*\). Finally \(\abs{x-x'}\le s(\sigma\abs{\tilde\sigma-\tilde\sigma'}+\tilde\sigma'\abs{\sigma-\sigma'})\le CB^{-1}s^{-3/2}\norm E_*\), so \(\abs{b(x)-b(x')}\le12\abs{x-x'}\) contributes \(s\cdot CB^{-1}s^{-3/2}\cdot Cs^{-1}\cdot C\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}\norm E_*=CB^{-1/2}s^{-3}\norm E_*\), which is at most \(Cs_\circ^{-1}B^{-1/2}s^{-2}\norm E_*\).

Proposition SE.3 (Scalar contraction)
In the setting of Proposition SE.2, let \(0\le k<j\) and \(g_k\defeq g(V_k)=\langle\Sigmaout D\Sigmain,V_k\rangle\). Then for every sample \(a\) of the batch of step \(t+j\),
\[ \big|\EE\big[F_s(T_a)\,g_k\big]\big|\le C\Big\{\sqrt B\,s^{-3/2}+\big(1+B\epsilon_\Pi\big)s^{-2}\bmom^{r-k}\Big\}, \]
with \(C\) depending only on \((s_\circ,c_\Pi,\ridge,\gamma_\star,C_\Sigma)\). No independence between \(V_k\) and the traces of the minor is used.
Proof
Proof.
The mean product is bounded by the finite mean. \(\abs{\EE g_k}\le\norm{\Sigmaout D\Sigmain}_\F\norm{\overline V(D)}_\F\le C\) by (SE.15), and \(\abs{\EE F_s(T_a)}\le C\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}\) by Lemma SE.12.
The covariance is bounded by Lemma SE.6, with the individual samples of all batches, the auxiliary ones included, as coordinates. A sample of the batch of relative time \(v\) enters \(N_k\) with weight \(\emaw{k-v}\) if \(v\le k\) and does not enter it if \(v>k\); it enters \(T_a\) with weight \(\emaw{r-v}\), except the target sample, which is absent from \(T_a\). Replacing it by an independent copy changes \(N_k\) and \(T_a\) by rank-two matrices with nuclear \(L^2\) norms at most \(14C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\emaw{k-v}\) and \(14C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\emaw{r-v}\), by Lemma SE.3(iii). By trace duality, Lemma SE.4(ii) and \(\norm{\Sigmaout D\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\delta\),
\[ \norm{g_k-g_k^{[l]}}_2\le C\delta\,\emaw{k-v},\qquad \norm{F_s(T_a)-F_s(T_a)^{[l]}}_2\le Cs^{-2}\big(\pi_\Pi+B^{-1/2}\big)\emaw{r-v}, \]
the second by Lemma SE.12. Only the samples with \(v\le k\) change both, and for them \(\emaw{r-v}=\bmom^{r-k}\emaw{k-v}\). With \(B\) samples per batch and \(\sum_v\emaw{k-v}^2\le\sw2\le1\),
\[ \abs{\Cov(F_s(T_a),g_k)}\le Cs^{-2}\big(B\delta\pi_\Pi+\sqrt B\delta\big)\bmom^{r-k}\le Cs^{-2}\big(1+B\epsilon_\Pi\big)\bmom^{r-k}, \]
using \(\sqrt B\delta\le\frac12(1+B\delta^2)\) and \(\delta\pi_\Pi+\delta^2=\epsilon_\Pi\). Adding the product of the means proves the claim.

Lemma SE.13 (Ridge susceptibility covariance)
Let \(D\) be deterministic with \(2\cR(D)=1\), consider the stationary chain at \((t,D)\) and let \(k,r\ge0\). Then
\[ \big|\EE\big[g_k\,\big\langle\Pi,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_r)\big|_{\ell=\ridge}\big\rangle\big]\big|\le C\big(1+B\delta\pi_\Pi\,\bmom^{\abs{r-k}}\big). \]
Proof
Proof.
The means are bounded by (SE.15): \(\abs{\EE g_k}\le C\) and
\[ \abs{\EE\langle\Pi,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_r)\rangle}\le c_\Pi\norm{\EE\,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_r)}_\F\le C. \]
For the covariance, a sample of relative time \(v\) changes \(\langle\Pi,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_r)\rangle\) by at most \(\pi_\Pi\frac12\ridge^{-3/2}\cdot14C_\Sigma\gamma_\star\emaw{r-v}\) in \(L^2\), by Lemma SE.4(iii) and trace duality, and changes \(g_k\) by at most \(C\delta\emaw{k-v}\) as in the previous proof. The shared samples are those with \(v\le\min(k,r)\), and Lemma SE.6 gives \(CB\delta\pi_\Pi\sum_{v\le\min(k,r)}\emaw{k-v}\emaw{r-v}\le CB\delta\pi_\Pi\bmom^{\abs{r-k}}\).

Proposition SE.4 (Stationary causal tangent)
Let \(D\) be deterministic with \(r_-\le\cR(D)\le r_+\), let \(\Pi\) be a test, and let \(\dot V_r\) be the tangent of Lemma SE.8 for the stationary chain at \((t,D)\), with its initial buffer held fixed. Then for \(r\ge0\),
\[ \lr\,\abs{\EE\langle\Pi,\dot V_r\rangle}\le C\Big\{\frac{r}{\sqrt B}+\frac1B+\epsilon_\Pi\Big\},\qquad \epsilon_\Pi=\delta(D)\big(\pi_\Pi+\delta(D)\big), \]
with \(C\) depending only on \((c_\Pi,r_-,r_+)\) and the fixed parameters.
Proof
Proof.
The statement reduces to \(2\cR(D)=1\). Put \(a\defeq\sqrt{2\cR(D)}\). Since \(G^\circ[\Delta]/a=G^\circ[\Delta/a]\), \(\ridgephys(\Delta)=a^2\ridgephys(\Delta/a)\) and \(\Qridge{a^2\ell}(aK)=\Qridge\ell(K)\), the chain of \((D,m_0)\) at step \(\varepsilon\) has the updates of the chain of \((D/a,m_0/a)\) at step \(\varepsilon/a\), and \(\lr\dot V_r(D)=(\lr/a)\dot V_r(D/a)\). The step \(\macrostep/a\) lies in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\) determined by \((r_-,r_+)\), and \(\delta(D/a)=\delta(D)\). We therefore assume \(2\cR(D)=1\).
By Lemma SE.8 and the integral representation \(D\Qridge\ridge(N)[R]=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty D\mathsf X_{\ridge+u^2}(N)[R]\,\dif u\),
\[ \begin{aligned} \EE\langle\Pi,\dot V_r\rangle={}&-\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\sum_{0\le k<j\le r}\EE\big\langle\Pi,D\mathsf X_{\ridge+u^2}(N_r)\big[\emaw{r-j}G^\circ_{t+j+1}[V_k]\big]\big\rangle\,\dif u\\ &-2\ridge\sum_{k<r}\EE\big[g_k\langle\Pi,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_r)\rangle\big]. \end{aligned} \]
The matrix part is bounded by the reduction and the scalar contraction. For \(k<j\) the direction \(V_k\) is determined by the auxiliary batches and the batches up to step \(t+k\), so the batch of step \(t+j\) is independent of \(V_k\) together with all other samples, with \(\norm{V_k}\le1\) and \(\norm{V_k}_\F\le\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\). By Proposition SE.2 and Proposition SE.3 with \(s_\circ=\ridge\),
\[ \big|\EE\big\langle\Pi,D\mathsf X_s(N_r)[\varpi G^\circ_{t+j+1}[V_k]]\big\rangle\big| \le\frac{C\sqrt B\varpi}{s}+C\varpi^2\Big\{\sqrt Bs^{-3/2}+(1+B\epsilon_\Pi)s^{-2}\bmom^{r-k}\Big\}, \]
with \(\varpi=\emaw{r-j}\). The integrals of \(s^{-1}\), \(s^{-3/2}\) and \(s^{-2}\) against \(\dif u\) at \(s=\ridge+u^2\) are finite. The causal sums are
\[ \sum_{j\le r}\emaw{r-j}\,j\le r,\qquad \sum_{j\le r}\emaw{r-j}^2\sum_{k<j}\bmom^{r-k}\le\frac{1}{1-\bmom}\sum_{j\le r}\emaw{r-j}^2\bmom^{r-j+1}\le1, \]
the second being zero at \(\bmom=0\). The matrix part is therefore at most \(C(\sqrt B\,r+1+B\epsilon_\Pi)\).
The ridge part is bounded by Lemma SE.13: its absolute value is at most \(C\sum_{k<r}(1+B\delta\pi_\Pi\bmom^{r-k})\le C(r+B\epsilon_\Pi)\).
Adding the two parts and multiplying by \(\lr=\macrostep/B\) proves the claim.

SE.7 Transport, concentration and diffuseness along the algorithm

From here on the hypotheses {{(H1)}}–{{(H4)}} of Theorem SE.1 are in force.
Blocks and admissible states. For \(0<r_-\le r_+\) and \(E<\infty\) let \(\mathcal K(r_-,r_+,E)\defeq\{\Delta:\ r_-\le\cR(\Delta)\le r_+,\ \norm\Delta_\F^2\le E\}\), and for \(\mathcal K=\mathcal K(r_-,r_+,E)\) let \(\mathcal K'\defeq\mathcal K(r_-/2,2r_+,2E+2)\). (\(C_0=1\) at \(\bmom=0\)), so that \(\bmom^{\mathsf q}\le(2B)^{-2}\), hence \(\sqrt B\bmom^{\mathsf q}\le1\) and \(\sqrt BL\bmom^{\mathsf q}\to0\), while \(\mathsf q\le L\) for large \(B\). The block starts are \(t_b\defeq bL\), \(b\ge0\). The admissible set for block starts and its enlargements are
\[ \mathcal A\defeq\mathcal K\Big(\frac{r_0}2,\,r_1+\frac12,\,E_\star+1\Big),\qquad \mathcal A^+\defeq\mathcal A',\qquad\mathcal A^{++}\defeq(\mathcal A^+)' , \]

Lemma SE.14 (Movement)
  1. (i)
    For \(t\le t'<t_{\rm stop}\), \(\norm{\Delta_{t'}-\Dt}\le\macrostep(t'-t)/B\), \(\norm{\Delta_{t'}-\Dt}_\F\le\macrostep\sqrt{\gamma_\star}\,(t'-t)/\sqrt B\) and \(\abs{\sqrt{2\cR(\Delta_{t'})}-\sqrt{2\cR(\Dt)}}\le C_\Sigma\macrostep\sqrt{\gamma_\star}\,(t'-t)/\sqrt B\).
  2. (ii)
    For every \(\mathcal K=\mathcal K(r_-,r_+,E)\) there is \(B_0\) such that for \(B\ge B_0\): if \(\Dt\in\mathcal K\), then \(t+4L<t_{\rm stop}\) and \(\Delta_{t'}\in\mathcal K'\) for all \(t'\ge0\) with \(\abs{t'-t}\le4L\).
Proof
Proof.
For (i), \(\Delta_{t'}-\Dt=-\lr\sum_{t\le u<t'}{\mathsf S}_u\), and \(\norm{{\mathsf S}_u}\le1\), \(\norm{{\mathsf S}_u}_\F\le\sqrt{\min(\Nout,\Nin)}\le\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\) by Lemma SA.2(i). The risk bound is the reverse triangle inequality for \(\sqrt{2\cR(\Delta)}=\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Delta\Sigmain^{1/2}}_\F\), with \(\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}E\Sigmain^{1/2}}_\F\le C_\Sigma\norm E_\F\).

Since \(\cR(\Dt)\ge r_->0\), \(t<t_{\rm stop}\), so the bounds of (i) hold between \(t'\) and \(t\) for \(t'<t\). Lemma SE.15 (Stopped buffer moments)
Let \(\mathcal K=\mathcal K(r_-,r_+,E)\), and let \(\tau\) be a time such that whether \(t<\tau\) is decided by the past \(\cF_t\), and such that \(\Dt\in\mathcal K\) for \(t<\tau\). Then for every \(t\ge0\),

Proof
Proof.

Lemma SE.16 (Coupling)
Let \(\mathcal K=\mathcal K(r_-,r_+,E)\) with \(B_0\) as in Lemma SE.14. At a step \(t\), on the event \(\{\Dt\in\mathcal K\}\), let the chains at time \(t\) be those of Definition SE.2 with \(D=\Dt\) and \(m=M^\circ_{t-1}\). Then for \(0\le r\le4L\):
  1. (i)

  2. (ii)
    \(\EE_t\norm{\widehat V_r-V_r}_\F^2\le C\bmom^{2(r+1)}(\norm m_\F^2+B)\) and \(\EE_t\norm{\widehat V_r-V_r}^2\le C\bmom^{2(r+1)}(\norm m^2+\log^2(2B))\);

    \(\EE_t\big\|\sum_{r<L}({\mathsf S}_{t+r}-V_r)\big\|\le C(\norm m+\log(2B))+CL^2\log(2B)/\sqrt B\).

Proof
Proof.
By Lemma SE.14(ii) the states \(\Delta_{t+r}\), \(r\le4L\), lie in \(\mathcal K'\), so the physical ridges are at least \(\ridge r_-\).

\[ M^\circ_{t+r}-\widehat N_r=\sum_{j\le r}\emaw{r-j}\,G^\circ_{t+j+1}\big[\Delta_{t+j}-\Dt\big]. \]
The matrix \(\Delta_{t+j}-\Dt\) is determined by the past and the batches before step \(t+j\), and its weighted Frobenius norm is at most \(C_\Sigma\macrostep\sqrt{\gamma_\star}j/\sqrt B\) by Lemma SE.14(i). \[ {\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\widehat V_r=\big[\Qridge{\ridgephys(\Delta_{t+r})}(M^\circ_{t+r})-\Qridge{\ridgephys(\Dt)}(M^\circ_{t+r})\big] +\big[\Qridge{\ridgephys(\Dt)}(M^\circ_{t+r})-\Qridge{\ridgephys(\Dt)}(\widehat N_r)\big]. \]

Lemma SE.17 (Stationary block sums and the startup cross term)
Let \(D\), \(m\) be deterministic with \(r_-\le\cR(D)\le r_+\), consider the chains at \((t,D,m)\), and put \(S\defeq\sum_{r<L}V_r\), \(A\defeq\sum_{r<L}(\widehat V_r-V_r)\) and \(a\defeq1+\norm m_\F/\sqrt B\). Then
  1. (i)
    \(\EE\norm{S-L\overline V(D)}_\F^2\le CBL\);
  2. (ii)
    for every linear map \(\mathcal W\) on \(\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) of Frobenius norm at most one, \(\abs{\EE\langle S,\mathcal WA\rangle}\le Ca\big(B+L\sqrt B\big)\).
Proof
Proof.
For (i), by Lemma SE.2(i) the lag covariances of \((V_r)\) are those of the volatility section at the state \(\widehat A(D)\). With the tests \(F=\Id\), \(G=\Id\) in Definition SD.1 and the envelopes of Theorem SD.2(ii),
\[ \EE\norm{S-L\overline V(D)}_\F^2=B\sum_{r,r'<L}C_{\abs{r-r'}}[\Id,\Id](\ridge,\ridge) \le BL\Big(\gamma_\star+\frac{2\mathsf K_\star\bmom}{1-\bmom}\Big). \]
For (ii), write \(A_r\defeq\widehat V_r-V_r\). By the proof of Lemma SE.16(ii), \(\norm{A_r}_{L^2(\F)}\le Ca\sqrt B\bmom^{r+1}\), where \(\norm X_{L^2(\F)}\defeq(\EE\norm X_\F^2)^{1/2}\). Expand \(\EE\langle S,\mathcal WA\rangle=\sum_{u,r<L}\EE\langle V_u,\mathcal WA_r\rangle\) and center \(V_u\) at \(\overline V\defeq\overline V(D)\). The mean part is at most \(\sum_{u,r}\norm{\overline V}_\F\norm{A_r}_{L^2(\F)}\le CLa\sqrt B\), by (SE.15). For \(u\le r\), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and \(\norm{V_u-\overline V}_\F\le2\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\) give \(\abs{\EE\langle V_u-\overline V,\mathcal WA_r\rangle}\le CaB\bmom^{r+1}\), and \(\sum_r(r+1)\bmom^{r+1}\le C\). For \(u>r\), let \(V'_u\) be the update obtained by replacing the auxiliary batches and the batches of the steps up to \(t+r\) by independent copies. It has the law of \(V_u\), it is independent of \(A_r\), which depends only on \(m\), the auxiliary batches and the batches up to step \(t+r\), and by the lag decomposition (SA.33) its buffer differs from \(N_u\) by \(\bmom^{u-r}\) times the difference of two buffers with second moments at most \(CB\). Lemma SE.18 (Taylor transfer)
In the setting of Lemma SE.16, let \(\dot{\widehat V}_r\) be the tangent of Lemma SE.8 for the same-buffer chain at \((t,\Dt,M^\circ_{t-1})\). On \(\{\Dt\in\mathcal K\}\), for \(0\le r\le4L\),
\[ \EE_t\big\|{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\widehat V_r-\lr\,\dot{\widehat V}_r\big\|_\F\le\frac{Cr^2\log(2B)}{\sqrt B}. \]
Proof
Proof.
Write \(D=\Dt\), \(\ell=\ridgephys(D)\), \(E_r\defeq\Delta_{t+r}-D\), \(R_r\defeq M^\circ_{t+r}-\widehat N_r\) and \(s_r\defeq\ridgephys(\Delta_{t+r})-\ell\). By Lemma SE.14 and Lemma SE.16(i),
\[ \norm{E_r}_\F\le\frac{Cr}{\sqrt B},\qquad\abs{s_r}\le\frac{Cr}{\sqrt B},\qquad \EE_t\norm{R_r}_\F^2\le Cr^2,\qquad\EE_t\norm{R_r}^2\le\frac{Cr^2\log^2(2B)}{B}. \]
The update is expanded to first order. Since \({\mathsf S}_{t+r}=\Qridge{\ell+s_r}(\widehat N_r+R_r)\),
\[ \begin{aligned} {\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\widehat V_r={}&\big[\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r+R_r)-\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)\big] +s_r\,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)\\ &+s_r\big[\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r+R_r)-\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)\big]\\ &+\big[\Qridge{\ell+s_r}(\widehat N_r+R_r)-\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r+R_r)-s_r\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r+R_r)\big]. \end{aligned} \]
By Lemma SE.5(ii) the first bracket is \(D\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)[R_r]\) up to \(C\norm{R_r}\norm{R_r}_\F\); by (iv) the third term is at most \(C\abs{s_r}\norm{R_r}_\F\) and the last bracket at most \(C\sqrt B\,s_r^2\). The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality bounds the conditional expectations of these three errors by \(Cr^2\log(2B)/\sqrt B\).
The arguments are replaced by their tangents. Put \(E_r^{(1)}\defeq\lr\dot\Delta_r=-\lr\sum_{k<r}\widehat V_k\), while \(E_r=-\lr\sum_{k<r}{\mathsf S}_{t+k}\). By (i) of Lemma SE.16, \((\EE_t\norm{E_r-E_r^{(1)}}_\F^2)^{1/2}\le\lr\sum_{k<r}Ck\le Cr^2/B\). The tangent of the buffer is \(\lr\dot{\widehat N}_r=\sum_{j\le r}\emaw{r-j}G^\circ_{t+j+1}[E^{(1)}_j]\), so \(R_r-\lr\dot{\widehat N}_r=\sum_{j\le r}\emaw{r-j}G^\circ_{t+j+1}[E_j-E_j^{(1)}]\), and Lemma SE.3(i) applied conditionally gives \((\EE_t\norm{R_r-\lr\dot{\widehat N}_r}_\F^2)^{1/2}\le C\sqrt B\cdot Cr^2/B\). The ridge is quadratic in the state, \(\ridgephys(D+E)-\ridgephys(D)=\ridge(2\langle D,E\rangle_\Sigma+\norm E_\Sigma^2)\), so
\[ s_r-\lr\dot\ell_r=2\ridge\langle D,E_r-E^{(1)}_r\rangle_\Sigma+\ridge\norm{E_r}_\Sigma^2,\qquad \EE_t\abs{s_r-\lr\dot\ell_r}\le\frac{Cr^2}{B}, \]
where \(\dot\ell_r=2\ridge\langle D,\dot\Delta_r\rangle_\Sigma\) is the ridge tangent of Lemma SE.8. By Lemma SE.5(i),(iv), replacing \(R_r\) by \(\lr\dot{\widehat N}_r\) in \(D\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)[\cdot]\) costs \(\ell^{-1/2}Cr^2/\sqrt B\), and replacing \(s_r\) by \(\lr\dot\ell_r\) in front of \(\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)\) costs \(C\sqrt B\cdot Cr^2/B\). By Lemma SE.8, \(\lr\dot{\widehat V}_r=D\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)[\lr\dot{\widehat N}_r]+\lr\dot\ell_r\,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)\), which completes the proof.

Lemma SE.19 (Projection variance)
Let \(D\) be deterministic with \(r_-\le\cR(D)\le r_+\), consider the stationary chain at \((t,D)\), and let \(T\in\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) be deterministic. Then
\[ \Var\langle T,V_r\rangle\le C\log^2(2B)\norm T_\F^2,\qquad \Var\Big\langle T,\sum_{r<L}V_r\Big\rangle\le CL^2\log^2(2B)\norm T_\F^2 . \]
No bound on \(\norm T\) and no diffuseness of \(D\) is assumed.
Proof
Proof.
First keep only the batches of the relative times \(r-J,\dots,r\), auxiliary batches included. The resulting update is a smooth function \(f\) of finitely many independent standard Gaussian coordinates, namely the whitened samples, and the Gaussian Poincaré inequality [84, Theorem 3.20] gives \(\Var f\le\EE\norm{\nabla f}^2\).
The gradient is computed by the chain rule. Write \(A_D\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}D\Sigmain^{1/2}\), so \(\norm{A_D}_\F^2=2\cR(D)\le2r_+\), and let \(\mathsf C\defeq D\Qridge\ell(N)^*[T]\) be the adjoint of the derivative of the filter applied to \(T\), at \(\ell=\ridgephys(D)\); by Lemma SE.5(i), \(\norm{\mathsf C}_\F\le\ell^{-1/2}\norm T_\F\) pointwise. A sample \((z,w)\) of a batch of weight \(\varpi\) enters the buffer as \(\varpi B^{-1}(z^\T A_Dw)\Sigmaout^{1/2}(z\tensor w)\Sigmain^{1/2}\), so with \(\mathsf q_a\defeq z_a^\T\Sigmaout^{1/2}\mathsf C\,\Sigmain^{1/2}w_a\) and \(d_a=z_a^\T A_Dw_a\),
\[ \begin{gathered} \nabla_{z_a}f=\frac\varpi B\big[(A_Dw_a)\,\mathsf q_a+d_a\,\Sigmaout^{1/2}\mathsf C\,\Sigmain^{1/2}w_a\big],\\ \nabla_{w_a}f=\frac\varpi B\big[(A_D^\T z_a)\,\mathsf q_a+d_a\,\Sigmain^{1/2}\mathsf C^\T\Sigmaout^{1/2}z_a\big]. \end{gathered} \]
With \(Y\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}[z_1\cdots z_B]\) and \(X\defeq\Sigmain^{1/2}[w_1\cdots w_B]\), pointwise matrix inequalities give
\[ \begin{gathered} \sum_a\mathsf q_a^2\le\max_a\norm{\Sigmain^{1/2}w_a}^2\,\norm{\mathsf C}_\F^2\norm Y^2,\\ \sum_ad_a^2\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\mathsf C\,\Sigmain^{1/2}w_a}^2\le C_\Sigma\max_ad_a^2\,\norm{\mathsf C}_\F^2\norm X^2, \end{gathered} \]
and the transposed bounds. Hence the squared gradient over one batch is at most
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{C\varpi^2}{B^2}\norm{\mathsf C}_\F^2\Big[&\max_a\norm{A_Dw_a}^2\max_a\norm{w_a}^2\norm Y^2+\max_a\norm{A_D^\T z_a}^2\max_a\norm{z_a}^2\norm X^2\\ &+\max_ad_a^2\big(\norm X^2+\norm Y^2\big)\Big]. \end{aligned} \]
In every fixed \(L^p\) norm, \(\max_a\norm{A_Dw_a}^2\) and \(\max_a\norm{A_D^\T z_a}^2\) are at most \(C_p\log(2B)\), \(\max_a\norm{w_a}^2\), \(\max_a\norm{z_a}^2\), \(\norm X^2\) and \(\norm Y^2\) are at most \(C_pB\), and \(\max_ad_a^2\) is at most \(C_p\log^2(2B)\). These follow from \(\norm{\norm{A_Dw}^2}_q\le\sum_ks_k(A_D)^2\norm{g^2}_q\le Cq\norm{A_D}_\F^2\) (a diagonalization and Minkowski’s inequality), from \(\norm{\norm w^2}_q\le C(\Nin+q)\), from the maximum over \(B\) samples taken with \(q\asymp\log(2B)\) as in the proof of Lemma SE.3(ii), and from the operator norm moments there. Hölder’s inequality, with no independence between \(\mathsf C\) and the samples, bounds the expected squared gradient over the batch by \(C\varpi^2\log^2(2B)\norm T_\F^2\). Summing over the batches with \(\sum_j\emaw j^2\le1\) gives the first bound for the truncated update. As \(J\to\infty\) the truncated update converges to \(V_r\) in \(L^2\), by Lemma SA.6(ii), so the bound passes to \(V_r\).
The second bound is the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality \(\Var\sum_{r<L}Y_r\le L\sum_{r<L}\Var Y_r\).

The block martingale. For bounded \(f,g\) on \([0,C_\Sigma]\) write \(\mathcal W=\mathcal W_{f,g}\) and \(X_{f,g}(\Delta)\defeq\int f\tensor g\,\dif\chi(\Delta)=\langle\Delta,\mathcal W\Delta\rangle\). Since \(\mathcal W\) is selfadjoint, for every \(\Delta,{\mathsf S}\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.26} X_{f,g}(\Delta-\lr {\mathsf S})=X_{f,g}(\Delta)-2\lr\langle\mathcal W\Delta,{\mathsf S}\rangle+\lr^2\langle {\mathsf S},\mathcal W{\mathsf S}\rangle . \end{equation}
Lemma SE.20 (Block martingale)
Let \(b_\star\) be a block index such that whether \(b<b_\star\) is decided by the past \(\cF_{t_b}\), and such that \(\Delta_{t_b}\in\mathcal A\) for \(b<b_\star\); put \(I_b\defeq\mathbf 1_{b<b_\star}\). Let \(\norm f_\infty,\norm g_\infty\le1\) and
\[ \xi_b\defeq X_{f,g}(\Delta_{t_{b+1}})-X_{f,g}(\Delta_{t_b})-\EE_{t_b}\big[X_{f,g}(\Delta_{t_{b+1}})-X_{f,g}(\Delta_{t_b})\big]. \]
Then for every \(T>0\),
\[ \EE\max_{j\le TB/L}\Big|\sum_{b<j}I_b\xi_b\Big|^2\le C(T+1)\Big\{\frac{L\log^2(2B)}{B}+\frac{L^3}{B}+\frac1L\Big\}. \]
Proof
Proof.
Fix a block \(b\) with \(I_b=1\) and write \(t=t_b\), \(D=\Dt\), \(m=M^\circ_{t-1}\). There is no stop in the block, by Lemma SE.14(ii), so \(\Delta_{t+L}=D-\lr\Sigma_{\mathsf S}\) with \(\Sigma_{\mathsf S}\defeq\sum_{r<L}{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\), and (SE.26) for the block gives
\[ X_{f,g}(\Delta_{t+L})-X_{f,g}(D)=-2\lr\langle\mathcal WD,S\rangle-2\lr\langle\mathcal WD,\mathcal E\rangle+\lr^2\langle\Sigma_{\mathsf S},\mathcal W\Sigma_{\mathsf S}\rangle, \]
where \(S=\sum_{r<L}V_r\) is the stationary sum of the chains at time \(t\) and \(\mathcal E\defeq\Sigma_{\mathsf S}-S\). The left side does not depend on the auxiliary batches, and conditionally on \(\cF_t\) its centered version is the sum of the centered versions of the three terms. Hence \(\EE_t\xi_b^2\le3\big(4\lr^2\Var_t\langle\mathcal WD,S\rangle+4\lr^2\EE_t\langle\mathcal WD,\mathcal E\rangle^2+\lr^4\EE_t\langle\Sigma_{\mathsf S},\mathcal W\Sigma_{\mathsf S}\rangle^2\big)\).
The three terms are bounded as follows. By Lemma SE.19, conditionally on \(\cF_t\) with \(T=\mathcal WD\) and \(\norm{\mathcal WD}_\F\le\norm D_\F\le C\), the first is at most \(C\lr^2L^2\log^2(2B)\). By Lemma SE.16(iii), the second is at most \(C\lr^2(L^4+B+\norm m_\F^2)\), and \(\EE[I_b\norm m_\F^2]\le CB\) by Lemma SE.15 with \(\tau=\tau^+\), since \(I_b\le\mathbf 1_{t_b\le\tau^+}\). The third is at most \(\lr^4(L^2\gamma_\star B)^2=CL^4/B^2\), because \(\norm{\Sigma_{\mathsf S}}_\F\le L\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\). Altogether
\[ \EE\big[I_b\xi_b^2\big]\le C\Big\{\frac{L^2\log^2(2B)}{B^2}+\frac{L^4}{B^2}+\frac1B\Big\}. \]
The variables \(I_b\xi_b\) are martingale differences for the pasts at the block starts, so \(\EE(\sum_{b<j}I_b\xi_b)^2=\sum_{b<j}\EE[I_b\xi_b^2]\), and Doob’s \(L^2\) maximal inequality [101, Theorem 14.11] bounds the expected squared maximum over \(j\le TB/L\) by four times the sum over the at most \(TB/L+1\) blocks.

Delayed projections. The linear term of a block increment is the projection of the updates on the state. Its first \(\mathsf q\) updates are not close to stationary, because they still depend on the buffer inherited from the previous block. They are controlled by freezing \(\mathsf q\) steps earlier.
Lemma SE.21 (Delayed projections and prefixes)
Let \(\mathcal W\) be a linear map on \(\R^{\Nout\times\Nin}\) with \(\norm{\mathcal W}_{\F\to\F}\le c_W\).
  1. (i)
    For every \(u\ge\mathsf q\), \(\EE\big[\mathbf 1_{u-\mathsf q<\tau^+}\abs{\langle\mathcal W\Delta_u,{\mathsf S}_u\rangle}\big]\le C\mathsf q\).
  2. (ii)
    For every \(t\ge\mathsf q\), \(\EE\big[\mathbf 1_{t<\tau^+}\big|\sum_{r<\mathsf q}\langle\mathcal W\Dt,{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle\big|\big]\le C\mathsf q^2\).
  3. (iii)
    For every \(t\ge\mathsf q\), with the chains at time \(t\) and \(0\le r\le\mathsf q\), \(\EE\big[\mathbf 1_{t<\tau^+}\abs{\langle\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain,\widehat V_r-V_r\rangle}\big]\le\min\big(C\mathsf q,\ C\sqrt B\bmom^{r+1}\big)\), and for every \(r\ge0\) the second bound holds.
Consequently, with \(\Omega_\bmom(\epsilon)\defeq\sum_{r\ge0}\min(\epsilon,\bmom^{r+1})\) and \(\epsilon_B\defeq\mathsf q/\sqrt B\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SE.27} \frac{1}{\sqrt B}\sum_{r<L}\EE\big[\mathbf 1_{t<\tau^+}\abs{\langle\Sigmaout\Dt\Sigmain,\widehat V_r-V_r\rangle}\big]\le C\big(\Omega_\bmom(\epsilon_B)+\bmom^{\mathsf q}\big), \end{equation}
and \(\Omega_\bmom(\epsilon)\le C\epsilon(1+\log(1/\epsilon))\) for \(0<\epsilon\le1\), with \(\Omega_0=0\).
Proof
Proof.
For (i), put \(a\defeq u-\mathsf q\) and \(D\defeq\Delta_a\), and consider the chains at time \(a\), with stationary updates \(V^{(a)}_r\) and same-buffer updates \(\widehat V^{(a)}_r\) built from \(m=M^\circ_{a-1}\). On \(\{a<\tau^+\}\), \(D\in\mathcal A^+\), and by Lemma SE.14(ii) the states up to step \(u\) lie in \(\mathcal A^{++}\); the risk stays bounded below on this window even if it crosses \(\tau^+\). Decompose
\[ \langle\mathcal W\Delta_u,{\mathsf S}_u\rangle=\langle\mathcal WD,V^{(a)}_{\mathsf q}\rangle+\langle\mathcal WD,{\mathsf S}_u-V^{(a)}_{\mathsf q}\rangle+\langle\mathcal W(\Delta_u-D),{\mathsf S}_u\rangle . \]
By (SE.15) and Lemma SE.19 with \(T=\mathcal WD\), \(\EE_a\abs{\langle\mathcal WD,V^{(a)}_{\mathsf q}\rangle}\le C+C\log(2B)\). By Lemma SE.16(i),(ii) with \(r=\mathsf q\), \(\EE_a\abs{\langle\mathcal WD,{\mathsf S}_u-V^{(a)}_{\mathsf q}\rangle}\le C\mathsf q+C\bmom^{\mathsf q+1}(\norm m_\F+\sqrt B)\). By Lemma SE.14(i), \(\abs{\langle\mathcal W(\Delta_u-D),{\mathsf S}_u\rangle}\le c_W\norm{\Delta_u-D}_\F\norm{{\mathsf S}_u}_\F\le C\mathsf q\). The indicator \(\mathbf 1_{a<\tau^+}\) is decided by the past at \(a\); multiplying by it, taking expectations and using \(\EE[\mathbf 1_{a<\tau^+}\norm m_\F]\le C\sqrt B\) from Lemma SE.15 gives at most \(C\log(2B)+C\mathsf q+C\sqrt B\bmom^{\mathsf q}\le C\mathsf q\).
For (ii), write \(\langle\mathcal W\Dt,{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle=\langle\mathcal W\Delta_{t+r},{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle+\langle\mathcal W(\Dt-\Delta_{t+r}),{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle\). Since \(t+r-\mathsf q\le t\), \(\mathbf 1_{t<\tau^+}\le\mathbf 1_{t+r-\mathsf q<\tau^+}\), and (i) with \(u=t+r\) bounds the first term by \(C\mathsf q\); the second is at most \(Cr\) by Lemma SE.14(i). Summing over \(r<\mathsf q\) proves (ii).
For (iii), let \(D=\Dt\) and write \(\Sigma_D\defeq\Sigmaout D\Sigmain\). The first bound follows from
\[ \abs{\langle\Sigma_D,\widehat V_r\rangle}\le\abs{\langle\Sigmaout\Delta_{t+r}\Sigmain,{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle}+\abs{\langle\Sigmaout(D-\Delta_{t+r})\Sigmain,{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle}+\abs{\langle\Sigma_D,\widehat V_r-{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\rangle}, \]
whose three terms have expectations, with the indicator, at most \(C\mathsf q\) by (i) for \(\mathcal WX=\Sigmaout X\Sigmain\), at most \(Cr\) by Lemma SE.14(i), and at most \(Cr\) by Lemma SE.16(i); together with \(\EE_t\abs{\langle\Sigma_D,V_r\rangle}\le C\log(2B)\), from (SE.15) and Lemma SE.19. The second bound is Lemma SE.16(ii) with Lemma SE.15.
For (SE.27), use the minimum of the two bounds of (iii) for \(r\le\mathsf q\), divided by \(\sqrt B\), and the second bound for \(r>\mathsf q\), whose sum is at most \(C\bmom^{\mathsf q}\). For the bound on \(\Omega_\bmom\), let \(r_\epsilon\) be the least \(r\) with \(\bmom^{r+1}\le\epsilon\); then \(r_\epsilon\le\log(1/\epsilon)/\log(1/\bmom)\), and \(\Omega_\bmom(\epsilon)\le r_\epsilon\epsilon+\sum_{r\ge r_\epsilon}\bmom^{r+1}\le r_\epsilon\epsilon+\epsilon/(1-\bmom)\).

Proposition SE.5 (Tangent transport)
Let \(t\ge\mathsf q\), let the chains at time \(t\) be those of Definition SE.2 with \(D=\Dt\) and \(m=M^\circ_{t-1}\), and let \(\Pi_t\) be a matrix determined by the past \(\cF_t\) with \(\norm{\Pi_t}_\F,\norm{\Pi_t}\le c_\Pi\). Then for \(0\le r<L\),
\[ \lr\,\EE\Big[\mathbf 1_{t<\tau^+}\big|\EE_t\langle\Pi_t,\dot{\widehat V}_r-\dot V_r\rangle\big|\Big] \le C\Big\{\frac{r}{\sqrt B}+\Omega_\bmom(\epsilon_B)+\bmom^{\mathsf q}+\sqrt B\,r\bmom^{r+1}\Big\}. \]
Proof
Proof.
Conditionally on \(\cF_t\), the matrices \(D\), \(m\) and \(\Pi_t\) are fixed, and by the scaling in the proof of Proposition SE.4 we may take \(2\cR(D)=1\). By Lemma SE.8, with \(A_k\defeq\widehat V_k-V_k\), \(\ell=\ridge\), \(\dot\ell^{\,\wedge}_r\defeq-2\ridge\sum_{k<r}\langle\Sigma_D,\widehat V_k\rangle\) and \(\dot\ell_r\defeq-2\ridge\sum_{k<r}\langle\Sigma_D,V_k\rangle\),
\[ \begin{aligned} \dot{\widehat V}_r-\dot V_r={}&\big[D\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)-D\Qridge\ell(N_r)\big]\big[\dot{\widehat N}_r\big] -D\Qridge\ell(N_r)\Big[\sum_{0\le k<j\le r}\emaw{r-j}G^\circ_{t+j+1}[A_k]\Big]\\ &+\dot\ell^{\,\wedge}_r\big[\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(\widehat N_r)-\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_r)\big] +\big(\dot\ell^{\,\wedge}_r-\dot\ell_r\big)\,\partial_\ell\Qridge\ell(N_r). \end{aligned} \]
The change of the derivative operators. By Lemma SE.5(iii) and Lemma SE.2(ii), the first term has Frobenius norm at most \(C\bmom^{r+1}\norm{m-N_{-1}}_\F\norm{\dot{\widehat N}_r}_\F\). Since \(\norm{\widehat V_k}_\F\le\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\), Lemma SE.3(i) gives \((\EE_t\norm{G^\circ_{t+j+1}[\widehat V_k]}_\F^2)^{1/2}\le CB\), so \((\EE_t\norm{\dot{\widehat N}_r}_\F^2)^{1/2}\le CBr\). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, Lemma SE.2(iii) and Lemma SE.15, the first term contributes at most \(\lr\cdot C\bmom^{r+1}\sqrt B\cdot CBr=C\sqrt Br\bmom^{r+1}\). The third term is at most \(C\sqrt Br\cdot C\bmom^{r+1}\norm{m-N_{-1}}_\F\) by Lemma SE.5(iv) and \(\abs{\dot\ell^{\,\wedge}_r}\le Cr\sqrt B\), and contributes at most \(\lr\cdot CBr\bmom^{r+1}\le Cr\bmom^{r+1}\).
The change of the argument. For \(k<j\) the direction \(A_k\) depends only on \(m\), the auxiliary batches and the batches up to step \(t+k\), so the batch of step \(t+j\) is independent of \(A_k\) together with all other samples; moreover \(\norm{A_k}\le2\) and \(\norm{A_k}_\F\le2\sqrt{\gamma_\star B}\). By the integral representation of \(D\Qridge\ell\), Proposition SE.2 with \(E=A_k\) and the size bound of Lemma SE.12,
\[ \Big|\EE_t\big\langle\Pi_t,D\Qridge\ell(N_r)\big[\emaw{r-j}G^\circ_{t+j+1}[A_k]\big]\big\rangle\Big| \le C\sqrt B\,\emaw{r-j}+C\sqrt B\,\emaw{r-j}^2\,\EE_t\abs{\langle\Sigma_D,A_k\rangle}, \]
after integration over \(s=\ridge+u^2\). Summing over \(0\le k<j\le r\) and multiplying by \(\lr\), the second term contributes at most \(Cr/\sqrt B+CB^{-1/2}\sum_{k<r}\EE_t\abs{\langle\Sigma_D,A_k\rangle}\). The fourth term is at most \(C\sqrt B\cdot2\ridge\sum_{k<r}\abs{\langle\Sigma_D,A_k\rangle}\) by Lemma SE.5(iv), and contributes at most \(CB^{-1/2}\sum_{k<r}\abs{\langle\Sigma_D,A_k\rangle}\).
Multiplying by \(\mathbf 1_{t<\tau^+}\), taking expectations and applying (SE.27) proves the claim.

Proposition SE.6 (Post-prefix response)
Let \(\delta_B\downarrow0\) be a deterministic sequence. Let \(t\ge\mathsf q\), let \(\Pi_t\) be determined by \(\cF_t\) with \(\norm{\Pi_t}_\F,\norm{\Pi_t}\le c_\Pi\), and let \(\mathfrak E_t\defeq\{t<\tau^+,\ \delta(\Dt)\le\delta_B\}\). Then
\[ \EE\Big[\mathbf 1_{\mathfrak E_t}\Big|\frac1L\sum_{r=\mathsf q}^{L-1}\big\langle\Pi_t,\EE_t{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\overline V(\Dt)\big\rangle\Big|\Big]\le\rho_B, \]
\[ \rho_B\defeq C\Big\{\frac{L^2\log(2B)}{\sqrt B}+\frac{L}{\sqrt B}+\frac1B+\delta_B(c_\Pi+\delta_B)+\Omega_\bmom(\epsilon_B)+\bmom^{\mathsf q}+\sqrt B\,L\bmom^{\mathsf q}\Big\}, \]
and \(\rho_B\to0\).
Proof
Proof.
For \(\mathsf q\le r<L\), since \(\EE_tV_r=\overline V(\Dt)\) by Lemma SE.2,
\[ \EE_t{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\overline V(\Dt)=\EE_t\big[{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\widehat V_r-\lr\dot{\widehat V}_r\big]+\EE_t\big[\widehat V_r-V_r\big]+\lr\,\EE_t\big[\dot{\widehat V}_r-\dot V_r\big]+\lr\,\EE_t\dot V_r . \]

Lemma SE.22 (Quadratic block response)

Proof
Proof.
Write \(\mathcal E=A+J\) with \(A=\sum_{r<L}(\widehat V_r-V_r)\) and \(J=\sum_{r<L}({\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\widehat V_r)\). Since \(\mathcal W\) is selfadjoint,
\[ 2\langle S,\mathcal W\mathcal E\rangle+\langle\mathcal E,\mathcal W\mathcal E\rangle =2\langle S,\mathcal WA\rangle+\langle A,\mathcal WA\rangle+2\langle S+A,\mathcal WJ\rangle+\langle J,\mathcal WJ\rangle . \]
By Lemma SE.17(ii) applied conditionally, the first term is at most \(Ca(B+L\sqrt B)\). By Lemma SE.16(ii), the second is at most \(Ca^2B\). Lemma SE.23 (Matrix martingale)
Let \(Z_0,Z_1,\dots\) be random selfadjoint \(d\times d\) matrices such that \(Z_b\) is determined by the past at block \(b+1\), has conditional mean zero given the past at block \(b\), and satisfies \(\norm{Z_b}\le c\). Then for \(x>0\) and every integer \(N\ge1\),
\[ \Pr\Big(\max_{j\le N}\Big\|\sum_{b<j}Z_b\Big\|\ge x\Big)\le2dN\exp\Big(-\frac{x^2}{2Nc^2}\Big). \]
Proof
Proof.
A centered increment has a subgaussian matrix moment generating function. For \(u>0\) and \(\abs y\le c\), convexity gives \(e^{uy}\le\cosh(uc)+(y/c)\sinh(uc)\), so by the spectral theorem \(e^{uZ_b}\preceq\cosh(uc)\Id+\sinh(uc)Z_b/c\); conditioning on the past at block \(b\) and using \(\cosh(uc)\le e^{u^2c^2/2}\) gives \(\EE_b\,e^{uZ_b}\preceq e^{u^2c^2/2}\Id\).
The trace exponential of the sum is bounded by induction. Put \(S_j\defeq\sum_{b<j}Z_b\). The Golden–Thompson inequality [81, Theorem IX.3.7 and (IX.19)] gives \(\Tr e^{uS_{j+1}}\le\Tr\big(e^{uS_j}e^{uZ_j}\big)\). Conditioning on the past at block \(j\), and using \(\Tr(PQ)\le\Tr(PQ')\) for \(P\succeq0\) and \(Q\preceq Q'\),
\[ \EE\,\Tr e^{uS_{j+1}}\le e^{u^2c^2/2}\,\EE\,\Tr e^{uS_j}\le d\,e^{(j+1)u^2c^2/2}. \]
Markov’s inequality applied to \(\Tr e^{uS_j}\ge e^{u\lambda_{\max}(S_j)}\) with \(u=x/(jc^2)\) gives \(\Pr(\lambda_{\max}(S_j)\ge x)\le d\,e^{-x^2/(2jc^2)}\le d\,e^{-x^2/(2Nc^2)}\) for \(j\le N\). The same bound for \(-S_j\) and a union bound over \(j\le N\) prove the claim.

Lemma SE.24 (Persistence of diffuseness)
Let \(b_c\defeq\inf\{b\ge0:\Delta_{t_b}\notin\mathcal A\}\). For every fixed \(T>0\),
\[ \max\big\{\delta(\Dt):\ t\le t_{b_c}\wedge TB\big\}\longrightarrow0\quad\text{in probability}. \]
No diffuseness beyond the initial condition {{(H3)}} is used.
Proof
Proof.
All states up to \(t_{b_c}\) lie in \(\mathcal A^+\), so their risk is at least \(r_0/4\) and \(\delta\) is defined there. Put \(Y_b\defeq\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Delta_{t_{b\wedge b_c}}\Sigmain^{1/2}\) and \(J\defeq\lceil TB/L\rceil\). For \(b<b_c\) write \(t=t_b\), \(D=\Dt\), \(m=M^\circ_{t-1}\) and \(\mathcal E=\sum_{r<L}{\mathsf S}_{t+r}-\sum_{r<L}V_r\) as before. Then \(Y_{b+1}-Y_b=-\lr\Sigmaout^{1/2}\big(\sum_{r<L}{\mathsf S}_{t+r}\big)\Sigmain^{1/2}\) and
\[ \EE_t\big[Y_{b+1}-Y_b\big]=-\lr\Sigmaout^{1/2}\big(L\overline V(D)+\EE_t\mathcal E\big)\Sigmain^{1/2}. \]
The drift. By (SE.15) and Lemma SE.16(iv),
\[ \norm{\EE_t[Y_{b+1}-Y_b]}\le C\lr L\big(\norm{Y_b}+r_B\big)+C\lr\big(\norm m+\log(2B)+L^2\log(2B)/\sqrt B\big). \]
By Lemma SE.15 with \(\tau=\tau^+\), \(\EE[\mathbf 1_{b<b_c}\norm{M^\circ_{t_b-1}}]\le\sqrt{2c_MB}\,\bmom^{t_b}+C\log(2B)\). Hence the sum \(\mathsf E_B\defeq\sum_{b<J}\mathbf 1_{b<b_c}C\lr(\norm{M^\circ_{t_b-1}}+\log(2B)+L^2\log(2B)/\sqrt B)\) satisfies
\[ \begin{aligned} \EE\,\mathsf E_B&\le\frac{C\macrostep}{B}\Big(\frac{\sqrt B}{1-\bmom^L}+(J+1)\Big(\log(2B)+\frac{L^2\log(2B)}{\sqrt B}\Big)\Big)\\ &\le C\Big(\frac1{\sqrt B}+\frac{(T+1)\log(2B)}{L}+\frac{(T+1)L\log(2B)}{\sqrt B}\Big)\to0 . \end{aligned} \]
The fluctuation. Put \(Z_b\defeq\mathbf 1_{b<b_c}(Y_{b+1}-Y_b-\EE_{t_b}[Y_{b+1}-Y_b])\). Since \(\norm{{\mathsf S}}\le1\), \(\norm{Y_{b+1}-Y_b}\le C_\Sigma\lr L\) and the same holds for its conditional mean, so \(\norm{Z_b}\le2C_\Sigma\macrostep L/B\). The symmetric dilations \(\mathcal D(Z_b)\) have dimension \(\Nout+\Nin\le2\gamma_\star B\) and the same norms, and Lemma SE.23 gives, for every \(x>0\),
\[ \Pr\Big(\max_{j\le J}\Big\|\sum_{b<j}Z_b\Big\|\ge x\Big)\le4\gamma_\star BJ\exp\Big(-\frac{x^2B}{8C_\Sigma^2\macrostep^2(T+1)L}\Big)\to0 . \]
Gronwall. For \(j\le J\), adding the drift and the fluctuation and using \(\lr LJ\le\macrostep(T+1)\),
\[ \norm{Y_j}\le\mathfrak a_B+\frac{C\macrostep L}{B}\sum_{b<j}\norm{Y_b},\qquad \mathfrak a_B\defeq\norm{Y_0}+C\macrostep(T+1)r_B+\mathsf E_B+\max_{j\le J}\Big\|\sum_{b<j}Z_b\Big\|. \]
Induction on \(j\) gives \(\norm{Y_j}\le\mathfrak a_B(1+C\macrostep L/B)^j\le\mathfrak a_Be^{C\macrostep(T+1)}\). Here \(\norm{Y_0}=\sqrt{2\cR(0)}\norm{\widehat A_0}\to0\) by {{(H3)}}, \(r_B\to0\) by Lemma SE.7, and the last two terms tend to zero in probability, so \(\max_{j\le J}\norm{Y_j}\to0\) in probability. Within a block the weighted state moves by at most \(C_\Sigma\macrostep L/B\) in operator norm, and \(\delta(\Dt)\le\norm{\Sigmaout^{1/2}\Dt\Sigmain^{1/2}}/\sqrt{r_0/2}\) for \(t\le t_{b_c}\), which proves the claim.

SE.8 Block freezing

The drift and volatility sections state their deterministic equivalents along sequences of diffuse frozen states. Along the algorithm the states are random and change from block to block, so the equivalents are needed uniformly over all states of small diffuseness.
Lemma SE.25 (Uniformity over diffuse states)
Assume {{(H1)}}. For each dimension let \(e_B\ge0\) be a function on the set \(\mathscr S_B\) of normalized states such that \(e_B(\widehat A_B)\to0\) along every subsequence of dimensions and every choice of \(\widehat A_B\in\mathscr S_B\) along it with \(\norm{\widehat A_B}\to0\). Then for every sequence \(\delta_B\to0\),
\[ \bar e_B\defeq\sup\big\{e_B(\widehat A):\ \widehat A\in\mathscr S_B,\ \norm{\widehat A}\le\delta_B\big\}\longrightarrow0 , \]
with the convention that the supremum of the empty set is zero.
Proof
Proof.
Suppose not. Then there are \(\epsilon>0\) and a subsequence of dimensions at which \(\bar e_B>\epsilon\), and at each of them a state \(\widehat A_B\in\mathscr S_B\) with \(\norm{\widehat A_B}\le\delta_B\) and \(e_B(\widehat A_B)>\epsilon/2\). Along this subsequence \(\norm{\widehat A_B}\to0\) while \(e_B(\widehat A_B)\) does not tend to zero, a contradiction.

SE.9 A positive interval

Lemma SE.27 (No exit before \(T_\star\))

Proof.

Let \(\epsilon>0\), let \(0=x_0<\dots<x_n=C_\Sigma\) be a grid of mesh at most \(\epsilon\), and let \(a_0,\dots,a_n\) be the piecewise linear hat functions with \(a_r(x_s)=\mathbf 1_{r=s}\), which form a partition of unity. By Lemma SE.26 applied to the finitely many tests \(a_r\tensor a_s\),
\[ \sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\sum_{r,s\le n}\Big|\int a_r\tensor a_s\,\dif\mathcal R_B(\macrotime)\Big|\longrightarrow0\quad\text{in probability}. \]
Every test \(\phi\) of the unit ball of the bounded-Lipschitz norm is close to its interpolant \(\phi_\epsilon\defeq\sum_{r,s}\phi(x_r,x_s)\,a_r\tensor a_s\): at each point \(\phi_\epsilon\) is a convex combination of values of \(\phi\) at points within \(\ell^1\) distance \(2\epsilon\), so \(\norm{\phi-\phi_\epsilon}_\infty\le2\epsilon\). Since \(\abs{\phi(x_r,x_s)}\le1\),
\[ \norm{\mathcal R_B(\macrotime)}_\BL\le\sum_{r,s}\Big|\int a_r\tensor a_s\,\dif\mathcal R_B(\macrotime)\Big|+2\epsilon\norm{\mathcal R_B(\macrotime)}_{\mathrm{TV}}, \]
and since \(\epsilon\) is arbitrary, \(\sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\mathcal R_B(\macrotime)}_\BL\to0\) in probability.
Gronwall’s inequality closes the argument. On the event of Lemma SE.27, \(\macrotime_c>T_\star\) and \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)\in\mathfrak M\) for \(\macrotime\le T_\star\), and by Proposition SE.1 so is \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)\). Subtracting (SE.9) from the definition of \(\mathcal R_B\), with \(\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(0)=\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(0)\), and using Lemma SE.1(iii),
\[ \norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)}_\BL\le\sup_{\sigma\le T_\star}\norm{\mathcal R_B(\sigma)}_\BL+C_{\mathsf F}\int_0^\macrotime\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\sigma)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\sigma)}_\BL\,\dif\sigma , \]
and Gronwall’s inequality [98, Lemma 2.7] gives
\[ \sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime)}_\BL\le e^{C_{\mathsf F}T_\star}\sup_\sigma\norm{\mathcal R_B(\sigma)}_\BL. \]
This proves (ii).
For (iii), multiplication by \(\mu\lambda\) maps the unit ball of the bounded-Lipschitz norm into \(C_\Sigma(C_\Sigma+1)\) times itself, because \(\norm{\mu\lambda\,\phi}_\infty\le C_\Sigma^2\) and \(\mu\lambda\phi\) is \((C_\Sigma^2+C_\Sigma)\)-Lipschitz for \(\abs\cdot_1\); and \(\cR(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor)-\fRisk(\macrotime)=\frac12\int\mu\lambda\,\dif(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}(\macrotime)-\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}(\macrotime))\).

Proof
Proof of Corollary SE.2.
(i), compactness. By (SE.6) the functions \(\mathfrak d_B\) are uniformly bounded and uniformly Lipschitz on \(\mathbb K\), so the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem [92, Theorem 7.25] gives a uniformly convergent subsequence. The measures \(\zeta_B\) are positive with masses at most \(m_\zeta\), and \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_B(0)\) are positive with masses at most \(E_\star\). Along a further subsequence the masses converge; if a limit mass is zero the measures tend to zero weakly, and otherwise the normalized measures are probability measures on the compact \(\mathbb K\), hence tight, and Prokhorov’s theorem [83, Theorem 5.1] gives a weakly convergent subsequence.
(ii), the limit data. Fix a subsequence as in (i). The limit \(\mathfrak d_\infty\) satisfies the bounds (SE.6), which pass to uniform limits; \(\zeta_\infty\) is positive with mass at most \(m_\zeta\); and \(\chi_0\) is positive, with \(\chi_0(\mathbb K)\le E_\star\) and \(\fRisk(\chi_0)=\lim\cR(\Delta_0)\in[r_0,r_1]\), since \(1\) and \(\mu\lambda\) are continuous on \(\mathbb K\). The field \(\mathsf F_\infty(\chi)\defeq-2\macrostep\fRisk(\chi)^{-1/2}\mathfrak d_\infty\chi+\macrostep^2\zeta_\infty\) therefore satisfies Lemma SE.1 with the same constants, whose proof uses only these bounds.
Weak convergence of positive measures on \(\mathbb K\) with bounded masses is convergence in the bounded-Lipschitz norm. Indeed, if \(c_B\to c\) are the masses and \(c>0\), the normalized measures converge weakly, and Dudley’s theorem [86, Theorem 11.3.3] gives convergence of their bounded-Lipschitz distance, which dominates ours up to a factor two; the difference of masses contributes \(\abs{c_B-c}\). If \(c=0\) the norm is at most the mass. Hence \(\norm{\zeta_B-\zeta_\infty}_\BL\to0\) and \(\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_B(0)-\chi_0}_\BL\to0\).
(ii), the Cauchy bound. For two dimensions \(B,B'\) of the subsequence write \(\chi_B=\chi^{\mathrm{ode}}_B\), which lies in \(\mathfrak M\) on \([0,T_\star]\) by Proposition SE.1. Adding and subtracting \(\mathsf F_B(\chi_{B'})\),
\[ \norm{\mathsf F_B(\chi_{B'})-\mathsf F_{B'}(\chi_{B'})}_\BL\le\frac{2\macrostep(E_\star+1)}{\sqrt{r_0/2}}\norm{\mathfrak d_B-\mathfrak d_{B'}}_\infty+\macrostep^2\norm{\zeta_B-\zeta_{B'}}_\BL , \]
and Lemma SE.1(iii) with Gronwall’s inequality give
\[ \begin{aligned} \sup_{\macrotime\le T_\star}\norm{\chi_B(\macrotime)-\chi_{B'}(\macrotime)}_\BL\le e^{C_{\mathsf F}T_\star}\Big(&\norm{\chi_B(0)-\chi_{B'}(0)}_\BL+CT_\star\norm{\mathfrak d_B-\mathfrak d_{B'}}_\infty\\ &+\macrostep^2T_\star\norm{\zeta_B-\zeta_{B'}}_\BL\Big), \end{aligned} \]
which tends to zero as \(B,B'\to\infty\) along the subsequence.
(ii), the limit path. For each \(\macrotime\), the measures \(\chi_B(\macrotime)\) lie in \(\mathfrak M\), so every subsequence has a weakly convergent subsequence, by Prokhorov’s theorem as in (i), and its limit is a limit in the bounded-Lipschitz norm. By the Cauchy bound all these limits coincide; call the common limit \(\chi(\macrotime)\). The convergence is uniform in \(\macrotime\le T_\star\), by the Cauchy bound, and \(\chi(\macrotime)\in\mathfrak M\), because mass and risk are integrals of continuous functions. Each \(\chi_B\) is Lipschitz in total variation with constant \(C_E\), by Lemma SE.1(i), so \(\chi\) is continuous for the bounded-Lipschitz norm.
(ii), the equation. For a test \(\phi\) of the unit ball and \(\sigma\le T_\star\),
\[ \begin{aligned} \Big|\int\phi\,\dif\big(\mathsf F_B(\chi_B(\sigma))-\mathsf F_\infty(\chi(\sigma))\big)\Big| \le{}&C_{\mathsf F}\norm{\chi_B(\sigma)-\chi(\sigma)}_\BL\\ &+\frac{2\macrostep(E_\star+1)}{\sqrt{r_0/2}}\norm{\mathfrak d_B-\mathfrak d_\infty}_\infty+\macrostep^2\norm{\zeta_B-\zeta_\infty}_\BL , \end{aligned} \]
which tends to zero uniformly in \(\sigma\). Passing to the limit in (SE.9) tested with \(\phi\) gives (SE.14) tested with \(\phi\). Bounded Lipschitz functions are dense in the continuous functions on \(\mathbb K\) for the uniform norm, so (SE.14) holds against every continuous test.
(ii), uniqueness and the actual measures. If \(\chi'\) is another solution with values in \(\mathfrak M\), continuous for the bounded-Lipschitz norm, then \(\norm{\chi(\macrotime)-\chi'(\macrotime)}_\BL\le C_{\mathsf F}\int_0^\macrotime\norm{\chi(\sigma)-\chi'(\sigma)}_\BL\dif\sigma\), and Gronwall’s inequality gives \(\chi=\chi'\). The statement for \(\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_B\) follows from Theorem SE.1(ii) and the triangle inequality.
(iii). As in the proof of Theorem SE.1(iii), \(\abs{\cR(\lfloor B\macrotime\rfloor)-\fRisk(\chi(\macrotime))}\le\frac12(C_\Sigma^2+C_\Sigma)\norm{\chi^{\mathrm{act}}_B(\macrotime)-\chi(\macrotime)}_\BL\).
