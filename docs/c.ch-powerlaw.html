Power-law spectra and the large-batch regime

This chapter computes the coefficients \(\mathfrak d_i\) and \(\mathfrak v_i\) of the row recursion (14) in the half-anisotropic setting \(\Sigmain=\Id_N\), \(\Nout=\Nin=N\), at zero normalized ridge (except Corollary SH.1, which concerns the expected update at a positive ridge). At each finite dimension these are deterministic functions of the spectrum \(\mu_1\ge\dots\ge\mu_N\), of \(B\) and of \(\bmom\), obtained as limits \(\ridge\downarrow0\) of the coefficients of the drift and volatility chapters; no result concerns the algorithm at zero ridge. Two regimes are treated with one set of tools (Section C.5). In the thin-batch regime the first eight results locate the crossover between resolved and unresolved rows and give both coefficients on either side of it, which is Table 1. In the oversampled regime the last six results concern the drift alone, for a general spectrum (the last one specializes to power laws), and give the square-root law of Theorem 4.1 and the modes that escape it.
The thin-batch results share the following setup. Assumption S4 fixes \(\alpha>0\) and \(\bmom\in(0,1)\) and lets \(N,B\to\infty\) with \(\gamma=N/B\to\infty\), with \(\mu_i=c_Ni^{-\alpha}\) for amplitudes \(c_N>0\). The weights are \(\emaw j=(1-\bmom)\bmom^j\), \(\sw p=\sum_j\emaw j^p\), so \(\sw2=(1-\bmom)/(1+\bmom)\), and \(c_0=\EE\abs g=\sqrt{2/\pi}\) for a standard Gaussian \(g\). With \(\Jfun(x)=\EE[g^2/(1+xg^2)]\) put
\[ \Fw(q)=\sum_j\emaw j^2\Jfun(\emaw j^2q),\qquad \Lw(q)=\sum_j\emaw j\Jfun(\emaw j^2q), \]
\[ r_\bmom(q)=q\Fw(q),\quad Q_m=r_\bmom^{-1}(m),\quad E(m)=Q_mr_\bmom'(Q_m), \]
and the participation \(m_N(\Lambda)=B^{-1}\sum_i\Lambda\mu_i/(1+\Lambda\mu_i)\) with inverse \(\Lambda_N:(0,\gamma)\to(0,\infty)\). The constants are
\[ a_N=\sqrt{c_N}N^{-\alpha/2},\quad K_\alpha=\frac{\pi}{\alpha\sin(\pi/\alpha)},\quad i_{\mathrm c}=\frac B{K_\alpha}, \]
\[ b_N=\sqrt{c_N}\big(\gamma i_{\mathrm c}^\alpha\big)^{-1/2},\quad d_\alpha=\frac{c_0\pi}{\alpha\cos(\pi/(2\alpha))}, \]
the last four used only for \(\alpha>1\). The zero-ridge drift \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\), row lag coefficients \(\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\) and integrated volatility \(\mathfrak v_i(0)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\) (with \(\overline C_{i,-\ell}=\overline C_{i,\ell}\)) are the limits as \(\ridge\downarrow0\) of the drift coefficient \(\mathfrak d_i(\ridge)=\mathfrak d(\mu_i,1)\) and of the row coefficients of the volatility chapter, at each fixed dimension (Definition SG.2).
The oversampled results share the following objects. The effective batch is \(B_{\rm eff}=B/\sw2\), the small parameter is \(\epsilon_{\rm os}=3(1-\bmom^2)\Nin/B\), and \(\hat\nu=\hat\nu_{\mathrm{out}}=\Nout^{-1}\sum_k\delta_{\mu_k}\) is the empirical spectral distribution of \(\Sigmaout\), with shape ratio \(r=\Nout/\Nin\). For a probability measure \(\nu\) on \([0,\infty)\) let \(m_\nu(c)=\int x\,\nu(\dif x)/(c+x)\), and let \(b_0(\hat s)\) be the unique root in \((0,1]\) of \(b+r\,m_\nu(\hat s/b)=1\). Then
\[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_{\nu,r}(\mu;\hat\ridge)=\frac{\sqrt2}\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{b_0(\hat s)\,\mu}{\hat s+b_0(\hat s)\,\mu}\,\dif v\quad(\hat s=\hat\ridge+v^2), \]
\[ \chi_\nu(\mu)=\int_0^\infty\varpi(y)\sqrt{1-m_\nu(\mu y)}\,\dif y,\qquad \varpi(y)=\frac2\pi\frac{\sqrt y}{(1+y)^2}, \]
and \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)=\mathfrak d^{\rm os}_{\nu,1}(\mu;0)\). The zero-ridge coefficient \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\) is the limit of \(\mathfrak d_i(\ridge)\) as \(\ridge\downarrow0\), which exists for every spectrum (Theorem SH.1(ii)).

Definition SG.3 (Profiles)
Let \(S_\alpha(V)\defeq\int_0^1V(V+t^\alpha)^{-1}\dif t\) for \(V>0\), a strictly increasing bijection onto \((0,1)\), and \(V_\alpha\defeq S_\alpha^{-1}\). For \(0<x\le1\) and \(0<p<1\) put
\[ f_x(p)\defeq\frac{V_\alpha(p)}{V_\alpha(p)+x^\alpha},\qquad U_\alpha(p)\defeq\frac{V_\alpha(p)}{p(1-p)} . \]
The bulk profiles are
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.24} \begin{gathered} \Psi_\alpha(x)\defeq c_0\int_0^1\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p\,V_\alpha(p)}}\;f_x(p)\,\dif p,\\ C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\defeq\int_0^{1-\tau}\frac{f_x(p+\tau)-f_x(p)}{\tau}\sqrt{\frac{U_\alpha(p)}{U_\alpha(p+\tau)}}\,\dif p\quad(0<\tau<1),\\ \Gamma_\alpha(x)\defeq2\int_0^1C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\,\dif\tau . \end{gathered} \end{equation}
For \(\alpha>1\), the crossover profiles are, for \(y,X>0\),
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.25} \begin{aligned} \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\defeq\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty&\sqrt q\,\Lw(q)\,r_\bmom(q)^{-\frac{\alpha+1}2}\\ &\times\frac{y\,r_\bmom(q)^\alpha}{1+y\,r_\bmom(q)^\alpha}\Big(1+(\alpha-1)\frac{q\,r_\bmom'(q)}{r_\bmom(q)}\Big)\frac{\dif q}{q}, \end{aligned} \end{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.26} \begin{aligned} G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\defeq\frac1{\pi^2}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty &\frac{\sqrt{\zeta_X(u)\big(1-\zeta_X(u)\big)\zeta_X(v)\big(1-\zeta_X(v)\big)}}{\sqrt{uv}}\\ &\times\frac{\mathsf D_\ell(u,v)\,\vartheta_\infty(u)\vartheta_\infty(v)}{\Delta_\ell^\infty(u,v)}\,\dif u\,\dif v, \end{aligned} \end{equation}
where \(\mathsf D_\ell\) and \(\mathsf M_\ell\) are the functions of (SG.20), which depend on \((m_1,m_2)=(u,v)\) only through \((Q_u,Q_v)\), and
\[ \zeta_X(u)\defeq\frac{u^\alpha}{X^\alpha+u^\alpha},\qquad \vartheta_\infty(u)\defeq\frac1{E(u)}+\frac{\alpha-1}u,\]
\[ t_\alpha(u,v)\defeq\frac{v^\alpha u-u^\alpha v}{v^\alpha-u^\alpha},\qquad \Delta_\ell^\infty\defeq1-\frac{t_\alpha(u,v)\,\mathsf M_\ell(u,v)}{uv}, \]
with \(t_\alpha(u,u)\defeq(\alpha-1)u/\alpha\), and with the convention of (SG.20) for negative lags.
These are the profiles of Table 1. The function \(S_\alpha\) is the continuum limit of the participation of a power law: a participation fraction \(p=m/\gamma\) corresponds to the level \(V_\alpha(p)\), and \(f_x(p)\) is the share of the row at position \(x=i/N\) that is whitened at that level (Section C.5). The drift profile \(\Psi_\alpha\) integrates this share against the weight \(c_0\sqrt{(1-p)/(pV_\alpha)}\) that the averaged momentum weights produce, and \(C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\) is the limiting correlation of two updates of row \(x\) that are \(\tau\gamma\) steps apart, so that \(\Gamma_\alpha\) is the integrated volatility per unit of \(\gamma\). Neither depends on \(\bmom\). For \(\alpha>1\) the crossover profiles \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\) and \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}\) describe the rows near the crossover index \(i_{\mathrm c}=B/K_\alpha\), and they keep the full momentum weight functions.
Proposition SG.5 (The profiles)
  1. (i)
    \(\Psi_\alpha\) and \(\Gamma_\alpha\) are finite, positive and continuous on \((0,1]\), \(\Psi_\alpha\) is bounded below by a positive constant there, and as \(x\downarrow0\)
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.27} \begin{gathered} \Psi_\alpha(x)\sim\begin{cases}\dfrac{c_0}{\sqrt{1-\alpha}}\log(x^{-\alpha}),&\alpha<1,\\[6pt] \dfrac23c_0\big[\log(1/x)\big]^{3/2},&\alpha=1,\\[6pt] d_\alpha\sqrt{K_\alpha}\,x^{-(\alpha-1)/2},&\alpha>1,\end{cases} \\ \Gamma_\alpha(x)\sim\begin{cases}\dfrac{x^\alpha\big[\log(x^{-\alpha})\big]^2}{1-\alpha},&\alpha<1,\\[6pt] \dfrac49x\big[\log(1/x)\big]^3,&\alpha=1,\\[6pt] \dfrac{4K_\alpha^2}{\alpha-1}\,x,&\alpha>1.\end{cases} \end{gathered} \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    For \(\alpha>1\), \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\) and \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}\) are finite, positive and continuous on \((0,\infty)\), and
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.28} \begin{gathered} \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\sim\sqrt{y/\sw2}\ (y\to\infty),\quad \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\sim d_\alpha\,y^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}\ (y\downarrow0),\\ G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\to\frac1{\sw2}\ (X\downarrow0),\quad G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\sim\frac{4K_\alpha}{\alpha-1}X\ (X\to\infty). \end{gathered} \end{equation}
Part (i) is the small-\(x\) table to the right of Table 1, with its constants: as \(x\downarrow0\) the unresolved drift profile grows only logarithmically in \(1/x\) for \(\alpha\le1\) and like \(x^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) for \(\alpha>1\), while the unresolved volatility per unit of \(\gamma\) vanishes like \(x^\alpha\) times logarithms for \(\alpha\le1\) and linearly in \(x\) for \(\alpha>1\). Part (ii) gives the two ends of the crossover profiles for \(\alpha>1\); they are the resolved and unresolved laws of Theorem SG.2 and Theorem SG.3, which the profiles join. All statements concern explicit functions and involve no dimension.
Proof sketch.
The drift profile is dominated, as \(x\downarrow0\), by small participation fractions, where the inverse \(V_\alpha\) has the power laws of Section C.5. For \(\alpha<1\), \(V_\alpha(p)\sim(1-\alpha)p\) turns the integrand, without the factor \(c_0\), into \((1-\alpha)^{-1/2}(p+x^\alpha/(1-\alpha))^{-1}\) on a fixed small interval, whose integral is \(\log(x^{-\alpha})(1+o(1))\); at \(\alpha=1\) the extra logarithm of \(V_1(p)\sim p/\log(1/p)\) gives the power \(3/2\). For \(\alpha>1\) the substitution \(p=K_\alpha xu\) isolates a convergent integral (Lemma SG.9),
\[ \Psi_\alpha(x)\sim c_0\sqrt{K_\alpha}\,x^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\int_0^\infty\frac{u^{(\alpha-1)/2}}{1+u^\alpha}\,\dif u =d_\alpha\sqrt{K_\alpha}\,x^{-(\alpha-1)/2}. \]
The volatility profile is a positive double integral, and for \(\alpha\le1\) it is asymptotically the square of a single integral. With \(A_x(p)=\sqrt{f_x(p)(1-f_x(p))(1-p)/p}\), a partial-fraction identity in the variable of the spectrum gives exactly (Lemma SG.21)
\[ \Gamma_\alpha(x)=2\int_{0<p<q<1}\frac{A_x(p)A_x(q)}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}(p,q)}\,\dif p\,\dif q,\qquad \Big(\int_0^1A_x\Big)^2=\frac{x^\alpha\Psi_\alpha(x)^2}{c_0^2}, \]
where \(\Delta_{\mathrm c}\le1\) is the continuum determinant of the lag kernel. Without the determinant the double integral is the square on the right. For \(\alpha\le1\) the integral of \(A_x\) is spread over a range of scales of \(p\) whose logarithmic length tends to infinity, so most of the double integral comes from pairs with \(q>Rp\) and \(p,q<\eta\), where \(1-\Delta_{\mathrm c}\le\eta+1/R\); the diagonal strip \(q\le Rp\) and the upper end \(p\to1\) are of lower order. This gives \(\Gamma_\alpha(x)\sim x^\alpha\Psi_\alpha(x)^2/c_0^2\) and the volatility equivalents of (i). For \(\alpha>1\) the mass of \(A_x\) sits at \(p\asymp x\), the determinant matters, and the substitution \(p=K_\alpha xr\) turns \(\Gamma_\alpha(x)/(K_\alpha x)\) into a double integral over the lag and the participation, with value \(4K_\alpha/(\alpha-1)\) (Lemma SG.23).
The ends of the crossover profiles in (ii) come from the two mechanisms of Section C.5. As \(y\to\infty\) only small participations count in \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\) and the head Lorentzian gives \(\sqrt{y/\sw2}\); as \(y\downarrow0\) the substitution \(m=y^{-1/\alpha}w\) moves it to large participation, where averaging gives the integral above (Lemma SG.13). For \(G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X)\) the rescaling \(m=Xr\) sends every lag term to \(\bmom^{\abs\ell}\) as \(X\downarrow0\), with sum \(1/\sw2\), and as \(X\to\infty\) the concentration of the lag kernel on \(m_2=m_1+\ell\) (Lemma SG.22) gives the double integral \(4K_\alpha/(\alpha-1)\) (Lemma SG.23). Finiteness, positivity and continuity follow from majorants that are locally uniform in the argument.

Theorem SG.2 (Drift crossover)
Assume Assumption S4 and let \(i=i_N\to\infty\) be a row sequence.
  1. (i)
    Let \(0<\alpha\le1\) and put
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.32} \begin{gathered} d\defeq\frac{c_0}{\sqrt{1-\alpha}},\quad i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\defeq\Big[\frac{BN^{\alpha-1}}{\sw2d^2\log^2\gamma}\Big]^{1/\alpha},\quad a_{\mathfrak d}\defeq d\,a_N\log\gamma\quad(\alpha<1);\\ d\defeq\frac23c_0,\quad i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\defeq\frac{B}{\sw2d^2\log^3\gamma},\quad a_{\mathfrak d}\defeq d\,a_N(\log\gamma)^{3/2}\quad(\alpha=1). \end{gathered} \end{equation}
    If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/a_{\mathfrak d}\to1+x^{-\alpha/2}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to0\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to\infty\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\log\big((N/i)^\alpha\big)\ \ (\alpha<1),\qquad \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d\,a_N\big[\log(N/i)\big]^{3/2}\ \ (\alpha=1). \]
  2. (ii)
    Let \(\alpha>1\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to x\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/b_N\to\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(x^{-\alpha})\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to0\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to\infty\), then \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim d_\alpha\sqrt{c_NK_\alpha}\,N^{-1/2}\,i^{-(\alpha-1)/2}. \]
This theorem gives the drift column of Table 1 for \(\bmom>0\), with the crossover between its two rows. Rows below the crossover index are resolved: \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}\), which is \(\mathfrak d_i\sim\sqrt{\mu_iB_{\rm eff}/(2N)}\), the square-root law of the large-batch section. Rows above it are not whitened individually, and their drift \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(i/N)\), with \(a_N=\sqrt{c_N}N^{-\alpha/2}\), is a profile of \(i/N\) that does not depend on \(\bmom\); for \(i\ll N\) it decays only logarithmically (\(\alpha\le1\)) or like \(i^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) (\(\alpha>1\)), far more slowly than \(\sqrt{\mu_i}\). For \(\alpha\le1\) the two laws add at the crossover, so that \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)/a_{\mathfrak d}\to1+x^{-\alpha/2}\); for \(\alpha>1\) the crossover is described by the profile \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\), which keeps the momentum. This sketch also proves the drift half of the uniform approximation behind Corollary SG.1.
Proof sketch.
The drift is the sum of a head and a tail, each with an explicit limit (Section C.5). Changing variables from \(u\) to the participation \(m\) gives (Lemma SG.8)
\[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)=\frac1{\pi\gamma}\int_0^\gamma\mathcal A(m)\,\mathcal B_i(m)\,E(m)\vartheta_N(m)\,\dif m, \]
\[ \mathcal B_i(\gamma p)=a_N\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p\,V_N(p)}}\,\frac{V_N(p)}{V_N(p)+x^\alpha},\quad x=\frac iN, \]
where \(\vartheta_N=-\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log s_N\) comes from the Jacobian of \(u^2=s_N(m)\), and \(E\vartheta_N\) is within \(C(1/m+1/(\gamma-m))\) of one for \(m\ge M\). On \(m>M\) the averaging of Section C.5 replaces \(\mathcal A\) by \(\sqrt{2\pi}\), the factor \(E\vartheta_N\) is replaced by one, and \(V_N\) by \(V_\alpha\), all with relative error \(C/M+o(1)\) uniformly in the row; the tail is therefore \(a_N\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\), the profile with the integral restricted to \(p\ge M/\gamma\) (Lemma SG.10). The head \(m\le M\) is at most \(C_M\sqrt{\mu_i/\gamma}\) for every row and equals \(\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}(1+o(1))\) on resolved rows (Lemma SG.11). For bulk rows, \(i/N\to x_0>0\), the head is \(o(a_N)\) and \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)\sim a_N\Psi_\alpha(x_0)\).
For \(\alpha\le1\) the crossover is the row at which head and tail are equal. With \(c_N=1\), write the head in units of \(a_N\) as
\[ W\defeq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}}{a_N}=\sw2^{-1/2}e^{(T-\log\gamma)/2},\qquad T=\log(x^{-\alpha})\ (\alpha<1),\quad T=\log(1/x)\ (\alpha=1), \]
and recall \(\Psi_\alpha(x)\sim dT^\nu\) with \(\nu=1\) for \(\alpha<1\) and \(\nu=3/2\) for \(\alpha=1\) (Proposition SG.5). The cutoff \(p\ge M/\gamma\) removes a part of the profile that is negligible as long as \(W\lesssim(\log\gamma)^\nu\), and when \(W\gg(\log\gamma)^\nu\) both profiles are \(o(W)\) (Lemma SG.12). In every case, after \(M\to\infty\),
\[ \sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)=a_N\big(W+\Psi_\alpha(x)\big)\big(1+o(1)\big), \]
which is the drift approximant of (SG.29). The index \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) is where \(W=d(\log\gamma)^\nu\): at \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\to x_0\) one has \(W=d(\log\gamma)^\nu x_0^{-\alpha/2}(1+o(1))\) and \(T=\log\gamma+O(\log\log\gamma)\), so both terms are multiples of \(a_{\mathfrak d}=d\,a_N(\log\gamma)^\nu\), which gives \(1+x_0^{-\alpha/2}\). On the rows \(i\asymp i_\#\), where \(i_\#=(c_N\resscale)^{1/\alpha}\) is the no-momentum threshold of Proposition SG.6, the drift exceeds the no-momentum drift scale \((\gamma\resscale)^{-1/2}\) by a factor of order \(\log\gamma\), which comes from the growth \(\log Q_m\sim2\abs{\log\bmom}\,m\) of the momentum participation (Remark SG.2); this is why \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) lies below the no-momentum threshold \(i_\#\) by a factor of order \((\log\gamma)^{-2/\alpha}\) (Proposition SG.6). The main-text display (15), which also writes \(i_\#\), is for \(\bmom>0\) and \(\alpha\le1\) of the order of \(i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}\) itself; in this chapter \(i_\#\) always means the no-momentum threshold.
For \(\alpha>1\) the crossover sits at participation of order one, where the weights cannot be averaged, so its profile keeps the momentum. At \(i\asymp B\) put \(y=(i_{\mathrm c}/i)^\alpha\). The scaling \(c_N\Lambda_N(m)\sim(Bm/K_\alpha)^\alpha\) of Section C.5 gives \(\Lambda_N(m)\mu_i\to ym^\alpha\) and \(m\Lambda_N'(m)/\Lambda_N(m)\to\alpha\), so that on compact sets of \(m\) the drift integrand, divided by \(b_N\), converges to that of ((SG.46))
\[ \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\mathcal A(m)E(m)\vartheta_\infty(m)\,m^{-\frac{\alpha+1}2}\,\frac{ym^\alpha}{1+ym^\alpha}\,\dif m, \]
while the head bounds and the tail bound \(CK_\alpha^{-\alpha/2}M^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) make small and large \(m\) negligible (Lemma SG.13). On the two windows the head and the tail are estimated directly, as for \(\alpha\le1\) (Lemma SG.13), and the identities \(b_N\sqrt y=\sqrt{\mu_i/\gamma}\) and \(b_Nd_\alpha y^{(\alpha-1)/(2\alpha)}=a_Nd_\alpha\sqrt{K_\alpha}(i/N)^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\) show that the results match the two endpoints of \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\) (Proposition SG.5(ii)).

Theorem SG.3 (Volatility crossover and bulk profile)
Assume Assumption S4 and let \(i=i_N\) be a row sequence.
  1. (i)
    (Bulk.) If \(i/N\to x\in(0,1]\) and \(\ell=\ell_N\) are integers with \(\ell/\gamma\to\tau\in(0,1)\), then
    \[ \overline C_{i,\ell}(0)\longrightarrow C_{\alpha,x}(\tau),\qquad \frac{\mathfrak v_i(0)}{\gamma}\longrightarrow\Gamma_\alpha(x), \]
    locally uniformly in \(x\in(0,1]\) and \(\tau\in(0,1)\).
  2. (ii)
    Let \(0<\alpha\le1\), let \(i\to\infty\), and put
    \begin{equation}\tag{SG.33} i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\defeq c_0^{2/\alpha}\,i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak d}=\begin{cases}N\big[(1-\alpha)/(\sw2\gamma\log^2\gamma)\big]^{1/\alpha},&\alpha<1,\\ 9B/(4\sw2\log^3\gamma),&\alpha=1.\end{cases} \end{equation}
    If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to y\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sw2\,\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1+2c_0y^{\alpha/2}+y^\alpha\), uniformly for \(y\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to0\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c,\mathfrak v}\to\infty\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac{\gamma(i/N)^\alpha\big[\log((N/i)^\alpha)\big]^2}{1-\alpha}\quad(\alpha<1),\qquad \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac49\,\frac iB\,\big[\log(N/i)\big]^3\quad(\alpha=1). \]
  3. (iii)
    Let \(\alpha>1\) and \(i\to\infty\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to X_0\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to G_{\alpha,\bmom}(X_0)\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to0\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2\). If \(i/i_{\mathrm c}\to\infty\), then \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), and if moreover \(i/N\to0\),
    \[ \mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\frac{4K_\alpha^2}{\alpha-1}\,\frac iB . \]
This theorem gives the volatility column of Table 1 for \(\bmom>0\). On resolved rows \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\to1/\sw2=B_{\rm eff}/B\): the row is fully whitened at every step, and (as the proof shows for \(\alpha>1\)) momentum correlates the updates \(\ell\) steps apart by \(\bmom^{\abs\ell}\). On unresolved rows \(\mathfrak v_i(0)\sim\gamma\Gamma_\alpha(i/N)\), which grows with \(\gamma=N/B\), and part (i) identifies the correlation of updates \(\tau\gamma\) steps apart in a bulk row as \(C_{\alpha,x}(\tau)\). For \(\alpha\le1\) the crossover profile \(1+2c_0y^{\alpha/2}+y^\alpha\) is not a sum of the two endpoint laws: it is the second moment of a sum of two correlated terms. Proof sketch.
The zero-ridge volatility is a positive double integral over two participations, summed over the lags (Proposition SG.4):
\[ \mathfrak v_i(0)=\frac1{\pi^2}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\int_0^\gamma\!\!\int_0^\gamma W_i(m_1)W_i(m_2)\,\frac{\mathsf D_\ell(m_1,m_2)}{\Delta^N_\ell(m_1,m_2)}\,\dif m_1\,\dif m_2 . \]
It is obtained from the closed-form lag kernel of Section C.3 by writing the regularized filter as an integral of resolvents and changing variables to the participation. Here \(W_i\) is the weight of the row, \(\mathsf D_\ell\) correlates two buffers \(\ell\) steps apart through the past batches they share, and the determinant \(\Delta^N_\ell\in(0,1]\) of the kernel is close to one when the smaller participation is much smaller than the larger one and than \(\gamma\) (Lemma SG.14).
In the bulk, the lag \(\ell\) couples the participation \(m\) to \(m+\ell\), because shifting the buffer by \(\ell\) steps moves every past batch by \(\ell\) places. Exactly, \(\mathsf D_\ell=(r_\bmom(q_1e^t)-r_\bmom(q_1))/(2\sinh(t/2))\) with \(t=\log(\bmom^{2\ell}q_2/q_1)\), and for large \(u\), \(r_\bmom(e^u)\) is within \(Ce^{-u/2}\) of a function that grows by exactly one when \(u\) grows by \(2\abs{\log\bmom}\). The kernel \(\mathsf D_\ell/(\pi^2E(m_1)E(m_2))\) therefore concentrates on the shell \(m_2=m_1+\ell+O(1)\) and carries mass one per unit of \(m_1\), by the identity (Lemma SG.22)
\[ \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{t}{2\sinh(t/2)}\,\dif t=\pi^2 . \]

The lag sum is then a Riemann sum with mesh \(1/\gamma\); lags beyond \((1+\eta)\gamma\) contribute \(O(\gamma\bmom^{\ell-\gamma})\) each, because the later buffer then shares no whitened batch, and \(\mathfrak v_i(0)/\gamma\to2\int_0^1C_{\alpha,x}=\Gamma_\alpha(x)\).

When \(\epsilon_{\rm os}=3(1-\bmom^2)\Nin/B\) stays below a fixed \(\bar\epsilon<1\), the expected regularized update is, up to an error of vanishing Frobenius norm, a function of \(\Sigmaout\) applied to the normalized error \(\Dt/\sqrt{\cR(t)}\): the factor \(\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/\Nin}\) times the oversampled profile \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}\) evaluated at \(\Sigmaout\), times an operator \(\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\) that is within \(\epsilon_{\rm os}/(1-\epsilon_{\rm os})\) of the identity at every dimension. On packed modes and at zero rescaled ridge the profile is within constants of \(\sqrt{\mu/2}\) (Corollary SH.2), which is the square-root preconditioner of the main text. The statement holds at ridges in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), in the proportional regime; it says nothing about the algorithm at zero ridge.
Proof sketch.
For an isotropic input side the deterministic equivalent of the expected update is an explicit function of \(\Sigmaout\) applied to the error. The drift theorem gives \(\EE\,\Qridge\ridge(\Mt)\) up to a vanishing Frobenius error as an integral, over the spectral parameters \(s=\ridge+u^2\), of a candidate built from the deterministic resolvents (Theorem SC.2, Section C.2). With \(\Sigmain=\Id\) these resolvents are \((s\Id+\tilde s_1\Sigmaout)^{-1}\) and \((s+s_1)^{-1}\Id\), so the candidate is the scalar kernel \(\mathsf k(s;\cdot)\) evaluated at \(\Sigmaout\) and applied to \(\Dt/\sqrt{2\cR(t)}\), and the integral is exactly ((SH.13))
\[ \Gtildebar_\ridge=\mathfrak d_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\,\frac{\Dt}{\sqrt{\cR(t)}} . \]
The oversampled law rewrites \(\mathfrak d_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\) at every finite dimension, with a relative error that depends on nothing but \(\epsilon_{\rm os}\). By the sandwich of Section C.5, applied eigenvalue by eigenvalue (Theorem SH.1(iv)),
\[ \mathfrak d_\ridge(\Sigmaout)=\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}{\Nin}}\;\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\,\mathfrak d^{\rm os}\Big(\Sigmaout;\frac{\ridge}{\sw2\gin}\Big),\qquad \operatorname{spec}\vartheta_\ridge(\Sigmaout)\subset\big[1-\epsilon_{\rm os},(1-\epsilon_{\rm os})^{-1}\big]. \]
Inserting this into the previous display gives the corollary. The bound on \(\vartheta_\ridge\) holds at every dimension, uniformly in the spectrum, the eigenvalue and the ridge, so the dimension limit of the drift theorem is taken with a factor that is already controlled and no exchange of limits occurs. Because the error is relative to the profile, the law is accurate also on modes where \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}(\mu)\ll\sqrt\mu\).
The profile sees the ridge only through the rescaled ridge \(\hat\ridge=\ridge/(\sw2\gin)\), and this decides when the square-root law is visible. The profile is at most \(\min\{\sqrt{\mu/2},\,\mu/\sqrt{2\hat\ridge}\}\) (Lemma SH.2), so at a fixed normalized ridge and \(\gin\downarrow0\) it becomes linear in \(\mu\); the square-root law concerns bounded \(\hat\ridge\), in particular the zero-ridge coefficient of Corollary SH.2.

Proposition SH.1 (Head modes)
  1. (i)
    For every probability measure \(\nu\), every \(\mu>0\) and every \(\delta>0\),
    \[ \frac{\delta}{2(1+\sqrt\delta)^2}\,\nu\big([\delta\mu,\infty)\big)\le1-\chi_\nu(\mu)\le\delta+\nu\big([\delta\mu,\infty)\big). \]
  2. (ii)
    For every sequence of probability measures \(\nu_N\) and numbers \(\mu_N>0\), one has \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}_{\nu_N}(\mu_N)/\sqrt{\mu_N/2}\to1\) if and only if \(\nu_N([\delta\mu_N,\infty))\to0\) for every \(\delta>0\).
This proposition supports the statement of Section 4.1 that for modes high in the spectrum the constant of the square-root law tends to one. By the representation of Section C.5, \(\mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)/\sqrt{\mu/2}\) is the efficiency \(\chi_\nu(\mu)\), and part (i) bounds its defect by the mass of the spectrum above a fraction \(\delta\) of the mode. Part (ii) says that the efficiency tends to one exactly for head modes, which lie above all but a vanishing fraction of the spectrum.
Proof sketch.
The defect \(1-\chi_\nu(\mu)\) is within a factor two of an exact average. The average of the soft count against \(\varpi\) has a closed form, by partial fractions in \(y=w^2\) (Lemma SH.9):
\[ \int_0^\infty\varpi(y)\,\frac{x}{\mu y+x}\,\dif y=\frac{x}{(\sqrt\mu+\sqrt x)^2},\quad m^\varpi_\nu(\mu)\defeq\int_0^\infty\varpi(y)\,m_\nu(\mu y)\,\dif y=\int\frac{x\,\nu(\dif x)}{(\sqrt\mu+\sqrt x)^2}. \]
The elementary bounds \(m/2\le1-\sqrt{1-m}\le m\) on \([0,1]\), integrated against \(\varpi\), give \(\frac12m^\varpi_\nu(\mu)\le1-\chi_\nu(\mu)\le m^\varpi_\nu(\mu)\) (Lemma SH.10).
The averaged count is then bounded by splitting the spectrum at \(\delta\mu\). Its integrand \(x/(\sqrt\mu+\sqrt x)^2\) is nondecreasing in \(x\), smaller than \(\delta\) below \(\delta\mu\), at most one above, and at least \(\delta/(1+\sqrt\delta)^2\) on \([\delta\mu,\infty)\), which gives (i). Part (ii) follows by letting \(N\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\) in the upper bound, and from the lower bound at each fixed \(\delta\) for the converse.

Corollary SH.2 (Packed modes)
Assume \(\Nout=\Nin=N\) and \(\epsilon_{\rm os}<1\), and let \(\mathfrak d_i(0)\) be the zero-ridge coefficient of Theorem SH.1(ii). For every mode with \(\mu_i>0\),
\[ \mathfrak d_i(0)\le\frac1{1-\epsilon_{\rm os}}\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}{2N}}\,\sqrt{\mu_i}, \]
and if \(\mu_i\) is \((K,\delta)\)-packed, then
\[ \mathfrak d_i(0)\ge(1-\epsilon_{\rm os})\,C_*\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{1+K}}\,\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}{2N}}\,\sqrt{\mu_i}, \qquad C_*=\frac12+\frac{2\log2}\pi . \]
This corollary is Theorem 4.1 of the main text, whose constant \(0.94\) is \(C_*\) rounded. When the effective batch exceeds \(3(1+\bmom)^2N\), the drift of every mode is at most the square-root law \(\sqrt{B_{\rm eff}/(2N)}\sqrt{\mu_i}\), up to the factor \((1-\epsilon_{\rm os})^{-1}\), and it is within a constant of that law whenever a fixed fraction \(\delta\) of the spectrum lies below a fixed multiple \(K\mu_i\) of the mode. Both bounds concern the deterministic zero-ridge coefficient.
Proof sketch.
The oversampled law reduces the coefficient to the profile. At zero ridge (Theorem SH.1(iii), Section C.5),
\[ \mathfrak d_i(0)=\vartheta_i\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}N}\;\mathfrak d^{\rm os}_{\hat\nu}(\mu_i)=\vartheta_i\sqrt{\frac{B_{\rm eff}}N}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}2}\;\chi_{\hat\nu}(\mu_i),\qquad \vartheta_i\in\big[1-\epsilon_{\rm os},(1-\epsilon_{\rm os})^{-1}\big], \]
and \(\chi\le1\) gives the upper bound.
The efficiency is at least \(C_*\) times the square root of the soft fraction of the spectrum below the mode. Since \(1-m_\nu\) is nondecreasing, and since \(\mu y/(\mu y+x)\ge y\,\mu/(\mu+x)\) for \(y\le1\),
\[ 1-m_\nu(\mu y)\ge1-m_\nu(\mu)\quad(y\ge1),\qquad 1-m_\nu(\mu y)\ge y\,\big(1-m_\nu(\mu)\big)\quad(y\le1). \]
Inserted in the definition of \(\chi_\nu\), these give \(\chi_\nu(\mu)\ge\sqrt{1-m_\nu(\mu)}\cdot\frac2\pi\big[(\log2-\frac12)+(\frac\pi4+\frac12)\big]=C_*\sqrt{1-m_\nu(\mu)}\), the two brackets being \(\int_0^1y(1+y)^{-2}\dif y\) and \(\int_1^\infty\sqrt y(1+y)^{-2}\dif y\) (Theorem SH.2(i)). Packing bounds the soft fraction below: \(\mu/(\mu+x)\ge1/(1+K)\) for \(x\le K\mu\), so \(1-m_{\hat\nu}(\mu_i)\ge\hat\nu([0,K\mu_i])/(1+K)\ge\delta/(1+K)\) (Theorem SH.2(ii)), which gives the lower bound.

Remark SH.3 (Where packing holds)
The count \(\#\{k:\mu_k\le K\mu\}\) is nondecreasing in \(\mu\), so if the smallest positive eigenvalue is \((K,\delta)\)-packed, then every mode is. If all eigenvalues lie in \([\mu_{\min},\kappa\mu_{\min}]\) with \(\mu_{\min}>0\), every mode is \((\kappa,1)\)-packed, since \(\mu_k\le\kappa\mu_{\min}\le\kappa\mu_i\) for all \(k\). If the spectrum consists of clusters of equal eigenvalues, each holding at least \(pN\) of them, every mode is \((1,p)\)-packed, since its own cluster lies in \([0,\mu_i]\). For the geometric spectrum \(\mu_k=q^k\) with fixed \(q\in(0,1)\), the smallest eigenvalue \(\mu_N\) has only \(1+\lfloor\log K/\log(1/q)\rfloor\) eigenvalues in \([0,K\mu_N]\), a vanishing fraction as \(N\to\infty\), so it is not \((K,\delta)\)-packed for any fixed \(K,\delta\) once \(N\) is large. For the power law \(\mu_k=c_Nk^{-\alpha}\), \(1\le k\le N\), every mode is \((2,1-2^{-1/\alpha})\)-packed, because \(\mu_k\le2\mu_i\) for every \(k\ge2^{-1/\alpha}i\), and there are at least \(N-2^{-1/\alpha}i\ge(1-2^{-1/\alpha})N\) such \(k\). Conversely, packing is necessary for a lower bound of this form: by Proposition SH.2(vi), along a sequence of spectra the efficiency \(\chi_{\nu_N}(\mu_N)\) stays bounded below only if, for some \(K\), the fraction of the spectrum in \([0,K\mu_N]\) stays bounded below, and by Proposition SH.2(ii),(iii) a mode far below most of the spectrum receives drift of gradient-descent order up to a logarithm.
This remark supports the examples after Definition 4.1 in the main text. Packing is monotone in the mode, so it is enough to check it at the smallest eigenvalue; bounded condition numbers and spectra made of large clusters are packed with explicit constants, and every mode of a power law is \((2,1-2^{-1/\alpha})\)-packed, because at least that fraction of the indices lies beyond \(2^{-1/\alpha}i\). A geometric spectrum is not packed at its smallest eigenvalue, since only boundedly many eigenvalues lie within a factor \(K\) of it. Packing is also necessary for a lower bound of the form of Corollary SH.2: by Proposition SH.2(vi) the efficiency stays bounded below only if a fixed fraction of the spectrum lies below a fixed multiple \(K\mu\) of the mode.
For a probability measure \(\nu\), \(\ell\ge0\), \(\mu>0\) and \(\Gamma\ge0\) put
\[ \mathcal I_\nu(\ell)=\int_{(\ell,\infty)}\frac{\nu(\dif x)}x,\qquad \mathcal I_\nu=\mathcal I_\nu(0),\qquad \mathcal E(\mu,\Gamma)=\frac{\sqrt2}\pi\,\mu\int_0^\infty\frac{\min\{\sqrt c,\,c\sqrt\Gamma\}}{(c+\mu)^2}\,\dif c, \]
and let \(C_*=\frac12+\frac{2\log2}\pi\) (Lemma SH.12 and Theorem SH.2).
Proposition SH.2 (Tail modes)
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure and \(\mu>0\).
  1. (i)
    (Split bound.) For every \(\ell\ge0\) with \(\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)<\infty\),
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\le\sqrt{\nu([0,\ell])}\,\sqrt{\frac\mu2}+\mathcal E\big(\mu,\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)\big). \]
  2. (ii)
    (Gradient-descent-like upper bound.) If \(\nu(\{0\})=0\) and \(\mathcal I_\nu<\infty\), then
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\le\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\mathcal I_\nu}\;\mu\Big[\log\Big(1+\frac1{\mu\mathcal I_\nu}\Big)+2\Big]. \]
  3. (iii)
    (Asymptotics.) If \(\nu(\{0\})=0\) and \(\mathcal I_\nu<\infty\), then, for fixed \(\nu\),
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\sim\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\mathcal I_\nu}\;\mu\log\frac1\mu\qquad(\mu\downarrow0). \]
  4. (iv)
    (Counts.) For every \(K>0\),
    \[ C_*\sqrt{\frac{\nu([0,K\mu])}{1+K}}\le\chi_\nu(\mu)\le\sqrt{\nu([0,K\mu])}+\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt K}\big[\log(1+K)+2\big]. \]
  5. (v)
    (Universal lower bound.) If \(\nu([0,X])=1\) for some \(X>0\), then
    \[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)\ge\frac1\pi\,\frac{\mu}{\sqrt X}\Big[\log\Big(1+\frac X\mu\Big)-\frac{X}{X+\mu}\Big]\ge\frac1{2\pi}\,\frac{\mu}{\sqrt X}\,\log\frac X\mu . \]
  6. (vi)
    (Sequences.) For probability measures \(\nu_N\) and numbers \(\mu_N>0\) put \(\chi_N\defeq\chi_{\nu_N}(\mu_N)\). Then \(\liminf_N\chi_N>0\) if and only if \(\liminf_N\nu_N([0,K\mu_N])>0\) for some \(K>0\); and \(\chi_N\to0\) if and only if \(\nu_N([0,K\mu_N])\to0\) for every \(K>0\).
This proposition supports the last sentence of Section 4.1: a mode that is not packed, such as an isolated small eigenvalue, can be as slow as under gradient descent, up to a logarithm. If the spectrum has a finite mean inverse \(\mathcal I_\nu\) and no atom at zero, the profile of a small mode is of order \(\mu\log(1/\mu)\) instead of \(\sqrt\mu\), by (ii) and (iii); by (v) it is never smaller than that for a bounded spectrum. Part (iv) brackets the efficiency by the fraction of the spectrum in \([0,K\mu]\), and (vi) turns this into the criterion along sequences of spectra.
Proof sketch.
All bounds come from the first form of the representation of Section C.5,
\[ \mathfrak d^{\rm os}_\nu(\mu)=\frac{\sqrt2}\pi\,\mu\int_0^\infty\frac{\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c)}}{(c+\mu)^2}\,\dif c,\qquad \Upsilon_\nu(c)=c\big(1-m_\nu(c)\big)=c^2\int\frac{\nu(\dif x)}{c+x}, \]
in which the mode enters only through the kernel \(\mu(c+\mu)^{-2}\), which concentrates near \(c=\mu\). Splitting the spectrum at \(\ell\) and using \(c/(c+x)\le c/x\) above \(\ell\) gives \(1-m_\nu(c)\le\nu([0,\ell])+c\,\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)\), hence \(\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c)}\le\sqrt{c\,\nu([0,\ell])}+\min\{\sqrt c,c\sqrt{\mathcal I_\nu(\ell)}\}\), and the two terms give the two terms of (i). At \(\ell=0\) the first term vanishes, and splitting the remaining integral at \(c=1/\mathcal I_\nu\), where the minimum changes branch, gives the logarithm of (ii) (Lemma SH.12).
The matching lower bounds use that \(\Upsilon_\nu(c)/c^2\) is nonincreasing in \(c\). Below a fixed \(c_1\) this gives \(\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c)}\ge c\sqrt{\Upsilon_\nu(c_1)}/c_1\), and the integral of \(c(c+\mu)^{-2}\) over \((0,c_1)\) is at least \(\log(1+c_1/\mu)-1\); letting \(\mu\downarrow0\) and then \(c_1\downarrow0\), so that \(\Upsilon_\nu(c_1)/c_1^2\uparrow\mathcal I_\nu\), gives (iii). If the spectrum lies in \([0,X]\), then \(\Upsilon_\nu(c)\ge c^2/(2X)\) for \(c\le X\), and the elementary inequality \(\log(1+k)-k/(1+k)\ge\frac12\log k\) gives (v) (Lemma SH.13). Part (iv) combines the square-root law of Corollary SH.2 (Theorem SH.2) with (i) at \(\ell=K\mu\), where \(\mathcal I_\nu(K\mu)\le1/(K\mu)\), and (vi) follows from (iv) by choosing \(K\) large.

Corollary SH.5 (Power-law spectra)
Let \(\alpha>0\), \(N\ge1\) and \(c_N>0\), let \(\mu_k=c_Nk^{-\alpha}\) for \(1\le k\le N\), and put \(\nu_N\defeq N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^N\delta_{\mu_k}\). For \(0<x\le1\) put
\[ \Xi_\alpha(x)\defeq\int_0^\infty\varpi(y)\Big(\int_0^1\frac{y\,u^\alpha}{y\,u^\alpha+x^\alpha}\,\dif u\Big)^{1/2}\dif y . \]
  1. (i)
    For every \(1\le i\le N\),
    \[ 0\le\chi_{\nu_N}(\mu_i)-\Xi_\alpha(i/N)\le N^{-1/2}. \]
  2. (ii)
    If \(i\le\varepsilon N\) with \(\varepsilon\in(0,1]\), then \(1-2\varepsilon^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)}\le\chi_{\nu_N}(\mu_i)\le1\). Consequently, for every sequence \(\varepsilon_N\to0\),
    \[ \max_{i\le\varepsilon_NN}\Big|\frac{\mathfrak d^{\rm os}_{\nu_N}(\mu_i)}{\sqrt{\mu_i/2}}-1\Big|\longrightarrow0 . \]
  3. (iii)
    The function \(\Xi_\alpha\) is nonincreasing on \((0,1]\), and
    \[ 1-\Xi_\alpha(x)\le2x^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)},\qquad \Xi_\alpha(x)\ge\frac{C_*}{\sqrt{2(1+\alpha)}} . \]
  4. (iv)
    For every \(1\le i\le N\),
    \[ \frac{C_*}{\sqrt{2(1+\alpha)}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}2}\le\mathfrak d^{\rm os}_{\nu_N}(\mu_i)\le\sqrt{\frac{\mu_i}2}. \]
  5. (v)
    If \(N^\alpha\ge2\), the acceleration of Corollary SH.4 for \(\nu=\nu_N\), \(\mu_+=\mu_1\) and \(\mu=\mu_N\), for which \(\varkappa=N^\alpha\), satisfies
    \[ \frac{C_*}{\sqrt{2(1+\alpha)}}\,N^{\alpha/2}\le\mathcal A\le\frac{\sqrt2}{C_*}\,N^{\alpha/2}. \]
This corollary is the large-batch paragraph of Section 4.2. For a power law every mode keeps the square-root law up to the constant \(C_*/\sqrt{2(1+\alpha)}\), which depends only on \(\alpha\), by (iv); the modes \(i\ll N\) have efficiency tending to one, by (ii), which with the factor \(\vartheta_i\to1\) of the oversampled law as \(\epsilon_{\rm os}\to0\) gives \(\mathfrak d_i\sim\sqrt{\mu_iB_{\rm eff}/(2N)}\) (Theorem SH.1). By (v) the slowest mode contracts faster than under gradient descent, relative to the top mode, by a factor of order \(N^{\alpha/2}\), the square root of the condition number. Proof sketch.

\[ 1-m_{\nu_N}(c)=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^N\phi_c(k/N),\qquad \phi_c(u)=\frac{c\,u^\alpha}{c\,u^\alpha+1}, \]
and since \(\phi_c\) is nondecreasing with values in \([0,1)\) the sum exceeds \(\int_0^1\phi_c\) by at most \(1/N\).
