Theorem SF.8 (Small-aspect integrated volatility)
Let \(\bmom\in(0,1)\), \(\Nin=\Nout=N\) and \(\gamma=N/B\le(1+\bmom^2)/96\). Then for every \(\ell\ge1\)
\[ \big|\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)-\Omega(\bmom^\ell)\big|\le\frac{96\gamma}{1+\bmom^2}\,\Omega(\bmom^\ell), \]
and the zero-ridge volatility of Definition SF.1 satisfies
\[ \Big|\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}}N-V_\bmom\Big|\le\frac{96\gamma}{1+\bmom^2}\,\big(V_\bmom-1\big),\qquad \Big|\frac{\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}}{N\,V_\bmom}-1\Big|\le96\gamma . \]

Definitions and assumptions used

Definition SF.1 (Zero-ridge isotropic volatility)
Definition SF.1 (Zero-ridge isotropic volatility)
Let \(\bmom\in[0,1)\) and let \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge)\) be the number of Theorem SF.2(ii) at momentum \(\bmom\). The zero-ridge isotropic volatility at momentum \(\bmom\) is
\[ \mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}\defeq\lim_{\ridge\downarrow0}\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso}}(\ridge), \]
the limit being taken at this fixed \(\bmom\), whenever it exists.
At large batch, in the square case, the zero-ridge volatility with momentum is \(NV_\bmom\) up to a relative error \(96\gamma\), uniformly in \(\bmom\) when \(\gamma\le1/96\). This is the large-batch form \(\mathfrak v_\bmom\approx c_\bmom NB_{\rm eff}/B\) of (9), with \(c_\bmom=\sw2V_\bmom\) (Lemma SF.10). The correlation of two updates \(\ell\) steps apart is \(\Omega(\bmom^\ell)\), not the correlation \(\bmom^\ell\) of the buffers. Since \(V_\bmom<1/\sw2\), momentum amplifies the volatility by less than the effective batch \(B_{\rm eff}=B/\sw2\) by which it acts on the drift (Theorem SF.7); Remark SF.1 draws the consequence.
Proof sketch.
The volatility is a sum over lags of explicit double integrals. By Proposition SF.3(ii), \(\mathfrak v_{\mathrm{iso},\bmom}/N=1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\), where the term \(1\) is the same-time value at zero ridge and \(\overline C_{1,\ell}(0)\) is the correlation of two updates \(\ell\) steps apart. In the square case this correlation is a double integral over two spectral parameters of a kernel built from the two roots \(q=q(-x^2)\) and \(q'=q(-y^2)\) (Lemma SF.11):
\[ \overline C_{1,\ell}(0)=\frac4{\pi^2}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty\frac{b_\ell\,q\,q'}{\gamma^3\,\Delta_\ell}\,\dif x\,\dif y,\qquad \Delta_\ell=\Big(1-\frac{qq'e_\ell}\gamma\Big)^2-\frac{qq'b_\ell^2}{\gamma^2}. \]
Here \(b_\ell=\bmom^\ell\,(r_\bmom(q)-r_\bmom(\bmom^{2\ell}q'))/(q-\bmom^{2\ell}q')\) is \(\bmom^\ell\) times a divided difference of the participation count, close to \(\bmom^\ell\sw2\) when both roots are small, and \(e_\ell=O(\bmom^{2\ell}\sw4)\).
At small aspect ratio the integrand is a fixed function of the linear root, up to a relative error of order \(\gamma\). Rescale both spectral parameters by \(\sqrt{\sw2\gamma}\). By the sandwich of Theorem SF.7, the rescaled roots \(\sw2q/\gamma\) lie within a factor \((1-\delta)^{-1}\) of \(y(\xi^2)\), where \(y\) is the root of \(xy=(1-y)^2\) and \(\delta=12\gamma(1-\bmom^2)/(1+\bmom^2)\); the normalized \(b_\ell/(\bmom^\ell\sw2)\) lies in \([1-\delta,1]\), and \(qq'e_\ell/\gamma\le\delta\). With \(r=\bmom^\ell\) and \(\mathsf Y_0=y(\xi^2)y(\xi'^2)\le1\), the integrand is increasing in each of these three quantities, so it is squeezed around \(\mathfrak f\defeq r\mathsf Y_0/(1-r^2\mathsf Y_0)\):
\[ \mathfrak f\,\Big(1-\frac{3\delta}{1-\bmom^2}\Big)\le\text{integrand}\le\mathfrak f\,\Big(1+\frac{8\delta}{1-\bmom^2}\Big),\qquad \frac{8\delta}{1-\bmom^2}=\frac{96\gamma}{1+\bmom^2}. \]
The perturbations are measured against the gap \(1-r^2\mathsf Y_0\ge1-\bmom^2\) in the denominator, which is why \(\delta\) is divided by \(1-\bmom^2\). The hypothesis \(\gamma\le(1+\bmom^2)/96\) is exactly \(4\delta\le(1-\bmom^2)/2\), which keeps the perturbed denominator above half the gap.
The limit kernel is a geometric series of products, and each product integrates to \(k_n\). Expanding \(\mathfrak f=\sum_{n\ge0}r^{2n+1}\mathsf Y_0^{n+1}\), the double integral factorizes into squares of one-dimensional integrals. With \(\xi=2\sinh\theta\) the linear root is \(y(\xi^2)=e^{-2\theta}\), so
\[ \int_0^\infty y(\xi^2)^{n+1}\,\dif\xi=\int_0^\infty e^{-2(n+1)\theta}\,2\cosh\theta\,\dif\theta=\frac1{2n+1}+\frac1{2n+3}=k_n , \]
and \(\frac4{\pi^2}\iint\mathfrak f=\Omega(\bmom^\ell)\). This is the lag bound. Summing it over \(\ell\) against \(V_\bmom=1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\Omega(\bmom^\ell)\) gives the first volatility bound, and dividing by \(V_\bmom\ge1\) gives the second.
