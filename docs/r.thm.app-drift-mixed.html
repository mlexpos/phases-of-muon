Theorem SC.1 (Mixed resolvent closure and tail bound)
  1. (i)
    Under Assumption S3, for every compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\),
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.5} \sup_{s\in I}\norm{\mathcal H(s)-T(s)}_\F\longrightarrow0 . \end{equation}
  2. (ii)
    In the frozen-state model under Assumption S1, with \(\max(\Nout,\Nin)\le\gamma_\star B\), \(\norm{\Sigmaout},\norm{\Sigmain}\le C_\Sigma\), \(\norm{\Ahat}_\F=1\) and weights satisfying (SC.1), but without any condition on \(\norm{\Ahat}\) and for every finite dimension, there is \(C\) depending only on \((C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\) such that
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.6} \norm{\mathcal H(s)}_\F\le\frac{C}{s}\quad(s\ge1),\qquad \norm{T(s)}_\F\le\frac{C_\Sigma}{s}\quad(s>0). \end{equation}
    Moreover, for every \(s_\circ>0\) there is \(C_\circ\) depending only on \((s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\) such that
    \begin{equation}\tag{SC.7} \norm{\mathcal H(z)}_\F\le\frac{C_\circ}{\Ree z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb S,\ \Ree z\ge s_\circ). \end{equation}
Proof of Theorem SC.1(i).
The response equation is compared with the exact equation of the candidate. The system (SB.2) at \(z=-s\) gives \(s_1=s\sigma\Fw(q)\), so
\[ \mathsf Y_\star(s)\defeq\Id_{\Nin}+\sigma(-s)\Fw(q(-s))\,\Sigmain=\frac{s\Id+s_1(-s)\Sigmain}{s}=\frac{1}{s}\,\Rtbar(-s)^{-1}, \]
and therefore, by the definition (SC.2) of \(T\),
\[ T(s)\,\mathsf Y_\star(s)=\Lw(q(-s))\,\Rbar(-s)\,\EE\Gt . \]
Subtracting this from (SC.35),
\[ \big(\mathcal H(s)-T(s)\big)\mathsf Y_B(s)=E_B(s)-T(s)\big(\mathsf Y_B(s)-\mathsf Y_\star(s)\big). \]
Both matrices \(\mathsf Y_B\) and \(\mathsf Y_\star\) are the identity plus a positive semidefinite matrix, so their inverses have operator norm at most \(1\), and \(\norm{\mathsf Y_B-\mathsf Y_\star}\le C_\Sigma\abs{b_B-\sigma\Fw(q)}\). With \(\norm{T(s)}_\F\le C_\Sigma/s_-\) from (SC.6), proved below,
\[ \norm{\mathcal H(s)-T(s)}_\F\le\norm{(\mathcal H(s)-T(s))\mathsf Y_B(s)}_\F\,\norm{\mathsf Y_B(s)^{-1}} \le\norm{E_B(s)}_\F+\frac{C_\Sigma^2}{s_-}\abs{b_B(s)-\sigma\Fw(q)} , \]
and both terms tend to zero uniformly in \(s\in I\) by (SC.36) and (SC.37).
□
Proof of Theorem SC.1(ii).
The candidate bound uses \(\Lw\le\sum_j\emaw j\le1\) (since \(\Jfun\le1\)), \(\norm{\Rbar(-s)},\norm{\Rtbar(-s)}\le1/s\) (the matrices \(\tilde s_1\Sigmaout+s\Id\) and \(s_1\Sigmain+s\Id\) are at least \(s\Id\)) and \(\norm{\EE\Gt}_\F\le C_\Sigma\):
\[ \norm{T(s)}_\F\le s\cdot1\cdot\frac1s\cdot C_\Sigma\cdot\frac1s=\frac{C_\Sigma}{s}. \]
The bound on \(\mathcal H\) uses the exact identity (SC.30) without any asymptotic replacement, with the finite lemmas at \(s_\circ=1\); no hypothesis on \(\norm{\Ahat}\) enters. For the source term, (SC.22), \(\norm{R_0^{(j)}}\le1/s\) and Lemma SC.2 give
\[ \Big\|B\,\EE\big[\alpha_{1,j}R_0^{(j)}(\mathsf u_j\tensor\mathsf v_j)\big]\Big\|_\F\le\frac1s\,C_\Sigma\norm{\alpha_{1,j}}_2\le\frac{C\emaw j}{s}. \]
For the Gram term we use the decomposition of the proof of Proposition SC.3, now without replacing the coefficients: \(\EE_{\mathcal G}[(w_j\tensor w_j)\alpha_{2,j}]=\phi_j\Id+\Gamma_j\) with \(\EE\norm{\Gamma_j}_\F\le\sqrt2\norm{\alpha_{2,j}}_2\le C\emaw j^2/s\) by Lemma SB.7 and Lemma SC.2, so that
\[ \Big\|B\,\EE\big[\alpha_{2,j}\mathsf X_0^{(j)}(\mathsf v_j\tensor\mathsf v_j)\big]-\EE\big[\phi_j\mathsf X_0^{(j)}\big]\Sigmain\Big\|_\F \le\frac{C_\Sigma}{2\sqrt s}\,\EE\norm{\Gamma_j}_\F\le\frac{C\emaw j^2}{s^{3/2}}, \]
and \(\EE[\phi_j\mathsf X_0^{(j)}]=b_j\mathcal H(s)+b_j(\mathcal H_j(s)-\mathcal H(s))+\mathcal C_j\) with \(0\le b_j\le C\emaw j^2/s\) (Lemma SC.2), \(\norm{\mathcal H_j-\mathcal H}_\F\le C\emaw j/(s\sqrt B)\) ((SC.21)) and \(\sum_j\norm{\mathcal C_j}_\F\le C\sw2/(s^{3/2}\sqrt B)\) (Lemma SC.5). Summing over \(j\) with (SC.1),
\[ \norm{\mathcal H(s)\mathsf Y_B(s)}_\F\le C\Big[\frac1s+\frac{1}{s^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{s^2\sqrt B}+\frac{1}{s^{3/2}\sqrt B}\Big]\le\frac{C}{s}\qquad(s\ge1,\ B\ge1), \]
where \(\mathsf Y_B=\Id+b_B\Sigmain\) as before. Since \(b_B\ge0\), \(\norm{\mathsf Y_B^{-1}}\le1\) and \(\norm{\mathcal H(s)}_\F\le\norm{\mathcal H(s)\mathsf Y_B(s)}_\F\norm{\mathsf Y_B(s)^{-1}}\le C/s\).
The sector bound (SC.7) is the same argument at \(z\in\mathbb S\) with \(x\defeq\Ree z\ge s_\circ\), using the finite lemmas at \(s_\circ\) in their sector forms and replacing \(s\) by \(x\) in every bound: Lemma SC.6 at \(z\), the norms \(1/x\) and \((2\sqrt x)^{-1}\) of \(R_0^{(j)}\) and \(\mathsf X_0^{(j)}\) from Lemma SB.3, (SC.22) and Lemma SB.7 for complex coefficients, and Lemma SC.2, Lemma SC.3 and Lemma SC.5 at \(z\). Two steps change. First, \(b_j\) is complex, with \(\Ree b_j\ge0\) and \(\abs{b_j}\le\norm{\alpha_{2,j}}_1\le C\emaw j^2/x\), so the series \(b_B=\sum_jb_j\) converges and \(\Ree b_B\ge0\). Second, \(\mathsf Y_B\) is diagonal in an eigenbasis of \(\Sigmain\) with eigenvalues \(1+b_B\lambda_j\), and \(\abs{1+b_B\lambda_j}\ge\Ree(1+b_B\lambda_j)\ge1\), so \(\norm{\mathsf Y_B^{-1}}\le1\). Summing over \(j\),
\[ \norm{\mathcal H(z)}_\F\le\norm{\mathcal H(z)\mathsf Y_B(z)}_\F\le C\Big[\frac1x+\frac{1}{x^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{x^2\sqrt B}+\frac{1}{x^{3/2}\sqrt B}\Big]\le\frac{C_\circ}{x}\qquad(x\ge s_\circ,\ B\ge1), \]
with \(C_\circ\) depending only on \((s_\circ,C_\Sigma,\gamma_\star)\).
□
