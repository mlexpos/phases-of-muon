Theorem SI.1 (Existence, uniqueness and floor)
Assume (M1). The Volterra equation (SI.4) has a unique solution \(Q\) on \([0,\infty)\). It satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{SI.7} 0<F(s)\le Q(s)\le M\defeq\Big(\frac{\bar k+\sqrt{\bar k^2+4F_0}}2\Big)^2\qquad(s\ge0), \end{equation}
and
\[ \lim_{s\to\infty}Q(s)=\bar k^2=\frac{\lr^2\mathcal S^2}4 . \]
If \(\mathcal S>0\) and \(\epsilon>0\), the unique step \(\lr>0\) with floor \(\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4=\epsilon\) is \(\lr=2\sqrt\epsilon/\mathcal S\).
Proof.
The solution is constructed by monotone iteration. Put \(Q_0\defeq F\) and \(Q_{n+1}\defeq F+k*\sqrt{Q_n}\). Positivity of \(k\) gives \(Q_1\ge Q_0\), and induction gives \(Q_{n+1}\ge Q_n\) for every \(n\). The constant \(M\) is the positive root of
\[ M=F_0+\bar k\sqrt M . \]
If \(Q_n\le M\), then (SI.5) gives \(Q_{n+1}\le F_0+\bar k\sqrt M=M\). Each iterate is continuous by Lemma SI.1. The iterates therefore increase to a limit \(Q\) with \(F\le Q\le M\), and letting \(n\to\infty\) in the iteration gives \(Q=F+k*\sqrt Q\). The function \(\sqrt Q\) is bounded, so \(k*\sqrt Q\) is continuous by Lemma SI.1, and \(Q\) is continuous.
Every solution is at least \(F\), because \(k*\sqrt Q\ge0\). On a compact interval \([0,S']\) every solution is therefore at least \(F(S')>0\). Lemma SI.3, with \(G=F\), \(\mathsf k=k\) and \(m=F(S')\), applied in both directions to two solutions, shows that they agree on \([0,S']\). This proves uniqueness.
For the limit, suppose first \(\mathcal S=0\). Then every \(\mathfrak v_i\) vanishes, \(k=0\), and \(Q=F\to0=\bar k^2\).
Suppose \(\mathcal S>0\). The limit inferior of \(Q\) is positive. To see this, choose an index \(j\) with \(\mathfrak v_j>0\), put \(b_j\defeq2\mathfrak d_j\) and \(c_j\defeq\lr\mu_j\mathfrak v_j/2\), and let
\[ z(s)\defeq c_j\int_0^se^{-b_j(s-v)}\sqrt{Q(v)}\,\dif v . \]
Then \(0<z\le k*\sqrt Q\le Q\) for \(s>0\). Differentiating this single integral gives
\[ z'=-b_jz+c_j\sqrt Q\ge-b_jz+c_j\sqrt z . \]
For a fixed \(s_0>0\) the function \(\mathsf r\defeq\sqrt z\) is differentiable on \([s_0,\infty)\) and satisfies \(\mathsf r'\ge-\frac{b_j}2\mathsf r+\frac{c_j}2\). Multiplying by \(e^{b_js/2}\) and integrating from \(s_0\) gives
\[ \mathsf r(s)\ge\frac{c_j}{b_j}+\Big(\mathsf r(s_0)-\frac{c_j}{b_j}\Big)e^{-b_j(s-s_0)/2} . \]
Hence \(\liminf_{s\to\infty}Q(s)\ge(c_j/b_j)^2>0\).
Write \(\ell_-\defeq\liminf Q\) and \(\ell_+\defeq\limsup Q\), both finite by (SI.7). Since \(F\to0\), Lemma SI.2 applied to \(f=\sqrt Q\) in (SI.4) gives
\[ \ell_-\ge\bar k\sqrt{\ell_-},\qquad \ell_+\le\bar k\sqrt{\ell_+} . \]
Because \(\ell_->0\), the first inequality gives \(\ell_-\ge\bar k^2\), and the second gives \(\ell_+\le\bar k^2\). Thus \(Q(s)\to\bar k^2\). Solving \(\lr^2\mathcal S^2/4=\epsilon\) for \(\lr>0\) gives the last assertion.
□
