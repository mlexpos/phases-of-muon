Lemma SG.13 (The supercritical drift profile)
Let \(\alpha>1\) and \(p_\alpha\defeq(\alpha-1)/(2\alpha)\). The function \(\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\) of (SG.25) is finite, positive and continuous on \((0,\infty)\), and
\[ \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\sim\sqrt{y/\sw2}\quad(y\to\infty),\qquad \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)\sim d_\alpha y^{p_\alpha}\quad(y\downarrow0). \]
Under Assumption S4, for every row sequence \(i\to\infty\) with \(i/N\to0\),
\[ \frac{\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i(0)}{b_N}\sim\Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}\big((i_{\mathrm c}/i)^\alpha\big). \]
Proof.
The profile has an exact single-integral form. Put \(t_\infty(m)\defeq m^{1-\alpha}/Q_m\) for \(m>0\). Since \(\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log t_\infty=(1-\alpha)/m-1/E(m)\le-\alpha/m\) by \(E(m)\le m\), the map \(t_\infty\) decreases from \(\infty\) to \(0\), with \(-\frac{\dif}{\dif m}\log t_\infty=\vartheta_\infty(m)\). Writing \(m(t)\) for its inverse, the computation of Lemma SG.8 with \(\Lambda=m^\alpha\) and \(\gamma-m\) replaced by \(\gamma\) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SG.46} \begin{aligned} \Phi_{\alpha,\bmom}(y)&=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{\Lw(Q_{m(u^2)})}{\Fw(Q_{m(u^2)})}\,\frac{y\,m(u^2)^\alpha}{1+y\,m(u^2)^\alpha}\,\dif u\\ &=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\mathcal A(m)E(m)\vartheta_\infty(m)\,m^{-\frac{\alpha+1}2}\,\frac{ym^\alpha}{1+ym^\alpha}\,\dif m . \end{aligned} \end{equation}
In the second form the integrand is \(O(y\,m^{\alpha/2-1})\) near \(m=0\), by \(\mathcal A E=\sqrt{Q_m}\Lw\asymp\sqrt m\) and \(E\vartheta_\infty\le\alpha\), and \(O(m^{-(\alpha+1)/2})\) at infinity, which is integrable because \(\alpha>1\); these bounds are locally uniform in \(y\) and give finiteness and continuity. Positivity is clear.
The large-\(y\) endpoint is the head computation of Lemma SG.11 in the first form. On \(m\le\delta\), \(t_\infty(m)=\sw2m^{-\alpha}(1+O(\delta))\) and \(\Lw/\Fw=\sw2^{-1}(1+O(\delta))\), so the integrand is \(\sw2^{-1}y\sw2/(u^2+y\sw2)\) up to factors \(1+O(\delta)\), with integral \(\frac\pi2\sqrt{y/\sw2}(1+O(\delta))\); the range \(m>\delta\) is a bounded interval of \(u\) with a bounded integrand, which is \(o(\sqrt y)\).
The small-\(y\) endpoint is an averaging in the second form. The term \((\alpha-1)E/m\) of \(E\vartheta_\infty=1+(\alpha-1)E/m\) contributes \(O(y)\) from \(m\le1\) and, since \(E\le C\), at most
\[ Cy^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2\alpha}}\int_{y^{1/\alpha}}^\infty w^{-\frac{\alpha+3}2}\frac{w^\alpha}{1+w^\alpha}\,\dif w \]
from \(m\ge1\) after \(m=y^{-1/\alpha}w\), which is \(o(y^{p_\alpha})\) because \(\alpha>1\). In the remaining term substitute \(m=y^{-1/\alpha}w\):
\[ \frac1\pi\int\mathcal A(m)\,m^{-\frac{\alpha+1}2}\frac{ym^\alpha}{1+ym^\alpha}\,\dif m =\frac{y^{p_\alpha}}\pi\int_0^\infty\mathcal A(y^{-1/\alpha}w)\,\frac{w^{(\alpha-1)/2}}{1+w^\alpha}\,\dif w . \]
On \(w\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]\), (SG.9) with \(W(m)=m^{-(\alpha+1)/2}ym^\alpha/(1+ym^\alpha)\), whose boundary values and variation on the corresponding \(m\)-interval are \(O_\delta(y^{p_\alpha+1/\alpha})\), replaces \(\mathcal A\) by \(\sqrt{2\pi}\) with an error \(o(y^{p_\alpha})\). The ranges \(w<\delta\) and \(w>\delta^{-1}\) contribute \(O(\delta^{(\alpha+1)/2})\) and \(O(\delta^{(\alpha-1)/2})\) times \(y^{p_\alpha}\), by the bounds on \(\mathcal A\). With \(\sqrt{2\pi}/\pi=c_0\) and the integral of Lemma SG.9, the limit is \(d_\alpha y^{p_\alpha}\).
For the joint statement put \(y\defeq(i_{\mathrm c}/i)^\alpha\) and normalize (SG.44) by \(b_N\). Compact ranges of \(m\) converge to the second form of (SG.46). Indeed, by Lemma SG.6(iv), \(\Lambda_N(m)\mu_i\to ym^\alpha\), \(m\Lambda_N'/\Lambda_N\to\alpha\), so that \(E\vartheta_N\to E\vartheta_\infty\), and
\[ \frac{\mathcal B_i(m)}{\pi\gamma b_N}=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma-m}\,\mu_i\,\Lambda_N^{1/2}}{\pi\gamma\,b_N\sqrt m\,(1+\Lambda_N\mu_i)} \longrightarrow\frac1\pi\,m^{-\frac{\alpha+1}2}\frac{ym^\alpha}{1+ym^\alpha}, \]
locally uniformly in \(m>0\) and in \(y\) on compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\). Near \(m=0\), Lemma SG.6(v) and Lemma SG.7 bound the normalized integrand by \(C_ym^{\alpha/2-1}\) for \(1/B\le m\le\delta\) and by \(C_y\sqrt B\,i_{\mathrm c}^{-\alpha/2}m^{-1/2}\) for \(m<1/B\), so the range \(m\le\delta\) contributes \(C_y(\delta^{\alpha/2}+B^{-\alpha/2})\). For \(m>M\), Lemma SG.10 and \(V_\alpha(p)\ge cp^\alpha\) on \(p\le1/2\) bound the normalized tail by
\[ \frac{C\,a_N}{b_N}\Big(\frac\gamma M\Big)^{\frac{\alpha-1}2}=CK_\alpha^{-\alpha/2}M^{-\frac{\alpha-1}2}, \]
using \(a_N/b_N=K_\alpha^{-\alpha/2}\gamma^{(1-\alpha)/2}\). This proves the joint statement for \(y\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\).
The two joint endpoints use the head and the tail. If \(y\to\infty\), then \(m_N(1/\mu_i)=\gamma S_N(x^\alpha)\sim K_\alpha i/B\to0\), so Lemma SG.11 gives \(\sqrt2\,\mathfrak d_i^{\le M}\sim\sqrt{\mu_i/(\gamma\sw2)}=b_N\sqrt{y/\sw2}\), while the tail is \(O(b_NM^{-(\alpha-1)/2})=o(b_N\sqrt y)\). If \(y\to0\), the head is \(O_M(\sqrt{\mu_i/\gamma})=O_M(b_N\sqrt y)=o(b_Ny^{p_\alpha})\). In the tail, the substitution \(p=K_\alpha xu\) of Lemma SG.9 has the lower cutoff \(u_0=M/(\gamma K_\alpha x)=MB/(K_\alpha i)\to0\), so
\[ a_N\Psi_\alpha^{[M/\gamma]}(x)\sim a_Nd_\alpha\sqrt{K_\alpha}\,x^{-(\alpha-1)/2}=b_Nd_\alpha y^{p_\alpha}, \]
the identity being a direct computation from (SG.23). With Lemma SG.10 and \(M\to\infty\) this gives the endpoint, and the three cases together prove the joint statement by passing to subsequences.
□
