Lemma SJ.11 (Haar reduction)
In the Haar model, put \(R_j\defeq h_j^\T\Lambda h_j/\norm{h_j}^2\). Then \(c\le R_j\le L\) almost surely, every entry of \(G^\circ\) is nonzero almost surely, \(\norm S_\F^2=\Nout\Nin\), and
\begin{equation}\tag{SJ.40} \mathcal Q=\Nin\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)\Tr\Lambda+\frac2\pi\,\Nout\sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\EE R_j,\qquad \EE\norm{S^\T u_i}^2=\Nin\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)+\frac2\pi\,\Nout\sum_{j=1}^{\Nin}\EE\frac{(h_j)_i^2}{\norm{h_j}^2}. \end{equation}
Proof.
Condition on \(H\), which is independent of \(U\) and has nonzero columns almost surely by Lemma SJ.10. The \(j\)th column of \(U^\T S\) is \(U^\T\sign(Uh_j)\), and Lemma SJ.7 applies to it. Taking the trace against \(\Lambda\), and the \((i,i)\) entry, gives
\begin{gather*} \EE_U\big[\sign(Uh_j)^\T U\Lambda U^\T\sign(Uh_j)\big]=\Big(1-\frac2\pi\Big)\Tr\Lambda+\frac2\pi\Nout R_j,\\ \EE_U\big[(e_i^\T U^\T\sign(Uh_j))^2\big]=1-\frac2\pi+\frac2\pi\Nout\frac{(h_j)_i^2}{\norm{h_j}^2}. \end{gather*}
Summing over \(j\) and averaging over \(H\) gives (SJ.40); no independence between columns is needed. A Haar rotation of a fixed nonzero vector has no zero coordinate almost surely, so every entry of \(UH\) is nonzero and \(\norm S_\F^2=\Nout\Nin\).
□
